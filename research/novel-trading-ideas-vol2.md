# 200+ Novel Unorthodox Trading Ideas — Volume 2: Market Forensics

218 original short-term trading-signal ideas focused on *market forensics* — reading what other
participants are doing or hiding: short positioning, synthetic share supply, accounting games, forced
mechanical flows, settlement stress, and legal/regulatory paper trails. Generated by a 16-lens agent
swarm seeded with the 12 new success patterns below (plus the 16 from
[Volume 1](novel-trading-ideas.md)) and explicitly banned from repeating any of the 333 Volume-1 entries.
Each idea specifies its data source, a concrete collection method, the instruments and horizon to trade,
a causal mechanism, why it is plausibly under-exploited, and its main failure mode. All were scored by
independent judge agents on **novelty**, **mechanism** plausibility, and **feasibility** (each 1–10,
total out of 30). Companion catalog of documented sources:
[unorthodox-data-points-vol2.md](unorthodox-data-points-vol2.md).

> **Scope & compliance note.** These are research hypotheses, not investment advice, and none have been
> backtested. Every idea here is designed to use **public or lawfully obtainable data** and to trade on
> *inferences* drawn from it. During generation, one lens drifted toward techniques for reaching
> corporate disclosures **before their official release** — probing content-delivery paths, reverse-
> engineering investor-relations back-ends, diffing sitemaps for unlinked pages, and racing embargoed
> wire feeds. Eight such ideas were **removed** before publication: accessing staged or embargoed
> material ahead of publication can constitute unauthorized system access and trading on material
> nonpublic information (MNPI). The retained ideas infer from information that is already public
> (filings, dockets, registries, published calendars, exchange notices). Before trading any signal,
> confirm the data source is genuinely public and that your use complies with securities law, exchange
> rules, and each provider's terms of service.

## 12 new success patterns (Volume 2)

Structural reasons the forensic/plumbing/positioning signals work, extracted from the documented Volume-2
sources and used to seed every brainstorming agent (these extend the 16 patterns from Volume 1):

1. **Regulatory close-out clocks turn failure into scheduled buying** — When a trade settles badly, regulation starts a countdown (Reg SHO T+35, 13-day threshold-list close-outs, buy-ins of 'like kind and quantity', TMPG fails charges) that converts the failure into dated, price-insensitive forced purchases. Fails-to-deliver tapes are therefore a public calendar of future forced demand — look for any rule that transforms an unsettled obligation into a mandatory market transaction on a knowable date.
2. **The financing market breaks before the asset market** — The market for carrying a position — stock borrow fees, utilization, recalls, repo specialness, option-implied borrow cost — prices crowding and squeeze risk before the underlying asset moves: 100% utilization means no new shorts can be added and existing ones are bleeding fees toward forced covering. Monitor the funding/borrow leg of any levered or short position; when its cost explodes or supply hits capacity, a mechanical unwind of the crowded side is imminent.
3. **Forced flows are computable functions of public observables** — Mandates and hedging rules make large flows a deterministic formula: time-triggered (Russell reconstitution prints, Goldman roll windows, leveraged/VIX ETP end-of-day rebalances, month-end pension trades, quarter-end NAV games) and level-triggered (dealer gamma flip points, the JPM collar strikes, autocallable knock-in barriers). Because both the calendar and the price map of the forced flow are reconstructable from public data (open interest, AUM, index rules), front-running it and providing liquidity into it are systematic trades.
4. **Reconciliation gaps between parallel ledgers expose synthetic supply** — The same quantity is recorded in multiple official places — transfer-agent/DRS counts vs street-name holdings, proxy votes cast vs shares outstanding, ETF short interest vs shares created, ADRs issued vs foreign shares deposited — and any mismatch directly measures hidden synthetic supply or phantom claims. The gap both flags overpricing from unbacked float and predicts the forced unwind when regulators or mechanics compel the two records to reconcile.
5. **Withdrawal by private-access counterparties is the tell** — Parties with legal access to the private books — auditors, money-market funds holding a bank's CP, relationship lenders, securities lenders issuing recalls — reveal what they've seen by quietly withdrawing service, demanding more compensation, or hedging (auditor resignations, audit-fee jumps, N-MFP line cuts, single-name CDS runs from insider banks). Refusal-to-keep-vouching is severe adverse selection: track any informed counterparty whose defensive retreat is observable in a filing or a price.
6. **Neglect arbitrage on dense mandatory text** — Outcome-determining details sit in long public documents that effectively nobody reads — 10-K language diffs, SEC comment letters buried on EDGAR, covenant trapdoors, CLO trustee reports — so the information diffuses into prices over months, not milliseconds. Unlike latency arbitrage, the edge is reading at all, not reading fastest: systematically parse any dense, compulsory document where a clause or wording change predates its eventual exercise or realization.
7. **Penalty-priced emergency liquidity is an unfakeable confession** — No actor pays above-market for backstop funding unless private options are exhausted, so take-up at stigmatized facilities — Fed discount window, Standing Repo Facility, SOMA securities lending, record FHLB issuance, outlier CD rates, panicked full revolver draws — is a revealed-preference distress signal that precedes failures, downgrades, and policy pivots. Find any lender-of-last-resort or desperation-priced funding channel whose usage is published, and treat rising usage as a hidden run made visible.
8. **Conservation laws in settlement accounts betray covert action** — Money that is moved secretly must still land somewhere in a regularly published balance sheet: FX intervention shows up in SNB weekly sight deposits, reserve liquidation drains the NY Fed custody line, Treasury's cash burn prints daily in the DTS. Accounting identities convert official secrecy into a weekly or daily tell — map where a hidden flow is forced to settle and watch that account.
9. **Collateral and margin triggers hard-wire cascades at knowable thresholds** — Leverage secured against the asset itself creates trigger levels — pledged-share margin covenants, CLO OC-test and CCC-bucket limits, NSCC clearing-fund VaR charges — where a price or ratings move forces non-discretionary selling (or, at the clearinghouse, shuts off the order flow driving the move). Because pledges, test cushions, and margin formulas are disclosed or computable, the cascade's ignition points can be mapped before they fire.
10. **The government customer publishes its order book before the income statement** — For companies with revenue concentrated in one sovereign customer, procurement exhaust — state DOT lettings, USACE low-bid abstracts, RFP intent-to-award notices, GAO protest dockets with statutory decision dates, FPDS termination mods — is the revenue line published months before guidance, often with dated catalysts attached. Sum the public purchase orders and cancellations of the price-insensitive state and you have a pre-earnings feed for every contractor pure-play.
11. **Influence expenditure prices private regulatory expectations** — What principals spend on persuasion — deal-specific lobbying surges, FARA registrations, revolving-door and politically connected hires, astroturf comment volume, donation-linked tariff exclusions — is a costly, disclosed revealed-preference estimate of the regulatory damage or windfall they privately foresee. Scraping influence registers turns the defense budget of the lobbied party into a forecast of the pending official decision.
12. **Accounting arithmetic schedules its own reversal** — Earnings pulled forward through accruals, stretched receivables, or capitalized costs (Sloan accruals, Beneish M-Score flags) are borrowed from future periods and must mechanically be repaid, so the ledger's internal identities predict negative surprises at subsequent, dated report releases. Screen for any metric where today's flattering number arithmetically becomes tomorrow's headwind, and position for the reckoning at the next scheduled print.

## Squeeze-setup & synthetic-supply detection

### 1. Short-Exempt Pulse  ·  18/30
- **Data:** The ignored ShortExemptVolume column in FINRA's daily Reg SHO short-sale volume files, joined against the exchanges' daily Rule 201 short-sale-circuit-breaker trigger lists, so exempt-flagged shorting can be classified as occurring inside or outside an active price-test restriction.
- **Collection:** Download FINRA's daily pipe-delimited files (cdn.finra.org/equity/regsho/daily/CNMSshvol{YYYYMMDD}.txt and the FNSQ/FNYX variants) and Nasdaq Trader's / NYSE's daily 'Short Sale Price Test Restricted' securities lists; compute per-ticker short-exempt/total-short ratios and z-scores versus each name's trailing 60-day baseline, flagging exempt spikes on days when no SSR is active (where exemption use is anomalous) separately from SSR-active days.
- **Trade:** Buy shares or 1-3 week call spreads on high-short-interest names showing a 5x+ short-exempt ratio spike concurrent with borrow stress, holding 2-10 sessions into the expected forced-cover window; fade (sell into) the pop once exempt ratios normalize.
- **Mechanism:** Short-exempt marking is dominated by market makers claiming bona-fide exemptions (locate exceptions, arbitrage, odd-lot obligations); a surge means liquidity providers are shorting on downticks faster than they can locate stock, which mechanically seeds fails and Rule 204 close-out obligations days later — a timestamped precursor of price-insensitive buy-to-cover flow.
- **Why under-exploited:** Everyone consumes the short-volume column and DIX-style aggregates; almost nobody parses the exempt sub-column, and nobody joins it to the SSR trigger calendar to separate legitimate from anomalous exemption use — the join requires stitching three fragmented exchange files daily, which is too fiddly for institutions chasing bigger datasets.
- **Risk:** Exempt volume is noisy and can be dominated by benign odd-lot and ETF-arbitrage activity; if the signal becomes popular, market makers' compliance desks will tighten marking behavior and the anomaly frequency collapses.

### 2. Record-Date Recall Wave  ·  18/30
- **Data:** Proxy-contest and special-meeting record dates buried in DEFC14A/PREC14A/DEF 14A filings and 8-K meeting announcements — dates on which securities lenders must recall lent shares if they want to vote, mechanically shrinking borrow supply in crowded shorts.
- **Collection:** Stream EDGAR's full-text search API for contested-solicitation form types and 8-K Item 5.07/meeting-notice language, extract the record date, and cross it against any public borrow-stress gauge (broker hard-to-borrow flags, exchange threshold-adjacent behavior, spiking retail HTB indicators) to build a calendar of high-utilization names with a recall-forcing vote 5-15 days out.
- **Trade:** Long shares or short-dated calls on high-utilization names beginning ~7 trading days before a contested record date, exiting on or just before the record date; size up when an activist has publicly urged holders to recall and vote.
- **Mechanism:** Beneficial owners cannot vote lent shares, so contested votes trigger institutional recall campaigns; recalls force borrowers to return stock by record-date settlement, and when utilization is near 100% there is no replacement supply, converting a governance formality into dated, price-insensitive forced covering.
- **Why under-exploited:** Proxy calendars and stock-loan data live in different departments of every fund; nobody systematically fuses them, and contested-meeting record dates in mid-caps are too small and episodic for quant desks while being unreadable-by-hand for retail.
- **Risk:** Vote apathy — many lenders now deliberately keep lending through votes for fee income, so recall waves fail to materialize; index-fund lending programs may have enough slack supply to absorb recalls without a squeeze.

### 3. Special-Dividend Short Tax  ·  17/30
- **Data:** Special cash dividend declarations (8-Ks and press releases) landing on names where borrow is already stressed — a declared, dated liability that every short must pay in full on top of exploding borrow fees.
- **Collection:** Run EDGAR full-text search and PR-wire scrapes for 'special cash dividend' / 'one-time dividend' declarations daily, pull ex-dates from the Nasdaq Daily List corporate-actions feed, and filter to names with utilization above ~90% or double-digit borrow fees using any public HTB indicator; rank by dividend as a percentage of share price.
- **Trade:** Long shares (not calls, which get strike-adjusted) from declaration date through two days before ex-date in screened names, a 1-3 week hold; optionally short the post-ex-date reversal once the forced-cover bid disappears.
- **Mechanism:** A short seller owes the full dividend to the lender in cash, so a large special dividend converts an open short from a financing bleed into a certain, dated cash loss; rational shorts race to cover before ex-date, and in a name with no borrow slack their covering is the marginal bid on a knowable schedule.
- **Why under-exploited:** Special dividends in smaller HTB names are announced in sleepy 8-Ks, and the interaction term (dividend size x borrow scarcity) isn't in any standard screen; the well-known options 'dividend play' crowd games open interest, not the equity forced-cover leg.
- **Risk:** Boards of heavily shorted companies rarely have the cash to declare meaningful specials, so the screen fires infrequently; shorts may pre-hedge with single-stock options, muting the cover bid, and the post-ex reversal can erase gains if exit timing slips.

### 4. ASR Borrow Shock  ·  19/30
- **Data:** Accelerated share repurchase (ASR) agreement disclosures in 8-Ks and 10-Qs — under an ASR the dealer borrows a large block to deliver shares to the issuer up front, then buys stock in the open market over weeks to close its own short.
- **Collection:** EDGAR full-text search for 'accelerated share repurchase' in 8-K and 10-Q filings, parsing notional size, upfront delivery percentage (typically ~80%), and the stated completion window; compute the implied daily dealer purchase rate as a percentage of ADV and flag names where the ASR notional exceeds ~10 days of volume or existing short interest is elevated.
- **Trade:** Long shares or 2-6 week call spreads from the ASR announcement through the estimated execution window in small/mid caps where implied dealer buying exceeds ~5% of daily volume; intraday VWAP-tracking longs on the heaviest execution days.
- **Mechanism:** The ASR dealer's upfront share delivery requires an immediate large borrow (spiking loan demand in already-tight names), and its hedge unwind is near price-insensitive open-market buying on a disclosed timetable — a double squeeze vector of borrow-supply drain plus scheduled forced demand.
- **Why under-exploited:** Buyback screens treat ASRs as generic repurchase news and miss the borrow-leg mechanics entirely; the dealer-borrow demand shock is invisible unless you understand ASR settlement structure, and the affected small-cap names are below institutional-desk size thresholds.
- **Risk:** Most ASRs are done by mega-caps where the flow is a rounding error of ADV; dealers may pre-position or source the upfront delivery internally, muting the borrow shock, and completion windows are estimates the dealer can stretch.

### 5. Self-Tender Float Vacuum  ·  19/30
- **Data:** Dutch-auction self-tender offers (Schedule TO-I filings) in shorted names — a dated, guaranteed removal of float that resets the shares-outstanding-versus-short-interest arithmetic on the expiration date printed in the filing.
- **Collection:** Poll EDGAR's daily index for SC TO-I and TO-I/A filings, extract tender size, price range, and expiration; join with FINRA bi-monthly short interest and float estimates to compute post-tender short-interest-as-percent-of-remaining-float; monitor the final proration 8-K for the actual retirement count.
- **Trade:** Long shares/calls in names where the tender would push SI above ~30% of post-tender float, entered after the tender announcement and held through expiration plus 1-2 weeks as the shrunken float reprices; a 2-4 week horizon.
- **Mechanism:** The tender retires real, deliverable shares while the short position is unchanged, so days-to-cover and borrow scarcity mechanically worsen the moment the tender settles; shorts modeling the pre-tender float wake up over-levered against a smaller lendable pool, and their covering meets a float that just shrank.
- **Why under-exploited:** SC TO-I is one of the sleepiest filing types on EDGAR — self-tenders are mostly done by boring cash-rich small caps that squeeze screens ignore, and the second-order float arithmetic is published nowhere; sell-side coverage of these names is near zero.
- **Risk:** Odd-lot arbitrageurs and proration mean the actual retired count often disappoints versus the announced maximum; if the stock trades above the tender range the offer fails entirely and the thesis evaporates.

### 6. Early-Exercise Tell  ·  18/30
- **Data:** Overnight open-interest collapses in deep in-the-money calls on non-dividend-paying stocks — visible in daily OCC/exchange open-interest files — which reveal early exercise, an act that is economically irrational unless the exerciser urgently needs deliverable shares (to satisfy recalls, buy-ins, or Rule 204 close-outs) or to capture extreme borrow.
- **Collection:** Diff consecutive days of free delayed open-interest data (OCC series files or Cboe delayed chains) for calls more than ~20% ITM; flag cases where OI drops by materially more than the prior day's volume in that series with no ex-dividend date within the option's life, and aggregate exercised-contract deltas into an implied 'shares demanded for delivery' count per ticker.
- **Trade:** Long shares or 1-2 week call verticals on names showing a cluster of non-dividend early exercises totaling more than ~0.5% of float, positioned for the delivery-stress resolution over the following 3-10 sessions.
- **Mechanism:** Exercising early forfeits remaining time value, so a rational holder only does it when physical shares are worth more than the optionality — i.e., borrow cost exceeds extrinsic value or a settlement obligation is due; clustered early exercise is therefore a costly, unfakeable confession that deliverable stock has become scarce, and the exercise itself transmits buy pressure to the assigned writers who must source shares.
- **Why under-exploited:** Everyone watches deep-ITM prints; almost nobody diffs OI files to catch the exercise event itself, which requires archiving daily chains — a storage-and-parsing chore with no vendor product because the events are episodic and concentrated in unglamorous names.
- **Risk:** OI changes also reflect position transfers, compression trades, and error corrections, producing false positives; the signal window is short and the OI data is T+1, so slow parsing eats most of the edge.

### 7. Phantom Float Census  ·  10/30
- **Data:** The reconciliation gap between summed institutional 13F holdings plus insider Form 4/5 positions and the issuer's XBRL-reported shares outstanding — an over-ownership ratio above 100% directly measures synthetic longs created by short selling beyond what the bi-monthly short-interest print admits.
- **Collection:** Bulk-download 13F-HR XML from EDGAR each quarterly deadline (Feb/May/Aug/Nov 14), dedupe manager-custodian double counting via CIK relationships, add insider holdings from Form 4/5 and any company-disclosed registered-holder counts, and divide by dei:EntityCommonStockSharesOutstanding from the XBRL frames API; rank names by the level and quarter-over-quarter jump in the over-ownership ratio.
- **Trade:** In the 1-3 weeks following each 13F deadline, buy shares or calls on names whose over-ownership ratio jumped well above 100% while reported short interest stayed flat (hidden synthetic supply), pairing against short positions in sector peers; also fade names where the ratio collapsed (covert covering already done).
- **Mechanism:** Every share sold short creates a second beneficial owner, so aggregate claimed ownership exceeding shares outstanding is an accounting identity for true economic short exposure — including swap-hidden and misreported shorts; when the identity-implied short position vastly exceeds the FINRA print, the market is underpricing the eventual forced covering that any catalyst will trigger.
- **Why under-exploited:** The assembly is miserable: 13F double-counting requires entity-graph cleaning that academics do with 18-month lags and no one does within days of the filing deadline; the signal only exists cross-sectionally in mid/small caps that big quants skip because position sizes are small.
- **Risk:** 13F reporting errors, ETF-holding double counts, and stale share counts generate ratios over 100% for boring reasons; the data is 45 days stale at publication, so the implied short may already be gone.

### 8. Dilution Ammo Gauge  ·  17/30
- **Data:** The remaining at-the-market (ATM) offering capacity of would-be squeeze names — reconstructed from S-3 shelf registrations, 424B5 prospectus supplements, 10-Q ATM-usage disclosures, and baby-shelf (Instruction I.B.6) one-third-of-float math — which determines whether management can print shares into any rally.
- **Collection:** For each high-short-interest small cap, pull S-3 effectiveness status and capacity from EDGAR, subtract shares sold under the ATM (disclosed each 10-Q and visible faster via diffs in the XBRL cover-page share count between filings), and apply the I.B.6 cap for sub-$75M-float issuers; maintain a per-ticker 'dilution ammo remaining' figure updated on every new 424B or 10-Q.
- **Trade:** Buy calls only on squeeze candidates with exhausted or legally capped ATM capacity (rallies can run); sell rips and buy puts on candidates with large live ATMs the moment a spike begins, a 2-10 day horizon on each squeeze event.
- **Mechanism:** A squeeze needs inelastic supply; an issuer with a live ATM is a price-insensitive seller of unlimited new shares into strength, capping the move and repaying the shorts, whereas a baby-shelf-capped issuer legally cannot supply the demand, so the same covering flow produces convex price response — the shelf paperwork is literally the supply curve.
- **Why under-exploited:** Retail squeeze chasers never read S-3s and quants don't model I.B.6 arithmetic; the capacity figure requires stitching three filing types per name, a manual-ish task perfectly sized for a small team and worthless at institutional scale.
- **Risk:** Issuers can file a new shelf overnight or do a direct offering outside the ATM, instantly restoring supply; XBRL cover-page counts lag actual issuance by weeks, so 'ammo remaining' can be overstated at the worst moment.

### 9. Tokyo Daily Short Ledger  ·  15/30
- **Data:** Japan's daily, name-by-name short-position disclosure regime — JPX/FSA publish every position above 0.5% of shares outstanding, per fund, every trading day — for issuers that also trade as US ADRs, giving a daily short tape the US market only gets bi-monthly and anonymously.
- **Collection:** Scrape the daily short-position CSV/XBRL files from jpx.co.jp (and ASIC's daily aggregate short reports for Australian dual-listeds), map local tickers to US ADRs (e.g., Japanese tech, banks, and industrial ADRs), and compute day-over-day position changes per named fund, flagging rapid multi-fund covering or building.
- **Trade:** Trade the US-listed ADR (shares or listed options) same-day on Tokyo's disclosure: buy ADRs when several named funds begin covering a crowded short (the local squeeze propagates), short ADRs on synchronized building; horizon 1-10 days, exploiting that US holders see only stale aggregate data.
- **Mechanism:** The named daily ledger reveals crowding, capitulation, and covering in near real time; ADR prices are set substantially by US investors without this granularity, so disclosed covering waves in Tokyo precede the ADR's repricing, and the ADR-local arbitrage transmits the forced flow with a lag of hours to days.
- **Why under-exploited:** The files are Japanese-language, fragmented across daily XBRL postings, and require a local-ticker-to-ADR mapping nobody maintains publicly; US squeeze traders are wholly focused on domestic FINRA data and ignore that a superior disclosure regime covers the same economic exposure.
- **Risk:** Positions below 0.5% are invisible, so covers can complete under the threshold; ADR liquidity in smaller Japanese names is thin, and vendor products could commoditize the mapping quickly once noticed.

### 10. DTC Chill Wire  ·  13/30
- **Data:** DTCC 'Important Notices' announcing chills and global locks on specific CUSIPs — administrative actions that freeze or restrict electronic book-entry settlement for a security, instantly breaking the plumbing that shorts and market makers rely on to deliver shares.
- **Collection:** Scrape the DTCC Important Notices page (dtcc.com legal notices, updated as PDFs with CUSIP lists) on a 15-minute loop, parse for 'chill', 'global lock', and release notices, and match CUSIPs to tickers; cross-reference against names with elevated short interest or active retail followings.
- **Trade:** Intraday-to-multi-day: buy the deliverability-frozen name (or its listed options if any) on a chill in a shorted stock, since fails become uncloseable and covering must happen at any price; conversely exit/short on chill-release notices that restore settlement supply.
- **Mechanism:** A chill halts the mechanical ability to move shares through DTC, so every pending delivery obligation — including short-sale close-outs — must be satisfied from a suddenly illiquid physical/registered channel; artificial supply routes are severed at the infrastructure layer, and the price of a genuine deliverable share gaps.
- **Why under-exploited:** The notices are PDFs on a compliance page no trading system watches, published for back-office audiences; the affected names are mostly micro-caps that institutions cannot touch, leaving the reaction window to whoever reads the plumbing announcements first.
- **Risk:** Most chills hit already-dead shell companies with no tradable market or borrow, making the signal unmonetizable; brokers may restrict trading in chilled names, trapping positions, and chills sometimes accompany fraud halts that gap the price down instead.

### 11. Wholesaler Internalization Census  ·  16/30
- **Data:** FINRA's OTC Transparency data — weekly per-security, per-member off-exchange volume showing exactly how many shares Citadel Securities, Virtu, and other wholesalers internalized in each name — a direct census of retail order-flow concentration, the fuel of every modern squeeze.
- **Collection:** Download the weekly non-ATS OTC Transparency files from otctransparency.finra.org (Tier 1 NMS published on a two-week delay, other names four weeks), compute each ticker's wholesaler share of total consolidated volume and its week-over-week z-score, and overlay on high-short-interest names.
- **Trade:** Buy 2-4 week calls on names where wholesaler internalization share is accelerating off a low base while borrow remains stressed (retail herd arriving into inelastic supply); fade names where internalization share peaks and rolls over (the retail bid is exhausting), trading the reversal over 1-2 weeks.
- **Mechanism:** Retail market orders route almost entirely to wholesalers, so a rising internalization share is a measured, per-name headcount of retail participation; in a supply-constrained short, sustained retail buying is the exogenous flow that pushes shorts through fee and margin pain into forced covering — and its exhaustion timestamps the top.
- **Why under-exploited:** The dataset is free but published as awkward weekly member-level files that almost nobody joins to short-positioning data; DIX-style products aggregate short volume, not wholesaler identity, and the two-to-four-week lag scares off HFTs while still leading multi-week squeeze cycles.
- **Risk:** The publication lag means the fastest squeezes are over before the data prints; wholesale internalization also rises on news-driven retail selling, so direction must be inferred from other sources, and FINRA could change the publication format or delay.

### 12. Lendable Float Ceiling  ·  18/30
- **Data:** Per-security securities-lending detail buried in fund N-PORT filings — each registered fund reports, position by position, shares on loan and lending program participation — enabling reconstruction of the true lendable supply ceiling for any stock, against which vendor 'utilization' numbers are miscalibrated.
- **Collection:** Bulk-parse N-PORT XML from EDGAR (quarterly, 60-day lag) to sum, per CUSIP, shares held by lending-enabled funds versus non-lending holders; add 13F concentration and insider/registered holdings to estimate maximum lendable float, and maintain a ratio of reported short interest to this ceiling rather than to total float.
- **Trade:** Use the ceiling to re-rank squeeze candidates: buy multi-week calls on names where short interest exceeds ~80% of true lendable supply even when headline SI-percent-of-float looks moderate; trigger entries on faster signals (fee spikes, catalyst dates) within this pre-screened universe.
- **Mechanism:** Squeezes ignite when borrow demand hits the supply ceiling, not when SI-percent-of-float crosses a round number; the ceiling is set by which holders actually lend — index funds lend, insiders and DRS holders don't — so a stock with concentrated non-lending ownership hits the wall far earlier than crowd models expect, and the mispricing of that distance is harvestable.
- **Why under-exploited:** N-PORT's lending fields are essentially unread — vendors sell utilization measured against their own visible pool, not the structural ceiling — and the fusion of N-PORT, 13F, and insider data per name is exactly the kind of dirty multi-filing join individuals can do and institutions won't for small caps.
- **Risk:** The 60-day N-PORT lag means ownership can shift before the ceiling updates; lending-program participation changes (funds switching agents) silently move the ceiling, and estimation errors produce false near-the-wall readings.

### 13. Own-Share Tape Collision  ·  19/30
- **Data:** The UK's daily 'Transaction in Own Shares' RNS announcements — issuers executing buybacks must disclose every day's purchases by the next morning — collided against disclosed short positions, yielding a daily arithmetic clock of float being retired underneath known shorts.
- **Collection:** Scrape the LSE RNS feed (or Investegate) each morning for 'Transaction in Own Shares' notices, parse shares bought and running totals, and maintain per-name cumulative-retirement-versus-free-float and versus-disclosed-short-position ratios; compute a 'days until buyback exceeds total disclosed short interest' countdown.
- **Trade:** Long UK single names (via IBKR shares, CFDs, or spread bets) where the countdown falls below ~20 trading days while disclosed shorts are not covering, holding 2-6 weeks; add on RNS days showing the issuer accelerating its daily purchase rate.
- **Mechanism:** A buyback is a price-insensitive daily buyer removing deliverable shares permanently, while the short position needs those same shares to close; the daily RNS tape makes the collision's arithmetic public in near real time, so the moment retirement pace outstrips lendable supply the shorts' exit narrows on a computable schedule.
- **Why under-exploited:** The RNS buyback tape is read name-by-name by IR watchers, never aggregated into a float-depletion clock, and never joined to the short register; UK mid-caps are beneath US squeeze crowds' attention and UK institutions treat buyback notices as boilerplate.
- **Risk:** Issuers can pause buybacks without notice (closed periods around results), stopping the clock; disclosed shorts below the 0.5% publication threshold hide part of the collision, and thin UK mid-cap liquidity raises implementation cost.

### 14. Reverse-Split Plumbing Pop  ·  13/30
- **Data:** The corporate-action calendar of reverse splits in heavily shorted small caps — from the Nasdaq Daily List and OTC Markets corporate-action feeds — which force a settlement-system-wide reconciliation of every open position, loan, and fail on a single effective date.
- **Collection:** Pull upcoming reverse splits daily from nasdaqtrader.com's Daily List and OTC Markets' corporate actions page, filter to names in the top decile of short interest relative to float, and build an event calendar of effective dates; track pre/post-split spread and volume behavior for each event.
- **Trade:** Buy short-dated straddles or strangles 2-3 days before the effective date and exit within a week after, monetizing the recurring post-split volatility spike; take the long side outright only when borrow indicators are already stressed pre-split.
- **Mechanism:** A reverse split forces every custodian, lender, and clearing firm to re-book positions and round fractional entitlements simultaneously; open stock loans get recalled for processing and unsettled obligations must reconcile at the new ratio, temporarily severing borrow supply and generating documented post-split settlement stress — a scheduled plumbing shock whose date is public weeks ahead.
- **Why under-exploited:** Reverse splits are coded as pure distress signals, so systematic traders avoid the names entirely and never model the settlement-mechanics window; the events are frequent, small-cap, and options-illiquid enough that no desk harvests the recurring vol pattern.
- **Risk:** Most reverse-split names keep bleeding, so directional longs face negative drift; wide options spreads in these names can eat the straddle edge, and brokers' improved corporate-action automation may be shrinking the plumbing disruption over time.


## Filing-diff archaeology

### 15. ATM Reload Diff  ·  16/30
- **Data:** SEC EDGAR 424B5 prospectus supplements and at-the-market (ATM) sales agreements filed as exhibits by small-cap issuers, versioned each time capacity is added or terms change.
- **Collection:** Poll the EDGAR full-text search API (efts.sec.gov) daily for 424B5/S-3 filings containing 'at the market offering' or 'sales agreement'; for each issuer, diff successive Plan of Distribution sections against the prior supplement to catch capacity upsizes, new placement agents, and deletion of minimum-price language.
- **Trade:** Short the issuer's stock or buy 2-6 week puts on microcap biotech and story stocks that quietly upsize ATM capacity after a price run-up; cover as the dilution drift plays out over days to weeks.
- **Mechanism:** An upsized ATM is a standing commitment to sell shares into strength; the agent's algorithmic supply caps rallies and mechanically pressures price, and the supplement diff typically precedes the actual share sales by days, before quarterly disclosure reveals volumes sold.
- **Why under-exploited:** Headline scanners flag brand-new shelf registrations but nobody diffs the boilerplate distribution sections of supplements, where capacity and agent changes hide; the work is per-issuer version control that institutions consider beneath them.
- **Risk:** The same microcaps carry brutal borrow fees, and some upsized ATMs are never used, so a momentum squeeze can run over the short before supply ever arrives.

### 16. Call Report Amendment Autopsy  ·  20/30
- **Data:** FFIEC Central Data Repository (CDR) quarterly call reports (FFIEC 031/041/051) for the roughly 700 exchange-listed US banks, including revised resubmissions and free-text itemizations in Schedule RI-E and memo items in RC-O/RC-N.
- **Collection:** Pull the CDR Public Data Distribution API daily and archive every facsimile version per bank so resubmissions diff against originals; parse RI-E 'other noninterest expense' text items, RC-N past-due migration, and uninsured-deposit memo lines, flagging adverse amendments and first-appearance text items like 'litigation settlement'.
- **Trade:** Short single-name community and regional banks (hedged with KRE) within days of an adverse call-report amendment or a new adverse footnote item, holding 1-6 weeks into the bank's 10-Q or next earnings release.
- **Mechanism:** Call reports land about 30 days after quarter-end with more granularity than earnings releases, and an amendment is a quasi-restatement: it almost always precedes the same bad news reaching the 10-Q or an 8-K, so the versioned diff front-runs the documents analysts actually read.
- **Why under-exploited:** Bank analysts consume CDR data as point-in-time levels via vendors; nobody treats it as a versioned document stream or reads the free-text footnotes for the long tail of $200M-$2B banks with zero coverage.
- **Risk:** Most amendments are clerical, so the signal is sparse and needs a benign-vs-adverse taxonomy; small bank stocks are also illiquid, hard to borrow, and slow to reprice.

### 17. FDD Item 19 AUV Leak  ·  18/30
- **Data:** Franchise Disclosure Documents filed annually (plus mid-year amendments) with state regulators; Wisconsin DFI and Minnesota CARDS run free searchable public FDD archives, as does California DFPI.
- **Collection:** Scrape the Wisconsin, Minnesota, and California franchise portals for new FDD versions from listed franchisors (WEN, PZZA, DNUT, WING, JACK, DIN, PLNT, SERV and peers); extract Item 19 average-unit-volume and cohort-performance tables plus Item 7 buildout-cost ranges and diff year-over-year and against interim amendments.
- **Trade:** Long/short the franchisor's equity in the weeks between the spring FDD posting and the next quarterly print, sized by the gap between disclosed AUV trends and street same-store-sales models; treat off-cycle Item 19 amendments as an immediate short trigger.
- **Mechanism:** Franchisors are legally required to publish unit-level economics to sell franchises, so Item 19 discloses granularity of restaurant performance that never appears in investor materials, and a mid-year amendment means results moved enough that the old representation became legally unusable.
- **Why under-exploited:** Item 20 unit counts are already picked over, but the Item 19 performance tables and Item 7 cost-inflation diffs sit in state PDF portals with no API, a curation job too manual for institutions covering large-cap restaurants.
- **Risk:** Item 19 typically covers the prior fiscal year, so part of the surprise may be priced by the time the FDD posts, and franchisors have discretion to change cohorts and presentation to blunt comparability.

### 18. EBITDA Definition Creep  ·  17/30
- **Data:** Credit agreement amendments filed as EX-10 exhibits to 8-Ks and 10-Qs on EDGAR, which restate defined terms and covenant levels each time the borrower and lenders renegotiate.
- **Collection:** Use EDGAR full-text search for exhibits containing 'Amendment No.' plus 'Credit Agreement'; extract and word-diff the definitions of Consolidated EBITDA, Restricted Payments, and Unrestricted Subsidiary and the covenant grid against the prior version of the same agreement, scoring add-back expansions and deferred covenant step-downs.
- **Trade:** Short the borrower's equity or buy put spreads into the following one or two earnings prints (2-8 week horizon) when EBITDA add-backs are loosened; secondarily trade BDCs and lenders publicly holding the same loan.
- **Mechanism:** Lenders concede definitional loosening only when projected covenant compliance is failing, so the amendment is a dated confession of management's private forecast that predates the guidance cut, while inflated add-backs overstate the headroom equity holders believe exists.
- **Why under-exploited:** Covenant Review sells redline intelligence to credit funds, but equity investors almost never read EX-10 diffs, and no public tool version-controls credit agreements per issuer — the ugliness of the parsing is the moat.
- **Risk:** Many amendments are benign repricings, and the immediate equity reaction to 'liquidity secured' can be a relief rally, so entries must be timed to the subsequent print rather than the amendment itself.

### 19. Warranty Fine-Print Retreat  ·  20/30
- **Data:** Manufacturer warranty booklets and warranty-terms webpages for autos, appliances, HVAC, power tools, solar inverters, and e-bikes (e.g., Hyundai/Kia powertrain pages, Generac, Carrier, SolarEdge, Whirlpool warranty PDFs).
- **Collection:** Run a monthly crawler over a curated list of warranty-term URLs and PDFs, backfilled with the Wayback Machine CDX API for history; diff coverage years, mileage caps, transferability, newly excluded components, and registration hurdles per product line.
- **Trade:** Short the manufacturer (or trade it against a competitor pair) for 2-6 weeks after a coverage retreat, positioned into the next earnings where warranty accruals surface; go long or cover when a maker extends coverage, a revealed-confidence signal from internal reliability data.
- **Mechanism:** Warranty terms are set by actuaries reading internal claims-frequency data outsiders never see; shortening coverage or newly excluding a specific component is a costly admission that failure rates are running hot, which later materializes as warranty-reserve builds, recalls, and margin misses.
- **Why under-exploited:** These changes are unannounced, live on marketing pages rather than filings, and no data vendor versions warranty language — the per-company curation is exactly the tedious, unscalable work institutions decline.
- **Risk:** Term changes can be driven by regulation or competitive positioning rather than reliability, and the lag from fine print to P&L can exceed a short horizon, so the trade needs an earnings catalyst to cash the signal.

### 20. Golden Parachute Freshening  ·  19/30
- **Data:** 8-K Item 5.02 filings and EX-10 exhibits containing amended change-in-control severance agreements, new executive retention plans, and the year-over-year diffs of proxy-statement CIC payout tables.
- **Collection:** Stream EDGAR 8-Ks and full-text search for 'change in control' plus 'amended and restated'; diff each company's severance/CIC exhibits against its own prior versions and score clusters where multiple executives' agreements are refreshed simultaneously outside the annual comp cycle, especially with cash-severance upsizing or excise-tax mechanics cleanup.
- **Trade:** Buy shares and 2-3 month out-of-the-money calls on a basket of mid-caps that freshen parachutes off-cycle, treating them as M&A-anticipation candidates; exit on deal news, 13D filings, or after 6-10 weeks.
- **Mechanism:** Bankers and comp consultants advise boards to modernize and synchronize CIC protection before starting a sale process, so the amendments are legal exhaust of a confidential process that otherwise stays hidden until a leak or definitive agreement, and takeover reveals carry large announcement premia.
- **Why under-exploited:** These filings read as boring compensation housekeeping and are ignored by news algorithms; the informative variant only emerges from diffing against each company's own filing history, which no one maintains.
- **Risk:** Many refreshes are routine consultant-driven hygiene with no deal behind them, so any single name is a coin flip and the strategy only works as a small-position basket with a measured base rate.

### 21. Bylaw Fortification Tell  ·  19/30
- **Data:** 8-K Item 5.03 bylaw-amendment filings and the amended-and-restated bylaws exhibits (EX-3.2) on EDGAR, diffable against each company's previously filed bylaws.
- **Collection:** Ingest all Item 5.03 8-Ks daily and machine-diff the new bylaws against the prior version to classify edits: advance-notice window tightening, restrictions on stockholder-called meetings, 'acting in concert' definitions, and forum-selection additions, separating these from routine universal-proxy conforming changes.
- **Trade:** Buy shares or calls in small/mid-caps that adopt genuine defensive tightenings with no public activist news, holding 2-8 weeks for the 13D, activist letter, or approach leak; confirm with unusual-volume screens suggesting accumulation.
- **Mechanism:** Boards harden bylaws reactively — after stock-watch services detect quiet accumulation or a private approach lands — so the mandatory amendment filing publicly timestamps a threat that is not yet public, and subsequent stake reveals produce sharp announcement moves.
- **Why under-exploited:** Bylaw 8-Ks are compulsory but unglamorous, and extracting signal requires full-text diffs to separate boilerplate from fortification — a fiddly legal-parsing job outside standard quant pipelines.
- **Risk:** The post-2022 universal-proxy rule triggered waves of routine amendments that pollute the signal, and a successful deterrence leaves no catalyst, stranding the position.

### 22. ABS-15G Repurchase Demand Tape  ·  19/30
- **Data:** SEC Form ABS-15G filings under Rule 15Ga-1, in which securitizers must periodically report investor demands to repurchase loans for representation-and-warranty breaches, including counts, balances, and dispute status.
- **Collection:** Pull all ABS-15G filings by form type from EDGAR, parse the demand/dispute/repurchase tables, and build a per-originator time series diffed period-over-period for listed originators and servicers (UWMC, RKT, COOP, CACC, OMF, PFSI and auto-trust sponsors).
- **Trade:** Short the originator's equity for 2-8 weeks when repurchase demands inflect upward, positioned into the next earnings where buyback reserves hit gain-on-sale margins; go long clean-tape names when the sector sells off on underwriting fears.
- **Mechanism:** A repurchase demand is the securitization market's formal accusation of defective underwriting; rising demands force the originator to take souring loans back at par onto its own balance sheet, mechanically hitting capital and gain-on-sale economics a quarter or two before management discloses reserve builds.
- **Why under-exploited:** ABS-15G is among the least-read recurring forms on EDGAR — un-XBRLed, irregularly scheduled, covering private-label deals equity investors ignore — so the tape diffuses into prices only when earnings force the issue.
- **Risk:** Filings lag and often show zero activity for long stretches; a spike can reflect one aggressive bondholder's campaign rather than systemic rot, and disputes frequently settle quietly without P&L damage.

### 23. DailyMed Label Diff  ·  19/30
- **Data:** FDA structured product labels (SPL) on NLM DailyMed, which posts revised prescription-drug labels daily as versioned XML with a machine-readable update feed.
- **Collection:** Poll the DailyMed API daily-update endpoint, diff successive SPL versions per set-id, and classify changes into boxed warnings, contraindications, interaction additions, and dosing/indication expansions; map labels to listed marketers via labeler codes and to revenue materiality via public sales rankings.
- **Trade:** Intraday-to-two-week trades: short the branded marketer when new safety language lands on a top revenue drug, long on quiet indication or dosing expansions, and cross-trade the maker of the competing drug in the same class.
- **Mechanism:** Safety label changes are negotiated with FDA and often posted to DailyMed without any press release; prescribers, payers, and plaintiffs' lawyers respond to the new language, so the diff precedes both the prescription-trend impact and the headline coverage that eventually moves the stock.
- **Why under-exploited:** Pharma investors watch approvals and AdComms, not the daily SPL stream; the XML makes diffs trivial but the materiality mapping from label paragraph to drug revenue is curation work that keeps generalist funds out.
- **Risk:** The vast majority of label changes are immaterial class-wide updates, and FDA sometimes scoops the diff with a public safety communication, so hit rate depends on a strict materiality filter.

### 24. CFPB Card Agreement Diff  ·  17/30
- **Data:** The CFPB Credit Card Agreement Database, to which card issuers must submit their currently offered consumer agreements quarterly, plus the semiannual Terms of Credit Card Plans survey.
- **Collection:** Bulk-download the quarterly agreement archives from consumerfinance.gov, OCR the pricing addenda, and diff each issuer's APR spreads over prime, penalty and late fees, promo-APR structures, and the appearance or disappearance of co-brand program agreements for SYF, BFH, COF, DFS and retail partners.
- **Trade:** Position in card issuers over the weeks into their next earnings based on repricing intensity — spread expansion sets up NIM beats, aggressive promo terms flag competition or credit-hungry growth — and short retailers whose co-brand agreements vanish from an issuer's submission, an early portfolio-loss tell.
- **Mechanism:** Issuers must file the actual contractual pricing currently offered, so the quarterly diff is a mandatory census of card repricing and product launches that flows arithmetically into net interest margin and fee income one to two quarters later.
- **Why under-exploited:** The database exists for consumer protection, is published as thousands of unstructured PDFs, and is essentially never cited in equity research; the OCR-and-diff burden deters large funds but is a weekend project for a small team.
- **Risk:** Submissions lag and occasionally omit portfolios, APR moves partly just track the prime rate so only spread changes are informative, and regulatory shocks like late-fee caps can swamp anything visible in the terms.

### 25. Patent Collateral Tape  ·  18/30
- **Data:** USPTO patent assignment recordations published as daily bulk XML, including conveyances typed as 'security agreement' and 'release of security interest' against patent portfolios (with trademark equivalents in TSDR).
- **Collection:** Download the daily assignment files from bulkdata.uspto.gov, filter conveyance text for security interests, entity-match assignor names to listed tickers, and diff each company's encumbrance state over time: first-time blanket pledges, scope expansions to all patents, lender substitutions, and releases.
- **Trade:** Short small/mid-cap equity or buy 2-6 week puts within days of a first-time blanket IP pledge to a non-bank lender, holding into the next liquidity disclosure; treat a release paired with a new mainstream lender as a refinancing-health long signal.
- **Mechanism:** Companies pledge crown-jewel IP only when cheaper secured capacity is exhausted, and the lender must record to perfect its lien, so the recordation publicly timestamps a desperation financing weeks before the 8-K or 10-Q describes the facility, and often before the market senses the liquidity cliff.
- **Why under-exploited:** Distress investors monitor state UCC filings through paid aggregators, but the free federal patent-assignment tape is unwatched for trading; the messy assignor-name-to-ticker matching is the moat.
- **Risk:** Recordation timing is at the lender's discretion and can lag an already-disclosed facility, and ordinary revolvers routinely include IP in the collateral package, so the model must isolate first-time and expanded pledges from boilerplate.

### 26. BDC Cross-Mark Ledger  ·  17/30
- **Data:** Schedules of Investments inside the quarterly 10-Qs/10-Ks of roughly 50 listed BDCs, each independently fair-valuing the same private-credit borrowers every quarter.
- **Collection:** Parse SOI tables from EDGAR (XBRL where available, PDF-table extraction otherwise), fuzzy-match borrower names and tranches across funds, and build a per-borrower mark panel; diff quarter-over-quarter and cross-sectionally to flag loans one fund cut 10+ points while peers held near par, then score each BDC's stale-mark exposure.
- **Trade:** Short the stale-mark laggard BDC hedged with BIZD into its next NAV print (2-10 week horizon); extend to shorting listed parents of flagged borrowers and to relative-value pairs between clean and dirty-book BDCs.
- **Mechanism:** Fair-value marks on the same instrument must eventually converge because auditors and third-party valuation agents force reconciliation, so a peer's markdown is a leading indicator of the laggard's NAV hit, and BDC equities trade tightly on NAV multiples.
- **Why under-exploited:** Inconsistent borrower naming and tranche structure defeat naive scrapers, and BDC coverage is a sell-side backwater; the mark-dispersion literature exists but nobody runs it as a live borrower-level short screen.
- **Risk:** Seniority and terms can legitimately justify divergent marks, BDC shorts bleed double-digit dividend yields while waiting, and stale marks can survive multiple audit cycles before reconciling.

### 27. Non-GAAP Definition Drift  ·  17/30
- **Data:** Quarterly earnings press releases filed as 8-K Exhibit 99.1, whose non-GAAP reconciliation footnotes and KPI definitions are republished, and quietly rewritten, every quarter.
- **Collection:** Pull each issuer's successive earnings-release exhibits from EDGAR and diff the definitional boilerplate of adjusted metrics — new add-back categories like 'transformation costs', changed constant-currency methodology, and redefinitions of KPIs like active users or ARR — flagging first-time changes per issuer.
- **Trade:** Short the definitional drifter into its next one or two scheduled earnings prints (2-8 week horizon), preferably via put spreads around the report date, and use drift as a veto filter on long candidates.
- **Mechanism:** Management changes its own scorecard only when the old scorecard is about to look bad; the redefinition mechanically flatters the current print while the underlying trend deteriorates, so drift predicts negative surprises once reality outgrows the cosmetic adjustment — the press-release cousin of the 10-K diff anomaly, on a document nobody versions.
- **Why under-exploited:** Lazy Prices pointed the whole industry at 10-K/10-Q diffs, but earnings-release exhibits are unstructured, formatted inconsistently, and their definitional footnotes are exactly the paragraphs skipped on earnings night.
- **Risk:** Some redefinitions are genuine comparability improvements or responses to SEC pressure that is already public, and sparse per-company history makes false-positive control hard until a cross-sectional base rate is built.

### 28. SERFF Exclusion-Form Prospector  ·  18/30
- **Data:** Insurance policy FORM filings (new exclusion endorsements and policy language, distinct from rate filings) that carriers must file for approval through state SERFF public-access portals before use.
- **Collection:** Scrape SERFF public-access portals in high-volume states (TX, IL, PA, OH, FL) for property/casualty form filings; extract new exclusion endorsements, diff them against ISO standard forms and each carrier's prior versions, and cluster filings by targeted peril or substance (PFAS, biometric-privacy liability, wildfire, specific chemical classes).
- **Trade:** When exclusions targeting a specific liability proliferate across multiple carriers within weeks, short the listed companies in the industry losing coverage (e.g., chemical producers with exposure to the newly excluded substance) over a 2-6 week window, and go long specialty insurers filing to write the peril at reset terms.
- **Mechanism:** A carrier adds an exclusion only after its actuaries and claims lawyers privately conclude the peril will generate losses; a cross-carrier exclusion wave is a disclosed consensus of private loss models and mechanically raises insurance costs or strands liabilities for the affected industry before litigation reserves and risk-factor language catch up.
- **Why under-exploited:** Insurtech analysts mine SERFF rate filings for insurer margins, but form-language filings are unstructured PDFs read only by coverage lawyers, and the trade crosses sectors — read insurance paper, trade chemicals — exploiting the attention gap between silos.
- **Risk:** SERFF public access and confidentiality vary by state, and the path from exclusion wave to repricing of the insured industry can be slow and diffuse, so only dense, sudden multi-carrier waves are tradeable on a short horizon.


## Gatekeeper & service-provider behavior

### 29. Filing-Agent Fingerprint Switch  ·  17/30
- **Data:** The EDGAR filer agent behind each SEC submission, recoverable from HTML/XBRL generator fingerprints (e.g. 'Toppan Merrill Bridge', Workiva, Novaworks GoFiler comments, file-naming conventions like 'e12345_10k.htm') embedded in every filing document.
- **Collection:** Pull every new submission from the EDGAR full-text index (efts.sec.gov + daily index files), regex the raw HTML/XML for generator strings and filename-style fingerprints, and maintain a per-CIK time series of which agent EDGARized each filing; a switch is a diff event.
- **Trade:** Short (or buy puts on) small caps that downgrade from a premium agent (Toppan, DFIN, Workiva) to a bargain agent ahead of their next 10-Q/financing; go long shells and microcaps that suddenly upgrade to a top-tier agent plus first-time S-1/S-4 activity, holding days to 3 weeks into the anticipated corporate event.
- **Mechanism:** The filer agent is paid per filing and is chosen weeks before the filings it will process: downgrades reveal cash-conservation and looming going-concern stress before the numbers print, while an upgrade at a dormant shell means lawyers and bankers are already engaged for a reverse merger, uplisting, or offering that hasn't been announced.
- **Why under-exploited:** The agent identity is not a field anywhere — it must be forensically reconstructed from document artifacts across millions of filings, which is exactly the kind of tedious parsing institutions skip because no vendor sells it.
- **Risk:** Agents increasingly converge on the same software (Workiva white-labels), blurring fingerprints; some switches are driven by agent M&A or price wars rather than issuer intent, adding noise that demands a hand-labeled agent taxonomy.

### 30. White-Shoe Counsel Upgrade  ·  18/30
- **Data:** The named legal counsel in Exhibit 5.1 legal opinions, S-3/S-8 filings, 8-K contact blocks, and proxy statements — a public, per-quarter record of which law firm each issuer actually uses.
- **Collection:** Use EDGAR full-text search API to extract law-firm names from Exhibit 5.x opinions and filing signature/contact blocks for every issuer, build a per-CIK counsel history, and flag switches from regional/generalist firms to M&A-and-defense elites (Wachtell, Skadden, Kirkland, Sidley, Olshan on the activist side).
- **Trade:** Buy calls or stock in sub-$2B companies that newly retain a takeover-defense or M&A powerhouse with no announced deal, holding 1-4 weeks for a 13D, unsolicited bid, or strategic-review announcement; fade names that switch to restructuring-heavy firms (Kirkland RX, Weil) via puts.
- **Mechanism:** Companies do not pay Wachtell rates speculatively — the engagement means the board already knows an approach, activist letter, or process is underway, and the firm's name leaks into routine SEC paperwork weeks before any Item 1.01 or 13D forces disclosure.
- **Why under-exploited:** Counsel names sit in boilerplate exhibits nobody diffs; the signal requires stitching identity across hundreds of filings per issuer and knowing the specialty map of 100+ firms, which is analyst folk knowledge that has never been industrialized.
- **Risk:** Big issuers use multiple firms simultaneously, so switches are cleanest only in small caps; a visible new elite counsel may reflect a completed (already-priced) matter, and false positives from routine capital-markets work dilute the hit rate.

### 31. Proxy-Solicitor Fee Siren  ·  21/30
- **Data:** The mandatory disclosure in DEF 14A/DEFM14A proxy statements of the proxy solicitor retained (Innisfree, MacKenzie, Okapi, Georgeson, Morrow Sodali) and the estimated fee, which runs $15-25k for routine meetings but $250k-$2M+ when a fight or close vote is expected.
- **Collection:** EDGAR full-text search for solicitor names in all newly filed proxies, regex out the fee sentence ('a fee of approximately $X'), and normalize against the issuer's own prior-year fee and market cap to score abnormal solicitation spend.
- **Trade:** In merger votes, an outsized solicitor fee (or dual-solicitor hire) signals the board privately fears the vote fails — short the arb spread or buy target puts into the meeting date; in annual meetings, abnormal fees flag undisclosed activist pressure — buy the stock for the 2-6 week window into a settlement or campaign reveal.
- **Mechanism:** The solicitor fee is a price the board pays for vote-getting muscle, set after private conversations with large holders and vote projections; it is a revealed-preference estimate of vote risk published weeks before the meeting, while the market prices the vote as safe.
- **Why under-exploited:** Everyone reads the deal terms in a merger proxy and nobody reads the solicitation-expenses paragraph; the fee is a single sentence buried at the back, and no data vendor structures it.
- **Risk:** Fees are stated as loose estimates and some issuers negotiate flat rates that mask expected difficulty; sophisticated arbs already handicap close votes from ISS/Glass Lewis, so the incremental edge concentrates in small/mid caps with thin arb coverage.

### 32. Loonie D&O Premium Tape  ·  17/30
- **Data:** Canadian management information circulars (Form 51-102F5) on SEDAR+, which — unlike US proxies — disclose the annual D&O insurance premium and deductible paid by each TSX/TSXV issuer, including US cross-listed names.
- **Collection:** Scrape SEDAR+ for every newly filed circular, extract the D&O premium and deductible figures with an LLM parser, and compute year-over-year premium change per issuer controlling for market-wide D&O rate cycles.
- **Trade:** Short (or buy puts on) cross-listed Canadian issuers whose D&O premium jumps far above the cohort trend or whose deductible balloons, holding 2-6 weeks into the next earnings or continuous-disclosure event; pair against sector peers with flat premiums.
- **Mechanism:** D&O underwriters conduct private diligence — they see claims history, pending investigations, and management-quality red flags before the market does — and their repricing is a written, disclosed opinion of litigation and fraud risk; a doubling premium means an informed counterparty demanded compensation to keep vouching.
- **Why under-exploited:** US investors don't read Canadian circulars, SEDAR+ has no clean API so the data must be hand-mined from PDFs, and the D&O line has never been commercialized as a dataset despite being a direct insider-risk price.
- **Risk:** Premium disclosures arrive with a lag of months after renewal, and hard-market cycles (all premiums rising) can swamp the idiosyncratic signal unless cohort-adjusted carefully.

### 33. Off-Cycle Audit-Partner Swap  ·  20/30
- **Data:** PCAOB Form AP (AuditorSearch) — a downloadable database naming the engagement partner for every US public-company audit since 2017, which makes partner tenure and mid-rotation changes computable.
- **Collection:** Download the Form AP dataset (pcaobus.org AuditorSearch bulk CSV) on each update, compute per-issuer partner tenure, and flag partner changes occurring before the mandatory 5-year rotation clock expires — especially those coinciding with no firm change (so no Item 4.01 8-K is triggered).
- **Trade:** Short or buy puts on issuers with an off-cycle partner swap ahead of their next 10-K/10-Q filing date (2-8 week horizon), sized up when paired with a fiscal-year-end audit and elevated accruals; the annual-report date is the catalyst.
- **Mechanism:** Partners rotate off-cycle when there is an internal dispute, an independence violation, or the firm quietly benches a partner over a problem client; unlike an auditor resignation, a partner swap requires no 8-K, so the only public trace is Form AP — and the disagreement that caused it tends to surface as a late filing, ICFR weakness, or restatement at the next report.
- **Why under-exploited:** Auditor-change 8-Ks are heavily mined but partner-level churn is not: Form AP is an obscure PCAOB dataset with clunky identifiers, and computing 'off-cycle' requires reconstructing rotation clocks nobody bothers to build.
- **Risk:** Form AP filings lag the audit report by up to 35 days, partners also change for benign reasons (retirement, office moves), and the base rate of adverse outcomes per swap may be low — requiring conditioning variables to be tradable.

### 34. Orphaned-Client Contagion Basket  ·  18/30
- **Data:** PCAOB disciplinary orders and registration revocations against small audit firms (published on the PCAOB enforcement page), joined to each sanctioned firm's public-company client list from Form AP and audit-opinion signatures.
- **Collection:** Monitor pcaobus.org enforcement RSS for orders barring or deregistering a firm, then instantly resolve its client roster via the Form AP dataset and 10-K auditor-signature parsing to produce a same-day list of issuers that just lost their auditor.
- **Trade:** Short a basket of the sanctioned firm's small-cap clients (or buy puts where listed) on announcement day, holding 1-4 weeks as re-audit costs, NT 12b-25 filings, and exchange-deficiency notices materialize; cover into the panic lows.
- **Mechanism:** When PCAOB kills an auditor, every client simultaneously needs a replacement willing to touch a stigmatized book, usually requiring re-audit of prior periods; this mechanically produces late filings, going-concern language, and delisting clocks — a predictable cascade the market prices client-by-client over weeks rather than instantly.
- **Why under-exploited:** The enforcement order names only the audit firm, not the tickers — the client mapping step is the work, and nobody publishes 'auditor died, here are the 23 affected microcaps' within the hour.
- **Risk:** Clients are mostly nano-caps with brutal borrow costs and wide spreads, so implementation eats the edge; occasionally clients transition smoothly and the shorts pay borrow for nothing.

### 35. Trustee Exit Tape  ·  20/30
- **Data:** Indenture-trustee resignations and successor-trustee appointments disclosed in 8-Ks, supplemental indentures, and press notices — particularly the appointment of default-specialist trustees (Wilmington Savings, Ankura Trust, UMB) replacing money-center banks.
- **Collection:** EDGAR full-text search daily for 'resignation of trustee', 'successor trustee', and supplemental-indenture exhibits; classify the incoming trustee by whether it is a known distressed-situations specialist versus a routine corporate trust bank.
- **Trade:** Short the stock or buy puts on leveraged issuers whose bank trustee resigns in favor of a distress-specialist trustee, holding 2-6 weeks into the covenant test, coupon date, or exchange offer that typically follows; unwind on the restructuring announcement.
- **Mechanism:** Bank trustees resign to shed liability precisely when they foresee a default or coercive liability-management exercise, and the distress-specialist successors are hired by creditor groups organizing for a fight — the trustee swap is the paperwork shadow of a creditor war that hasn't hit headlines.
- **Why under-exploited:** Trustee changes are filed as dry Item 8.01/exhibit boilerplate that neither equity screens nor news algos flag, and interpreting them requires knowing which trustees only show up for funerals — trade folklore from the restructuring bar, not a vendor field.
- **Risk:** Some trustee shuffles follow corporate-trust business divestitures (benign M&A among trustees), and by the time equity is shortable much distress may be priced; the signal is strongest in crossover credits where equity still trades as if healthy.

### 36. Special-Committee Counsel Sniffer  ·  19/30
- **Data:** 8-Ks, press releases, and 13D/13E-3 amendments disclosing that a board special committee has retained independent legal counsel and financial advisors — the mandatory first paper trail of a going-private bid, controller conflict, or internal investigation.
- **Collection:** EDGAR full-text search for 'special committee' within new 8-K/DEFA/SC 13E-3 filings each day, with an LLM classifier splitting occurrences into buyout-process context (controller proposal, strategic alternatives) versus investigation context (accounting review, whistleblower).
- **Trade:** Buyout context: buy stock/calls in the minority shares of controlled companies at the first committee-formation disclosure, holding 1-6 weeks for the formal bid or bump (committees exist to extract a higher price); investigation context: buy puts into the delayed-filing window.
- **Mechanism:** Delaware fiduciary law forces boards to paper the committee and its advisors at the very start of a conflicted process, so the committee-formation filing is a legally compelled early leak that a transaction or problem already exists, typically weeks before the price-moving announcement.
- **Why under-exploited:** The two contexts have opposite signs, so naive text screens wash out — the value is in the classification and in covering the hundreds of small controlled companies (dual-class, family, PE-stub) that arb desks ignore until a deal is formally proposed.
- **Risk:** Some committee reveals arrive simultaneously with the proposal itself (no lead time), and investigation-context shorts face borrow scarcity and halt risk; misclassification flips the trade's sign.

### 37. Merger-Sub Registry Trail  ·  13/30
- **Data:** New corporate registrations with acquisition-vehicle naming patterns ('… Merger Sub, Inc.', 'BidCo', 'Project [codename] Parent LLC') in state incorporation feeds, filed by deal law firms' registered agents days-to-weeks before merger announcements.
- **Collection:** Poll OpenCorporates' new-filings API plus the free bulk daily feeds of Delaware-alternative states (Colorado, Florida SOS downloads) and Delaware's entity search for pattern-matched names; cluster codenames against sector rumors, and note the registered agent (CSC and CT Corp filings by known M&A counsel).
- **Trade:** When a fresh merger-sub matches a rumored or bank-coverage-implied target (codename overlap, matching state of the target's incorporation, sector-specific parent name), buy short-dated calls on the candidate target, holding 1-3 weeks into the announcement window.
- **Mechanism:** Signing a merger agreement requires the sub to legally exist, so the shell must be incorporated before announcement; the formation is a public record created by the gatekeeper (registered agent) as a mechanical prerequisite of the deal, making it a structural pre-announcement leak.
- **Why under-exploited:** Everyone watches HSR and 13Ds after announcement; almost nobody watches Secretary-of-State new-entity tapes because target attribution is hard — which is precisely why the few mappable cases are unpriced.
- **Risk:** Attribution is the killer: most subs are unmappable to a target until the deal breaks, Delaware lacks a free bulk feed, and law firms increasingly use meaningless names — so the strategy is low-frequency and needs strict discipline to avoid lottery-ticket bleed on wrong guesses.

### 38. IR-Roster Churn Monitor  ·  17/30
- **Data:** The client rosters of investor-relations and financial-PR firms (ICR, Gateway Group, MZ Group, CORE IR, Lytham) published on their websites, plus the IR-contact footer block on every issuer press release.
- **Collection:** Crawl and diff IR-firm client pages weekly, and parse the 'Investor Relations:' contact block of all GlobeNewswire/Accesswire/Business Wire releases to detect per-ticker changes in IR representation, building an engagement/termination event stream.
- **Trade:** Fade microcaps that newly engage promotion-heavy IR shops after a quiet period — buy puts or short the post-campaign spike over 1-3 weeks; short names whose IR firm silently disappears mid-quarter (nonpayment tell) into the next filing date; long small caps that upgrade to institutional-grade IR (ICR) 2-4 weeks ahead of conference circuits and coverage initiations.
- **Mechanism:** IR firms are hired immediately before financings, promotional pushes, and uplisting attempts and are fired (or quit) when invoices go unpaid, so roster churn timestamps the issuer's capital-markets calendar and cash position before any filing reveals it.
- **Why under-exploited:** The data lives on marketing webpages and press-release footers — unglamorous, unstructured, and beneath institutional notice — yet IR engagement letters are signed weeks before the events they service.
- **Risk:** Client pages update lazily (stale rosters create false exits), and the promotional-fade leg competes with existing pump-detection shorts in crowded microcap borrow.

### 39. Newswire Downgrade Index  ·  19/30
- **Data:** The distribution vendor of each issuer's press releases — Business Wire and PR Newswire (premium, $1-3k+ per release) versus Accesswire, Newsfile, and Globex (discount) — observable in the release's own source attribution and URL.
- **Collection:** Ingest all releases per ticker from the aggregator feeds (each wire's RSS/API plus Benzinga/StockTitan mirrors that preserve the source wire), and maintain a per-issuer time series of wire tier; flag downgrades, and separately flag earnings-call hosting downgrades (Notified/Nasdaq webcast to a bare Zoom link in the release text).
- **Trade:** Short or buy puts on cash-burning small caps that downgrade wire tier, positioned into their next earnings or financing announcement over 2-6 weeks; use as a confirming overlay that upgrades dilution-risk shorts.
- **Mechanism:** Wire spend is one of the first discretionary vendor costs a CFO cuts when the cash runway compresses, and the cut is made by the corporate-communications gatekeeper weeks before the balance sheet confirms the stress — the downgrade is a tiny, involuntary confession embedded in the company's own announcements.
- **Why under-exploited:** The vendor identity is metadata every terminal displays and no one tabulates; the signal is too small-cap, too cheap-looking, and too silly-sounding for institutional research budgets.
- **Risk:** Some issuers switch wires for genuine cost rationality or bundled IR packages with no distress, so standalone hit rates are modest — this works as an ensemble member, not a solo signal, and decays if wire pricing converges.

### 40. Counsel Nonpayment Withdrawal Alarm  ·  18/30
- **Data:** Motions to withdraw as counsel citing unpaid fees ('failure to fulfill financial obligations', 'substantial arrears') filed by law firms representing public-company litigants in federal and state dockets.
- **Collection:** Use CourtListener's RECAP API with saved alerts on docket-entry text ('motion to withdraw as attorney' plus nonpayment language), filter to parties matched against a ticker-entity table (including subsidiaries via 10-K Exhibit 21), and score severity by case importance and firm size.
- **Trade:** Short or buy puts on small/mid caps whose litigation counsel moves to withdraw for nonpayment, holding 2-6 weeks into the next 10-Q, NT filing, or credit event; escalate sizing when multiple firms withdraw across dockets in the same quarter.
- **Mechanism:** Law firms carry client receivables for months and withdraw only when they conclude they will never be paid — a creditor with privileged visibility into the company's finances and legal exposure publicly refusing further credit; because withdrawal requires court permission, the confession is docketed in writing well before any 8-K.
- **Why under-exploited:** This is a specific, low-frequency event type buried in millions of routine docket entries — distinct from speed-based docket scraping — and matching defendants' obscure subsidiary names to parent tickers is manual work with no commercial feed.
- **Risk:** Withdrawals sometimes reflect fee disputes or conflicts at otherwise solvent companies, state-court coverage is patchy and non-digitized, and by the time counsel bails, bonds and borrow rates may already scream distress.

### 41. Toxic Transfer-Agent Switch  ·  15/30
- **Data:** The transfer-agent-of-record field on OTC Markets company profiles and in issuer disclosures — specifically switches from mainstream agents (Computershare, Equiniti) to the handful of discount agents that historically service death-spiral convertible issuers and serial diluters.
- **Collection:** Poll OTC Markets' JSON company-profile endpoints (backend.otcmarkets.com otcapi profile per ticker) daily and diff the transferAgents field across the OTC universe; build the toxicity classification of agents empirically by back-linking past 500%+ share-count expansions to the agent that processed them.
- **Trade:** Short (where borrow exists) or systematically fade rallies in issuers that switch to a dilution-mill agent, over a 1-4 week horizon as convertible-note conversions begin printing into volume; a switch toward a premium agent plus a reverse split is a long setup into uplisting.
- **Mechanism:** Toxic lenders require agents who process conversion share issuances quickly and without friction, so the financier often dictates the agent switch as a funding precondition — the change is operational plumbing installed before the dilution wave, visible before share counts move.
- **Why under-exploited:** Transfer-agent identity is displayed on profile pages nobody diffs, the OTC venue is beneath institutional mandates, and the agent-toxicity map requires manual forensic history that no vendor sells.
- **Risk:** OTC shorting is constrained (no borrow, unshortable tiers), OTC Markets could restrict the API or agents could consolidate, and using the signal only as a fade-filter shrinks it to risk-avoidance value rather than direct alpha.

### 42. Second-Opinion Bump Detector  ·  19/30
- **Data:** Merger proxies (DEFM14A/SC 13E-3) disclosing that the board or special committee engaged a second, fee-flat valuation firm (Houlihan Lokey, Kroll/Duff & Phelps) for an additional fairness opinion alongside the lead sell-side bank.
- **Collection:** Parse every new merger proxy from EDGAR full-text search for the 'Opinion of Financial Advisor' sections, count distinct opinion providers, and extract each advisor's fee structure (contingent success fee versus flat opinion fee) and engagement date relative to signing.
- **Trade:** In announced deals where a flat-fee second opinion was added late — especially controller squeeze-outs — buy target stock or calls against the deal price, holding 2-8 weeks for a price bump, appraisal-driven sweetener, or topping bid; conversely, single-conflicted-advisor squeeze-outs with tight spreads are short-spread candidates into the vote.
- **Mechanism:** Boards pay for a second opinion only when they fear the first one won't survive litigation or shareholder opposition — a disclosed, costly admission that the negotiated price is contestable, which historically correlates with bumps and appraisal fights the arb spread underprices.
- **Why under-exploited:** Arb desks model spread on regulatory and financing risk and skim the fairness sections; the advisor-count/fee-structure detail is dense proxy text where the tell lives, and small squeeze-outs are below desk size thresholds.
- **Risk:** Second opinions are becoming boilerplate risk management at larger deals (signal dilution), and bump probability per event may be modest — the strategy needs the controller-squeeze-out conditioning and patience across many small positions to realize the statistical edge.


## Corporate calendar & scheduling tells

### 43. Ghost Board Meeting Detector  ·  19/30
- **Data:** SEC Form 4 filings — specifically equity-grant transaction dates (code A), which cluster on scheduled board/compensation-committee meeting dates and therefore reveal each company's board calendar; an off-cycle grant betrays an unscheduled emergency board meeting.
- **Collection:** Parse the EDGAR real-time Form 4 RSS/full-index feed; for each issuer build the historical distribution of grant dates (e.g., 'third Thursday after quarter-end') from 5+ years of filings, then flag any new grant whose date falls outside the issuer's habitual meeting windows.
- **Trade:** Buy short-dated straddles or calls on the flagged single name within days of the off-cycle grant date; hold 1-4 weeks for the M&A announcement, CEO transition, or strategic 8-K the emergency meeting was convened to approve.
- **Mechanism:** Boards act by convened meeting, and grant administration is procedurally tied to those meetings; convening off-schedule means a decision material enough to justify an emergency session, which later prints as a market-moving 8-K — so the grant date is a timestamped public leak of the meeting itself, before the news.
- **Why under-exploited:** The entire market reads Form 4s for buy/sell direction; essentially nobody treats grant dates as a covert board-meeting calendar, and building per-issuer meeting-date baselines is fiddly archival work no vendor sells.
- **Risk:** Many off-cycle grants are benign (new-hire, promotion, retention packages), so precision is low without cross-filters; boards acting by unanimous written consent leave no grant trace at all.

### 44. Conference No-Show Sniffer  ·  19/30
- **Data:** Public agendas of sell-side and industry conferences (JPM Healthcare, Goldman Communacopia, Morgan Stanley TMT, Jefferies verticals) plus company IR 'Events' pages listing scheduled fireside chats and presentations.
- **Collection:** Daily-scrape and diff the bank conference microsites and Q4 Inc./Notified-hosted IR event pages (many expose stable JSON endpoints); flag any company that was on an agenda and silently disappears, or cancels an appearance inside two weeks of the date.
- **Trade:** Buy the name and/or call spreads when the withdrawal profile fits a sale process (small/mid-cap in a consolidating sector, no negative tape); short or buy puts when it pairs with other stress tells like CFO churn. Horizon: days to ~3 weeks.
- **Mechanism:** Securities counsel pulls executives from public Q&A slots the moment the company enters a material nonpublic process — merger negotiations, restatement, leadership change — because live questions create Reg FD landmines; the cancellation is the observable shadow of a legal quiet period that precedes the disclosure.
- **Why under-exploited:** Deletions are silent (no press release), fragmented across dozens of bank microsites, and only visible to someone who stored yesterday's version of the page — trivially automatable for one person, too small and grubby for institutional data buyers.
- **Risk:** Most cancellations are scheduling trivia (illness, travel conflicts), and banks increasingly gate agendas behind client logins, shrinking the observable universe.

### 45. Confirmation-Lag Alarm  ·  19/30
- **Data:** The scheduling press release itself ('XYZ to report Q2 results on ...') — specifically its timing relative to that company's habitual lead time, from GlobeNewswire/Business Wire/PR Newswire archives and IR pages.
- **Collection:** From several years of newswire archives and EDGAR, compute each issuer's median gap between quarter-end and its date-announcement release; run a daily job flagging companies now materially past their usual announcement day with no scheduling release, ranked by leverage and accounting complexity.
- **Trade:** Short or buy puts on flagged small/mid-caps 1-3 weeks before the eventually-announced report date; unwind at the announcement or the print.
- **Mechanism:** Committing publicly to a report date requires the CFO and auditors to be comfortable with the close; silence past the habitual scheduling day means the close is contested — impairment debates, auditor pushback — which statistically resolves into delayed reports and negative surprises.
- **Why under-exploited:** Vendors and academics key on the report date moving; the meta-signal — lateness of the announcement about the date — is one derivative earlier and requires boring per-company baselines nobody sells.
- **Risk:** Habits shift benignly (new IR firm, policy change), and the base rate of genuine accounting trouble is low, so an unfiltered short book bleeds on quiet non-events.

### 46. ICS Slot Stretch  ·  18/30
- **Data:** The 'Add to calendar' .ics files and webcast landing pages for earnings calls, whose DTSTART/DTEND fields encode the scheduled call duration — revealing whether the company booked its usual 45-60 minutes or stretched to 90+.
- **Collection:** Download the auto-generated ICS files from Q4/Notified IR event pages for each covered name each quarter; diff the scheduled duration against the company's own baseline and flag added co-speakers in the event description text.
- **Trade:** A stretched slot flags an oversized event (guidance reset, restructuring, surprise strategic announcement) without revealing its sign: buy straddles/strangles a few days before the call while implied moves are still priced off history, exit after the print.
- **Mechanism:** IR teams provision call length for the content they already know is coming; extraordinary agendas need more scheduled time, so DTEND leaks the magnitude of the pending news — exactly the parameter event-option pricing should reflect but doesn't, because nobody reads scheduling metadata.
- **Why under-exploited:** ICS metadata is machine-readable, free, and completely ignored because it looks like calendar trivia rather than disclosure.
- **Risk:** Many webcast templates hard-code a default one-hour block, muting the signal to the subset of issuers whose IR actually adjusts durations; a platform-side normalization would kill it entirely.

### 47. Dividend Clock Slip  ·  20/30
- **Data:** Dividend declaration press releases and 8-Ks — specifically the declaration date measured against the board's historical declaration cadence (e.g., 'declares the second Tuesday after quarter close, 30 quarters running').
- **Collection:** Build per-company declaration-date histories from Nasdaq.com dividend history, EDGAR, and newswire archives; a daily cron flags payers whose expected declaration window has passed with silence, ranked by payout ratio, leverage, and coverage deterioration.
- **Trade:** Short or buy puts on flagged high-yield names (REITs, BDCs, midstream, telecom) immediately when the habitual window expires; resolution — a cut, a 'dividend review,' or a late-but-intact declaration — usually lands within 1-3 weeks.
- **Mechanism:** Dividend cuts come out of contentious board meetings that run past the routine calendar; the gap between the habitual declaration date and the eventual announcement is the observable shadow of that internal debate, and cuts disproportionately follow such slips, repricing the equity 10-30%.
- **Why under-exploited:** Income investors watch amounts and ex-dates, not the declaration timestamp against a per-board baseline; constructing those baselines is tedious archival work with no commercial feed.
- **Risk:** Boards occasionally shift cadence for benign reasons (calendar reshuffles, merger integration), and in broad selloffs the cut is priced before the window even expires.

### 48. AGM Bunker Pivot  ·  16/30
- **Data:** DEF 14A proxy statements and meeting-notice 8-Ks — year-over-year changes in annual-meeting format (in-person to virtual-only) and venue (HQ to remote, hard-to-reach locations), plus mid-solicitation adjournments.
- **Collection:** Use the EDGAR full-text search API (efts.sec.gov) to pull meeting-logistics language from each issuer's current and prior DEF 14A and diff them; flag format/venue switches that follow a 13D, withhold campaign, or scandal, and separately flag announced postponements or adjournments.
- **Trade:** Trade the proxy-contest event path over the 2-6 weeks into the meeting: long the target when a credible activist is engaged (a bunker pivot signals management fears the vote count, raising settlement odds), short when the pivot accompanies deteriorating fundamentals with no activist to force change.
- **Mechanism:** Changing the meeting's format or venue is costly signaling — boards suppress confrontation precisely when their proxy solicitor's private tally looks bad, so the pivot reveals the unobservable vote count and the fight's severity before results print.
- **Why under-exploited:** Meeting logistics sit in proxy boilerplate that neither quants nor most event desks parse, and the signal only exists as a year-over-year diff, not as any extractable field.
- **Risk:** Post-COVID virtual-only meetings are a common cost-saver, so the unconditional signal is weak — it works only conditioned on a live contest, which shrinks the opportunity set.

### 49. Fiscal Calendar Reset Flag  ·  17/30
- **Data:** 8-K Item 5.03 filings announcing a change of fiscal year-end, plus the transition-period reports (10-KT/10-QT) they trigger.
- **Collection:** Subscribe to EDGAR real-time RSS filtered on Item 5.03 and the 10-KT/10-QT form types (only a few dozen non-merger cases a year — reviewable by hand); enrich each with leverage, covenant-test dates, and auditor tenure.
- **Trade:** Short or buy 1-6 week puts on non-merger-driven year-end changers, especially levered small caps shifting the year-end away from a seasonal peak or just ahead of a covenant measurement date; skip changes explained by acquisition alignment.
- **Mechanism:** Moving the fiscal year-end resets comparability, inserts an obscuring 'stub' transition period, and can literally relocate covenant measurement dates — a costly administrative maneuver that is rational mainly when the normal calendar would print something bad, so it predicts negative news in the transition filings that follow on knowable dates.
- **Why under-exploited:** The filings are rare, have no dedicated vendor product, and the market files each one away as an administrative footnote because every instance looks idiosyncratic.
- **Risk:** A meaningful fraction are genuinely benign (aligning to an acquirer or industry norm), and borrow in the small caps where the signal fires is often expensive or unavailable.

### 50. The Dog That Didn't Pre-Announce  ·  18/30
- **Data:** Each company's own history of intra-quarter pre-announcements and guidance updates — some issuers reliably pre-release when results diverge from guidance, making their silence past the habitual date informative.
- **Collection:** From newswire and EDGAR 8-K Item 2.02 archives, catalog which companies pre-announced in prior quarters and on which day of the quarter; a daily screen flags habitual pre-announcers (or their peers during a sector shock) still silent past their historical 75th-percentile date.
- **Trade:** Directional positions into the scheduled print: for a company that reliably pre-warns misses, silence past the window is bullish — buy calls into earnings; invert for chronic positive pre-announcers gone quiet. Horizon: the days between window expiry and the report.
- **Mechanism:** Pre-announcement behavior is sticky corporate policy; conditioning on the issuer's own conditional-disclosure habit converts the absence of a release into a signed forecast of the earnings surprise, tradable before the mechanical repricing at the scheduled print.
- **Why under-exploited:** Absence-of-event signals are structurally under-researched because there is no filing to ingest — the signal exists only relative to a hand-built per-company behavioral baseline.
- **Risk:** A new CFO who dislikes pre-announcing silently breaks the baseline, and pre-announcement frequency is drifting down market-wide, shrinking the habitual-pre-announcer universe.

### 51. Investor Day Punt  ·  19/30
- **Data:** Press releases and IR calendar entries scheduling — and, crucially, postponing, virtualizing, or quietly deleting — analyst days, capital-markets days, and investor days.
- **Collection:** Scrape newswires and IR event pages for 'investor day'/'analyst day' announcements, maintain a forward calendar, and diff daily; the trigger is a postponement, a switch to virtual, or a silent webpage deletion inside ~6 weeks of the scheduled date.
- **Trade:** Short or buy puts on punts (they precede guidance resets and strategy vacuums); go long into confirmed first-time investor days to harvest pre-event drift and sell-side preview upgrades. Horizons of 1-4 weeks around the (re)scheduled date.
- **Mechanism:** Companies hold investor days to showcase multi-year targets; pulling one means the targets no longer survive internal review — a self-revealed downgrade of management's own plan that reliably precedes the public guidance cut.
- **Why under-exploited:** No consolidated machine-readable feed of investor-day changes exists — the events live on fragmented IR pages, and the punt frequently happens by silent page edit rather than press release, visible only to someone diffing.
- **Risk:** Punts occasionally precede good news (pending M&A makes targets moot), and macro shocks cause innocent mass postponements that swamp the idiosyncratic signal.

### 52. Late Gavel  ·  17/30
- **Data:** The live earnings webcast itself: the measurable gap between the scheduled start time and the moment the operator actually begins speaking, plus whether the CEO or a stand-in opens.
- **Collection:** Auto-join webcast streams for a watchlist at the scheduled minute using headless browsers with audio capture, timestamp first speech with simple voice-activity detection, and stream a 'minutes late' metric to the trading screen in real time.
- **Trade:** Intraday: on calls starting unusually late relative to the company's own on-time history, fade post-release rallies or lean short into the Q&A via stock or same-week options; hold minutes to next morning.
- **Mechanism:** Calls start late when something is being argued about backstage after the numbers are already out — a wrong figure, a feared question, last-second script surgery; the delay is operational exhaust correlated with negative intra-call revelations that transcript-reading algorithms only catch later.
- **Why under-exploited:** Transcript NLP is saturated, but nobody instruments the telephony/AV layer; the data exists for exactly one live moment, is archived by no vendor, and therefore cannot be bought or conventionally backtested.
- **Risk:** Weak per-event signal with innocuous causes (webcast vendor glitches), and it requires live capture across many simultaneous calls at earnings-season peaks to reach statistical significance.

### 53. Blackout Re-Entry Clock  ·  17/30
- **Data:** The mechanical link between each company's earnings calendar and its insider-trading/buyback window policy — now publicly disclosed as the insider-trading-policy exhibit (19.1) to the 10-K under the 2023 SEC rules — which dictates exactly when discretionary repurchases resume after results.
- **Collection:** Parse exhibit 19.1 policies from EDGAR for window-open rules (e.g., 'opens two trading days after release'), join with confirmed earnings dates scraped from IR calendars and current buyback authorizations from 10-Qs; compute a daily map of when each large repurchaser's corporate bid switches back on, and how any earnings-date move shifts it.
- **Trade:** Buy heavy-buyback names 1-3 days before their window reopens — especially after a blackout-period selloff that occurred while the corporate bid was absent; at index level, go long into weeks when an unusually large share of S&P buyback dollars re-enters. Horizon: days to two weeks.
- **Mechanism:** Corporate repurchases are the largest single source of US equity demand, and blackout policies make that flow's on/off schedule a deterministic function of the earnings calendar; the return of a price-insensitive buyer measurably lifts returns, so the per-company clock front-runs a computable forced flow.
- **Why under-exploited:** Strategists publish only coarse aggregate blackout estimates; the mandatory policy exhibits are new enough that almost nobody has parsed them into a per-company window calendar.
- **Risk:** A large share of buybacks run through 10b5-1 plans that never pause, diluting the blackout effect, and policies as written are approximations of what legal actually permits in a given quarter.

### 54. 53rd-Week Mirage  ·  18/30
- **Data:** The 4-4-5 retail fiscal calendar: every 5-6 years, 52/53-week filers add a 53rd week, inflating that year's revenue and mechanically distorting next year's growth and comp-week alignment — all disclosed deep in 10-K calendar footnotes.
- **Collection:** From 10-K fiscal-calendar disclosures and the NRF retail calendar, list every US retailer whose current or prior fiscal year contains 53 weeks; compute the mechanical growth headwind/tailwind and the one-week holiday-shift distortion for each upcoming quarter — an afternoon of spreadsheet work per year.
- **Trade:** Around scheduled prints: short via short-dated puts where consensus under-adjusts for lapping a 53rd week (optical headline miss), long where the week-shift flatters the quarter; horizon is the earnings week itself.
- **Mechanism:** Aggregated consensus persistently mishandles the extra week and the shifted holiday selling periods; the distortion is arithmetically knowable a year in advance, so part of the 'surprise' at the print is pre-computable from the calendar alone, and quant screens keyed on reported growth inherit the error mechanically.
- **Why under-exploited:** It is too boring and too infrequent to be crowded — each name is affected once every ~6 years, the adjustment is buried in footnotes, and no vendor flags which upcoming prints carry the distortion.
- **Risk:** Competent sell-side analysts do adjust on large-cap names, so residual mispricing concentrates in thinly covered small-cap retailers where liquidity and borrow limit the size of the trade.

### 55. Holiday Shadow Drop  ·  16/30
- **Data:** Timestamps of material disclosures by cross-listed companies (ADRs, dual listings) measured against the holiday calendars of their trading venues — e.g., China ADRs releasing news during Golden Week while HK/mainland are dark, or European duals filing on US Thanksgiving.
- **Collection:** Cross EDGAR, HKEX news, and European RNS release timestamps against freely published exchange holiday calendars; build a live alert for material filings by any dual-listed name landing while one venue is closed, plus a per-company propensity score for repeat holiday-timing.
- **Trade:** Two legs: trade the open venue's initial reaction and carry into the closed venue's reopen, where the gap is predictable from the fungible line's move (hours-to-days horizon); and treat habitual holiday-timers as a short-bias overlay, since managements that route news into dark windows have more bad news to route.
- **Mechanism:** Managers time bad news for minimum-attention windows; when one listing venue is closed, initial price discovery happens in the thinner market with fewer natural holders, producing underreaction that completes mechanically at the home market's reopen — a dated, forced repricing.
- **Why under-exploited:** It requires stitching together multiple exchanges' calendars, filing feeds, and cross-listed line pricing — jurisdiction-spanning grunt work that falls between US-focused event desks and local investors who are literally on holiday when it matters.
- **Risk:** Futures and other proxies often price the reopen gap before the bell, ADR-arbitrage desks compress the mechanical leg, and the short-bias screen needs many events to reach significance.


## Executive personal-asset forensics

### 56. Tail-Number Title Tape  ·  16/30
- **Data:** FAA Civil Aviation Registry ownership records (the nightly Releasable Aircraft Database from registry.faa.gov) plus business-jet for-sale listings on Controller.com and AvBuyer, mapped to executive-linked LLCs and trusts.
- **Collection:** Diff the FAA releasable database daily for registration transfers, deregistrations, and moves into privacy trusts; build an N-number-to-executive map from proxy-statement personal-aircraft-use disclosures, Delaware LLC names, and past journalism; scrape Controller/AvBuyer listings and match serial numbers to that map.
- **Trade:** Buy 2-8 week puts (or short) on companies whose founder-CEO's personal jet hits the sale listings or transfers out of his LLC with no replacement aircraft purchased; conversely treat a founder upsizing to a larger jet as a soft bullish overlay.
- **Mechanism:** A founder liquidating an $8-40M personal aircraft is a costly, revealed-preference confession of personal liquidity strain or board-imposed austerity; it precedes pledged-share trouble, insider selling waves, guidance cuts, or departure, because the jet is the last trophy asset sold before stock must be dumped.
- **Why under-exploited:** Everyone tracks where jets fly (ADS-B is crowded); almost nobody diffs the title chain, because linking N-numbers through shell LLCs to specific executives is slow entity-resolution work with no vendor product.
- **Risk:** Fleet upgrades and sale-leaseback refinancings generate benign transfers, and Bank of Utah-style owner trusts can hide the true seller, so uncorroborated signals have a high false-positive rate.

### 57. Hangar Lien Ledger  ·  17/30
- **Data:** Security agreements recorded against aircraft at the FAA registry in Oklahoma City and international interests filed on the Cape Town Convention International Registry, on jets already attributed to executives.
- **Collection:** Order recorded-document indexes for tracked N-numbers via FAA document requests or an aircraft-title service (AIC Title, Insured Aircraft Title), and run priced searches on the International Registry for the same airframes; alert on any new lien where the debtor is the exec's LLC rather than a charter operator.
- **Trade:** Short or buy puts (2-6 week horizon) on the executive's company when a fresh security interest appears on a previously unencumbered personal jet, sized up if the lender is a specialty/asset-based shop rather than a private bank.
- **Mechanism:** Mortgaging the jet is how an executive raises cash while avoiding Form 4 optics and pledge-policy limits on his stock; it is the step immediately before margin pledges rise or forced share sales begin, so the lien is an early frame in the personal-leverage cascade.
- **Why under-exploited:** Aircraft liens sit in a paper-era registry that requires per-tail document pulls, so no systematic screen exists; the audience for these filings is title lawyers, not portfolio managers.
- **Risk:** Cheap secured credit makes jet refinancing routine treasury management for the ultra-rich, so the signal only works conditioned on other stress markers (falling stock, rising borrow, insider selling).

### 58. Decree Overhang Docket  ·  17/30
- **Data:** County family-court dockets (divorce/dissolution petitions) in the home counties of founder-CEOs with concentrated or super-voting stakes, e.g., King County WA, Travis and Harris County TX, Santa Clara CA, Miami-Dade FL.
- **Collection:** Poll county e-filing portals (Tyler/Odyssey-based) and aggregators like UniCourt and Trellis daily with name searches built from proxy-statement officer rosters and 13D/G founder lists; flag new dissolution petitions, property-division schedules, and ATRO/asset-restraint stipulations.
- **Trade:** On petition discovery, buy 1-3 month puts or put spreads on the founder's company (community-property states weighted heaviest) and buy vol ahead of the media pickup; later, position for the discounted block or secondary that the settlement forces.
- **Mechanism:** Dividing a concentrated founder stake mechanically creates a large non-discretionary seller and can break super-voting control (Bezos, Wynn, Hamm precedents); the docket entry exists weeks before financial media notices, and the eventual settlement schedules a knowable share-supply shock.
- **Why under-exploited:** Family-law dockets are the least-scraped corner of the court system — fragmented across thousands of counties, embarrassing to admit monitoring, and useless for 99.9% of cases, so institutions ignore them.
- **Risk:** Many states allow sealing or filing under initials, and settlements increasingly use loans or private transfers instead of open-market sales, muting or delaying the supply shock beyond a short-term horizon.

### 59. Quitclaim Shield Detector  ·  17/30
- **Data:** County recorder deed indexes showing interspousal transfers, quitclaims to spouses, and conveyances of executive homes into asset-protection trusts (Nevada/South Dakota DAPTs) for nominal consideration.
- **Collection:** Pull daily grantor/grantee indexes from major-county recorder sites and bulk feeds (ATTOM, DataTree, PropertyRadar), match grantor names against officer/director rosters, and flag $0-or-nominal-consideration transfers out of the executive's name.
- **Trade:** Short or buy 4-8 week puts on the employer when a sitting executive strips titled assets to a spouse or DAPT, especially if the company is under SEC comment-letter, short-seller, or litigation pressure.
- **Mechanism:** Executives who anticipate personal liability — fraud revelation, indictment, clawback, or crushing judgment — pre-position assets beyond creditors' reach, a fraudulent-conveyance-adjacent pattern that has preceded numerous corporate blowups; the deed is recorded weeks-to-months before the adverse event becomes public.
- **Why under-exploited:** Deed transfers are free public records but the signal requires interpreting consideration amounts and trust names case-by-case, and no vendor cross-references recorder indexes against SEC officer rosters.
- **Risk:** Ordinary estate planning generates a large benign base rate of interspousal and trust transfers, so the signal needs clustering with independent stress evidence or it degrades into noise.

### 60. For-Sale Sign 5.02  ·  16/30
- **Data:** MLS-fed listing sites (Zillow, Redfin, Realtor.com) showing an unannounced listing of a CEO's or CFO's primary residence near headquarters, with addresses resolved from county assessor and voter records.
- **Collection:** Build an address book for the top 3-5 officers of a few hundred mid/small-caps using assessor parcel searches on names, trusts, and known LLCs; run daily new-listing scrapes within a radius of each address and alert on matches, including price cuts and 'coming soon' status.
- **Trade:** Buy 1-2 month straddles or calendar vol on the company when the executive's home lists with no disclosed relocation reason — an Item 5.02 departure 8-K, HQ move, or merger announcement tends to follow; lean long the stock if the CEO is widely blamed for underperformance.
- **Mechanism:** Executives list houses when they privately know they are leaving, being replaced, or relocating for a deal — the family's logistics move before the board's press release — so the listing is a personal-life pre-echo of a market-moving 8-K.
- **Why under-exploited:** This is famous as anecdote (analysts occasionally notice) but never industrialized, because the address-resolution step is manual, legally delicate for institutions, and unscalable past a few hundred names — ideal terrain for a small team.
- **Risk:** Pocket listings and agent-remarks-only sales bypass public MLS feeds, and an exec may simply be moving across town, so precision depends on radius logic and confirming no local repurchase.

### 61. Sunbelt Domicile Clock  ·  18/30
- **Data:** Florida declarations of domicile (filed with county clerks), homestead-exemption applications at county property appraisers, and Florida/Texas voter-registration rolls, for founders and executives of soon-to-be-liquid companies.
- **Collection:** Scrape Florida clerk official-records indexes (declarations of domicile are indexed documents), county property-appraiser homestead files (Palm Beach, Miami-Dade, Collier), and FL/TX voter files; match against founders of recently public, lockup-pending, or persistently rumored-takeover companies domiciled in CA/NY/NJ.
- **Trade:** When a high-state-tax founder establishes Sunbelt domicile, position over the following weeks for the liquidity event it front-runs: sell into announced secondaries and lockup expiries, or buy modest M&A-optionality calls if the company is a plausible target.
- **Mechanism:** A founder facing a nine-figure capital gain saves 10-13% by perfecting Florida/Texas residency before the sale, so the domicile paperwork is a tax-motivated, dated, public commitment that a major stock disposal or company sale is being planned — the filing precedes the taxable event by design.
- **Why under-exploited:** The filings are indexed under personal names in county systems nobody associates with equities, and the tax logic that makes the sequence near-mandatory is known to estate lawyers, not traders.
- **Risk:** The lag between domicile and the liquidity event is variable (months to a year), stretching past a short-term book unless paired with catalysts like lockup calendars; some moves are pure lifestyle.

### 62. Cellar-and-Canvas Collateral Watch  ·  17/30
- **Data:** State UCC-1 financing statements where the debtor is a named public-company executive and the collateral is art, wine, jewelry, or collectibles — typically filed by Sotheby's Financial Services, Christie's/Bank of America art-lending desks, JPMorgan, or Goldman private bank.
- **Collection:** Query state UCC search portals and APIs (Delaware, New York DOS, California SOS, Florida) both by debtor names from proxy officer rosters and by secured-party names of the handful of art/luxury lenders; diff weekly for new filings, amendments, and continuations.
- **Trade:** Short or buy 1-2 month puts on the executive's company when a first-time personal-collection pledge appears while the stock is weak or borrow is rising; treat lien terminations as a covering/de-risking tell.
- **Mechanism:** Pledging the Basquiat or the Burgundy cellar is the discreet way to raise personal cash without a Form 4 or a pledge-policy waiver; it reveals the executive has exhausted comfortable liquidity and often precedes disclosed share pledges, hedges, or outright sales.
- **Why under-exploited:** UCC search is used by credit analysts on corporate debtors, never on individuals from proxy rosters, and the secured-party-side query (indexing off five art lenders) is an unexploited inversion of the search problem.
- **Risk:** Art lending doubled as cheap leverage for the ultra-wealthy in low-rate years, so many pledges are opportunistic rather than distressed; state-by-state name-format quirks create matching errors.

### 63. Collar Footnote X-Ray  ·  18/30
- **Data:** Free-text footnotes in SEC Form 4 filings disclosing prepaid variable forwards, zero-cost collars, exchange funds, and similar insider monetization/hedging structures — details that never make it into the structured transaction fields.
- **Collection:** Stream Form 4 XML from EDGAR in real time and regex/LLM-parse footnotes and Table II descriptions for 'prepaid variable forward', 'collar', 'pledged... as security', 'exchange fund'; score by notional as a fraction of the insider's stake.
- **Trade:** Short or buy puts over 1-8 weeks on mid/small-caps where a top-3 insider newly hedges a large fraction of his position, entering on the filing print; fade the signal at large caps where hedging is banned and filings are stale.
- **Mechanism:** Academic evidence (Bettis-Bizjak-Lemmon and successors) shows insider collar/PVF initiation precedes abnormal underperformance — the executive is capping his own upside, the strongest bearish revealed preference short of selling — but the disclosure hides in unstructured footnotes that quant pipelines drop.
- **Why under-exploited:** Everyone parses Form 4 transaction codes; almost nobody parses the footnotes, so the hedging tell survives despite living on EDGAR in plain sight.
- **Risk:** Anti-hedging policies have shrunk the universe to smaller names with wide spreads, and some structures are disclosed late or described so vaguely that notional sizing is guesswork.

### 64. GRAT Window Tell  ·  18/30
- **Data:** Form 4 footnotes and holdings-nature fields revealing insider transfers of company stock into newly funded grantor retained annuity trusts (GRATs), plus county-recorded trust instruments where visible.
- **Collection:** Full-text-search EDGAR Form 4s and Section 16 filings for 'grantor retained annuity trust' and 'GRAT', timestamp initial funding events (as opposed to annuity distributions back out), and aggregate per-company insider GRAT-funding clusters.
- **Trade:** Go long or buy 2-6 week calls on companies where multiple insiders concurrently fund fresh GRATs with company stock, especially after a drawdown; use annuity-payout unwind filings as a neutral-to-exit marker.
- **Mechanism:** A GRAT only wins if the asset appreciates above the IRS 7520 hurdle during the trust term, so funding one with company stock is a tax-structured bet by the best-informed holder that shares are temporarily cheap — the estate-tax code pays insiders to time the bottom, and the filing is public within two business days.
- **Why under-exploited:** GRAT funding is coded as a transfer, not a purchase, so insider-buying screens score it as zero information; the bullish content lives entirely in the trust-type footnote nobody reads.
- **Risk:** GRAT waves are also driven by interest-rate levels and threatened tax-law changes, producing market-wide funding bursts with no firm-specific signal; two-year trust terms mean the insider's horizon may exceed the trade's.

### 65. Family-Office Scaffolding Radar  ·  15/30
- **Data:** The formation exhaust of a founder standing up a family office: state LLC registrations containing 'family office'/'capital'/surname patterns, executive-search postings for founding CIOs/controllers at family-office recruiters, SEC Form D and first-time 13F filers, and family-office association memberships.
- **Collection:** Diff OpenCorporates/state SOS new-entity feeds against founder surnames of concentrated public companies; scrape family-office job boards and recruiters (BellCast, Agreus, LinkedIn postings mentioning 'single family office' plus a city matching the founder); watch EDGAR for first-time 13F/Form D filers with matching names.
- **Trade:** Buy 1-3 month call spreads or M&A-optionality structures on companies whose controlling founder is visibly building liquidity infrastructure, and position to provide liquidity in the eventual block trade or secondary; horizon is the weeks around the anticipated announcement.
- **Mechanism:** Nobody hires a $500K CIO to manage money they do not yet have: family-office scaffolding is built 6-18 months before a company sale, take-private, or mega-block disposal, so the hiring and formation paper trail front-runs the liquidity event that creates the office's assets.
- **Why under-exploited:** The signal is scattered across recruiters, SOS filings, and EDGAR corners with no common key except a surname, which defeats automated vendors but yields to a human-curated watchlist of a few hundred founders.
- **Risk:** Long and uncertain lead times, deliberately unnamed shell entities, and founders who already realized wealth (making the office backward-looking) all dilute precision; direction is ambiguous between 'company gets sold' (bullish) and 'founder dumps stock' (bearish).

### 66. Corner-Office Lien Siren  ·  16/30
- **Data:** IRS Notices of Federal Tax Lien, state tax liens, lis pendens, and notices of default recorded at county level against sitting officers and directors of public companies.
- **Collection:** Run officer/director rosters (from DEF 14A parsing) against county recorder daily indexes in the executives' home counties and against aggregated lien/foreclosure feeds (ATTOM, PropertyRadar, LexisNexis public-records API); manually verify identity on middle names and addresses.
- **Trade:** Buy 2-8 week puts on the employer when an acute personal-distress instrument hits a CEO/CFO, sized up when the exec also holds pledged or margined company stock disclosed in the proxy.
- **Mechanism:** A federal tax lien or home foreclosure on a sitting executive means private liquidity is exhausted — the literature ties CEO personal financial distress and personal leverage to earnings management, risk-shifting, and abrupt departures, and the lien mechanically precedes forced Form 4 sales and margin-call cascades in the pledged stock.
- **Why under-exploited:** Personal-distress records are fragmented across thousands of recorders, socially awkward for institutions to mine, and worthless for 99% of names — exactly the neglect profile a small operation can arbitrage on a curated exec universe.
- **Risk:** Common-name false positives are severe and liens often record months after the underlying delinquency, so some signals arrive after the stock damage is done.

### 67. Form 706 Countdown  ·  17/30
- **Data:** Probate-court dockets for deceased founders/controlling holders — petitions for authority to sell securities, inventories showing concentrated stock, and elections visible in filings — combined with the fixed estate-tax deadline nine months after death.
- **Collection:** When a controlling holder of a public company dies, docket-watch the relevant county probate court (via the court portal, UniCourt, or a local runner) for the estate inventory and any petition to sell or pledge the shares; calendar the nine-month Form 706 due date from the date of death.
- **Trade:** Short or buy puts into the weeks approaching the estate's forced-sale window when the estate is stock-heavy and cash-poor, then flip to buying the discounted block/secondary print when it comes; horizon is a defined multi-week window per event.
- **Mechanism:** Estate tax of roughly 40% is due in cash nine months after death, so an illiquid estate holding a concentrated public stake becomes a dated, price-insensitive seller; the probate docket reveals the estate's cash position and sale authorizations, converting a tragedy into a computable supply calendar.
- **Why under-exploited:** Obituary-reaction trading is instant and crowded, but the slow probate-mechanics phase months later is followed by nobody — the docket sits in a county courthouse and the tax deadline is known only to trusts-and-estates lawyers.
- **Risk:** Estates can elect Section 6166 installment deferral, borrow against the stake (Graegin loans), or use prearranged buybacks with the company, deferring or privatizing the supply that the trade anticipates.

### 68. Ship-Mortgage Tripwire  ·  15/30
- **Data:** US Coast Guard National Vessel Documentation Center records: preferred ship mortgages, documentation transfers, and manager-of-record changes on yachts attributable to executives, plus brokerage listings on YachtWorld and Boat International.
- **Collection:** Query the NVDC/PSIX vessel database and order abstracts of title (a few dollars each) for a curated list of exec-attributed hulls built from press coverage, marina records, and LLC names; scrape brokerage sites for the same hulls appearing for sale, including asking-price cuts.
- **Trade:** Short or buy 2-6 week puts on the owner's company when a first preferred mortgage is recorded on a previously clean yacht or the boat lists for sale at successive price cuts, corroborated by any equity-side stress.
- **Mechanism:** Like the jet, the yacht is late-stage collateral: encumbering or dumping it signals the executive's private liquidity waterfall is nearly drained, which historically precedes disclosed share pledges, forced insider selling, and blowups — and the mortgage is a formal public record with a filing date.
- **Why under-exploited:** Vessel-title records are even more obscure than aircraft records, cost pocket change to pull, and are examined only by maritime lenders; no alt-data vendor touches US-documented yacht abstracts.
- **Risk:** Serious money flags yachts in Cayman or Marshall Islands registries whose records are private, so the observable US-flag sample is small and skewed toward mid-tier wealth.

### 69. Single-Owner Consignment Tell  ·  16/30
- **Data:** Christie's, Sotheby's, and Phillips online sale catalogs and press releases announcing single-owner or 'Property from the Collection of...' consignments attributable to living founders/executives of public companies.
- **Collection:** Scrape the three houses' auction-calendar and lot pages weekly for provenance lines and single-owner sale announcements, plus trade press (ARTnews, Artnet) consignment scoops; match named or thinly veiled consignors ('a distinguished West Coast technology founder') against a founder watchlist.
- **Trade:** Buy 3-8 week puts on the consignor's company between catalog publication and the hammer date, sized up if the consignment carries a house guarantee (the owner demanded certainty of cash); the auction date provides a natural exit.
- **Mechanism:** A living collector liquidating a named, lifetime collection is raising eight-to-nine figures of certain cash — a step taken for divorce, debt, margin exposure, or planned exit — and catalog publication precedes both the cash need's public explanation and any related stock disposals by weeks.
- **Why under-exploited:** Art-world press and equity desks do not share readers; the consignment is announced loudly in one world and inaudibly in the other, and mapping euphemistic provenance lines to tickers is manual detective work no fund staffs.
- **Risk:** Houses increasingly anonymize consignors, and some single-owner sales are philanthropy or estate planning rather than distress, so unconfirmed attribution and benign motives cap the hit rate.


## Counterparty & network contagion

### 70. Partner-Book Fallout  ·  18/30
- **Data:** PCAOB Form AP filings (the AuditorSearch database), which since 2017 disclose the named engagement partner for every US public-company audit, cross-referenced against blowup events (Item 4.02 non-reliance 8-Ks, SEC AAERs, activist short reports).
- **Collection:** Download the free Form AP bulk dataset from auditorsearch.pcaobus.org and build a partner-to-issuer map; watch EDGAR's full-text feed for Item 4.02 restatements and the SEC AAER page for enforcement, then instantly query which other listed issuers share the same engagement partner (not just the same firm).
- **Trade:** Short (or buy puts on, where optionable) the 3-10 sibling clients of a tainted partner within a day of the trigger client's restatement or fraud revelation; hold 1-3 weeks, adding pressure into each sibling's next scheduled 10-Q/10-K filing date.
- **Mechanism:** Audit quality is substantially partner-specific: a partner who signed off on one manipulated set of books applied the same skeptical bar to their other clients, so those clients carry elevated restatement and late-filing probability; as the shared-partner link surfaces in press and proxy-advisor notes over following weeks, audit-risk premia get repriced into the siblings.
- **Why under-exploited:** Academic contagion work runs at audit-firm or office level; partner-level US data is young, requires messy entity matching from a regulator database no trading desk touches, and the sibling clients are usually small caps with no analyst coverage.
- **Risk:** Partner books are small and rotation makes the map stale, so many triggers yield no liquid sibling; contagion can be too diffuse or slow to beat borrow costs on illiquid names.

### 71. Creditor-Matrix Shrapnel  ·  19/30
- **Data:** The top-20/30 unsecured creditor list (with dollar amounts owed) attached to every Chapter 11 first-day petition, published on claims-agent websites (Kroll Restructuring, Stretto, Epiq, Omni, Verita) within minutes to hours of filing — no PACER account needed.
- **Collection:** Poll the claims agents' new-case pages and RSS-like docket feeds, OCR the petition PDF, and fuzzy-match creditor names to public tickers (including foreign parents of US subsidiaries); rank by disclosed exposure divided by the creditor's market cap and by the creditor's revenue dependence on the debtor.
- **Trade:** Within hours of a filing, short the small/mid-cap suppliers whose disclosed receivable exceeds ~2-5% of their market cap (equity or single-stock puts); cover into their own we-have-exposure 8-Ks or over 5-10 trading days.
- **Mechanism:** The petition quantifies exactly who eats a bad-debt writeoff plus loses forward revenue, but the market prices only the debtor on day one; suppliers — especially foreign-listed or micro-cap ones — take days to be identified by humans, then file their own exposure disclosures that confirm the loss and force the reprice.
- **Why under-exploited:** Everyone watches the bankrupt company; almost nobody machine-parses the creditor matrix and resolves the entities in the first hour, because it is tedious PDF-and-entity-resolution work with no vendor product.
- **Risk:** Large exposures are sometimes credit-insured or already whispered into the price during the pre-filing death spiral; the biggest listed creditors get repriced by headline algos in minutes, leaving only the thin names.

### 72. Collateral-Field Mirror  ·  19/30
- **Data:** UCC-1 financing statements filed by factoring companies against a big buyer's supplier base — read not for the debtor's health but for the buyer named in the collateral description (e.g., 'accounts receivable due from [Retailer X]').
- **Collection:** Use state UCC search portals that expose collateral text or sell bulk images (Texas SOS, FloridaUCC.com, California bizfile bulk data), plus known factor secured-party names (Rosenthal & Rosenthal, White Oak, eCapital, CIT Commercial Services); index filings by the buyer name appearing in collateral fields and track filing velocity, amendments, and terminations per buyer.
- **Trade:** Short the referenced buyer's equity or buy 1-3 month puts when factoring intensity against its supplier base accelerates sharply, and especially when factors begin filing terminations en masse (the tell that credit insurers pulled cover on that buyer's paper); pre-collapse analogs are Sears and Bed Bath & Beyond suppliers.
- **Mechanism:** Factors and trade-credit insurers see supplier invoices and payment behavior in real time; when they stop advancing against one buyer's receivables, suppliers shift that buyer to cash-in-advance, which drains its liquidity and accelerates the spiral weeks before an 8-K or covenant event makes it public.
- **Why under-exploited:** UCC data is fragmented across 50 states and the collateral field is unstructured free text, so existing UCC products count filings per debtor; nobody inverts the index to measure the creditworthiness of the buyer named inside the collateral.
- **Risk:** Most collateral descriptions are generic ('all accounts') and never name the buyer, so coverage is sparse and biased toward apparel/CPG where factoring is routine anyway — separating normal industry factoring from distress-driven withdrawal is the hard part.

### 73. Lease-Rejection Cartography  ·  16/30
- **Data:** Section 365 lease-rejection motions and store-closing schedules in retail and restaurant Chapter 11s, which list exact street addresses of every rejected location, posted on the claims agent's free docket site with hearing dates attached.
- **Collection:** Scrape rejection schedules from Kroll/Stretto/Epiq case pages, geocode the addresses, and match them to landlords using REIT quarterly supplemental property lists (SPG, MAC, KIM, BRX, FRT, O publish property-level tables) and county assessor parcel owners for the remainder.
- **Trade:** Same-day, short the mall/strip REITs with the highest rejected square footage relative to ABR (or buy puts), and pair against low-exposure peer REITs; hold through the rejection-motion hearing date and the REIT's next tenant-exposure disclosure, typically 1-4 weeks.
- **Mechanism:** Rejected leases convert directly into lost rent and capped bankruptcy claims, and anchor rejections trip co-tenancy clauses letting healthy neighbors demand rent cuts — a second-order hit the market rarely models; the docket quantifies landlord-level damage days-to-weeks before the REIT quantifies it itself.
- **Why under-exploited:** Address-to-landlord matching across thousands of parcels is grunt work sell-side analysts skip, waiting instead for the REIT's own exposure press release; the co-tenancy second leg is almost never priced at all.
- **Risk:** REITs pre-disclose top-tenant concentration so first-order exposure is partly priced, and auction outcomes can flip the signal when a buyer assumes the leases instead of rejecting them.

### 74. Admin-Agent Dependency Map  ·  19/30
- **Data:** The administrative-agent and lead-lender identities named in Exhibit 10 credit agreements on EDGAR, which reveal exactly which bank each small-cap's revolver depends on.
- **Collection:** Use EDGAR full-text search (efts.sec.gov) for phrases like 'as Administrative Agent' plus bank names across Ex-10 filings to build a lender-to-borrower graph, refreshed weekly; maintain a stress trigger on each lender (equity gap-down vs KRE, preferred/CoCo yield blowout, deposit-run headlines).
- **Trade:** When a specific bank enters a stress spiral, intraday-to-two-week shorts in the small caps whose sole or lead revolver bank it is (long a basket of peers banked by money-centers as the hedge); the SVB week of March 2023 is the template, when companies with SVB-led facilities had to file 8-Ks about frozen liquidity.
- **Mechanism:** A revolver led by a failing bank is not reliably drawable, and replacing an admin agent takes weeks of syndication work; the market sells first and asks later once the dependency is known, but the dependency is knowable in advance from exhibits nobody reads.
- **Why under-exploited:** Linking thousands of buried credit-agreement exhibits to lender tickers is pure neglected-text work; during a bank scare everyone screens deposits and held-to-maturity books, not the borrower-side blast radius.
- **Risk:** Payoff is concentrated in rare bank-failure windows, and syndicated facilities usually let other lenders fund, so many flagged borrowers suffer only headline risk that mean-reverts against you.

### 75. Underwriter Cohort Fade  ·  14/30
- **Data:** The underwriter-to-issuer map from 424B prospectuses of sub-$100M IPOs, where a handful of boutique banks (Boustead, Aegis, EF Hutton, Network 1, Univest, Maxim) account for most of the pump-and-dump-prone cohort.
- **Collection:** Parse EDGAR 424B4/424B5 filings for lead underwriter names to build cohort lists; watch FINRA disciplinary actions, SEC litigation releases, and Nasdaq halt feeds for any action against a boutique or a spectacular collapse (-70% day) in one of its recent deals.
- **Trade:** When one client of a flagged underwriter is charged, suspended, or implodes, fade the rest of that underwriter's trailing-18-month cohort: short where borrow exists, otherwise sell any post-halt rips and buy puts on the few optionable names; horizon days to three weeks.
- **Mechanism:** These cohorts share not just a banker but overlapping promotional financing and nominee-account networks; an enforcement action or blowup disrupts the coordinated support bid across every listing the network sustains, so siblings deflate in sequence rather than simultaneously — leaving a tradable lag.
- **Why under-exploited:** The names are too small, too toxic, and too hard-to-borrow for institutions, and nobody systematically indexes microcaps by underwriter identity even though it is printed on the cover of every prospectus.
- **Risk:** Borrow is scarce and squeeze risk in exactly these names is extreme; a wrongly timed short into an active pump is the classic way this trade dies.

### 76. Reverse-Factoring Iceberg  ·  18/30
- **Data:** The new quarterly supplier-finance-program disclosures mandated by ASU 2022-04, machine-readable via the us-gaap SupplierFinanceProgramObligation XBRL tag in 10-Q/10-K filings since 2023.
- **Collection:** Pull the tag across all filers using the SEC's XBRL Frames API / financial-statement data sets, plus a text search for 'supplier finance program' to catch custom extensions; screen for SCF obligations large or fast-growing relative to trade payables and pair with the issuer's credit trajectory (downgrades, spread widening).
- **Trade:** Buy 1-2 quarter puts (or short into the next scheduled print) on crossover-credit industrials and retailers whose SCF balance is both big (>15-20% of payables) and whose credit is deteriorating — the population where the banks pull the program; Carillion and the Greensill client base are the archetypes.
- **Mechanism:** Reverse factoring is disguised debt that flatters operating cash flow; when the sponsoring banks cut limits on a weakening credit, payment terms snap back from 180 to 45 days, producing a sudden, arithmetic working-capital outflow that surfaces as a guidance cut or liquidity disclosure at a dated, knowable filing.
- **Why under-exploited:** The tag is barely two years old, sparsely covered by sell-side models, and the interesting names hide balances in custom extensions that require manual mapping — a reading-at-all edge, not a speed edge.
- **Risk:** For investment-grade sponsors SCF is benign and the short bleeds; the bank-withdrawal moment itself is unobservable, so timing rests on credit-market proxies that can lag the equity.

### 77. Shared-Whale Fire-Sale Radar  ·  18/30
- **Data:** Overlapping concentrated ownership extracted from 13F, 13D/G, and HK/foreign shareholding disclosures — identifying funds that hold >5-10% of float across several mid/small caps simultaneously.
- **Collection:** Ingest SEC bulk 13F/13D/G data quarterly and maintain a graph of whales with multiple concentrated positions; run a daily monitor that fires when one of a whale's holdings gaps down >25% on idiosyncratic news, flagging the rest of that whale's book.
- **Trade:** Short or buy 1-4 week puts on the whale's other concentrated holdings within a day of the trigger crash, sized by the whale's ownership share and the name's liquidity; buy back into the volume-climax days when liquidation prints exhaust (the Archegos week pattern in VIAC/DISCA/GSX siblings).
- **Mechanism:** A levered concentrated holder hit by one position's crash faces margin calls satisfiable only by selling its other positions, and its ownership share is large relative to daily volume, so the selling is price-insensitive, multi-day, and mechanically predictable from public ownership records.
- **Why under-exploited:** Fire-sale spillover is documented academically but implemented nowhere as a real-time event trigger on the concentrated-smallcap segment, where the flows are biggest relative to liquidity and institutional attention is thinnest.
- **Risk:** Swap-hidden books (Archegos filed no 13F) escape the map entirely, quarterly filings go stale, and an unlevered whale produces no forced flow — the signal cannot distinguish leverage ex ante.

### 78. Franchise Default Ledger  ·  19/30
- **Data:** The SBA's loan-level 7(a) and 504 FOIA datasets on sba.gov open data, which include franchise brand identifiers, loan status, defaults, and charge-offs, refreshed quarterly.
- **Collection:** Download the CSVs each refresh, normalize franchise brand names against the SBA franchise directory, and compute brand-level default and charge-off velocity plus new-origination counts (a leading read on unit growth appetite) for brands owned by public franchisors.
- **Trade:** Puts or short positions into the next earnings print of public franchisors (DENN, DIN, JACK, WEN, PZZA, franchised fitness/service roll-ups like XPOF) whose brand shows accelerating franchisee defaults; long the improving brands against them; horizon 2-6 weeks around scheduled reports.
- **Mechanism:** Franchisee loan distress precedes store closures, royalty shortfalls, bad-debt accruals, and refranchising writedowns by one to three quarters; the franchisor's income statement is arithmetically downstream of its franchisees' solvency, which the SBA tape measures directly at the unit-operator level.
- **Why under-exploited:** The dataset is a clunky government FOIA artifact with dirty brand strings, never cited in sell-side models; restaurant analysts track same-store sales, not their franchisees' loan performance.
- **Risk:** SBA borrowers skew to the weakest, smallest franchisees, data arrives with a quarter's lag, and large franchisors' international and refranchised mix can dilute the domestic default signal below tradability.

### 79. Co-Defendant Shockwave  ·  18/30
- **Data:** MDL master dockets and bellwether trial calendars (JPML statistics, CourtListener/RECAP docket alerts), which pre-schedule verdict and Daubert-ruling dates binding an identifiable set of public co-defendants.
- **Collection:** Build an exposure map per MDL from master complaints plus 10-K legal-proceedings text (which defendants, indemnity chains, insurer layers); subscribe to RECAP alerts on the bellwether dockets and maintain the calendar of trial and ruling dates.
- **Trade:** On a plaintiff verdict or adverse science ruling against the lead defendant, buy puts or short the lagging co-defendants and indemnitors in the same MDL within minutes-to-hours (Zantac 2022 moved GSK, Sanofi, Haleon, and Pfizer on each ruling, sloppily and sequentially); also sell premium into scheduled dates where the basket's implied moves overprice a settled question.
- **Mechanism:** A bellwether outcome reprices the settlement distribution for every defendant in the tort simultaneously, but headline algorithms hit only the named defendant; smaller co-defendants, supply-chain indemnitors, and their insurers reprice over hours to days as humans re-read who else is in the caption.
- **Why under-exploited:** The exposure map lives in dense filings that require legal reading, and the tradable inefficiency is in the second- and third-tier names that event desks do not cover.
- **Risk:** Legal outcomes are high-variance and appeals reverse verdicts; options around well-known trial dates carry rich premia that can eat the edge.

### 80. Sponsor-Bank Cascade  ·  18/30
- **Data:** FDIC, OCC, and Federal Reserve enforcement/consent-order releases (published on fixed monthly/weekly schedules) flagged for banking-as-a-service and third-party program language, joined to a scraped map of which fintech programs sit on which sponsor bank.
- **Collection:** Scrape the three regulators' enforcement pages on release days and NLP-flag orders citing third-party/fintech partner oversight or BSA remediation; separately crawl fintech websites' footers and terms of service for 'Banking services provided by X Bank, Member FDIC' to maintain the sponsor-to-program dependency graph.
- **Trade:** Short the sanctioned sponsor bank (fee income loss, forced program offboarding, deposit runoff) over days-to-weeks after the order posts — small listed sponsors like the Green Dot or Blue Ridge cases moved hard — and short public fintechs whose sole sponsor gets an order restricting new programs.
- **Mechanism:** A consent order on a BaaS bank mechanically freezes new program onboarding and often forces existing programs to migrate, which for a fee-concentrated sponsor is a direct revenue and deposit hit, and for a single-sponsor fintech is an operational kill switch — both knowable the hour the order publishes.
- **Why under-exploited:** Enforcement PDFs are read by bank compliance lawyers, not equity traders, and the sponsor-to-fintech dependency map exists nowhere publicly — it must be assembled from website footers.
- **Risk:** Orders are sometimes leaked or pre-announced in the bank's own 8-K, and many sponsors are diversified enough that remediation is a non-event; most affected fintechs are private, shrinking the tradable set.

### 81. Red-Zone Withdrawal Map  ·  16/30
- **Data:** DOL EFAST2 Form 5500 filings for multiemployer pension plans — Schedule R names every employer contributing more than 5% of the plan — combined with the DOL's public list of critical ('red zone') and critical-and-declining status certifications.
- **Collection:** Bulk-download EFAST2 data annually to map plans to their major public contributing employers (trucking, grocery, construction: e.g., Central States to carriers, UFCW funds to KR/ACI); monitor new zone-status certifications and, crucially, bankruptcies of large co-contributors, which shift orphaned liability onto survivors.
- **Trade:** Short or buy multi-week puts on public employers when their plan is newly certified critical or when a major co-contributor files Chapter 11 (the Yellow bankruptcy raised withdrawal-liability math for surviving Teamsters-plan carriers); horizon runs from the certification headline to the next 10-K footnote that forces sell-side to model it.
- **Mechanism:** Multiemployer plans are joint-and-several webs: one employer's exit concentrates unfunded liability on the remaining contributors as a real, computable claim that surfaces late in footnotes; the certification and the co-contributor bankruptcy are the dated events that reprice it.
- **Why under-exploited:** EFAST2 is arcane pension plumbing with no vendor coverage of the plan-to-employer graph; equity analysts ignore withdrawal liability until a 10-K quantifies it, which is exactly the lag being traded.
- **Risk:** The liability is contingent and long-dated so the market may rationally shrug, filings lag a year, and PBGC special financial assistance under ARPA has defused many red-zone plans, shrinking the live signal set.

### 82. IR-Firm Fingerprint  ·  16/30
- **Data:** The investor-relations contact footer printed on every GlobeNewswire/ACCESSWIRE/PRNewswire release, which reveals which IR/promotion shop represents which microcap — a shared-service web that clusters tightly with pump-and-dump cohorts.
- **Collection:** Continuously scrape newswire releases, extract the IR firm and contact from the boilerplate footer, and maintain an IR-firm-to-client graph with client-cohort return statistics; flag shops whose client lists show repeated halt/suspension/collapse patterns.
- **Trade:** When one client of a flagged IR shop is SEC-suspended, halted, or collapses, fade the shop's other active clients over days-to-weeks (short where borrowable, sell rips post-halt, avoid longs); also treat a company newly signing a flagged shop as a standing short-bias signal for its next promotional spike.
- **Mechanism:** Promotion campaigns share infrastructure — the same IR shop, email lists, and paid-newsletter channels — so an enforcement action or blowup at one client disables the distribution machine supporting the whole roster, and the remaining clients' artificial demand decays on a lag as the network goes quiet.
- **Why under-exploited:** Footer metadata is treated as noise by every parser; assembling the promoter graph from it is trivially available yet done by essentially no one because the tradable names are beneath institutional size.
- **Risk:** Sharing an IR firm is not proof of a shared scheme, so false positives are common; execution in these microcaps (borrow, squeeze, halt risk) can overwhelm a correct thesis.

### 83. Circular-Deal Seismograph  ·  17/30
- **Data:** The web of vendor-financing and circular AI deals disclosed in 8-Ks, press releases, and 10-Q related-party/concentration footnotes — chip and cloud vendors taking equity in, or extending credit to, the same customers who commit to buy their capacity.
- **Collection:** Parse EDGAR 8-Ks and 10-Q footnotes plus deal press releases to maintain a directed graph of who finances whom and who is contractually committed to buy from whom (vendor equity stakes, prepayments, backstopped leases, take-or-pay commitments among GPU vendors, neoclouds, and model labs); monitor the credit leg of each weak node (high-yield spreads, lease-rate chatter, convert pricing).
- **Trade:** When the weakest financed node shows credit stress (bond/convert selloff, missed capacity milestone, downgrade), short or buy 2-6 week puts on the upstream vendors with the largest disclosed receivable/equity/backstop exposure to it, and on other similarly-financed customers of the same vendor — the contagion path the equity market prices last.
- **Mechanism:** Circular deals convert one customer's credit problem into the vendor's problem twice over — impaired receivables/stakes plus revealed demand that was partly vendor-funded — so a single node's wobble mechanically propagates through disclosed contractual edges before consolidated financials show it.
- **Why under-exploited:** The graph is assembled from scattered filings and press releases rather than any single database, and sell-side models treat each announced deal as independent revenue rather than as correlated credit exposure on a shared balance-sheet web.
- **Risk:** The mega-cap vendors are liquid and heavily analyzed, so the trade only pays via the second-tier nodes; a liquidity rescue by a deep-pocketed partner can truncate the cascade and squeeze the shorts.

- **Why under-exploited:** This is not the banned prediction-market forecaster cloning: it is equity copy-flow whose exact future size (copier AUM x allocation %) is computable from public pages that institutions ignore because per-name flow is too small for a fund but meaningful in sub-$5B names.

### 86. Overnight Retail Fade  ·  21/30
- **Data:** The 24/5 overnight equity session (Blue Ocean ATS, reached via Robinhood's 24 Hour Market and IBKR's overnight trading), where the marginal price-setter between 8pm and 4am ET is almost purely retail with no institutional liquidity present.
- **Collection:** Pull overnight quotes and prints via the IBKR API's overnight-session market data and Robinhood's app quote endpoints for the ~1,000 eligible symbols; reconcile against Form T timestamped prints on the consolidated tape the next morning to size overnight volume and VWAP displacement from the 4pm close.
- **Trade:** For names with overnight news, measure the overnight retail move versus a peer-implied fair value; fade the excess at or just before the 9:30 open (short the overshoot, buy the panic) with an intraday horizon, sized up when overnight volume is high but one-sided.
- **Mechanism:** Prices set exclusively by retail traders reacting to headlines systematically overshoot because there is no professional liquidity to lean against the flow; when institutions and arbitrageurs arrive at the open, the overnight-session price reverts toward information-efficient levels within hours.
- **Why under-exploited:** The overnight session is new (Blue Ocean's retail volume exploded post-2023), its data isn't in standard institutional feeds, and it is the cleanest natural experiment available: a market state where the retail herd is 100% of the tape.
- **Risk:** As 24-hour trading matures, market makers and overnight arbitrageurs will compress the overshoot; occasionally the overnight crowd is right about the news and the fade gets run over, so the strategy needs a news-severity filter.

### 87. Refund Ramp  ·  15/30
- **Data:** IRS Filing Season Statistics (weekly refund counts and dollar amounts on irs.gov) plus the PATH Act's statutory rule that EITC/ACTC refunds cannot be released before mid-February — a legally scheduled, multi-billion-dollar cash injection into exactly the demographic that funds small brokerage and crypto accounts.
- **Collection:** Scrape the IRS weekly filing-season statistics page each Friday and the daily refund-disbursement EFT line from the fiscaldata.treasury.gov API; build a calendar of large refund-batch release days, anchored on the known post-February-15 EITC bulge.
- **Trade:** Go long a retail-favorite basket (high-retail-ownership momentum names, TQQQ/SOXL, bitcoin via IBIT or CME micro futures) versus short SPY for the 2-5 trading days following major refund-batch disbursement days, concentrated in the late-February EITC window.
- **Mechanism:** Tax refunds are the single largest lump-sum payment many retail traders receive; deposits hit bank accounts within days of IRS batch release and a measurable fraction flows into brokerage buys of the same crowded tickers, creating short-lived, timeable demand pressure in retail-heavy assets.
- **Why under-exploited:** Institutions model macro tax flows for Treasury supply, not for retail equity micro-pressure; the EITC hold-and-release is a statutory quirk that concentrates the flow into one identifiable week, and refund statistics are free but published on obscure IRS pages nobody parses for trading.
- **Risk:** Refunds increasingly fund debt paydown rather than speculation in tight consumer years, so signal strength varies with the household balance-sheet cycle; the February window is only annual, limiting sample size and inviting overfitting.

### 88. Autocomplete Frontier  ·  17/30
- **Data:** Google and YouTube autocomplete suggestion endpoints — the moment 'how to buy [name] stock' or a ticker first appears as a suggested completion marks a threshold of first-time-buyer query volume, capturing the extensive margin of people who do not yet own the stock.

### 89. Tourist Ratio  ·  18/30
- **Data:** Reddit account metadata within single-stock subreddits (r/PLTR, r/SOFI, r/RKLB and dozens more): every commenter's account-creation date is public, so the share of activity from accounts younger than 30 days is a demographic census of newcomer influx — a different dimension entirely from mention counts.
- **Collection:** Use the Reddit JSON API to pull all comments in ticker-specific subreddits daily, join each author to their created_utc, and compute the young-account share plus subreddit subscriber second-derivatives; also flag brand-new subreddit creations for tickers as an early event.

### 90. Gain-Porn Ledger  ·  18/30
- **Data:** Position-screenshot image posts (gain porn / loss porn) on r/wallstreetbets and ticker subreddits — actual broker P&L screenshots containing tickers, position sizes, entry prices, and whether gains are realized or open.

- **Risk:** Screenshots can be photoshopped or cherry-picked (survivorship in what gets posted), and subreddit culture shifts — if flexing migrates to Discord or TikTok, the ledger goes dark.

### 91. CFD Crowd Fade  ·  15/30
- **Data:** Live retail client-positioning ratios published by CFD brokers: IG Client Sentiment (via DailyFX), OANDA's open position ratios, and Myfxbook's Community Outlook — the percentage of retail accounts long or short EURUSD, gold, oil, and equity indices, updated intraday.

- **Data:** SEC registration filings (N-1A / 485APOS / 485BPOS on EDGAR) for new 2x and -1x single-stock ETFs from Direxion, GraniteShares, Defiance, and Tuttle — issuers file these only when they judge retail speculative demand for one name to be at saturation, and the effective date schedules a mechanical hedge-buying event.

- **Trade:** Two legs: buy the underlying (or calls) into the ETF's launch day, when swap counterparties must accumulate delta as retail assets arrive; then fade the underlying over the following 2-4 weeks, since a single-stock-ETF filing historically marks late-cycle retail euphoria in that name.
- **Mechanism:** Issuers only pay to wrap a single stock in leverage when retail demand is demonstrably frenzied, so the filing is a costly revealed-preference gauge of herd saturation; separately, launch-day creations force dealers to buy the underlying to hedge swap exposure, a small but dated and price-insensitive flow.
- **Why under-exploited:** Everyone watches ETF flows after launch; almost nobody treats the registration filing itself as the sentiment print, and the universe is small enough (a few filings a month) that it's a human-scale docket-reading edge, not a data-engineering race.
- **Risk:** The launch-flow leg is tiny for mega-caps, so the tradable effect concentrates in mid-cap wrap targets; issuers increasingly file speculative shelf batches for dozens of names at once, diluting the saturation signal.

### 93. DRIP Drumbeat  ·  16/30
- **Data:** The dividend pay-date calendar for retail-darling income names (O, MAIN, ARCC, JEPI/JEPQ, high-yield BDCs and covered-call ETFs), where retail DRIP participation is extreme and brokers execute reinvestment purchases mechanically on or the morning after pay date.
- **Collection:** Pull ex/pay-date calendars from the Nasdaq dividend API, estimate the DRIP-eligible dollar flow per name (shares outstanding x retail ownership share from 13F residuals x payout x historical reinvestment rates), and log each broker's documented reinvestment-execution timing from their help pages.
- **Trade:** Buy the highest-DRIP-intensity names (dividend dollars reinvested as a fraction of median daily volume) at the close before pay date and exit within 1-2 days after, harvesting the reinvestment price pressure; scale the book across the monthly-payer calendar.
- **Mechanism:** Dividend reinvestment is a price-insensitive, calendar-locked retail buy program: on pay date brokers sweep cash into repurchases regardless of price, and in monthly-payer names beloved by income retail, that flow is a meaningful share of daily volume, producing measurable, repeatable pay-date drift.
- **Why under-exploited:** Academic work established pay-date price pressure exists, but nobody runs it as a cross-sectional calendar strategy in the modern monthly-payer/covered-call-ETF complex, where retail concentration and DRIP rates are far higher than in the large caps the literature studied.
- **Risk:** Per-event edge is a few basis points, so execution costs dominate unless confined to the highest flow-to-volume names; if the effect gets published for these specific vehicles, faster liquidity providers absorb it.

### 94. Free-Share Bait Gauge  ·  19/30

- **Mechanism:** Customer-acquisition bribes are a cost brokers pay only when organic retail inflow is weakening, so promo escalation is a revealed-preference confession about the funded-accounts and net-deposit KPIs the stock trades on; the market only learns those KPIs at the scheduled earnings release.

### 95. Top-Mover Carousel  ·  20/30
- **Data:** The algorithmic 'Top Movers' and 'Trending' carousels inside Coinbase and Robinhood apps, whose entry rules (24h percent change thresholds) trigger push notifications and home-screen placement to tens of millions of retail users — a mechanical attention amplifier with observable inputs.

- **Mechanism:** Placement in the app UI causes flow, not just correlates with it: a coin crossing the mover threshold gets broadcast to millions of users whose purchase friction is one tap, generating a second wave of buying mechanically after the initial move, followed by the well-documented reversal of attention-driven retail buying.
- **Why under-exploited:** The trigger is a deterministic function of public price data — you can compute carousel entry seconds after it happens, before the notification wave lands — yet crypto quant desks model order flow, not the retail app UI that generates it.

### 96. ESPP Flip Pressure  ·  18/30
- **Data:** Employee stock purchase plan terms buried in 10-Ks and proxies: purchase dates (typically semiannual, clustered around Jan 31/Jul 31 or Jun 30/Dec 31), 15% discounts with lookbacks, and participation rates — defining dated waves of employee-retail share issuance that a large fraction flips immediately.
- **Collection:** Parse ESPP sections of 10-Ks/DEF 14As via EDGAR full-text search for a universe of high-participation tech companies (offering-period dates, discount, shares issued per period); build a purchase-date calendar and estimate flip volume from historical shares-issued disclosures and Form 144/insider-sale seasonality.

- **Mechanism:** ESPP participants are retail investors with an instant risk-free 15%+ gain; a stable, large fraction sells the moment shares settle, so purchase dates schedule a burst of price-insensitive supply whose size is computable from disclosed shares-issued-per-period — a recurring, dated micro-overhang.
- **Why under-exploited:** The dates and volumes are in documents everyone has but reads only for compensation-accounting purposes; the flow is too small per event for institutions to care, yet across dozens of names on a synchronized calendar it aggregates into a harvestable cross-sectional effect.
- **Risk:** Settlement and sale timing varies by plan administrator, smearing the event window; per-name pressure is small relative to mega-cap liquidity, so the effect is confined to mid-caps with unusually high ESPP participation.

### 97. Recurring-Buy Minute  ·  15/30
- **Data:** The fixed daily/weekly execution windows in which retail apps batch-execute recurring investments and round-ups — Robinhood recurring orders execute in a documented daily window, Acorns trades on set days, and the batched flow lands in the same short list of names (VOO, SPY, QQQ, AAPL, TSLA, bitcoin).
- **Collection:** Mine broker help-center pages and terms for documented execution windows, then locate them empirically in the tape: use polygon.io or dxFeed odd-lot and fractional (sub-share notional) print data to find recurring minute-of-day spikes in retail-flagged prints per ticker, day-of-week and day-of-month (payday) resolved.

- **Mechanism:** Dollar-cost-averaging apps convert payroll timing into deterministic, clock-scheduled market buying: deposits cluster on paydays, apps batch them at fixed minutes, and the resulting flow is price-insensitive by design — a miniature, daily version of the index-rebalance front-run, visible in fractional-print clustering.
- **Why under-exploited:** The fractional/odd-lot print census the scope invites is public in the consolidated tape but almost never aggregated by minute-of-day and pay-calendar; the per-event flow is far too small for any fund to chase, which is exactly why it persists for a small account trading micros.
- **Risk:** Per-event drift may be sub-basis-point in mega-liquid names — the strategy lives or dies on execution costs and may only clear economics in bitcoin and the thinnest DCA-heavy names; brokers can randomize batch timing at any point.


## Passive-flow physics

### 98. Fourth-Quarter Clincher  ·  19/30
- **Data:** SEC XBRL company-facts data (quarterly GAAP net income) crossed against the S&P U.S. Indices methodology's earnings rule: an S&P 500 add needs a positive sum of the last four GAAP quarters AND a positive most-recent quarter.
- **Collection:** Pull us-gaap:NetIncomeLoss for every non-member above the current S&P 500 float-cap minimum from the free EDGAR companyfacts/frames JSON API each earnings night; maintain a rolling four-quarter GAAP sum (including one-time charges most screeners ignore) and flag the exact earnings print that flips a mega-cap from ineligible to eligible.
- **Trade:** Buy the stock (or short-dated calls) the morning after the qualifying quarter prints, holding days-to-weeks into the S&P committee's quarterly announcement windows; add the ad-hoc window when an M&A deletion opens a seat. Precedent path: Tesla 2020, Palantir, SMCI, Coinbase all rallied on eligibility-flip mechanics.
- **Mechanism:** Eligibility is a deterministic function of public GAAP arithmetic, but inclusion buying (~$200B+ indexed per 1% weight) only arrives after the committee acts; the eligibility flip therefore starts a datable speculation-and-front-running cascade, and being long from the flip harvests the anticipatory repricing before the announcement pop.
- **Why under-exploited:** Sell-side inclusion previews focus on market cap and float; almost nobody maintains the four-quarter GAAP sum with one-time items and impairments handled exactly as S&P does, so quiet flips (a small positive quarter erasing an old write-down) go unnoticed for weeks.
- **Risk:** Committee discretion — eligibility is necessary but not sufficient, and a famous candidate (Tesla-style) is already fully crowded, so returns concentrate in the unglamorous flips where the committee may simply never act.

### 99. Lockup-to-Float Relay  ·  19/30
- **Data:** IPO lockup expiry terms in 424B4 prospectuses on EDGAR, paired against MSCI/FTSE/S&P free-float (FIF/IWF) review calendars that convert newly unlocked shares into higher index weights on a later, published effective date.
- **Collection:** Parse lockup provisions from each 424B4 (expiry date, share counts, early-release triggers), compute the post-expiry free-float jump, and map it to the next scheduled float review (MSCI quarterly index review dates, S&P quarterly IWF update on the third Friday); rank names by expected passive buy as a percent of ADV.
- **Trade:** Buy the recent IPO on the post-lockup-expiry dip and exit into the float-review effective date when trackers must buy the uplifted weight; single stock or calls, horizon two to six weeks per name.
- **Mechanism:** Insider supply hits at expiry (a known, front-run event) but the offsetting passive demand is deferred to the index provider's float-update date; the market prices the supply shock immediately while the dated, price-insensitive demand shock is ignored, creating a V-shaped path between two knowable dates.
- **Why under-exploited:** Lockup-expiry traders and index-flow traders are different tribes; almost no one links the two calendars per name, and the required inputs are buried in prospectus legalese plus index-provider PDF schedules.
- **Risk:** Providers sometimes assign near-full float at inclusion or apply buffers that delay the uplift a full quarter, and a concurrent insider secondary offering can absorb the entire passive bid.

### 100. ATM Absorption Gauge  ·  18/30
- **Data:** 424B5 at-the-market offering supplements and cover-page/8-K share-count disclosures for serial ATM issuers (MSTR-style treasury companies, shippers, miners, biotechs), reconciled against the stale share counts index providers are still using.
- **Collection:** Scrape EDGAR for 424B5 filings and weekly/quarterly share-count updates (MSTR publishes counts in 8-Ks), maintain a live 'true shares outstanding' ledger per issuer, and diff it against the count implied by current index weights; S&P applies changes over 5% intra-quarter and the rest at quarterly share updates, MSCI at reviews — both on published dates.
- **Trade:** Buy names where accumulated unrecognized issuance is large ahead of the share-update effective date (index funds must purchase a fixed fraction of the new shares that day), and fade names whose ATM has quietly stopped; single stocks and options, horizon days around each effective date.
- **Mechanism:** Float-weighted index funds mechanically absorb a computable percentage of every newly recognized share; for heavy ATM issuers that absorption is a recurring, dated, price-insensitive bid that lands only when the provider's count catches up, so the recognition lag is a tradable schedule of forced buying.
- **Why under-exploited:** Everyone debates the ATM's dilution; nobody reconciles cover-page counts against the index provider's lagged count to time when passive money is forced to eat the dilution — it is pure clerical drudgery.
- **Risk:** Providers accelerate recognition for extreme issuers (shrinking the lag to days), and for the biggest names the passive bid is small relative to the ATM's own selling pressure.

### 101. Foreign Room Tripwire  ·  16/30
- **Data:** Daily foreign-ownership data published by Asian exchanges — KRX foreign holding ratios, TWSE daily foreign shareholding files, Thailand SET/NVDR limits, India's NSDL FPI monitoring and RBI breach lists — versus MSCI's published foreign-room thresholds (the 3.75% exclusion floor and 15% adjustment-factor buffer).

- **Trade:** Position in local single-stock futures (Korea/Taiwan SSFs), ADRs, or the stock via IBKR from the review announcement to the effective date (~2 weeks), long names regaining room (weight restoration) and short names about to be cut or excluded.
- **Mechanism:** MSCI weight changes from foreign-room rules trigger mandatory tracker flows measured in days of ADV on a single effective date; because the exchanges publish the underlying ownership data daily, the crossing is forecastable weeks before MSCI announces, and the announcement itself starts a second front-running leg into the effective date.
- **Why under-exploited:** The inputs are fragmented across half a dozen local-language exchange sites and the arb capacity is too small for global quant funds, leaving it to a handful of regional desks; retail and generalist funds are entirely absent.
- **Risk:** MSCI's buffers and occasional discretionary treatment make marginal crossings unreliable, and the regional prop desks that do play compress the announcement-to-effective drift.

### 102. Reclassification Docket  ·  16/30
- **Data:** FTSE Russell and MSCI country-classification watchlists and consultation documents — the scheduled annual verdicts on market upgrades/downgrades (Vietnam's FTSE secondary-EM upgrade, Korea DM watch, Greece/Kuwait precedents) that reassign billions of benchmarked AUM.
- **Collection:** Monitor FTSE's September annual country-classification review and interim March updates plus MSCI's June market-accessibility review (all published PDFs on their sites), and track the named remediation criteria (settlement, FX, omnibus accounts) through local regulator announcements (e.g., Vietnam SSC circulars) to handicap the verdict before the scheduled date.
- **Trade:** Country ETFs (VNM, EWY, GREK-analogues) and their options bought one to three weeks before the scheduled decision date, plus the post-announcement drift into implementation tranches; the decision dates are known to the day.
- **Mechanism:** An upgrade mechanically redirects index-tracking AUM (EM benchmarks are ~20x frontier benchmarks), so the verdict converts directly into dated inflows; because the criteria checklist is public and progress on each item is observable in local regulatory filings, the binary can be handicapped better than the market prices it.
- **Why under-exploited:** The catalyst is annual and slow, so systematic funds ignore it; handicapping requires reading local-market plumbing circulars in Vietnamese or Korean, which almost nobody benchmarked to the outcome actually does.
- **Risk:** Multi-year deferrals are common ('remains on watchlist' is the modal outcome), and by decision day the famous cases carry heavy anticipatory positioning that unwinds violently on any delay.

### 103. Fire-Sale Forecaster  ·  15/30
- **Data:** SEC Form N-PORT monthly holdings of active mutual funds combined with daily flow estimates inferred from published fund AUM and NAV series — identifying stocks whose ownership is concentrated in closet-indexers and concentrated funds currently hemorrhaging assets.
- **Collection:** Bulk-download N-PORT filings from EDGAR (machine-readable XML), compute each fund's active overweights versus its prospectus benchmark, then estimate daily flows from fund-company AUM pages and Morningstar's free flow data; score each mid-cap by 'distressed-owner supply' = sum of overweight positions held by funds in persistent outflow, scaled by ADV.
- **Trade:** Short (or avoid/underweight) the top-decile distressed-ownership mid-caps for one to four weeks while outflows persist, and buy the reversal once flow stabilizes; express via single stocks or puts against a sector ETF hedge.
- **Mechanism:** Coval–Stafford fire-sale mechanics: redeeming funds sell their liquid overweights near-pro-rata regardless of price, so mapped ownership plus live flow gives weeks of forecastable price-insensitive supply in specific names, followed by the documented post-fire-sale rebound.
- **Why under-exploited:** The anomaly is 15 years old academically but operationally miserable — stitching N-PORT XML to daily AUM scrapes is manual plumbing, and institutions big enough to do it can't deploy in the mid-caps where the effect is strongest.
- **Risk:** N-PORT's 60-day publication lag means holdings drift, and modern funds increasingly meet redemptions with futures or cash buffers, muting the single-name footprint.

### 104. CapGains Exodus  ·  16/30
- **Data:** Fund families' October–November estimated capital-gains distribution notices (PDFs on T. Rowe, American Funds, Fidelity, Columbia sites; aggregated free at CapGainsValet), which pre-announce which active funds will force taxable distributions of 10%+ of NAV in December.
- **Collection:** Scrape the estimate pages and CapGainsValet weekly from mid-October, join to each fund's latest N-PORT holdings and flow trend, and rank funds by (estimated distribution % of NAV) x (taxable-account share) x (concentration of idiosyncratic holdings).
- **Trade:** Short the top active-share holdings of high-distribution, outflow-prone funds from early November into the distribution record dates, then reverse long after the ex-dates; two-to-five-week horizon in single mid-cap names with sector hedges.
- **Mechanism:** Large announced distributions trigger pre-ex-date redemptions by tax-aware holders (sell before you're handed the tax bill), forcing the fund to liquidate portfolio positions into a datable window; the selling concentrates in the fund's distinctive overweights and mechanically reverses once the record date passes.
- **Why under-exploited:** The estimates are scattered across dozens of fund-family PDF pages nobody machine-reads, and December-flow research fixates on tax-loss selling in losers while this flow hits the winners the distributing funds actually hold.
- **Risk:** The gains were often realized (and the stock sold) months earlier, so the residual forced selling can be small, and the signal overlaps messy December seasonals that add noise.

### 105. Creation-Halt Premium Fade  ·  17/30
- **Data:** ETF/ETN issuer filings and press releases announcing suspension or resumption of share creations (USO 2020, capacity-capped 2x crypto and single-stock ETPs, delisting ETNs) — plus the tell that predicts resumption: a new 424 shelf registering additional shares.

- **Why under-exploited:** The registration-filing breadcrumb is an obscure EDGAR artifact nobody systematically links to ETP premiums, and each episode is too small and idiosyncratic for institutional arb desks to bother with.
- **Risk:** Borrow on a halted, premium-rich ETP is scarce and expensive, and premiums can double before they die (GBTC-style pain) if resumption slips.

### 106. Angel Drop Window  ·  14/30
- **Data:** Rating-agency downgrade actions crossing the IG/HY boundary (free rating-action feeds on Moody's/S&P/Fitch sites) mapped onto the index calendar: investment-grade benchmarks eject fallen angels at month-end and fallen-angel indices (ANGL/FALN trackers) absorb them shortly after.

### 107. Cap Rule Cliff  ·  18/30
- **Data:** The deterministic capping algorithms of concentrated sector/industry ETFs — S&P Select Sector's 25/4.5% RIC rules for XLK/XLY/XLC and MarketVector's caps behind SMH — whose only inputs are closing prices on a published quarterly reference date.

- **Why under-exploited:** The 2024 XLK episode got one news cycle, but continuous replication across every capped sector/industry ETF is still done by only a few index-arb desks; the knife-edge conditionality (flows appear only when ranks are close) keeps it off systematic platforms.

### 109. Divisor Day  ·  17/30
- **Data:** Stock-split announcements by members of the two great price-weighted indices — the Dow 30 and Nikkei 225 — where trackers hold equal share counts per name and a split mechanically slashes that name's index weight on a published effective date.

### 111. Rights Tail Bid  ·  17/30
- **Data:** European and Asian rights-issue calendars (LSE RNS, Euronext and SGX corporate-action notices) where index funds and passive holders, unable to inject cash, must sell their nil-paid rights into a thin rights market — predictably dumping near the end of the rights-trading window.

- **Data:** MSCI's free public ESG Ratings search tool and controversy flags, diffed over time and mapped to the exclusion rules of ESG index families (MSCI SRI/ESG Leaders/Paris-Aligned) that large European passive AUM tracks — a rating drop below the index floor schedules a deletion at the next quarterly review.

- **Trade:** Short the flagged European name (or its US ADR) from the ratings breach into the review effective date, covering into the deletion-day flow; reverse long after the effective date for the rebound; two-to-six-week horizon per name.
- **Mechanism:** The rating diff is public weeks before the mechanical index deletion, and ESG trackers in Europe hold multi-day-ADV positions in mid-caps; the deletion date is scheduled, so the flow is a computable forced sale that fundamentals-focused holders neither see coming nor offset.
- **Why under-exploited:** ESG data is unfashionable with quants and the ratings-diff-to-index-rule mapping requires manually encoding several dull methodology PDFs, so the flow calendar sits in plain sight largely unmodeled.
- **Risk:** European ESG AUM is shrinking and index rules keep mutating, so both the flow size and the rule mapping decay; scraping the ratings tool also lives at the mercy of MSCI's terms and page structure.

### 113. House-Margin Diff Tape  ·  17/30
- **Data:** Per-ticker special/house margin requirement lists at retail brokers: Interactive Brokers' public margin-requirements pages and TWS API what-if margin queries, Schwab/Fidelity special-margin lists, and broker client notices announcing 100% maintenance on specific tickers.
- **Collection:** Nightly scrape IBKR's margin search tool and run automated what-if order margin queries via the TWS API across a universe of high-beta, high-short-interest names, diffing results daily to catch house-margin hikes. - **Trade:** When a major retail broker lifts maintenance margin on a crowded momentum ticker (meme names, quantum/AI/uranium microcaps), buy short-dated puts or short the stock for a 1-5 day forced-deleveraging window, then fade the move after the flush.
- **Mechanism:** Retail leverage concentrated at a few brokers is the marginal bid in these names; a house-margin hike issues synchronized margin calls with a stated effective date, converting levered holders into price-insensitive sellers. - **Why under-exploited:** Margin schedules are scattered across broker portals and APIs that nobody systematically diffs; hikes are communicated to clients rather than the market, so the information leaks through account notices and forums hours to days before the forced flow completes.
- **Risk:** Brokers sometimes hike only after the top is in, making the signal coincident rather than leading; scraping and what-if endpoints can be rate-limited or ToS-restricted, and the best setups occur mid-crash when borrow for shorting is already unavailable.

### 114. Performance-Bond Spiral Forecaster  ·  20/30
- **Data:** CME Clearing performance-bond (margin) change advisories and LME margin circulars, combined with the clearinghouses' published margin-model mechanics (SPAN/SPAN2 volatility lookbacks) that make hikes forecastable from realized vol.
- **Collection:** Scrape the CME Clearing Advisories page (daily notices with per-contract margin changes and effective dates) and lme.com circulars; in parallel run a shadow SPAN calculation on settlement prices to forecast which contracts are one to two days from a margin-coverage breach that will compel a hike.
- **Trade:** In a trending, open-interest-heavy contract (nickel, natgas, cocoa), position with the trend into a forecast margin hike and hold through the 1-3 sessions after the effective date as undercapitalized holders cut, then fade once open interest has flushed. Futures and futures options, 1-10 day horizon.
- **Mechanism:** A margin hike is a synchronized capital call on every open position with a known effective date; weak hands must post or liquidate, so the hike mechanically extends the move that caused it, and the subsequent open-interest drop removes the forced flow, setting up the reversal leg.
- **Why under-exploited:** The advisories are public PDFs with effective dates that almost no one parses systematically, and virtually nobody runs the margin model forward to trade the hike before it is announced — institutions treat clearing margin as an operations problem, not an alpha source.
- **Risk:** Clearinghouses sometimes hike pre-emptively in calm markets, producing no forced flow, and the signal is confounded with the volatility spike itself — separating margin-flow alpha from simple momentum requires careful open-interest conditioning.

### 156. Extension Epidemic Gauge  ·  19/30

- **Risk:** Servicers can change extension policy for operational reasons (hurricanes, system migrations) creating false positives, and if the signal becomes popular the reaction compresses into the 10-D filing minute, erasing the multi-week lead.

- **Why under-exploited:** Odd-lot prints were historically excluded from the SIP and are still mentally excluded by professionals; nobody baselines per-ticker odd-lot trade counts against tender announcement dates, and the harvest leg is capped at 99 shares per account so institutions ignore the whole niche.
- **Risk:** Issuers can amend terms to drop odd-lot priority mid-tender, and algorithmic child orders generate odd-lot prints unrelated to tendering, so the census needs careful baseline differencing or it reads noise as signal.

### 171. Round-Up Rush  ·  18/30
- **Data:** Reverse-split 8-Ks and press releases whose fractional-share treatment says 'rounded up to the nearest whole share' rather than cash-in-lieu.
- **Collection:** EDGAR full-text search API for 'reverse stock split' co-occurring with 'rounded up to the nearest whole share' in 8-K/14A filings, plus FINRA's Daily List for the exact effective date; flag the subset with float under ~$50M where a retail swarm moves the price.
- **Trade:** Buy the micro-cap common at announcement and exit the session before the effective date; optionally short or stand aside immediately post-split; horizon 2-10 days per event.
- **Mechanism:** Round-up terms invite multi-account farming (buy 1 share in each of many accounts, receive a whole post-split share worth 10-50x), which creates genuine aggregate buying into the effective date in illiquid names; after the split the farmed shares are dumped en masse and the round-up issuance slightly inflates the count, producing a predictable pre-date lift and post-date fade.
- **Why under-exploited:** The round-up vs cash-in-lieu distinction lives in one sentence of boilerplate that no institutional screen parses, and per-event capacity is far too small for funds while being ideal for an individual.
- **Risk:** Brokers increasingly cash out sub-share positions regardless of issuer terms, issuers switch to cash-in-lieu at the eleventh hour, and Reddit tutorials are steadily crowding the farm — the fade leg may outlive the lift leg.

### 172. Split-Then-Shelf Countdown  ·  16/30

- **Why under-exploited:** Each event is a few hundred thousand dollars of capacity with ugly borrow logistics, and the ATM-headroom join across three filing types is manual enough that no systematic desk bothers.

### 174. CEF Rights Dilution Fade  ·  18/30

### 175. Backstop Tell  ·  18/30

- **Why under-exploited:** Backstop exhibits attached to micro-cap rights deals are effectively unread — the names have no coverage, and the insider-vs-mercenary classification requires human reading of who the backstopper actually is.

### 176. Warrant Redemption Odometer  ·  16/30

- **Why under-exploited:** The trigger arithmetic is trivially public but scattered across hundreds of bespoke legal documents for sub-$1B companies; no one publishes a centralized odometer, so the notice still lands as a surprise on notice day.

### 177. Undeliverable Dividend Squeeze  ·  18/30

### 179. 13E-3 Cash-Out Harvest  ·  20/30

### 182. Election-Proration Handicap  ·  17/30

### 187. Fungibility Headroom Ledger (India)  ·  16/30

- **Mechanism:** FAA airworthiness action is a bureaucratic function of SDR clusters, and an AD converts a statistical defect into grounded aircraft, shop-visit backlogs, and compensation liabilities — quantifiable hits that reprice both OEM and concentrated operators on announcement.
- **Why under-exploited:** SDR data is ATA-chapter arcana consumed by maintenance engineers, not equity analysts; the join from part-number clusters to listed-company exposure (who makes it, who flies it, who overhauls it) is niche aviation knowledge a two-person team can own.
- **Risk:** Operator reporting discipline varies wildly, so cross-operator normalization is hard; big ADs are often pre-telegraphed by OEM service bulletins the street already saw, leaving only the smaller, slower-burn defects.

### 215. OIG Work-Plan Diff  ·  15/30

### 216. SOX-806 Docket Sniffer  ·  16/30
- **Data:** DOL Office of Administrative Law Judges dockets and ARB decisions for Sarbanes-Oxley Section 806 whistleblower-retaliation cases — each one names a respondent company and represents an employee who formally alleged securities fraud internally.
- **Collection:** Scrape the OALJ public case search and decision database (dol.gov/agencies/oalj) plus OSHA whistleblower-determination FOIA logs for new SOX-806 respondents; join respondent names to tickers and layer over accounting red flags (accrual extremity, auditor churn, serial NT filings) already computable from EDGAR.
- **Trade:** Maintain a rolling short/put basket of small and mid-cap respondents that also screen dirty on accruals, refreshed on 2-6 week cycles, and trade the acute event when a complaint's factual allegations (which the ALJ filings spell out) surface in media or an 8-K.
- **Mechanism:** Academic evidence shows retaliation complaints lead restatements and SEC enforcement by one to three years; a fresh 806 filing is a costly, sworn signal that an insider saw something worth being fired over, and the ALJ record often contains the substantive fraud allegation itself long before any securities filing does.
- **Why under-exploited:** OALJ is a backwater tribunal no financial-data vendor indexes; the respondents are named in plain text, but nobody has productionized the docket-to-ticker join or combined it with accrual screens.
- **Risk:** Most 806 complaints are garden-variety HR disputes dressed in securities language, and cases reach OALJ only after an OSHA-level finding is appealed — adding months of lag and survivorship distortion.

### 217. NRC Spill Blotter  ·  15/30
- **Data:** The US Coast Guard National Response Center database — the federally mandated hotline where every significant spill, pipeline rupture, rail release, and chemical incident must be phoned in by the responsible party, published as a rolling public file.
- **Collection:** Download the NRC incident CSVs from nrc.uscg.mil on their weekly refresh (plus standing FOIA for faster pulls); filter by responsible-party name, material, and reported quantity, and maintain an entity map from party names to tickers (pipelines: ET, EPD, KMI; rails: NSC, CSX, UNP; refiners: PBF, DINO, MPC).
- **Trade:** Short the responsible party or buy short-dated puts within days of a large-quantity release report, and trade the commodity leg (RBOB cracks on refinery-unit releases, chlor-alkali chains on chemical-plant incidents) when the incident sits at a supply chokepoint; East Palestine hit this database essentially immediately.
- **Mechanism:** The hotline call is legally compelled within hours of the incident, while the 8-K, EPA statement, and remediation-cost estimate arrive days to weeks later — the blotter is a self-confession tape that leads the company's own disclosure cycle.
- **Why under-exploited:** The file is ugly, self-reported, and uncurated, and media consult it only after an incident is already famous; systematically joining responsible-party strings to tickers and sizing by material toxicity is unsexy work no vendor does.
- **Risk:** The weekly publication cadence loses the race to scanners and local news for the very biggest events, and initial self-reported quantities are wrong in both directions, generating false severity readings.

### 218. BrokerCheck Dispute Swarm  ·  15/30
- **Data:** FINRA BrokerCheck individual disclosure records — new 'pending customer dispute' entries appearing across the registered reps of a single listed broker-dealer, usually clustered around one failed product.
- **Collection:** Enumerate each firm's rep CRDs and diff the BrokerCheck JSON endpoints (api.brokercheck.finra.org) daily for new pending-dispute disclosures; extract product keywords from allegation text (the GWG L Bonds collapse printed here as a rep-level dispute swarm) and aggregate to firm-level velocity.
- **Trade:** Short or buy 1-2 month puts on wealth-management pure-plays (LPLA, RJF, SF, AMP, B. Riley-type names) when a product-linked dispute swarm accelerates, positioning for arbitration-reserve builds, regulatory sweeps, and advisor-recruiting exodus headlines over 1-6 weeks.
- **Mechanism:** Reps must report customer disputes promptly, so a swarm is a distributed, timestamped census of a product blow-up working through a firm's client base — it mechanically precedes the mass-arbitration filings, FINRA enforcement, and reserve charges that reprice the stock.
- **Why under-exploited:** BrokerCheck is designed for one-name-at-a-time retail lookups; nobody diffs it at firm scale because the enumeration is tedious and the ToS gray, which is exactly the kind of friction that preserves a signal for a small operator.
- **Risk:** FINRA rate-limiting or access-term changes can kill collection overnight, and a single plaintiff firm's templated mass filing can simulate a swarm whose ultimate P&L impact on a diversified firm is negligible.
