# The Edge Hunt: What ~760 Candidate Ideas Taught Us

*The synthesis across the whole program. Three independent rounds generated, and then adversarially validated, hundreds of unorthodox trading-signal ideas — plus one signal I mined from 27.5M rows of raw data myself. This is the honest account of what survived. Research notes, not investment advice.*

**Roughly 760 distinct candidate signals. Zero earned capital.** That is the finding, and it was reached three separate times by independent swarms using web-verified research.

## Why nothing survived — and why that is the useful answer

The near-zero survival rate is not a framework failure. It *is* the framework doing its job: the filters that kill hardest are detectors for "this frontier is already picked over," and they fire on nearly everything.

- **Filter 2 — no informational lead (40 of 100 kills).** The answer is already published for free. US sportsbooks publish per-operator hold; the SEC publishes the Reg SHO threshold list; the USDA, EIA, and NBS publish the official numbers the "nowcast" was trying to beat. If the reference number is public, there is no lead to sell.
- **Filter 1 — the data physically cannot be obtained (15 kills).** Sub-scene, sub-minute thermal signatures from orbit; realized airline yield; insurer reserve triangles. The pitch assumes a resolution or cadence that does not exist.
- **Filter 7 — the market is commoditized (14 kills).** Refinery throughput, dark-tanker flow, SAR port stockpiles, China NO₂ activity, used-vehicle and restaurant-comp nowcasts — all already sold as products by Kpler, Vortexa, Mysteel, YipitData, M Science, Genscape.

**The deep lesson:** imitating a winner's DNA does not transfer its edge, because the edge *was being early* to exclusive, not-yet-commoditized data and processing. RS Metrics and Orbital Insight won by parsing imagery nobody else parsed — in ~2013. By 2026 that same satellite/thermal/nowcast frontier is a saturated subscription business, so the very lead that made them has been arbitraged away and repackaged. You cannot buy your way into an edge that a vendor already sells to everyone.

## What actually survived the whole program

Three things, none of which is a conventional trade:

1. **The do-not-own screen (the single validated survivor).** From the FTD work: a real, persistent, reproducible statistical pattern — cheap chronically-failing stocks *do* underperform — whose edge lives *inside the tick* (2–4 cents against a $0.01 minimum spread). Worthless as a trade, but valuable as a **free risk control**, because declining to own something costs no borrow, no spread, no squeeze tail. Reframe any small-but-real pattern this way before calling it alpha.

2. **The dollar-and-tick discipline (the meta-tool).** Measure every edge in **dollars against the minimum tick and full round-trip cost before believing any percentage**, and **de-overlap windows before believing any t-statistic.** This one habit turned two celebrated "monotone economic gradients" into arithmetic — a constant few-cents drift over a shrinking price. It is the cheapest test available and it should run first, always. It caught its own author twice.

## Where the productive frontier actually is

The winner-DNA patterns are still sound archetypes; what changed is *where* they can be hunted:

- **Parking-lots DNA** (physical observation with a disclosure lead) survives only on a **thinly-covered small/mid-cap whose value concentrates in one dominant asset AND that no live dashboard or vendor already tracks.** Pre-screen for the lead *first* — "is the reference number published for free?" — before spending a minute on physics or dollars.
- **Benter DNA** (structural mispricing against a recreational crowd) survives only where the structural edge exists **AND the outcome variable is not published.** It dies wherever the answer is disseminated for free. A narrow, shrinking, but real space.
- **Genuinely proprietary or early data** where you are not competing with a vendor — you collect or license something others cannot simply subscribe to.

## The 14 winner patterns (reference)

Distilled from the 60 documented successes, retained as generative seeds for future work:

1. **Build-Moat on Commodity Raw Material** — The largest cluster (RS Metrics, Orbital Insight, Descartes Labs, SpaceKnow, CargoMetrics, container-port CNN, YipitData, PriceStats, Revelio Labs) shares one DNA: the raw feed — satellite imagery, AIS pings, web pages, resumes — was cheap or outright public, and therefore worthless on its own. The entire edge lived in the expensive, unglamorous BUILD: a computer-vision pipeline over 65,000 parking lots, a scraper-plus-reconciliation engine that maps messy pages to a company's exact reported KPI definition, entity resolution over a billion profiles. A rival would have to redo years of engineering, not write a check. This is the sharpest winner/loser divide in the set: losers buy a finished number anyone else can also buy and the edge is already arbitraged; winners own the processing that converts raw exhaust into the number nobody else has computed. The signal fires every reporting cycle across many liquid names, so the build amortizes.
2. **The Metric That Finishes Before the Announcement** — Car counts, worker-shift geolocation, foot traffic, card spend and e-receipts (RS Metrics, Thasos/Tesla, Foursquare/Chipotle, Second Measure/Blue Apron, Slice, Edison, Froot-Kang-Ozik-Sadka) all exploit a mechanical TIMING edge distinct from macro front-running: the quantity you measure IS a tight proxy for the exact number the company will report, and it accumulates continuously through the reporting window — so it is essentially complete weeks before the print while sell-side consensus and slow retail flow still price off stale data. Peer-reviewed work (JFQA, MIT/Sigmetrics, Berkeley Haas) confirmed prices did NOT embed the signal before disclosure and that the abnormal return came at retail's expense. Losers measure something loosely correlated and noisy; winners measure a direct, already-finished proxy for a scheduled binary catalyst.
3. **Front-Run the Slow Official Statistic** — EIA inventories, USDA WASDE, BLS non-farm payrolls, CPI, ICSG copper output and the official PMIs are scheduled, market-moving numbers produced by a slow surveyor. Genscape, Ursa Space, Kayrros, Earth-i, LinkUp, PriceStats and Descartes each produced an independent, higher-frequency estimate of the SAME quantity days-to-weeks ahead of the official print (LinkUp beat NFP consensus ~65% of the time; PriceStats runs daily vs a monthly CPI; Genscape published Cushing stocks during an EIA shutdown), then traded it into the deepest, most liquid macro instrument — crude, ag futures, rates, FX — where transaction costs clear trivially. The unsophisticated counterparty is the entire market still waiting for the lagging release.
4. **Measure What Has No Trustworthy Official Number** — SpaceKnow's China Satellite Manufacturing Index, Orbital Insight's China/Iran crude stocks, PriceStats exposing Argentina's understated inflation, and Kayrros' read on opaque floating storage worked partly for a special reason: the official series was manipulated, smoothed, delayed indefinitely, or simply did not exist. That has two consequences money can't buy — there is no cheaper substitute to bootstrap from, and there is no consensus number for your edge to converge on and decay against. Distrust or absence of the official figure is itself the durable opportunity: the market must price SOMETHING, and your independent read becomes the only credible input.
5. **Own the Panel Nobody Can License** — Foursquare's ~50M-user location panel, App Annie's Connect SDK telemetry, Edison's own mail client, Slice/Rakuten's receipt app and Envestnet|Yodlee's bank-aggregation layer produced first-party data that was structurally impossible for a rival to buy or scrape — because it flowed from an asset the firm OWNED outright (an app, an SDK, an inbox, a payment/aggregation chokepoint). Exclusivity here is not 'we processed it better,' it is 'this data physically cannot exist for anyone else.' The App Annie SEC case marks the boundary of the pattern: secretly using confidential SDK telemetry to sharpen estimates is exactly where an ownership moat crosses into securities fraud.
6. **The Physical or Exclusive-License Access Moat** — Some moats cannot be coded. Genscape chartered aircraft with infrared cameras and bolted electromagnetic sensors to power-plant and refinery perimeters; Premise Data paid on-the-ground contributors in 120+ countries where web-price density is too thin to scrape; Majestic/M Science and Quandl locked datasets under EXCLUSIVE license. The barrier is capex, field operations, or a signed exclusivity that a software-only rival cannot leap with money-plus-code — and this class of moat kept producing even when official channels stopped (Genscape crude data during the EIA furlough). Corporate-jet M&A tracking is the hybrid: public ADS-B, but tail-number-to-owner resolution fused across five feeds, several exclusively licensed.
7. **Bet Against a Structurally Biased Crowd** — Every parimutuel and sports case (Benter, Woods, Ranogajec, Bloom/Starlizard, Benham/Smartodds, Voulgaris, Walters/Computer Group) shares one counterparty: an unsophisticated crowd — or a margin-laden book fed by recreational money — whose bias is persistent and structural (favorite-longshot bias, home/popular-team shading). Benter formalized the winning move: build a fundamental probability model, then fold the crowd's OWN implied probability back in as a model variable, so the final estimate strictly dominates both your raw model and the consensus. It fires thousands of times per season with enormous capacity, and the edge is durable precisely because the bias is behavioral and recurs every event.
8. **Transfer a Proven Edge-Discipline Into a Softer Arena** — Thorp (blackjack card-counting → warrants/convertibles at Princeton Newport), Yass/SIG (poker and racetrack pricing → options market making) and Hull (blackjack → options market making) all carried the SAME machinery — quantify a probabilistic edge, size by Kelly, manage variance, never be the least-informed player at the table — from a game they had already beaten into a financial market where the marginal counterparty was less sophisticated than they were. Thorp was pricing options before Black-Scholes was published. The generalizable insight: a rigorous framework that wins decisively in one venue is often simply un-applied one venue over.
9. **Front-Run Mechanical, Price-Insensitive Forced Flow** — Index inclusions (Tesla Dec 2020), Russell reconstitution, the Goldman/GSCI roll, leveraged-and-inverse ETF end-of-day resets and USO's front-month roll all feature a counterparty that MUST trade a computable size at a publicly known time regardless of price, to control tracking error. Model the imbalance from published rules and get in front of it. This pattern also carries the set's loudest failure lessons about durability: the S&P additions premium decayed from ~7.4% (1990s) to a statistically insignificant ~0.8% as arbitrageurs crowded in, and Mou showed the Goldman-roll edge compresses as arb capital deploys. It survives only where the forced flow overwhelms the front-runners (Tesla's ~1.7% index weight) or a structural constraint makes it asymmetric (USO into a full Cushing with no buyer, driving WTI negative).
10. **Infer Positioning That Forces Future Flow** — COT (over-extended speculators mean-revert while informed commercial hedgers sit on the other side), dealer gamma/GEX (short-gamma hedging is self-reinforcing), and the GameStop >100%-of-float short plus concentrated call buying all reward inferring WHO is long or short, so you can predict the flow their hedging or capitulation will be forced to produce. The processing moat is the inference itself: COT must be normalized correctly (Briese's COT Index across 63 commodities), and dealer positioning must be reconstructed from options open interest with the ownership assumptions SqueezeMetrics/SpotGamma built. The read is available before the move it predicts — open interest telegraphs where hedging pressure will build.
11. **Get Paid by a Counterparty With No P&L Discipline** — The most durable 'counterparties' in the set aren't traders. Cum-ex extracted multiple refunds from a tax authority whose reclaim system never reconciled withholding to a single claim; Ranogajec's Punters Club earned more from tote and casino REBATES on LOSING bets (~US$44M) than from picking winners (~US$8M), because its billions in turnover fattened operator commissions; index rollers pay the roll every month by mandate. These institutions disburse money mechanically for structure or volume, not because you were right. The edge is legal/operational engineering or sheer scale — with the explicit caveat that cum-ex's engineering eventually drew criminal prosecution, so legality must be settled before profitability.
12. **Out-Handicap a Binary Catalyst, Then Diversify the Tail** — Paulson's merger-arbitrage desk (and COT-style mean-reversion) win by reading a discrete outcome's odds — deal completion under antitrust, financing, shareholder-vote and MAC-clause risk — better than the marginal holder, and by holding many independent bets so any single break is survivable. The spread IS the compensation for binary risk; the edge is superior odds-estimation (Paulson's seven-point completion checklist) plus portfolio construction that absorbs size. The failure mode is written into the same case: concentration killed returns when he over-sized breaking deals (Valeant, failed mergers) in 2015-2018.
13. **A Bespoke Private Signal Aimed at One Big Catalyst** — Theo's self-funded neighbor-effect election polling on Polymarket, corporate-jet ADS-B tracking that caught Occidental's Gulfstream at Omaha two days before Buffett's US$10B commitment, and Enron's proprietary long-range weather forecasting each created a PRIVATE input that corrected a SPECIFIC consensus error ahead of a SPECIFIC high-stakes event. Unlike always-on panels, these are commissioned or bespoke observations built for one catalyst where the public consensus rests on a known, correctable bias (public-poll bias, no visibility into private jets, market-implied temperature). The counterparty is everyone pricing off the same flawed public input.
14. **Machine-Read Recurring Text Faster and More Consistently** — Prattle (later Liquidnet) replaced slow, inconsistent, human-biased interpretation of central-bank statements with an instant, identical quantitative score derived from a per-institution lexicon covering 15 central banks and ~3,000 companies. It beat the marginal human reader on BOTH speed and consistency at every scheduled meeting and print, into enormous rates/FX liquidity. The unsophisticated counterparty is a human parsing dense policy language slowly and idiosyncratically; the moat is the years-built, trained lexicon that cannot be reconstructed overnight. It fires on a known calendar, so the edge is repeatable and scalable.

## Honest closing

Two independent batches, each explicitly bred to imitate documented winners, each terminating at ~0 validated edges — plus a self-mined signal that turned out to be a measurement artifact. If the goal was a ready-to-trade strategy, the program returned none. If the goal was to know the truth about this search space cheaply, it succeeded completely: it mapped the frontier, killed every darling before it cost money, and produced one genuinely useful free risk control and one durable analytical discipline. Negative knowledge, delivered honestly, is the real product here.
