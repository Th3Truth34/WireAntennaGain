# The Edge Ledger

A growing catalog of **underutilized data sources that are predictive of future market or gambling outcomes** — in the spirit of jockey weights, satellite parking-lot counts, Google Trends, presidential tweets, and weather-vs-commodities.

Each entry names the exact data source, how to collect it, the **specific bet or trade** it implies, the causal mechanism, who is on the other side, the real information lead, why it is still under-exploited, and a rough +EV estimate with confidence.

This is **open-ended discovery**, not a pass/fail screen. Entries are hypotheses worth investigating, ranked by nothing but the order they were found. No source is ever repeated. Research notes, not investment advice — every entry needs its own backtest before real money.

**Discipline carried over from earlier work:** before staking anything here, measure the expected move in *dollars* against the tick/spread/vig, and confirm the information lead is real (is the reference number already published free?). Those two checks killed ~760 prior candidates.

## Scope boundaries

Two rules govern what belongs here. Both were written after candidates were generated and **dropped** for violating them — the discovery swarm reliably finds edges that fail these tests, so they are enforced on every batch.

**1. Watch institutions, not people.** Entries observe **institutions, infrastructure, commerce, and physical/public phenomena** — corporate filings, product catalogs, sensors, regulator databases, physical activity. Entries do **not** surveil the private behavior of named individuals. *Dropped under this rule:* "The 3 A.M. Gamertag," which proposed building identity maps of specific named athletes and profiling their overnight gaming-presence to bet against them; its stated moat was that the athletes don't realize those APIs expose them. The test is not "is the data technically public" — it is *whether you are watching an institution or a person*. Contrast entry #7, The Tungsten Tell: reading a manufacturer's published catalog to learn a darts pro changed barrel weight is commerce observing commerce, and is fine.

**2. Use the front door.** Collection must run through **sanctioned access paths** — public or documented APIs, published files and filings, bulk data offered for download, or your own sensors. Entries do **not** evade anti-bot controls, use credentials not issued to you, exploit error-message side channels, or scrape systems whose operators have signalled refusal (robots.txt, no public API). *Dropped under this rule:* "The Endcap Census" (mass-scraping retailers' internal inventory via residential proxies to evade bot defenses, plus a cart-endpoint error message used as a side channel to extract non-public stock counts) and "The Heartworm Denominator" (a nonprofit's undocumented internal API accessed with a bearer token harvested from page JavaScript, against a robots-blocked endpoint). An edge that depends on getting into someone else's system is not an edge, it is a liability.

> **Note on entry #18, The Embargoed Sounding.** Passive reception of unencrypted radio you are not accessing anyone's system to obtain is categorically different from the dropped scrapers, and it was not flagged. But it is the one entry here whose *use* — as opposed to its collection — needs legal review before any commercial application: check the ECPA rules on divulging intercepted communications and the airlines' proprietary claims over MDCRS/AMDAR payloads. Receiving is not the same as being free to monetize.

## 1. PurpleAir Ballpark Fade

**Arena:** gambling (MLB — and MLS — game/player props and team totals at mainstream sportsbooks: DraftKings, FanDuel, Pinnacle)
**Domain:** Sports betting props & live markets

- **Data source:** The PurpleAir citizen air-quality sensor network: crowd-deployed sensors reporting raw PM2.5 (plus temp/humidity/pressure) at the individual-sensor level, updating roughly every 2 minutes, with several units typically within 1-2 km of most downtown outdoor ballparks. This is hyperlocal, sensor-level particulate at the stadium — not the sparse, hourly EPA/AirNow regulatory grid that books (if they look at air at all) would see. Pair it with EPA AirNow's CO feed, because the umpire study found CO has an even larger error effect than PM2.5.
- **How to collect it:** Free PurpleAir Developer API key (Google-linked). Query sensors inside a bounding box around each outdoor MLB/MLS stadium; apply the published EPA correction factor to raw PM2.5 and drop dead/divergent sensors (dual-laser flag). Pull CO from the EPA AirNow API. Join to confirmed lineups via the free MLB Stats API (statsapi.mlb.com) and to opening/closing totals via an odds API (e.g., The Odds API). Backtest against historical PurpleAir (data to 2016), Retrosheet/Statcast game logs, and closing lines to fix the sign and threshold before staking. Whole pipeline is buildable by one person.
- **The bet / trade:** Single-game MLB props, entered after lineups post and before first pitch, held to settlement. Primary: ROAD team-total UNDER when stadium PM2.5 is elevated AND materially above the road team's home-city baseline (visitors are hit ~2-3x harder than acclimatized home hitters). Secondary: game-total UNDER and starting-pitcher strikeouts OVER on the same trigger. Trigger band: corrected PM2.5 ~35+ ug/m3 (sub-advisory drift days the book ignores) up through wildfire-smoke spikes (55-200+), excluding levels high enough to threaten postponement.
- **Mechanism:** Elevated fine-particulate/CO exposure causes short-term cognitive and aerobic impairment. Three reinforcing channels suppress scoring: (1) hitters lose reaction time and bat speed, degrading contact quality — worse for the ROAD team, which isn't acclimatized to that city's air; (2) umpires make measurably more incorrect ball/strike calls, adding strike-zone noise that favors pitchers; (3) position players fatigue faster, cutting late-game rallies. Net effect: fewer runs and more strikeouts, concentrated on the visiting offense.
- **Who's on the other side:** The book and sharp syndicates on the other side of the total. Every serious book buys a weather feed (temp, wind, humidity, precip — DTN/NWS-type) and models park factors, but PM2.5/CO is not in those feeds and not in any standard totals model. They react to air only on a news event (postponement chatter, an AQI advisory, TV showing haze). Official regulatory monitors are hourly and often 5-15 km from the park, so even attentive sharps see degraded air late and coarse. On ordinary drift/inversion days below the news threshold, no one on the other side is pricing air at all.
- **Information lead:** On smoke-plume days: ~30-90 minutes, the gap between a 2-minute hyperlocal sensor at the park and the hourly, distant regulatory monitor / eventual broadcast mention. On sub-advisory days (PM2.5 ~35-90, common during western/midwestern/northeastern smoke season and urban inversions): effectively unlimited — the market never prices it, so the entire study-documented effect is uncaptured by the line.
- **Why still under-exploited:** Air quality is absent from every commercial sports-betting data product (weather is universally bought; particulate is not). PurpleAir is fragmented citizen-sensor data that needs correction factors, bad-sensor flagging, and geospatial matching to stadiums — no vendor packages it for bettors. The causal evidence lives in an environmental-economics journal (JAERE 2018 umpire study) and public-health/soccer journals, not sports-analytics circles, so it hasn't crossed over. It is exactly the weird/manual/unpackaged source that stays mispriced.
- **Rough +EV:** Modest, seasonal, concentrated. Documented effects are small per unit (10 ug/m3 PM2.5 ~ +2.6% umpire errors; road-hitter pass/accuracy analogs ~2-8% in soccer), implying maybe 0.1-0.4 runs of true suppression on a triggered game. Fires on only ~3-8% of the slate in a normal week, spiking hard during June-October smoke events. On triggered games I'd estimate a ~2-4% edge on road-total unders and pitcher-K overs — beats -110 vig with discipline, but low volume and clustered risk.
- **Confidence:** Medium-low. Biggest uncertainty: the umpire study proves error rate rises, not that RUNS fall — I'm inferring net run-suppression direction and magnitude from adjacent soccer/athlete-performance literature, and the umpire-error channel could partially cancel (zone expansion vs. contraction is unproven). The sign and size must be confirmed by a historical PurpleAir-vs-closing-line backtest before real money; if the net run effect is smaller than ~0.15 runs it won't clear vig.

## 2. Cold-Air Bleeder Fade

**Arena:** Gambling — US parimutuel Thoroughbred racing (win pool plus vertical/horizontal exotics; lay on exchanges/fixed-odds where legal)
**Domain:** Horse & greyhound racing / parimutuel

- **Data source:** The unpriced signal is a three-way intersection that no one assembles: (1) per-start Lasix/furosemide medication designations and "bled / bled through Lasix" chart footnotes from Equibase charts and past performances — Equibase is the official DB and every US state is required to list Lasix in the charts — which identify CHRONIC BLEEDERS now entered in a Lasix-PROHIBITED condition; (2) the HISA national Veterinarians'/EIPH bleeder list (hisaus.org); (3) hyperlocal ambient temperature + dewpoint at that specific track and post time from a free weather API. The novel, unmodeled factor is the ambient-temperature × forced-no-Lasix × bleeding-history interaction. This is only exploitable because HISA banned race-day Lasix in 2yo and stakes races (effective May 2023, reaffirmed 2026), creating a brand-new population of chronic bleeders running pharmacologically UNPROTECTED.
- **How to collect it:** Scrape Equibase result charts / DRF Formulator PP files for each entrant's full medication history (L = on Lasix, L1 = first-time Lasix, blank = none) and footnote comments; a horse showing "L" across recent allowance/claiming starts but now entered in a stakes or 2yo race = "first time OFF Lasix," i.e., an unprotected chronic bleeder. Pull the HISA Vet's/bleeder list. Geocode each track and query NWS api.weather.gov or Open-Meteo for the hourly forecast (temperature, humidity/dewpoint) at post time — available days ahead; optionally add PurpleAir particulate AQI near the track. Flag rule: entrant in a Lasix-prohibited race (2yo or stakes) + established prior Lasix/bleeder history + forecast post-time temp roughly below 55-60°F (dry/cold).
- **The bet / trade:** Fade the flagged unprotected bleeder in a single race, settled at race completion the same day. In pure parimutuel: exclude it from your win bet and from exacta/trifecta/superfecta keys, and build "against" tickets spreading the rest of the field to capture the overlay its suppressed chance leaves on every other runner. Where fixed-odds or exchange betting is legal (international books, US fixed-odds markets), lay the horse to win / back the field.
- **Mechanism:** Cold, dry air raises EIPH (exercise-induced pulmonary hemorrhage) severity — veterinary literature ties ambient temps under ~68°F to markedly higher EIPH risk. A chronic bleeder built its recent, market-visible form WITH Lasix, but HISA bars furosemide in 2yo and stakes races, so it must run unmedicated. On a cold day it bleeds harder, and horses with moderate/severe EIPH are about 4x LESS likely to win and ~2x less likely to hit the board than mild/no-EIPH horses. Its true win probability therefore collapses relative to the Lasix-aided, warm-weather form line the crowd is pricing off of.
- **Who's on the other side:** Recreational bettors and speed-figure/form models keying on recent Beyer figures — all earned WITH Lasix in warmer conditions — take the other side by overbetting the horse. Even sharp handicappers who note the "first-time-off-Lasix" angle essentially never quantify ambient temperature or EIPH severity, and the tote crowd prices neither. Trainers and vets know the horse bleeds but are legally barred from treating it and cannot act on the information — a clean asymmetry favoring whoever mines the medication line plus the forecast.
- **Information lead:** Real and durable. Entries (which fix both the horse's Lasix status and the Lasix-prohibited race condition) publish 2-4 days before post; the temperature forecast is available days ahead. The tote never incorporates ambient temperature or the off-Lasix×cold interaction at any point, even at post time — so you identify the setup days before the market forms AND the edge persists all the way to pool close because nobody re-prices it. Lead is days over when (if ever) the book learns it.
- **Why still under-exploited:** The enabling structural change — HISA's race-day Lasix ban limited to 2yo and stakes races — has only existed since May 2023 and is still politically contested, so the "unprotected chronic bleeder" population is a genuinely new feature of the game. No commercial handicapping product (EquinEdge, TrackSmart AI, Alezan.ai, DRF) crosses an individual horse's bleeding history with hyperlocal race-day temperature; the AI tools chase figures and trip, not physiology-vs-weather. Extracting it requires manually stitching Equibase medication chart lines + "bled" footnotes + HISA bleeder lists + a weather API per track — fragmented, manual, unglamorous, and outside every existing data product.
- **Rough +EV:** Fires narrowly: a chronic bleeder, in a Lasix-prohibited (2yo/stakes) race, on a cold day, at a track carding such races — perhaps 0-3 qualifying horses per cold race-day at northern/winter meets. Where it fires the effect size is large (up to ~4x win-rate suppression), so fading a horse bet like a 3-1 second choice whose true chance is depressed 25-40% leaves a meaningful overlay across the rest of the field. Rough: +5-15% ROI on the against-tickets/lays when the setup triggers, at low volume — a specialist angle, not a high-frequency machine.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Biggest uncertainty: whether ambient temperature predicts race-day EIPH SEVERITY (not merely diagnosis risk) strongly and consistently enough at the single-race level to beat noise — an individual cold day does not guarantee a severe bleed. Secondary: volume is thin, and 2yos often lack an established bleeding history, concentrating the reliable plays on older stakes horses whose entire recent form was built on Lasix they can no longer use.

## 3. Ad-Reservation Surrender Index (FCC OPIF)

**Arena:** gambling — regulated/crypto prediction markets (Kalshi + Polymarket), individual-seat and chamber-control election contracts
**Domain:** Elections & prediction markets (Polymarket/Kalshi)

- **Data source:** The FCC Online Public Inspection File (OPIF) "Political Files" — the raw, per-station broadcast and local-cable ad contracts, order revisions, and CANCELLATION/non-renewal notices that campaigns, party committees (NRSC/DSCC/NRCC/DCCC), and super PACs (Senate Leadership Fund, etc.) file. The alpha event is specifically a cancellation or failure-to-renew of reserved fall airtime in the DMAs covering a competitive 2026 seat (and, inversely, brand-new reservations appearing). This is a distinct, faster, richer source than FEC ad-spend filings: OPIF captures super-PAC/dark-money issue ads, forward-dated reservations, and cancellations in near-real-time, none of which FEC shows early.
- **How to collect it:** publicfiles.fcc.gov REST API (developer portal) to enumerate every TV/radio/cable entity in each target DMA, then poll each station's "Political File" folder for newly uploaded or revised documents (each carries an upload timestamp). Download the order-form/contract/invoice/cancellation PDFs, OCR + parse advertiser name, flight dates, week count, and gross spend, and DIFF against the prior order to detect reductions, cancellations, or unconverted reservations. Attribute each advertiser to a partisan side via the FEC committee registry. Supplement with NCC (local-cable) order forms, also in OPIF. Mechanically this is ProPublica's "Free the Files" dataset — but automated, event-diffed, and wired to market prices instead of crowdsourced by hand.
- **The bet / trade:** On the specific-seat Kalshi/Polymarket contract: SHORT (buy the opponent's YES) the side that cancels or declines to renew reserved airtime in that seat's markets; LONG the side that books NEW reservations. Aggregate detected pullouts across the tipping-point seats to trade the Senate-control and House-control contracts. Horizon: days to ~3 weeks — hold until the pullout surfaces publicly and the seat reprices, or ride to Election-Day settlement.
- **Mechanism:** Ad money is the most honest signal in politics because it is costly and set by private internal tracking polls. Committees and PACs reserve airtime months ahead, then cancel or decline to renew when their own polling says a seat is unwinnable (or safely banked). A cancellation is a near-concession; a fresh reservation is a wager that the seat just became competitive. The contract change hits the station's OPIF political file almost immediately, and the win-probability implication is strong and directional — historically the side that pulls out loses that seat.
- **Who's on the other side:** Retail and semi-pro prediction-market traders anchored to public polls, topline forecasts (Cook/Sabato/538-style), and vibes; they reprice a seat only when a "Party pulls out of Race X" headline lands or the race is called. Even sharper desks that eyeball marquee ad cancellations rarely wire down-ballot OPIF filings to the specific thin seat contracts, and commercial ad-trackers (AdImpact, Vivvix/Kantar) sell cleaned data to campaigns and TV networks for ad intelligence — it surfaces as lagged journalism, not as market positioning.
- **Information lead:** Cancellations/revisions post to the station political file within roughly 24–48h of the contract change — typically several days ahead of the Axios/Politico/Hill scoop for marquee races, and for unreported down-ballot or single-market pullouts the OPIF is the ONLY early signal, giving days-to-weeks of lead before the market ever notices. You learn the "surrender" the moment the traffic order changes; the book learns it when a reporter writes it up or the race is called.
- **Why still under-exploited:** OPIF political files are thousands of non-standardized, per-station PDFs (order forms, invoices, hand-annotated revisions) with no clean structured feed — ProPublica had to crowdsource the OCR in its "Free the Files" project precisely because the data is public but unparseable at a glance. Cleaned commercial versions exist (AdImpact, Vivvix) but are sold to campaigns and networks, not connected to prediction markets, and no one runs an automated OPIF cancellation-detector feeding Kalshi/Polymarket seat contracts. The parsing friction IS the moat.
- **Rough +EV:** When a side pulls reserved airtime from a competitive seat in the final ~6 weeks, its win probability historically craters (NH-2022 Bolduc, AZ-2022 Masters, WV-2018 Morrisey all lost after their side's pullout). If the abandoned side is still priced ~30–40c when the cancellation files versus a true ~10–15%, that is a ~15–25 percentage-point edge per event. It fires an estimated 8–15 times per midterm cycle across competitive seats, plus off-year and special races. Thin-market liquidity caps position size (hundreds to low-tens-of-thousands of dollars per contract), so per-trade edge is large but absolute capacity is modest.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Biggest uncertainty: reliably parsing OPIF at scale AND separating a true "surrender" from benign cancellations — rate re-shopping, a shift of dollars to digital/streaming (which OPIF does not cover), or pulling from a genuinely SAFE seat. False positives erode the edge, and for marquee races the media scoop closes the lead fast, so the durable, repeatable edge concentrates in thin down-ballot, state, and single-market contracts rather than the headline Senate seats.

## 4. Lot Rot

**Arena:** financial-market (US single-name equities: RV / marine / powersports manufacturers — WGO, THO, PII, BC, MBUU, MCFT, PATK, LCII)
**Domain:** Single-name equity operational exhaust

- **Data source:** Real-time DEALER ON-LOT inventory for RVs, boats, and powersports — the physical units sitting on dealer lots right now, tracked per manufacturer/brand: unit counts, days-on-lot (listing age), model-year mix (aging), and list-price cuts / discount depth. This is the channel-inventory metric management itself guides on (Thor literally reports "North American independent dealer inventory ~75,000 units"; Winnebago's guidance cuts cite "measured dealer ordering" and "dealer caution on inventory replenishment"). Crucially, it is NOT the same as the incumbent paid product: Statistical Surveys Inc. (owned by Trader Interactive) sells lagged, revised RETAIL REGISTRATION data (sell-through from state DMVs), not real-time on-lot channel inventory (sell-in / lot fullness).
- **How to collect it:** Scrape the national trader marketplaces by make/model: RVTrader.com, BoatTrader.com, ATVTrader / PWCTrader / CycleTrader (all Trader Interactive), plus individual franchised-dealer inventory pages. Structured feeds exist for a small team: MarketCheck's Recreational Vehicles API advertises "real-time dealer inventory"; Boats Group / boats.com exposes a REST Inventory API; Apify has ready BoatTrader scrapers. Pull make/model/year/price/dealer/listing-date daily, dedupe by VIN/hull-ID, and build a per-brand index of (a) new-unit lot count, (b) median days-on-lot, (c) share of prior-model-year units (aging), (d) discount depth vs list. Map brands to parent tickers (e.g., Grand Design/Jayco/Airstream -> THO; Winnebago/Grand Design->WGO; Bennington/Sea-Ray/Boston Whaler/Mercury->BC; Malibu/Cobalt/Pursuit->MBUU).
- **The bet / trade:** Event-driven around quarterly prints. When your scraped on-lot index shows lot counts rising + days-on-lot lengthening + discounting deepening beyond the seasonal norm ahead of a print, get short (or buy puts) on the manufacturer 2-6 weeks before earnings and hold through the report/guidance. Reverse (cover/long calls) when lots are drawing down and aged model-year units clear — the setup for a wholesale restock and shipment beat. Primary names: WGO, THO (RV), BC, MBUU, MCFT (marine), PII (powersports); PATK/LCII as component read-throughs. Horizon: weeks (position into the print), event settlement at the guidance update.
- **Mechanism:** Dealers set wholesale orders off two things: how full their lots are and how fast units turn. When on-lot units climb, age, and require discounting to move, dealers slam the brakes on replenishment ordering. That directly cuts the manufacturer's WHOLESALE shipments and forces guidance down — the exact "measured dealer ordering / destocking" language that gaps these stocks 5-15%. On-lot inventory + aging + price cuts is the leading input; reported shipments and the guidance revision are the lagging output you can measure the cause of, continuously and in advance.
- **Who's on the other side:** Sell-side and consensus, who wait for (1) Statistical Surveys' lagged/revised RETAIL registration prints (measures sell-through, not channel fill) and (2) management's own quarterly channel-inventory disclosure on the call; plus passive/index and retail holders. They're slow because assembling a per-brand on-lot count + aging + discount index across thousands of fragmented dealer sites and four separate trader marketplaces is tedious, unglamorous engineering, and the packaged incumbent (SSI) sells the wrong metric (retail regs), so almost no one is nowcasting the channel-inventory number management actually guides on.
- **Information lead:** Daily/real-time vs. a quarterly disclosure: roughly 1-3 months ahead of the company's own channel-inventory number and guidance revision, and ahead of SSI's lagged, multi-week-revised retail registration reports. You watch dealer lots fill and units age in near real time while the Street is still working off last quarter's management color and stale registration scans.
- **Why still under-exploited:** The raw listing data is sold to BUILD marketplaces (MarketCheck, Boats Group), not as an equity signal; the recognized paid alt-data product in this space (Statistical Surveys/Trader Interactive) is RETAIL registrations, a different and lagged metric. No one packages a "dealer on-lot channel-inventory + aging + discount nowcast" as trading alpha the way Yipit/M Science do for consumer names. These are thinly-covered mid/small-caps in a fragmented, niche, manual-scrape corner — precisely the kind of operational exhaust that stays under-exploited.
- **Rough +EV:** Fires ~4x/yr across ~8 names (~30 setups/yr); only a subset show a channel signal materially diverging from consensus. Base-rate move on a guidance-cut/beat surprise in this group is high single to mid-double-digit percent single-day. Expect a handful of high-conviction, double-digit-edge setups per year plus many small ones; positioned via defined-risk options into prints, comfortably +EV if the index leads the disclosed channel-inventory delta even 60-65% of the time.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Biggest uncertainty: online listings blend new vs. used and don't cleanly separate wholesale sell-in from retail sell-through, some dealers under-list online, and you must strip out strong seasonality plus judge whether the channel read is already in consensus — so the signal is most reliably NEW information at inflection points and for the thinner-covered names, less so for a fully-telegraphed destocking cycle everyone is already modeling.

## 5. River-Temp Reactor Derate Nowcast

**Arena:** financial-market (European power: EEX French/German baseload futures + day-ahead auction; optionally the France-minus-Germany spread)
**Domain:** Energy & commodity physical telemetry

- **Data source:** Real-time French river water-temperature and river-flow telemetry, cross-referenced reactor-by-reactor against EDF's legal per-site thermal-discharge thresholds. The underexploited combination is: (1) EDF Open Data dataset "Seuils et limites des rejets thermiques aux abords des centrales nucleaires d'EDF" (per-site max downstream river temperature, e.g. 28C on the Garonne at Golfech, ~26-28C at Rhone/Loire/Moselle sites, plus min-flow limits); (2) Hub'Eau "Temperature des cours d'eau" API (near-real-time river temps, synced live since July 2022); (3) Hub'Eau "Hydrometrie" API (dense real-time river flow/debit at gauges up/downstream of each riverside reactor). This lets you compute a live per-reactor "distance to legal cutoff" that governs curtailment.
- **How to collect it:** All free REST/JSON-CSV: hubeau.eaufrance.fr (temperature + hydrometrie endpoints) for live river temp and flow; opendata.edf.fr dataset API for the threshold table per reactor; RTE eCO2mix / RTE data portal for real-time actual nuclear generation (to measure the realized derate); EDF's REMIT "Unavailability and message list" for the official curtailment notices you are front-running. Drive a simple river-thermal nowcast with ECMWF/Meteo-France air-temp and precip forecasts feeding each reactor's upstream gauge, geolocated to the ~19 riverside sites (Golfech, Bugey, St-Alban, Tricastin, Cruas, Blayais, Chooz, Cattenom, Civaux, the Loire cluster, etc.).
- **The bet / trade:** Long EEX French front-month baseload power futures and/or the day-ahead auction (alternatively long the France-minus-Germany baseload spread) when the nowcast projects river-temperature curtailment that the curve is underpricing; and the mirror trade: SHORT the day-ahead "heat premium" when a forecast hot spell will NOT actually breach thresholds because rivers are still cool or high-flow. Horizon: intraday/day-ahead out to front-month (days to ~3 weeks), concentrated June-September and worst in drought years.
- **Mechanism:** River temperature rises with a 2-4 day lag behind air temperature and is amplified nonlinearly by low flow. When the downstream temperature (or the plant's discharge delta-T) crosses the ASN legal limit, EDF is legally required to derate or shut the riverside reactor. France loses gigawatts of its cheapest baseload, its normal daytime export surplus collapses (in June 2026 it fell from ~11-12 GW to under 3 GW within days), and the marginal MWh flips to gas or imports. French wholesale power spikes (month-ahead moved up to ~+11.7% in the June 2026 episode) and France's usual discount to Germany compresses or inverts.
- **Who's on the other side:** Naive weather-driven power algos, CTAs, and macro flow that trade the heatwave AIR-temperature headline with no river-flow context, plus retail/CFD power flow and participants who wait for EDF's official REMIT confirmation. They are slow/wrong because curtailment is nonlinear in river flow: the same 35C spell barely bites after a wet spring (high flow, cool rivers) but is severe after drought (low flow, rivers already near threshold). Air-temp-only models therefore systematically over-price short hot spells on cool/high-flow rivers (your fade) and under-price prolonged heat on drought-stressed rivers (your press).
- **Information lead:** Roughly 1-4 days on the specific curtailment magnitude and timing versus EDF's REMIT unavailability message (which is typically issued only a few days to ~a week ahead and gets revised), plus a continuous accuracy edge over air-temperature-only models across the whole episode. You know which reactors will breach, by how much, and for how long, before the official notice and before the naive crowd reprices.
- **Why still under-exploited:** It is not a packaged product like Kpler/Vortexa/Genscape — there is no commoditized "river-temp-to-reactor-derate" feed. The edge requires bespoke assembly of three fragmented free sources (EDF's threshold table, sparse Hub'Eau temperature stations of which only ~50 are active, and separate flow gauges) plus reactor geolocation and a small river-thermal model. Because station coverage is patchy and per-reactor thresholds live in legal decrees, most participants fall back on crude air-temperature heat forecasts as a proxy rather than modeling the actual physical constraint.
- **Rough +EV:** Event-driven and seasonal: roughly 5-15 tradable episodes per summer (June-Sept), larger and more frequent in drought years. Each curtailment call the curve underprices can be worth several to low-tens of EUR/MWh on day-ahead / front-month; assuming a ~55-60% hit rate with disciplined sizing gives modest absolute EV but an attractive concentrated-summer Sharpe. The fade side (heat that won't breach) adds symmetric opportunities and reduces outright weather beta if expressed as the France-vs-Germany spread.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Biggest uncertainty: whether a small team's nowcast reliably beats well-resourced EU power desks that already model nuclear availability. Secondary risks: sparse real-time river-temperature station coverage (forcing flow + air-temp + EDF's published downstream temps as proxies), and ASN "security-of-supply" derogations that let EDF legally run reactors above the normal limit when the grid needs it — which loosens the derate-to-price mapping and injects noise. EEX power access is also a hurdle for non-institutional traders (needs an FCM, prop desk, or CFD route).

## 6. The Multisig Signature Window

**Arena:** financial-market
**Domain:** Crypto on-chain & behavioral

- **Data source:** Not-yet-executed transactions sitting in project treasury / foundation / DAO multisig queues: (a) off-chain proposals collecting signatures in the Safe Transaction Service, and (b) on-chain governance Timelock transactions already queued with a fixed execution ETA. These reveal a treasury's exact intent (recipient, amount, calldata) hours-to-days before the transaction actually lands on-chain.
- **How to collect it:** Two free, public feeds, no auth: (1) Safe Transaction Service REST API — GET api.safe.global/tx-service/{chain}/api/v1/safes/{safeAddress}/multisig-transactions/?executed=false (verified live: returns JSON with to/value/data/nonce/confirmations/confirmationsRequired/submissionDate; per-chain slugs eth, arb1, base, oeth, matic, etc.). (2) An Ethereum/L2 archive node or log-subscription (own node, or Alchemy/QuickNode free tier) filtering for Compound/Uniswap Timelock QueueTransaction(bytes32,address,uint256,string,bytes,uint256) and OpenZeppelin TimelockController CallScheduled events. Supporting infra you must build: a curated map of token -> its treasury/foundation Safe addresses + governance Timelock contracts (from docs, Etherscan tags, Safe app URLs) across chains; a calldata decoder to classify intent (ERC20 transfer vs proxy upgradeToAndCall vs param change); and a recipient label set (CEX deposit clusters, OTC desks, market-maker wallets, vesting/airdrop distributors) from Arkham/Etherscan tags plus your own deposit-address clustering. Poll every 1-5 min.
- **The bet / trade:** When a monitored treasury/foundation queues (but has not executed) a large ERC20 transfer whose decoded recipient resolves to a CEX deposit address, OTC desk, or vesting/airdrop distributor, SHORT the token via perpetual swap (or buy a short-dated put where options exist). Size to the smaller of borrow/liquidity limits. Entry on detection; hold through execution and the ~24-72h sell-through window, then cover. Mirror-image long setups exist for queued bullish calldata (proxy upgrade shipping a launched product, new Curve/Balancer gauge, major-protocol integration), but the bearish supply signal is the higher-conviction core trade.
- **Mechanism:** Multisig-governed treasuries cannot move funds atomically: they collect M-of-N signatures off-chain in the Safe service, or queue on-chain behind a Timelock, before anyone can execute. The full intent — who gets how much of what token — is legible at proposal time. A queued transfer to an exchange deposit address / OTC desk / distributor is net new sell-side supply that has not yet hit the tape. Price only reacts once the tokens actually move and are sold; you position in the gap between proposal and execution, front-running the mechanical supply shock the queue has already committed to.
- **Who's on the other side:** The liquidity providers and momentum traders on the perp who take the short side, plus everyone whose alerting fires only on executed transfers. Nansen Smart-Money/exchange-flow alerts, Arkham, DeBank, and CryptoQuant all trigger on confirmed on-chain movement — they are structurally blind to an off-chain Safe proposal and mostly do not decode Timelock queue calldata for treasury transfers. The tools that DO read the queue (Safe Watcher, BlockSec Phalcon Safe{Wallet} Monitor) are sold to the wallet's own owners to catch hacks, not to traders. So the other side literally does not have the data yet; they are slow because their pipelines are keyed on block confirmation, not signature-collection state.
- **Information lead:** Off-chain Safe queue: minutes to several days between first proposal and reaching the signature threshold + execution — treasuries with distributed, multi-timezone signers routinely take hours to 1-2 days. On-chain Timelock: a hard, deterministic delay (Compound 2 days; many DAOs 2-7 days) with the exact executable block window known in advance from the ETA. Versus the market, which learns only when the transfer confirms and the coins start selling, the lead is the entire signing/timelock window — hours to a week.
- **Why still under-exploited:** The reading infrastructure exists but is framed 100% defensively (protect your own multisig), so no one packages queued-multisig intent as a trading feed — there is no Kpler/Ortex equivalent for it. The work is unglamorous curation, not a clever model: you must hand-maintain a token->safe/timelock address map across a dozen chains, decode arbitrary calldata to separate a real sell from a benign internal reshuffle, and keep a fresh CEX/OTC/MM deposit-label set as addresses rotate. That fragmented, manual, per-chain plumbing is exactly the kind of moat that keeps quant desks (who want clean turnkey datasets) and retail (who watch price) out, leaving the pre-execution window on the table.
- **Rough +EV:** Event-driven and episodic. Across a watchlist of a few hundred mid/small-cap governance tokens, clean qualifying signals (sizable queued transfer to a genuine exchange/OTC/distributor endpoint) fire on the order of dozens per month. On the qualifying subset where the queued outflow is meaningful vs daily volume (>~0.5-2x ADV), expect roughly 55-65% directional hit rate and ~1-3% average adverse move captured per event over the 1-3 day window, net of false positives (internal moves, superseded/cancelled proposals). Edge concentrates in small-caps where borrow and depth cap size; large-caps absorb it. Realistic as a steady stream of small, low-correlation, repeatable event trades rather than any single big hit.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Biggest uncertainty is recipient classification: distinguishing a true sell (CEX/OTC/distributor) from a benign treasury reshuffle depends on a good, constantly-refreshed deposit-address label set, and mislabels turn into whipsaw. Secondary risks: coverage is patchy (some teams sign fully on-chain or via private relayers and never touch the Safe service; Timelock adoption varies), and queued != executed, so cancelled/superseded proposals cost you some entries. Liquidity and borrow on the smallest names — where the edge is largest — cap deployable capital.

## 7. The Tungsten Tell

**Arena:** gambling — professional darts match markets (PDC Players Championship / Challenge Tour / European Tour qualifiers, plus MODUS Super Series), on exchanges (Betfair) and soft books
**Domain:** International & niche betting markets (soccer, cricket, tennis, esports, darts)

- **Data source:** A dated ledger of professional darts players' EQUIPMENT CHANGES — specifically the exact barrel model and gram weight each pro throws, and the timestamp at which it changes. This is the literal "jockey weights" of darts and no darts stats provider carries an equipment field. The signal lives in three fragmented places: (1) darts retailer/manufacturer product catalogs, which list every new signature barrel with its exact weight in grams and a machine-readable creation timestamp; (2) sponsor-switch press releases, which force a change (canonical case: Michael van Gerwen leaving his 8-year-unchanged setup when he signed with Winmau, and Winmau then selling "the actual darts used at the 2026 World Championship" as player sets); (3) enthusiast ID threads and stage stills that confirm the debut date. Cross-joined against per-event 3-dart averages, this yields "player X switched barrel/weight on date D" — a variable the entire betting market is blind to.
- **How to collect it:** Confirmed working by direct probe. PRIMARY (mechanical, free, no auth): darts retailers run Shopify, so the full catalog is a JSON API. `curl "https://www.dartscorner.com/products.json?limit=250&page=N"` returns every product with `vendor` (Target / Winmau / Red Dragon / Unicorn / Harrows / Shot / Mission / One80 / Loxley / Bulls), `product_type`, `sku`, `created_at`, `published_at`, `updated_at`, and variants whose titles carry the gram weight ("22g", "23g"). Verified live: returned full records with timestamps. Same endpoint verified on `https://www.reddragondarts.com/products.json` (a Red Dragon record showed created_at 2026-04-27 vs published_at 2026-07-31 — a 3-month catalog-build lead), and present on `winmau.com/products.json` and `dartshopper.com/products.json` (returns 429, needs backoff). Poll hourly, diff the SKU set → a new signature barrel appearing (e.g. "Bunting Gen 5 22g" when he previously threw a different weight) is the alert. SPONSOR-SWITCH LAYER: RSS/scrape dartsnews.com, live-darts.com, dartsworld.com, and the manufacturer news blogs (winmau.com/blogs/news etc.). CONFIRMATION LAYER: dartsnutz.net forum equipment-ID threads (a real thread "Adjusting To a New Set Up: How Long?" exists, thread-39226) plus walk-on/practice-room stills from PDC and MODUS streams. OUTCOME/BACKTEST LAYER: dartsorakel.com per-event averages (verified 200 at `https://dartsorakel.com/events/averages/{event_id}`), `https://pdpa.co.uk/averages/` (200), DartConnect at app.dartconnect.com (200) for floor-event leg-by-leg, and modussuperseries.com/results for six-days-a-week averages on the tier-2 pool.
- **The bet / trade:** Fade the switcher. On a CONFIRMED forced barrel change, lay the player (or back his opponent) in PDC Players Championship / Challenge Tour / European Tour-qualifier MATCH WINNER markets, plus back the opponent on the -1.5 / -2.5 leg handicap and take UNDER on the switcher's "most 180s" and "player total 180s" props. Window: every match he plays from stage debut until ~4-6 weeks / ~2 Super Series weekends after the switch. Settlement horizon is per-match (30-60 minutes), so it is a high-turnover, fast-recycling book of ~15-40 bets per switch event. The mirror trade: back him when the ledger shows a REVERSION (he quietly relists/returns to the old barrel model — visible as the old SKU reappearing in his hand on stream) or when the post-switch average curve on MODUS/floor data crosses back above his pre-switch baseline, which is when the market is still marking him down.
- **Mechanism:** Darts is a pure proprioceptive-repetition sport: the same throw, ~15,000 times a week, calibrated to a specific mass, length, centre of gravity, and grip texture. Change the barrel and you change release timing and trajectory, and the player must rebuild the motor program. This is settled lore inside the sport — there is a published coaching guide literally titled "Embrace the Slump" about the average drop that follows a setup change, and Sporting Life has run features on when pros should and should not change sets. The effect is measurable in the single number that determines darts outcomes: the 3-dart average, which maps near-monotonically to leg-win probability. Between two evenly matched pros, a 2-3 point average deficit is worth roughly 4-8% of match win probability. Critically, the switch is often NOT the player's choice — sponsor contracts and the annual "Generation N" product cycle force it. Winmau's 2026 Frontier launch shipped MvG and Gurney player sets; Target runs Generation X refreshes. A player who has thrown one barrel for eight years and is contractually moved to a new brand is an exogenous shock to his own skill level, on a date you can read off a JSON endpoint.
- **Who's on the other side:** Darts is priced by a small number of traders and semi-automated models off two inputs: (a) the PDC Order of Merit, which is a TWO-YEAR ROLLING PRIZE MONEY list — a structurally lagged, path-dependent proxy for skill that cannot react to anything for months; and (b) trailing 3-12 month 3-dart averages. Floor events are the soft spot: a Players Championship "Super Series" weekend is four events, 128 players, several hundred priced matches in 96 hours, which forces near-automated pricing with almost no trader attention per match; Challenge Tour and European Tour qualifiers are softer still. The recreational money on the other side is following names and TV familiarity. Nobody on either side of the book has an equipment field in their model — the information is treated as fan trivia sold by retailers, not as a performance covariate. The trader only learns after the degraded averages accumulate inside his trailing window.
- **Information lead:** Two to eight weeks of live, bettable matches. The retail SKU with the exact new gram weight appears for pre-order typically 1-6 weeks before the player debuts the barrel on the oche (and the Shopify `created_at` field is often months earlier than `published_at` — one verified Red Dragon record showed a 3-month gap). Sponsor-switch announcements land weeks to months ahead of the first competitive throw; MvG's Winmau deal was public while he was still openly undecided on which barrel he would actually use. On the other side, the market's trailing-average window needs roughly 3-6 weeks and 20+ post-switch matches before the new, worse numbers dominate the lookback and the price adjusts. There is also a faster second loop the market ignores entirely: MODUS Super Series runs six days a week year-round with full averages published, so for the tier-2 pool you can measure the actual post-switch damage on Monday-to-Saturday streamed data days before those same players appear in a PDC floor event that is priced off their PDC history.
- **Why still under-exploited:** There is no commercial dataset of professional darts equipment history — not from Opta, not from any betting data vendor, not from the darts stats sites themselves. dartsorakel and dartsdatabase provide averages, checkout %, and 180s but have no equipment field, so even the hobbyist analytics layer cannot join on it. The information sits in retailer product catalogs (commercial marketing data, not sports data), manufacturer press releases, a bulletin-board forum, and photographs — fragmented, multilingual, partly manual, and culturally filed under "fan trivia." Darts betting is also a small, low-limit market that quantitative shops structurally ignore, so the usual arb-away pressure is weak. The Shopify `/products.json` trick that turns the retail layer into a timestamped API is a general-purpose scraping technique nobody has pointed at a sport, because in no other sport does a $60 consumer product tell you that an athlete's motor program was just reset.
- **Rough +EV:** Roughly 40-90 confirmable equipment changes per year across PDC tour-card holders, Challenge/Development Tour, and the WDF/MODUS pool, of which maybe half are cleanly FORCED (sponsor change or contractual product-cycle refresh) rather than self-selected. Each forced switch generates ~15-40 priced matches inside the adjustment window, so ~600-1,500 clean bettable matches a year. If the true effect is a 1.5-3.0 point average deficit for 4-6 weeks, that is a 3-7% win-probability mispricing on evenly matched floor pairings; net of a 4-6% exchange/soft-book margin, expect ~2-4% ROI on turnover overall, with the best subset (a recognizable top-30 name laid at a short price in his first two weekends on a contractually forced new barrel) plausibly 6-10%. The binding constraint is capacity, not edge: Players Championship match markets clear maybe £50-500 per match on the exchange and soft books limit winners fast, so realistic scale is £50k-200k turnover a year, i.e. £2k-8k profit. Scaling up means carrying the same signal into European Tour and TV-major markets where limits are 10-50x larger but the pricing is meaningfully sharper.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Biggest uncertainty by far is ENDOGENEITY of the switch: players frequently change barrels BECAUSE they are already slumping, which means a naive backtest will show a huge post-switch average drop that is mostly reverse causality, and the market has arguably already priced the pre-existing decline. The strategy only survives if you can reliably partition forced switches (sponsor contract change, mandated "Generation N" refresh, brand exit) from voluntary ones — the forced subset is the clean exogenous shock, and isolating it roughly halves the usable sample. Secondary uncertainty is date precision: the retail listing date is not the competitive debut date, so the forum/stream confirmation layer is load-bearing rather than optional, and a mis-dated switch turns a fade into noise. Third, effect size is currently supported by strong sport-insider consensus and vivid single cases (Bunting improving on Gen 5, MvG forced off an 8-year setup) rather than by a published quantitative study — the first real work is building the historical equipment ledger and regressing average deltas on forced switches before staking anything.

## 8. The 1.132 Confession

**Arena:** financial-market
**Domain:** Biotech / pharma binary catalysts

- **Data source:** Substantive documents inside patent prosecution file wrappers of clinical-stage biotechs — specifically Office Action responses and 37 CFR §1.132 inventor/expert declarations filed to overcome §103 obviousness rejections on method-of-treatment and dosing-regimen claims. To argue "unexpected results," biotech applicants routinely put actual, previously undisclosed human clinical data (response rates, lesion closure, biomarker deltas, dose-response tables, safety observations) into these filings — and the claim amendments themselves reveal which dose, schedule, and patient subpopulation the sponsor is actually betting the Phase 3 on. The parallel EPO channel is richer post-G 2/21 (March 2023), which expressly blessed post-published data to establish inventive step, increasing the rate at which pharma applicants dump clinical evidence into EP examination and opposition files. Proof this is real and live: on 21 July 2026 Krystal Biotech (KRYS) filed a USPTO Office Action response for KB801 (gene therapy, neurotrophic keratitis) containing single-patient complete corneal epithelial defect closure data from a discontinued study it had never disclosed — and filed an 8-K the same day explicitly because the data "becomes publicly available through the patent prosecution record." Krystal self-reported. Almost nobody else does: there is no securities-law duty to 8-K what you put in a patent file, so the vast majority of this material sits in the public record unannounced.
- **How to collect it:** Three free, verified pipes. (1) USPTO Open Data Portal (data.uspto.gov), Patent File Wrapper APIs — free API key from a USPTO.gov account (note: since 18 Jun 2026 sign-in is required, and from 18 Aug 2026 four extra profile fields). Use the Search endpoint (data.uspto.gov/apis/patent-file-wrapper/search) to resolve an assignee/applicant watchlist to application numbers, the Assignments endpoint to catch reassignments and undisclosed in-licensing, then poll GET https://api.uspto.gov/api/v1/patent/applications/{applicationNumberText}/documents daily — it returns document metadata plus a download URI per document, refreshed daily. (2) EPO European Patent Register — free online file inspection of every publicly accessible document in a published EP file (examination reports, applicant replies, experimental declarations, and the very rich 9-month opposition submissions), plus the free Register Alert service which monitors up to 5,000 files and emails you on every register event. Bulk/biblio via the OPS API (python-epo-ops-client). (3) Global Dossier for the JPO/CNIPA/KIPO sides of the same families. The processing step is the actual work: USPTO IFW documents are encoded as page images with no native text layer, so every PDF must go through OCR/a vision model before extraction. Universe to monitor: ~400-700 US-listed clinical-stage biotechs, roughly 3,000-6,000 live published applications, cross-joined against a catalyst calendar built from ClinicalTrials.gov and company guidance.
- **The bet / trade:** Single-name US-listed clinical-stage biotech equity and options, directional. Two sub-trades. (A) Event trade: when a wrapper document lands containing materially new human data, take the stock (or 1-3 month calls/puts) immediately and hold 1-10 trading days for the drift as news scrapers, an 8-K, or message-board discovery catches up. (B) The real trade — pre-catalyst positioning: use the wrapper content to update your probability on the name's next binary (Phase 2b/3 topline, PDUFA, AdComm) and express it in options expiring just after the catalyst — long calls or call spreads when the declaration shows genuine unexpected efficacy or the applicant keeps broad ITT-scope claims; long puts or put spreads when the amendments quietly narrow to a rescue subgroup, a single dose arm, or a biomarker-defined slice that the disclosed Phase 3 design does not match. Horizon: 1-9 months, held through the readout. Defined-risk structures mandatory — these names gap 60-80%.
- **Mechanism:** A patent examiner rejects a method-of-treatment or dosing claim as obvious. The applicant's only winning move is evidence of unexpected results, and for a clinical-stage asset the most persuasive evidence is human data — so the company's own patent counsel, working to a completely different objective function than the IR department, files the trial data into a public government record. Two independent signals fall out. First, direct: the numbers themselves are the answer to the binary, months before the topline. Second, and more reliable, structural: claim scope is a revealed preference. Patent counsel writes claims around what the company believes it can actually get approved and sell. When a sponsor amends from "treating [indication]" to "treating [indication] in patients with [biomarker] at [specific dose] on [specific schedule]," they are telling you they saw the effect only in that slice — which is a bearish read on any Phase 3 powered on the broad ITT population, and a bullish read if the disclosed pivotal design already matches the narrowed claim. The causal chain is tight because the same internal dataset drives both the patent strategy and the clinical outcome, and the patent strategy becomes public first.
- **Who's on the other side:** Retail and generalist longs, biotech-specialist funds, and options market makers pricing the catalyst off consensus. Every one of them reads press releases, ClinicalTrials.gov, SEC filings, and conference abstracts; none of them reads Office Action responses. The people who do read them — IP counsel and patent prosecution firms — work for BD and legal, are behind information walls, and have no mandate to talk to a trading desk. Sell-side biotech analysts have no patent-prosecution workflow at all. The existing patent-and-biotech investing literature is entirely about a different thing: patent expiry cliffs, Orange Book listings, and Kyle-Bass-style IPR short campaigns — i.e. patent *validity and duration*, never the substantive experimental content of the prosecution file. And the mechanical barrier is real: IFW documents are unsearchable page images, so Google, Espacenet full-text, PatentsView, and every free full-text patent search miss them entirely. Nobody stumbles onto this.
- **Information lead:** Against the market's awareness of the document: applicant-filed submissions post to the public USPTO file wrapper within roughly 1-5 business days of e-filing, and daily ODP polling gets you them the day they post. For the rare case a company self-reports (Krystal), your lead is hours to days. For the large majority that never announce, the market never learns from the document at all — your lead is until the underlying binary resolves. Against the market's awareness of the *information*: this is the big one — the same data typically surfaces publicly only at the next conference presentation or topline release, 3-24 months later. The 18-month pre-grant publication of the specification itself is a separate mechanical clock that puts Phase 1/2 working-example data in the public record long before the sponsor presents it. On the EPO side, Register Alert emails you the register event and the underlying document hits free file inspection within days to weeks; opposition submissions containing clinical evidence publish months ahead of the oral proceedings.
- **Why still under-exploited:** Four compounding reasons. (1) The moat is an encoding artifact: USPTO file wrapper documents are page images with no text layer, so they are invisible to every free full-text search on earth — you cannot find this by searching, only by systematically pulling and OCRing. (2) The adjacent commercial product is aimed at the wrong customer: LexisNexis PatentAdvisor sells OCR'd searchable file wrappers, but to patent attorneys hunting winning arguments, organized by examiner and art unit — not to investors, not joined to assignee watchlists, ticker mappings, NCT numbers, or catalyst calendars. Cortellis, PatSnap, Derwent and DrugPatentWatch index bibliographic and legal-status data, not the substantive experimental content of prosecution filings. (3) It requires a rare skill stack — patent prosecution literacy plus clinical trial statistics plus event-driven equity/options trading — that essentially never coexists in one seat. (4) The EPO leg is brand new: G 2/21 only landed in March 2023 and materially increased the incentive to file post-published clinical data into EP examination, so the richest vein has been open for about three years and there is almost no history of anyone mining it for financial signal.
- **Rough +EV:** Setup: 4-8 weeks for one engineer plus one patent-literate analyst; ~$1-3k/mo running cost (OCR/VLM inference dominates; the data itself is free). Firing rate, estimated top-down: ~3,000-6,000 monitored published applications, roughly 1-2 substantive applicant responses each per year, so ~5,000 wrapper events/yr; call 3-8% of those contain new human data or a materially informative claim narrowing (~150-400/yr); of those maybe 15-25% are tradeable — right market cap, adequate options liquidity, a catalyst inside 9 months — leaving ~30-80 positions/yr. Edge per position: the smaller Bucket A (perhaps 5-15/yr of genuine undisclosed clinical observations in sub-$2B names) is worth a 4-10% expected capture with a fat right tail; the larger Bucket B (claim-narrowing/dose-selection tells) is worth roughly a 5-15 percentage-point probability shift on a binary that moves ±60%, i.e. ~4-8% expected on the position. Blended: ~3-6% gross expected edge per position, extremely fat-tailed, with brutal per-trade variance. At 5-10 concurrent positions and $10-25M cumulative annual notional, that is plausibly a mid-single-digit to low-teens percent annual return contribution on deployed capital — real money for a two-person team, not a money printer, and only survivable with defined-risk options because individual names gap 70%. One clean bonus: this is government-published public record, not expert-network channel checks, so there is no MNPI exposure.
- **Confidence:** Medium. The source, the access, and the mechanism are all verified — the ODP Documents API, the EPO Register's free file inspection and 5,000-file Register Alert, the image-PDF searchability gap, and a July 2026 real-world instance (Krystal/KB801) where undisclosed clinical data entered the world through a prosecution filing. Biggest uncertainty by far is the base rate: I have estimated top-down that 3-8% of biotech prosecution filings carry materially new, correctly interpretable clinical content, and I have not measured it. If the true rate is a quarter of that, the strategy degrades from a standalone book to a occasional-idea generator that cannot cover its own analyst. The mandatory first step is a backtest — pull every wrapper document for 100 clinical-stage names over 2021-2026, OCR them, and hand-count how many contained data or claim moves that were genuinely predictive of the subsequent catalyst. Secondary risks: interpretive false positives (claims often narrow for pure prior-art reasons with no efficacy signal at all), invisibility of pre-18-month and non-publication-request applications, and the fact that good early data still fails in Phase 3 far more often than the numbers on the page suggest.

## 9. The Advice-Letter Calendar

**Arena:** financial-market (US inflation: CPI-linked instruments + CFTC-regulated CPI event contracts)
**Domain:** Macro & rates surprises

- **Data source:** State public-utility-commission tariff filings — specifically the forward-dated, per-kWh/per-therm rate changes buried in (a) monthly/quarterly fuel-adjustment and Purchased Gas Adjustment riders (FAC/PGA/GCR/ECR), (b) "advice letter" / compliance-tariff filings implementing rate-case decisions, and (c) municipal water/sewer/trash rate ordinances. These are the administratively-set prices behind ~4% of the CPI basket (electricity ~2.4%, utility piped gas ~0.7%, water/sewer/trash ~1.1%), and BLS prices them by applying the published tariff — including "additional charges and credits, such as purchased fuel adjustments" and taxes — to a fixed DOE-derived consumption quantity. The price change is therefore not a market outcome to be forecast; it is a published number with a future effective date sitting in a docket weeks to months early.
- **How to collect it:** Roughly 50 heterogeneous but individually scrapable systems. Concrete entry points verified: CPUC "Rate Change Advisories" page and Energy Division Advice Letter site (cpuc.ca.gov/industries-and-topics/electrical-energy/energy-utility-advice-letter-and-tariff-information), with utility-side mirrors at predictable URLs (pge.com/tariffs/assets/pdf/adviceletter/ELEC_7307-E.pdf pattern; sce.com/regulatory/regulatory-information/advice-letters); PA PUC document server (puc.pa.gov/pcdocs/{id}.pdf — e.g. National Fuel Gas 2025 PGA at /1900985.pdf); NY DPS DMM (dmm.dps.ny.gov); Texas PUC Interchange (interchange.puc.texas.gov); Illinois ICC e-Docket; Colorado PUC e-Filings; CT PURA's standing monthly-PGA docket; Vermont/Maine PUC tariff-filing pages. Utility rider sheets are directly downloadable PDFs with the new per-therm/per-kWh number and effective date on the face (e.g. ameren.com/-/media/rates/files/illinois/aigs23rdpga.ashx; PGW tariff supplements). Supporting free layers: NREL/OpenEI Utility Rate Database API (developer.nrel.gov utility_rates/v3, ~3,700 utilities) for base tariff structures and the fixed-bill math; EIA-861M for monthly state-level average revenue per kWh (~2-month lag) to calibrate/backtest; EIA-861 residential customer counts to weight utilities into a national index; BLS Average Price series (electricity per kWh APU000072610, utility gas per therm APU000072620) plus the 23 published CPI metro indexes as the backtest target; CAP/NRDC "Electric and Natural Gas Utility Rate Hikes Tracker" as a free coverage cross-check. Pipeline: scrape → parse tariff sheet to a $/kWh and $/therm delta → apply BLS's fixed consumption quantity → weight by customer counts mapped to CPI metro-area relative importances → produce a forward NSA MoM forecast for the electricity, piped-gas, and water/sewer/trash indexes 1-3 months out.
- **The bet / trade:** Primary, small-team-executable: buy/sell Kalshi headline CPI contracts (KXCPI month-over-month strikes and KXCPIYOY) 2-6 weeks before the release, sized on the gap between your bottom-up utility forecast and the strike ladder's implied mean; hold to settlement on the BLS print. Institutional expression of the identical view: pay/receive the 1-month CPI fixing swap for the target reference month, or trade the front-end TIPS breakeven (buy/sell the nearest-maturing TIPS against a matched-maturity nominal), horizon 1-3 months, held through the reference months — both settle on headline NSA CPI, which is exactly what a tariff calendar predicts. Tactical overlay for the 8:30am print: 2-year note futures (ZT) or front SR3, entered the prior session and flattened within the hour, but only in months where your electricity+gas deviation exceeds ~2bp of headline.
- **Mechanism:** BLS does not observe a market price for electricity, piped gas, or water — it collects a bill. Per the BLS household-energy methodology, field staff price a fixed consumption quantity ("based on consumption averages as provided by the Department of Energy") against the utility's approved rate schedule, capturing service charges, consumption charges, "additional charges and credits, such as purchased fuel adjustments," and taxes. So once a PUC approves a rider or a compliance tariff with an effective date, the CPI print for that utility's metro area is arithmetically determined. Meanwhile the entire forecasting apparatus points the wrong way: the Cleveland Fed nowcast — and the street models built on the same logic — nowcast CPI energy from daily oil and weekly gasoline prices. That works beautifully for motor fuel (48% of energy CPI, near-perfectly tracked by RBOB) and fails structurally for electricity (38%) and piped gas (12%), because retail rates are set by regulatory lag, deferred-account true-ups, and hedged gas portfolios, and therefore step discretely on approval dates rather than tracking Henry Hub. The result is that the residual, unexplained variance in headline energy CPI is concentrated almost entirely in the two components that are knowable in advance. This is not theoretical: electricity CPI fell 1.0% MoM in June 2026 — the largest monthly decline since 2019 and a real disinflationary surprise — while running +4.0% YoY into that month. A 1% MoM move in electricity alone is ~2.5bp of headline; add piped gas and water/sewer/trash and a single month's regulated-price calendar can swing 3-5bp of headline NSA CPI, against a typical unrounded consensus error of roughly 6-8bp.
- **Who's on the other side:** Three groups, each slow for a different structural reason. (1) Kalshi CPI-market participants and macro punters, who anchor to the published Bloomberg/Reuters consensus and to prediction-market aggregates — the Fed's own 2026 FEDS paper "Kalshi and the Rise of Macro Markets" documents these markets forming around headline consensus, not component decomposition. (2) Sell-side CPI forecasters, who run three-to-five-person teams across the whole basket and model electricity as a seasonal factor plus a natural-gas regression; nobody is reading 50 dockets, and their revision cycle only tightens in the week before the print. (3) The people who actually possess this data and don't care about CPI: S&P Global's Regulatory Research Associates and RegulatorIndex sell PUC rate-case intelligence to utility *equity* analysts modeling ROE and rate base; Genability and the NREL URDB serve solar/EV payback calculators. The tariff data is already priced and consumed — by a completely different desk, for a completely different question. Nobody in that supply chain converts it to a CPI component nowcast. Confirming this: the visible CPI-nowcasting cottage industry (e.g. the CPI Edge writeups on how hedge funds replicate BLS) attacks OER/rent, new and used vehicles, and eggs — the market-priced, crowded components — and treats household energy as residual seasonal noise.
- **Information lead:** Layered. Monthly/quarterly PGA and fuel-adjustment riders are filed 2-4 weeks before their effective date, and the effective date precedes the BLS collection month by up to 5 weeks, which itself precedes the CPI release by ~2 weeks — so 4-10 weeks of lead on the print, every month, recurring. Base rate-case decisions and compliance tariffs run far longer: the PA PUC's June 2026 PPL decision specified rates for service rendered on and after July 1, 2026, and a Colorado decision expected Q3 2026 carries August 2026 implementation — 3-12 months of visibility. Against the marginal price-setter the lead is effectively the full window, because consensus does not incorporate any of it until the print lands; the closest public confirmation, EIA-861M average revenue per kWh, arrives ~2 months *after* the fact, and the CAP/NRDC tracker updates quarterly in dollar-revenue terms with no per-kWh or CPI mapping. Practically: you know the January 1 and July 1 rate-reset cliff sizes in November and May respectively, while the market learns them at 8:30am on release day.
- **Why still under-exploited:** The data is public, free, and horrible to collect: ~50 incompatible e-filing systems, scanned and semi-structured PDF tariff sheets, per-utility rider arithmetic, block-rate and seasonal-schedule switches, and a manual mapping from utility service territory to CPI metro area. There is no vendor selling "PUC tariff → CPI component nowcast" because the two natural buyer pools are disjoint — tariff data vendors serve utility equity and distributed-energy customers, while the CPI-nowcast community concentrates on shelter, autos, and food where the data is clean and the components are core-relevant. Electricity has also only recently become macro-relevant: AI data-center load growth, aging-grid capex, and the resulting wave of rate cases (77 large-load tariffs proposed or in place across 36 states, 29 approved in 2025) have turned a sleepy 2.4% weight into a source of genuine monthly surprises, and the analytical tooling has not caught up with the payoff. The one deliberate blind spot in existing work is telling: CPI-nowcasting writeups explicitly note that electricity must be *excluded* from OER imputation — it is treated as a nuisance to be netted out, not as a forecastable object.
- **Rough +EV:** Fires 12x/year but only pays in the ~4-6 months where the regulated-price calendar deviates materially from a seasonal-naive forecast — concentrated around the Jan 1, Jun 1, Jul 1, and Oct 1 reset cliffs and around large PGA true-ups. In those months, expect to reduce headline NSA MoM forecast error by ~1.5-3bp versus consensus, capturing perhaps 20-35% of typical surprise variance (consensus unrounded error runs ~6-8bp). On Kalshi MoM strikes, a 2bp shift in your posterior mean against a strike sitting near the market's mean is worth roughly 8-15 cents of fair value on a $1 contract, so ~10-20% expected return per qualifying trade before fees and slippage; across 5 good months that is plausibly 15-40% annually on deployed capital, but Kalshi CPI depth caps realistic size at roughly $250k-$2m notional. The TIPS-breakeven/CPI-fixing expression has far more capacity but a much thinner per-unit edge — call it 0.3-0.6 Sharpe standalone, worth running as an overlay rather than a standalone book. Build cost is real: ~2-3 months of engineering for a 20-utility MVP covering maybe 45% of national residential customers, which is enough to get most of the signal.
- **Confidence:** Medium. The mechanism is close to deterministic and the lead is unambiguous, which is rare. The single biggest uncertainty is that BLS's utility sample and its area weighting are confidential — you are approximating which utilities BLS actually prices with a customer-count-weighted proxy, and in any single month a sample that overweights a few metros can diverge sharply from your national aggregate. Backtest hard against the BLS Average Price series (per kWh, per therm) and the 23 published metro CPIs before sizing. Secondary risks: BLS seasonal factors can absorb part of a correctly forecast NSA move on the SA prints (which argues for the NSA-settling expressions — Kalshi, CPI fixings, breakevens — over rates futures), within-month collection timing means a mid-month effective date only partially lands, and the venue where the edge is cleanest is also the venue with the least capacity.

## 10. Railcam Livery Census (The JBHU Count)

**Arena:** financial-market
**Domain:** Shipping, aviation & logistics telemetry

- **Data source:** Free 24/7 hobbyist "railfan" livestreams pointed at freight-rail chokepoints, used not for train counts but for per-container-owner attribution. Every North American domestic intermodal box is a uniquely liveried, company-branded object: J.B. Hunt (JBHU, 120,000+ company-owned 53' boxes, white/gold with the scroll logo), Hub Group (white/blue "HUB"), EMP (green), Schneider (orange), UMAX/UMXU (Union Pacific silver), Amazon (dark blue), plus international ISO 40'/45' boxes in carrier colors (Maersk light blue, MSC yellow, ONE magenta, Hapag orange, COSCO, Evergreen). A side-on camera at a single-track chokepoint sees a near-census of the traffic on that route. The extractable series: daily counts by brand, domestic-53' vs international-ISO mix, and well-car double-stack fill rate.
- **How to collect it:** Verified live and free: Virtual Railfan's YouTube channel (youtube.com/@VirtualRailfan/streams) runs permanent 24/7 streams at Rochelle, IL (BNSF Transcon crossing the UP Geneva Sub at grade, ~80-100 trains/day, both transcon intermodal routes from LA/Long Beach pass here), La Plata, MO (pure BNSF Transcon, youtube.com/watch?v=sPhHwTDg_XA), and Fort Madison, IA (BNSF Transcon over the Mississippi). Eastern-network coverage from the same network plus Trains.com railcams (trains.com webcams) at Deshler OH, Berea OH, Folkston GA, Ashland VA, Cresson/Horseshoe Curve PA on CSX/NS. The city of Rochelle also hosts an independent municipal cam (rochellerailroadpark.org/webcam.php). Pull: `yt-dlp -g <live URL>` returns the HLS manifest; pipe to ffmpeg and record continuously, or sample 8-15 fps and discard frames. ~$60/mo of cloud storage and one consumer GPU. Pipeline: motion-gated frame extraction when a train occupies the counting line → per-container crop → a ~25-class livery classifier (color histogram + small CNN; brand/livery classification is far easier than reporting-mark OCR at 50 mph and is sufficient) → well-slot occupancy detector for the double-stack fill rate. Discard non-canonical views automatically (PTZ operators pan/zoom) and drop low-light hours. Cross-check the total against AAR's weekly aggregate intermodal units to calibrate the sampling fraction.
- **The bet / trade:** Primary: directional equity/short-dated call-or-put position in J.B. Hunt (JBHT) taken 3-10 trading days before its quarterly print (reports ~2 weeks after quarter end: mid-Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct, after the close), sized on the sign and magnitude of the camera-implied intermodal load-growth surprise vs. consensus. Hold through the print and exit 1-3 days after. Secondary, continuous: long/short JBHT vs. Hub Group (HUBG) as a market-neutral pair, rebalanced monthly on relative brand-share drift in the box census. Tertiary: the loaded-slot / international-ISO index on the BNSF Transcon as a 2-6 week lead on Port of LA/Long Beach monthly TEU releases and on rail intermodal volume commentary (UNP, CSX, NSC).
- **Mechanism:** JBHT's Intermodal segment is the dominant driver of its revenue and EPS, and the segment reduces to one number the market fixates on: loads. A "load" is physically one branded box on one well. Because JBHT's transcontinental service runs on its BNSF alliance, and the BNSF Transcon funnels through a handful of two-track chokepoints in Missouri and Iowa, essentially every transcon JBHT load rolls past a fixed public camera within days of being lifted. Counting JBHU-liveried boxes over 90 days therefore reconstructs, in real time, the same quantity the company will disclose two weeks after the quarter closes. The same frames simultaneously yield the domestic-vs-international mix (transpacific import penetration inland) and the double-stack fill rate (true well-car utilization, which is what determines rail intermodal pricing power).
- **Who's on the other side:** Sell-side transport analysts and quant funds modeling JBHT loads from (a) AAR's weekly rail traffic report, which publishes only aggregate intermodal units with no company split, ever; (b) management's prior-quarter guidance; and (c) soft channel checks. They are structurally blind at the company level because the only parties holding box-level attribution — BNSF, Union Pacific, and Railinc/Umler — are contractually barred from selling shipper-identified movement data. Critically, the one altdata vendor in wayside rail (RailState, 21 employees, $4M raised, 304 sensors, already selling train counts and car types to commodity traders and hedge funds) explicitly excludes customer attribution: their own material states there is "no capture of sensitive waybill and other movement information such as rates and weights by customer." They avoid it to preserve railroad goodwill. A public webcam operator has no such constraint, and neither does a viewer. Evidence the counterparty is genuinely slow: JBHT's Q2 2026 print beat consensus revenue by 9.5% and EPS by 11.7% on a 10% intermodal load increase — a surprise that large, on a quantity physically visible rolling past a free public camera for 90 days, is a pure information failure.
- **Information lead:** Against the number that moves the stock: up to ~15 weeks at the start of a quarter, ~7-8 weeks on average, since loads accumulate observably from day one and JBHT discloses them ~14 days after quarter end. Against AAR's weekly report: 4-11 days on volume direction, and infinite on the company split, which AAR never publishes. Against Port of LA/Long Beach monthly TEU: 2-6 weeks on the inland import read. Against RailState's commercial feed: no lead on train counts, but a category they have chosen not to sell at all on brand attribution.
- **Why still under-exploited:** Three compounding reasons. (1) The feed is hobbyist entertainment video on YouTube, not a data product — there is no vendor, no sales deck, no Bloomberg ticker, and nobody in finance mentally files "railfan livestream" under market data. (2) The work is unglamorous, uncontrolled outdoor computer vision: night, snow, glare, occlusion by a train on the near track, and PTZ operators who pan the camera mid-consist. High engineering pain, zero prestige, so it stays undone — exactly the profile of jockey weights before anyone tabulated them. (3) The parties who could productize it won't: railroads and Railinc are contractually prohibited, and the one independent wayside-sensor company deliberately carves customer identity out of its product to keep railroads cooperating. That leaves a permanent, structurally protected gap that only an outsider with no relationships to protect will fill.
- **Rough +EV:** JBHT's earnings-day absolute move averages roughly 5-12%; consensus error on intermodal load growth is routinely 2-4 percentage points. If the census predicts the sign of the volume surprise ~65% of the time versus a 50% coin flip, expected gross per event on notional is about (0.65 − 0.35) × 7% ≈ 2.0-2.1%, before slippage and option premium decay. Four JBHT events per year, plus HUBG and the rail intermodal prints, plus the continuous JBHT/HUBG pair overlay, gives roughly 12-20 firings annually. On a book where each event is sized at 3-5% of capital, that is a realistic 3-6 percentage points of annualized gross return from this signal alone, on a very small capacity footprint (which is fine — it is uncrowded precisely because it does not scale to a $10B fund). Setup cost is one engineer-quarter and under $1,500/year of compute and storage.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Biggest uncertainty by far: sampling-fraction drift. Fixed cameras on the BNSF Transcon see JBHT's transcontinental network but not its Eastern network, and those diverge violently — in Q2 2026 transcon loads grew 5% while Eastern grew 16%, so a Transcon-only census would have understated total growth by roughly half and produced a losing trade on a quarter the thesis "got right." The signal is only usable if you instrument eastern chokepoints on NS and CSX simultaneously and fit the mix weights against several quarters of reported segment splits before risking capital. Secondary risks: train length and stack density change independently of load count (mitigated by counting boxes, not trains); 30-40% of hours are lost to darkness and weather, requiring a seasonally-adjusted daylight normalization; and PTZ camera repositioning silently corrupts the counting line unless you auto-detect frame stability and discard. Budget two to three quarters of paper-trading to establish the camera-to-reported-loads regression before sizing anything.

## 11. The Salvage Tape

**Arena:** financial-market — US single-name retail equities and equity options (earnings-cycle direction + cross-sectional quarterly basket)
**Domain:** Consumer & retail demand nowcasts

- **Data source:** Live lot-level auction data from B-Stock's ~40+ retailer-branded liquidation storefronts (walmart.bstock.com / liquidations.walmart.com, target, homedepot, lowes, costco, bestbuy, samsclub, gamestop, amazon, macys, etc.), mirrored against Liquidity Services' Liquidation.com/AllSurplus, Direct Liquidation, BULQ and 888lots. The load-bearing fields are public on the listing pages without a bid: lot ID, seller storefront, ship-from facility, category, INVENTORY TYPE (B-Stock's taxonomy explicitly separates Overstock / Shelf Pulls / Customer Returns / Salvage), condition grade, unit count, TOTAL EXTENDED RETAIL VALUE (manifest MSRP), current bid, bid count, and close time. The critical derived series is the recovery rate = final winning bid ÷ extended manifest MSRP, computed per retailer, per inventory type, per category, per week — plus overstock supply volume and the share of lots shipping from import/regional DCs rather than returns centers.
- **How to collect it:** Poll bstock.com/all-auctions plus each retailer storefront's JSON listing endpoint every 15-30 minutes (the pages are JSON-backed; off-the-shelf Apify actors like parseforge/bstock-scraper and phmlabs/bstock-deal-finder already return 24 fields per lot — current bid, retail value, units, condition, seller facility, bid count, close time — proving the surface is scrapable, but they only cover Amazon storefronts and only live auctions). The essential trick nobody does: re-poll each lot in its final 2 minutes and again after close to capture the WINNING bid, allowing for B-Stock's "popcorn bidding" close-time extensions. Free buyer registration unlocks the full manifest spreadsheets (SKU/UPC/ASIN, per-item MSRP, extended retail, return reason codes) for category-level and SKU-level attribution. Mirror Liquidation.com/AllSurplus and Direct Liquidation on the same cadence to control for channel mix. Store everything: the source is ephemeral — listings vanish at close and NO public archive of historical closing prices exists, so the time series must be self-minted. Cost: one VPS, residential proxies, and a Postgres table. Roughly two weeks of engineering, then it compounds.
- **The bet / trade:** Primary: 3-5 weeks before a covered retailer's earnings print, when its 4-week Overstock+Shelf-Pull supply (extended-retail $, seasonally adjusted YoY) is >1.5σ above its own seasonal norm AND the recovery rate is falling AND the pre-shelf ratio (import/regional-DC-origin lots) is rising, buy ~5-10% OTM put spreads or short-delta risk reversals expiring the first Friday after the report — on names where gross margin is the swing factor and options are liquid: TGT, BBY, KSS, M, DKS, W, BYON, LOW, WSM, FL. Close the day after the print. Symmetric long side: when the channel is starving (supply below seasonal norm, recovery rates firming), buy call spreads / sell earnings vol. Secondary, lower-variance: a quarterly-rebalanced cross-sectional basket across ~25 covered retailers — long the quintile with the tightest liquidation channel, short the glut quintile, held one quarter into the print. Tertiary: long Liquidity Services (LQDT) equity when AGGREGATE industry lot supply surges, since total secondary-market supply is a direct read on their GMV, 4-10 weeks before their own quarterly report.
- **Mechanism:** Retail earnings surprises are driven far more by gross margin than by revenue, and gross margin is driven by markdowns on merchandise that failed. The liquidation channel is the terminal disposal path for exactly that merchandise, and it is a physical, contractual process that leaves a public price trail. Causal chain: (1) buy plan overshoots demand or the season breaks wrong → (2) goods pile up in DCs and stores → (3) the retailer's asset-recovery team routes excess to B-Stock as Overstock and Shelf-Pull lots, at which point the markdown is already economically taken → (4) supply of manifested lots for that retailer spikes and recovery rate (cents per MSRP dollar) collapses because the secondary market is a genuine supply-demand clearing venue with a fairly inelastic reseller bid → (5) the write-down and markdown cadence lands in reported gross margin one to two quarters later. The strongest variant is the pre-shelf signal: in the 2022 glut, liquidators were picking up pallets "at the ports or from a warehouse without the goods ever hitting store shelves." That inventory is invisible to every transaction-based dataset in existence — it never touched a register, a card, a store door counter, or an app. The only place it becomes visible is the auction block, weeks or months before the balance sheet.
- **Who's on the other side:** Sell-side analysts building gross-margin estimates off management guidance and last quarter's inventory-to-sales ratio — a lagging, quarterly, self-reported number. Alt-data funds running credit-card panels, receipt panels, and foot-traffic geolocation: these are excellent revenue nowcasts and are structurally blind to margin, because a card panel records a sale at whatever price cleared and cannot distinguish a full-price sale from a 60%-off clearance sale, and it records nothing at all for goods diverted from a port straight to a pallet auction. Options market makers pricing earnings straddles off historical realized moves, who have no directional view. And the closeout ecosystem itself — B-Stock, Liquidity Services, Ollie's, Big Lots buyers, and thousands of resellers — who see this data daily, understand it perfectly, and have zero interest in or infrastructure for expressing it as a securities position. They are optimizing for a $400 pallet, not a $4bn market cap.
- **Information lead:** Versus the 10-Q / earnings release: 4-14 weeks. A merchandising failure hits the auction block within roughly 2-8 weeks of the decision to dump, while the resulting margin damage is disclosed 3-5 weeks after quarter-end. Versus the mainstream alt-data complex: effectively infinite on the pre-shelf portion, because port- and DC-diverted goods generate no consumer transaction at any point and therefore never enter a card, receipt, or traffic panel. Versus the sell-side: the first public acknowledgment usually arrives as trade-press stories about "liquidators being flooded," which historically ran one to two quarters AFTER the auction volumes had already turned. Intra-signal, closing prices are known in real time on a 15-minute poll while no vendor publishes them at all.
- **Why still under-exploited:** Three compounding reasons. First, the data is public but NON-PERSISTENT: auctions close, listings disappear, and there is no archive of historical winning bids anywhere — the existing scrapers (Apify, BStalker) explicitly return only live auctions. You cannot buy this history; you can only have started collecting it. That makes it structurally unavailable to a fund doing a vendor search and gives a small team a real head start that money cannot close. Second, it is fragmented and unglamorous: 40+ separate storefronts on one platform plus five or six competing platforms, inconsistent taxonomies, manifests as spreadsheet attachments, ship-from facilities as free-text strings. It is grubby normalization work with no vendor-provided ticker mapping. Third, the entire existing tooling ecosystem points the wrong way — every scraper, alert service and Discord bot around B-Stock is built to help a reseller find one underpriced pallet before it closes, and nobody aggregates the same records upward into a per-retailer supply-and-recovery index. The big alt-data vendors (Yipit, M Science, Consumer Edge, Placer, Similarweb) all sell demand and revenue proxies; none of them sell a margin proxy, because margin has no consumer-facing transaction to instrument. The salvage channel is the one place where failed inventory is forced to reveal a market-clearing price in public.
- **Rough +EV:** Universe of ~25 US retailers with meaningful liquidation-channel presence = ~100 retailer-quarters per year. Expect the composite signal (supply >1.5σ + falling recovery rate + rising pre-shelf ratio) to fire on ~15-25 of them, so roughly 20 trades/year, of which maybe 3-6 are high conviction. Assume it calls the direction of the gross-margin surprise correctly ~60-68% of the time against a ~50% base rate; one-day absolute earnings moves for these names run 6-9% on average with a fat tail (TGT dropped ~25% in a single session in May 2022 on precisely this failure mode). Sizing ~1% of strategy capital in premium per fire into put spreads with ~3-5x max payoff gives roughly +20-35% expected return on premium risked per fire, or approximately +4-8% annualized on strategy capital — heavily right-skewed, with most of the P&L concentrated in two or three prints a year and long dry stretches in between. The cross-sectional quarterly basket is the lower-variance expression: expect something in the neighborhood of 3-6% annualized spread with far higher trade count and lower per-trade edge. Realistically this is a satellite sleeve, not a core strategy — but the marginal cost of running it is one VPS and it is orthogonal to every revenue-based signal in a book.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Existence and collectability are verified and cheap; the mechanism is sound and has a clean historical anchor in 2022. The single biggest uncertainty is DISPOSAL-POLICY CONFOUNDING: a surge in a retailer's B-Stock lot supply may reflect a contract change (moving volume from Liquidity Services to B-Stock, opening a new returns DC, insourcing or outsourcing asset recovery) rather than any deterioration in demand — and retailers deliberately throttle liquidation timing to manage margin optics, which can both delay and smear the signal. Mitigations: track total supply across all major platforms simultaneously so channel migration nets out; lean primarily on RECOVERY RATE (a price, which is far less policy-sensitive than a quantity); use the Customer Returns series as a within-retailer control for pure operational-throughput changes; and cross-check against closeout retailers' commentary about deal flow. Secondary uncertainty: with no historical archive, the seasonal normalization is untrustworthy for the first 6-8 quarters of collection, so the strategy has a real cold-start cost before it can be traded with size.

## 12. The Beaching Ratio

**Arena:** gambling — motorsport race props (F1 primarily; MotoGP/WEC/F2 secondary)
**Domain:** Golf, motorsport & individual-sport betting

- **Data source:** Corner-level run-off surface conversions (asphalt → gravel) at racing circuits, joined to historical per-corner off-track excursion traffic. Three fused feeds: (1) the FIA Race Director's Event Notes PDF issued for every F1 round, which enumerates circuit modifications corner-by-corner with metric geometry — e.g. Red Bull Ring 2024: "we have installed a 2.5m wide gravel strip directly behind the kerb at exit of Turn 9 and Turn 10 on the left-hand side and in Turns 1, 3, 4 and 6 we've moved the white line to make the distance to the gravel less than 2 metres"; Qatar: 2.0m gravel strips behind kerbs at T1-5, 12, 13; Imola 2024: gravel at Piratella, the Acque Minerali exits (T11/12/13) and Variante Alta (T14/15); Albert Park 2025: gravel extended to the back of the kerb at T6/T7. (2) FastF1's `session.race_control_messages` and `laps.DeletedReason` — every "TRACK LIMITS TURN N CAR X LAP L" message plus every SC/VSC/yellow/red with timestamps, free and complete from 2018, which gives the per-corner excursion count under the OLD asphalt regime. (3) Free satellite/aerial imagery of run-off polygons (Sentinel-2 L2A via Copernicus Browser, plus Esri Wayback / Google Earth historical) — asphalt is dark, gravel is bright, so a season-over-season albedo diff over a corner polygon measures gravel-bed area added, catching changes the Event Notes understate. Supplementary: FIA/FIM circuit licence documents published as event documents on Al Kamel noticeboards (e.g. fiawec.alkamelsystems.com/Results_NoticeBoard/), which include the dated circuit plan, and circuit operators' own winter-works announcements.
- **How to collect it:** Scrape fia.com/documents/championships/fia-formula-one-world-championship-14 each race week for the "Race Director's Event Notes" doc (v1 typically Thursday, revised versions through Saturday); the unofficial HTML mirror at tracinginsights-archive.github.io/fiahtml gives clean machine-readable text of the same numbered documents (HTML + PDF, chronologically indexed). Regex the notes for gravel/kerb/white-line/barrier language and pull turn numbers + offsets in metres. Pull historical excursion counts with `pip install fastf1`, then per session iterate `session.race_control_messages` filtering Category == 'Other' / Message contains 'TRACK LIMITS', parsing the turn number out of the message string, plus `session.laps[['DeletedReason']]`; build a per-circuit, per-corner excursion histogram for the last 3-5 visits. For imagery, define a small GeoJSON polygon per corner run-off, pull Sentinel-2 L2A B2/B3/B4 (10 m, ~5-day revisit) from the Copernicus Data Space free API and track mean reflectance across the off-season; a step change from ~0.05-0.08 to ~0.20+ is new gravel. Cross-check MotoGP venues against the FIM Circuit Safety Commission's annual Grade A inspection outputs and circuit press releases (Portimão: "some asphalt run-offs have been reduced to increase the gravel areas"). Entire pipeline is one person, a laptop, and free APIs.
- **The bet / trade:** Pre-race, placed Thursday-Saturday of a race weekend at any circuit that has gained gravel run-off (or lost asphalt run-off) since the series last visited: BACK "Safety Car — Yes" and, where offered, "Safety Car or VSC — Yes" / "2+ Safety Cars"; BACK "Number of classified finishers — Under X" or lay individual mid/back-grid drivers in "To Be Classified" markets. Secondary, higher-limit leg: back longshot each-way podium/top-6 on drivers qualifying P8-P14 (attrition compresses the field) and back "Winner not from front row." Settles on race day — a 1-4 day holding period, no carry, no correlation to anything else in a portfolio. Fires on roughly 4-8 F1 rounds a season plus MotoGP/WEC/support-series rounds at the same modified circuits.
- **Mechanism:** Asphalt run-off is forgiving in a specific, mechanical way: a car that overshoots a corner drives across tarmac and rejoins with zero consequence. Gravel converts the identical excursion into a beached car that cannot self-recover and needs a crane or tractor, which under current FIA practice almost always produces a VSC or a full Safety Car, and always produces a retirement. So the physical change does not alter driver behaviour much in its first outing — it alters the CONSEQUENCE FUNCTION applied to unchanged behaviour. That makes it computable: expected beachings ≈ (historical excursion count at that corner from race-control track-limits messages) × (fraction of excursions deep enough to reach the new bed, from the bed offset in metres in the Event Notes and its measured area from satellite) × P(beached | reaching gravel). Since 2024 the FIA has been systematically re-installing gravel across the calendar to solve the track-limits problem — the 2023 Austrian GP alone produced 1,200+ potential track-limits reports — so the excursion traffic that used to be free is now being routed into a hazard. The 2025 Imola race is the visible confirmation of the chain: after gravel went in at Piratella, Acque Minerali and Variante Alta, "mistakes in these specific areas now carry a high probability of leading to retirement from the race," and the race ran with both a VSC and a full Safety Car.
- **Who's on the other side:** Bookmakers and the recreational money on the "No" side. Books price safety-car and attrition props explicitly off circuit-level historical deployment rates plus layout, weather and reliability trends — the industry-standard recipe. That prior is computed over seasons in which the run-off at those exact corners was asphalt, which is precisely the variable that changed, so the model's key input is stale by construction. The staleness is worst — and the "No SC" price fattest — at circuits with a long, clean, low-caution history that just got gravel (Austria, Qatar, Suzuka, Albert Park), which is exactly where the mispricing is largest. Recreational bettors reinforce it by hammering "no safety car" at circuits with a reputation for processional races. Structurally, motorsport is a rounding error in a book's handle, priced by generalist traders covering many sports; nobody on a trading desk is reading a nine-page Race Director's Event Notes PDF and rebuilding a corner-level hazard model, and the quant syndicates that would ignore these markets because the limits are too small to matter — which is exactly why they stay soft.
- **Information lead:** Three nested leads. (a) Months: circuits announce resurfacing and run-off works in the off-season, and Sentinel-2 catches new gravel within ~5 days of the works finishing — often before the market for that race even opens. (b) Days, and this is the hard one: the Race Director's Event Notes drop Thursday of race week with the definitive corner-by-corner list and metric offsets, while many books post SC/attrition props Thursday through Saturday straight off the stale base rate and never revise them for the change. So 2-4 days on the authoritative document, with the priced number provably not incorporating it. (c) Hours, and this is the free natural experiment: at shared venues an earlier meeting on the same modified track gives an empirical beaching rate before the main event — WEC/ELMS/GT World Challenge/WorldSBK weeks ahead, and critically F2/F3, which race Friday and Saturday of the same F1 weekend on the identical gravel. A Saturday F2 feature race with three beachings at the new Turn 9 bed is a hard readout hours before the Sunday F1 market settles, published in full via FIA/Al Kamel results and race-control logs, and essentially nobody trades off it.
- **Why still under-exploited:** The gravel-return story is covered constantly by F1 media — but always as a track-limits and safety narrative, never once as a quantified change in the race's hazard rate. The three ingredients have never been joined: (1) per-corner excursion counts sit inside FastF1's race-control message stream, a text field almost nobody parses (the community uses FastF1 for lap times and telemetry, not for mining track-limits strings); (2) the geometry of each new gravel bed — 2.5m wide, 2.0m offset, "extends to the back of the kerb" — lives only in a Thursday PDF that reads like a procedural memo; (3) run-off albedo diffing over corner polygons is a five-line Sentinel-2 job that no motorsport analyst has any reason to think of. No vendor sells "circuit hazard delta"; there is no Kpler for gravel. The target markets are small-limit props that professional syndicates skip on capacity grounds, which is the standard reason a soft market stays soft. And the whole thing observes only circuit infrastructure and official sanctioning-body documents — no individual is surveilled, which is also why nobody treats it as an information asset.
- **Rough +EV:** SC/attrition props carry roughly 6-10% overround. Converting 3-6 high-traffic corners from asphalt to gravel plausibly moves P(at least one SC or VSC) up by 8-20 percentage points at a previously-clean circuit. Concretely: a book showing "No Safety Car" at 1.90 implies ~53% after vig; if the true no-SC probability drops to ~35-40%, backing "Safety Car — Yes" at 1.85-1.95 is a 15-25% edge on that ticket. Realistic firing rate is 15-30 qualifying bets a year across F1, MotoGP, WEC and support series. The binding constraint is not edge, it is capacity: SC props typically cap around £200-£2,000 per book per race and get limited fast, so a determined solo operator across a book network realistically nets maybe $5k-$40k a year at 15-20% ROI on turnover. The classified-finishers and each-way longshot legs take larger stakes but carry a thinner 3-8% edge. This is a high-ROI, low-capacity edge — a good personal book, not a fund.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Biggest uncertainty by far: how fast drivers adapt. The entire purpose of installing gravel is deterrence, so drivers stop running wide once it is there — meaning the effect is probably strongly front-loaded to the FIRST running after installation and decays on subsequent visits. That is a real threat to a persistent edge, but it points the strategy at exactly the right window, since the first running is also when the book's historical base rate is most stale; treat it as a one-shot-per-modification trade, not a standing position. Two secondary risks: F1 increasingly resolves a beached car with a VSC rather than a full SC (see the Ocon recovery at Imola 2025, VSC only), which loses a naive "Safety Car — Yes" ticket, so the combined "SC or VSC" market is the clean instrument and a haircut of roughly 30-40% is needed where only full-SC is offered; and account limitation on prop markets will throttle the strategy within a season or two per book.

## 13. The 0-70 Muster

**Arena:** financial-market (US coastal P&C small-cap equities and their options; secondary read-across to Florida-exposed reinsurance/ILS)
**Domain:** Insurance, catastrophe & weather-linked securities

- **Data source:** Florida "Emergency Adjuster" licenses — license type 0-70 — and their appointment records. These exist ONLY while a state of emergency is declared, and under Fla. Admin. Code R. 69B-220.001 the application is filed by the Florida authorized insurer, eligible surplus lines insurer, or licensed independent adjusting firm that will employ the adjuster, not by the adjuster. The appointing entity's name, the license issue date, and the adjuster's Florida county are therefore all public and timestamped. Every emergency adjuster a carrier licenses is one unit of that carrier's own private, pre-disclosure estimate of how many claims it is about to receive. Parallel regimes exist at Texas TDI ("emergency/catastrophe adjuster", sponsored by the insurer under Tex. Ins. Code 4101.002(b)/4101.101) and Louisiana LDI, giving Gulf-wide coverage.
- **How to collect it:** Primary: https://licenseesearch.fldfs.com/ (free, no login). The search form exposes exactly the fields needed — Appointment Category dropdown includes "Emergency Adjuster"; plus Appointing Entity, appointment status + status date range, license issue date range, license status, and Florida county. Scrape the POST endpoint behind the form on a 6-12 hour cadence from the moment the Governor's executive order declares the emergency (DFS only publishes the 0-70 application once it does), grouping by appointing entity, issue date, and county. Snapshot daily — licenses are valid 6 months and never renew, so they age into "inactive" and you want your own time series. Supplement with the free daily CSVs at https://licenseesearch.fldfs.com/BulkDownload (11 files incl. "All Active Appointments – Individual/Business", 102KB–321MB) for entity-name normalization and baseline headcount. Texas: TDI agent/adjuster lookup plus the licensee datasets on data.texas.gov (license-type field carries the emergency adjuster class). Louisiana: LDI emergency adjuster registration search. Then hand-build the crosswalk from appointing entity to ticker — UPCIC/Universal (UVE), Homeowners Choice + TypTap (HCI), Narragansett Bay/Heritage (HRTG), American Coastal/Interboro (ACIC), Slide (SLDE), Citizens as the state benchmark — and separately tag the independent-adjuster firms (Crawford CRD-A, Sedgwick, Eberl, Alacrity, Worley, CNC) whose rosters get allocated across carriers via known panel relationships. Off-the-shelf Apify actors already scrape this site (sold to recruiters and compliance teams), so build cost is days, not months.
- **The bet / trade:** Post-landfall cross-sectional pair in Florida-concentrated P&C equities, entered T+2 to T+7 days. Compute each carrier's mobilization ratio = emergency adjusters licensed by that carrier (direct + IA-firm allocation) divided by its policies-in-force in the affected counties, then z-score it against that carrier's own ratio in prior declared storms. Short (or buy 1–3 month ~10% OTM puts on) the name mobilizing far above its storm-normalized baseline and above peers; long the peer mobilizing muted. Hold through the carrier's loss-estimate 8-K and into the next quarterly report — roughly 3 to 10 weeks. Second, slower leg: at month ~5-6 the rule permits a six-month extension application; carriers that file extensions still have open claim inventory, so short those names into the following two quarters as an adverse-development / reserve-strengthening trade. Size small — these are $0.5–3B caps with thin options chains.
- **Mechanism:** A carrier's claims organization must provision surge staffing before it knows its loss, and it provisions off an internal exposure run: policies inside the forecast wind swath times an expected claim frequency. That internal number is the single most valuable unknown in the weeks after a landfall. Adjuster licensing is the one step in the provisioning chain that leaves a public, entity-attributed, timestamped record, because Florida law makes the appointing insurer file individually for each body it deploys. Chain: mobilization intensity → the carrier's own expected gross claim count → gross claim count × average severity → gross loss → whether gross loss pierces the disclosed retention, how far up the reinsurance tower it climbs, and how much reinstatement premium burns → book value and EPS hit → equity. The cross-sectional dispersion matters more than the level: the industry loss number is public within 72 hours, but WHICH carrier ate it is not, and that is precisely what the mobilization ratio discriminates.
- **Who's on the other side:** Retail and generalist funds trading the Florida P&C complex off television damage footage and the single Karen Clark / Verisk / Moody's RMS industry-loss headline, which is industry-wide and carries no carrier split. Sell-side analysts modeling from cat-model output plus the company's disclosed retention and tower, with literally zero carrier-specific claim-count information until management speaks. Options market makers who mark post-landfall implied vol roughly uniformly across the small-cap complex without a dispersion view. They are slow for structural reasons: the data sits in a state insurance-licensing compliance system that no financial data vendor indexes; it only exists during declared emergencies so there is no standing product anyone can subscribe to; and reading it requires knowing that one specific Florida rule puts the appointing insurer's name on every filing, then doing unglamorous entity-name normalization across IA firms. The alt-data conversation in insurance is dominated by satellite damage imagery and cat models — which is exactly what the other side already has.
- **Information lead:** The state-of-emergency declaration typically lands T-4 to T-3 days, and DFS opens the 0-70 application at that moment; carriers begin filing immediately, so you have carrier-attributed mobilization data continuously from before landfall. Compare to the public clock: Verisk/KCC/Moody's RMS industry estimates at T+1 to T+3 (industry only); Florida OIR's catastrophe claims data call first public numbers at roughly T+5 to T+10 (aggregate, company detail lagging); individual carrier loss estimates by 8-K at T+14 to T+45; full picture at quarterly earnings T+35 to T+70. Net carrier-attributed lead: about 1–2 weeks over any carrier-level claim data and 3–9 weeks over the carrier's own loss estimate. On the extension leg the lead is roughly two quarters over the reserve-strengthening disclosure.
- **Why still under-exploited:** This is a licensing-and-compliance database, not a market database. The scrapers that already exist for licenseesearch.fldfs.com are sold to insurance recruiters, agency-management software vendors, and compliance departments — nobody packages emergency adjuster counts for investors, and no ILS or equity-research note I could find references them. Three frictions keep it that way: it is episodic (the 0-70 license class does not exist except during a declared emergency, so there is nothing to subscribe to between storms), it requires domain knowledge of Rule 69B-220.001 to realize the appointing insurer is named at all, and the payoff depends on messy manual work — normalizing "UPCIC" against "Universal Property & Casualty", allocating IA-firm rosters across carrier panels, and reconstructing policies-in-force by county from OIR QUASR filings. It is fragmented across at least three separate state regulators with three different interfaces. That is exactly the profile of a source that stays cheap.
- **Rough +EV:** Fires roughly 1–3 times per year on the landfall leg (US hurricanes triggering a FL/TX/LA emergency declaration) plus 1–2 times per year on the extension leg. The dispersion being forecast is genuinely large: Florida small-cap P&C names routinely move 15–40% in the three weeks after a major landfall, and the spread between the best and worst performer in the complex is typically 20–30 points. If the mobilization ratio calls the direction of relative loss ~60–65% of the time, a pair sized at 10–15% gross per event returns roughly +4 to +8% on deployed capital per event, i.e. call it 8–20% annualized on dedicated capital, materially higher via puts since post-landfall IV is elevated but not dispersion-aware. Honest expectation: a real but modest edge of maybe 3–6 points of relative return per event, with most of the value concentrated in the tail cases where one carrier's mobilization diverges sharply from what the cat-model consensus implies. Capacity is small — low seven figures, not a fund-scale strategy.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Biggest uncertainty is attribution leakage through independent adjusting firms: a large share of emergency adjusters are appointed by Crawford, Sedgwick, Eberl, Alacrity and Worley, whose rosters serve many carriers at once, so the carrier-specific signal is diluted down to whatever fraction of the complex uses in-house or captive claims operations. Secondary risks: mobilization may be a defensive rather than actuarial decision (Florida DOI scrutinizes claim-handling timelines, so carriers may over-staff for regulatory optics regardless of expected loss), and heavy reinsurance means gross claim count does not map linearly to net EPS. All of this is testable before risking money — the DFS search retains issue-date and inactive-license filters, so a backtest against Ian (2022), Idalia (2023), and Helene/Milton (2024) is buildable in a week, and I would not size this until that backtest shows the ratio separated the winners from the losers in at least two of those three.

## 14. The Clearcut Calendar

**Arena:** financial-market
**Domain:** Real assets: timber, water, farmland, mining

- **Data source:** Owner-attributed forward timber harvest permits from Pacific Northwest state forestry regulators — specifically Oregon ODF's FERNS "Notification of Operations" (NOAP) unit polygons and Washington DNR's Forest Practices Application (FPA) records. Every stick of private timber in the PNW is legally pre-announced, per harvest unit, with acreage, silvicultural prescription, start date, and (in Oregon) the landowner company name, weeks to months before a log moves. This is forward permitted supply attributable to named public companies — categorically different from the transacted stumpage prices sold by Forest2Market/Forisk/RISI or the post-hoc canopy-loss detection from Global Forest Watch/Hansen.
- **How to collect it:** All verified live against the endpoints. OREGON (the clean side): https://gis.odf.oregon.gov/ags1/rest/services/Public/FERNSNotifications/MapServer/0/query — public ArcGIS REST, no auth, no key, 2000 records/page, supports SQL where-clauses and outStatistics server-side aggregation. Confirmed fields: LandOwners, OperatorName, UnitName, UnitArea (acres), ActType (Clearcut/Overstory Removal, Commercial Thinning/Selective, Salvage, Partial Cut), SubmitDate, StartDate, EndDate, ContinuationIssueDate/ContinuationExpiryDate, NoapYear, plus geometry. 257,759 records; max SubmitDate 2026-08-13 and last_edited 2026-08-14 (same-day fresh); ~1,598 new notifications per 30 days statewide. Confirmed named-owner counts: Weyerhaeuser 2,236, Roseburg 9,271, Sierra Pacific 3,109, Stimson 1,495, Manulife/Hancock 1,002. Per-notification HTML detail pages at https://ferns.odf.oregon.gov/E-Notification/noap/{id}?View=Summary and ?View=Landowners use sequential integer IDs and give landowner company, operator company, per-unit acres, and ODF district. WASHINGTON (the laborious side, and the moat): https://gis.dnr.wa.gov/site2/rest/services/Public_Forest_Practices/WADNR_PUBLIC_FP_FPA/MapServer/8/query — 67,944 records, fields FP_ID, TIMHARV_FP_TY_LABEL_NM (Even Aged/Uneven Aged), TIMHARV_RPT_AREA (acres), REGION_NM, RECEIVED_DT, EFFECTIVE_DT, EXPIRATION_DT, DECISION, PDF_LOCATION; ~1,292 new FPAs per 90 days. Landowner name is deliberately absent from the GIS layer and the public search UI at fpansearch.dnr.wa.gov/publicSearch offers no owner filter (verified) — owner appears only inside each application PDF at fpansearch.dnr.wa.gov/publicSearch/getDocument?GUID=..., so the WA panel requires fetching and parsing ~68k PDFs, or joining the search tool's tax-parcel field to county assessor ownership rolls. Extend to full PNW coverage with California CalTREES (THPs) and Idaho Dept of Lands notifications. Build: nightly cron, trivial storage; the entire cost is entity-name normalization across subsidiaries, LLCs, and TIMO managers.
- **The bet / trade:** Two expressions off one signal. PRIMARY (event): directional position in Weyerhaeuser (WY), secondarily Rayonier (RYN) and PotlatchDeltic (PCH), entered ~4-6 weeks before the quarterly print and held through it (5-8 week horizon), expressed as equity plus 1-2 month options straddling the report date. Direction set by owner-attributed notified acres for the quarter (OR+WA, clearcut-weighted, converted to MBF at west-side Douglas-fir yields of roughly 35-50 MBF/acre) measured against the company's guided annual harvest run-rate and the same window a year prior: notified acres materially below run-rate implies a harvest deferral and a Timberlands volume miss, so short/puts; materially above implies a volume beat, so long/calls. SECONDARY (structural, capacity-friendly): long timberland owner versus short lumber converter — WY or RYN against West Fraser (WFG) or Interfor (IFP) — sized off the regional aggregate Douglas-fir notified-acre index, 1-2 quarter hold. Falling regional notified acres means log scarcity, so long owner / short mill; a surge means log glut, so reverse. The spread nets out housing, rates, and lumber-price beta and isolates the log-supply term.
- **Mechanism:** Oregon's Forest Practices Act requires a landowner to notify ODF at least 15 days before beginning any operation on private forestland; Washington requires an approved FPA before harvest. The regulatory purpose is environmental review, but the byproduct is a complete, legally-compelled, forward-looking registry of intended timber harvest, unit by unit. Notified acres times regional per-acre yield equals forward log volume, which drives two distinct outcomes. First, the owner's own harvest volume: Timberlands segment EBITDA is harvest volume times realized log price, and volume is the single largest swing factor and the most common source of quarterly misses, because REITs quietly defer harvest into weak log markets and disclose it only on the earnings call. Because Oregon exposes the landowner company as a queryable GIS attribute, you are reading a specific public company's forward operating plan directly. Second, regional log supply: aggregate notified acreage in the Douglas-fir region sets delivered log cost, which is the largest input cost for PNW mills and simultaneously the revenue line for timberland owners. Those two cash flows move in opposite directions off the same variable, which is exactly why the owner-versus-mill pair isolates the signal from common housing beta. A supplementary tell sits in the ContinuationIssueDate field: a unit renewed rather than executed is a deferral made visible.
- **Who's on the other side:** Sell-side timber analysts (RBC, BofA, Truist, Raymond James) model harvest volume from company guidance and Random Lengths lumber prints; there is no forward-supply input anywhere in those models, so their volume estimate is essentially management's number echoed back. The marginal timber REIT shareholder is a REIT generalist or a dividend/income fund treating WY as a housing-and-rates proxy and not modeling the operating line at all. Mill procurement managers and log traders know their own local supply intimately but nobody aggregates across owners, districts, and state lines. Critically, the official statistic the market eventually cites — Washington DNR's Timber Harvest Report — is constructed from Department of Revenue timber excise-tax receipts and published annually, so the consensus reference series is up to a year stale by construction. The data's intended audience is conservation groups and adjacent landowners; organizations like Oregon Wild and yourforestwatershed.org already run subscriber alerts on this exact feed for comment-filing purposes. It has simply never been read by anyone with a position on.
- **Information lead:** Three stacked leads, all verified in the data. (1) Against physical operations: minimum 15 days by statute, typically 2-8 weeks in practice — confirmed in the records, where Weyerhaeuser units submitted 2025-12-18 carry a start date of 2026-01-02, exactly the statutory floor. (2) Against the company's own disclosure: roughly 4-6 weeks. You hold a complete picture of a quarter's notified footprint by quarter-end, while WY reports about three to four weeks after that, and you can call an annual-guidance revision a full quarter or more early since guidance is set in February and the notification flow through H1 reveals whether it will hold. (3) Against the published official statistic: roughly 12 months, because WA's harvest report is a tax-receipt lagging series released annually. The feed itself carries essentially zero latency — Oregon's maximum SubmitDate was 2026-08-13 against a query run on 2026-08-14.
- **Why still under-exploited:** Five separate state systems, no shared schema, and none built for finance. The decisive asymmetry: Oregon publishes landowner company as a GIS attribute while Washington does not — WA's owner name exists only inside per-application PDFs behind GUID URLs, and the public search tool offers no owner filter whatsoever (verified directly). Building a name-level panel therefore means scraping roughly 68,000 PDFs, or joining tax parcels to county assessor rolls, then normalizing filthy free-text entity strings across subsidiaries, shell LLCs, TIMO managers like Manulife/Hancock, and the recurring landowner-versus-contract-operator ambiguity. That grunt work is the entire moat and the reason no vendor packages it. The existing commercial layer is orthogonal rather than competitive: Forest2Market, Forisk, and RISI sell backward-looking transacted stumpage and log prices; Global Forest Watch and Hansen detect harvest after canopy loss with months of lag and no owner attribution at all. Nobody sells forward, owner-attributed permitted acreage — the thing that actually predicts the print.
- **Rough +EV:** The underlying variance is real and large, verified from the feed: Weyerhaeuser's Oregon notified acres ran 13,925 (2021), 4,994 (2022), 26,827 (2023), 7,874 (2024), 11,689 (2025), 7,336 (2026 YTD through mid-August) — swings of 3-5x. The control validates the data rather than the noise: Sierra Pacific, a stable private operator, filed 46,748 / 46,783 / 44,814 / 54,356 acres over 2023-2026, so coverage is complete and the volatility is genuine operator decision-making, not collection artifact (Sierra Pacific is also running +21% in 2026, itself a live supply signal). Sizing honestly: the event leg fires about 4 times a year per name across WY/RYN/PCH. WY typically moves 4-7% on an earnings day and a Timberlands volume surprise plausibly explains a third of that. At a realistic 60-63% hit rate on the sign of the volume surprise, an options-overlay expression nets roughly 1.5-3% per event on deployed capital, or about 6-12% annually on the sleeve. The owner-versus-mill spread is slower but more capacity-friendly, targeting 3-6% per 1-2 quarter turn. Total capacity is genuinely small — this is a $10-50M idea, not a $500M one.
- **Confidence:** Medium. The biggest uncertainty by far is that a notification is permission, not commitment. Owners systematically over-notify to preserve optionality, Oregon notifications carry one-year validity with continuations, and the notified-to-harvested conversion rate is itself unstable — worse, it almost certainly rises and falls with log prices, which is the very thing being predicted. That endogeneity can partially self-cancel the signal, and it is the failure mode a backtest must isolate. The continuation-rate tell is weaker than hoped: Weyerhaeuser ran 10 of 154 units continued in 2025 (6.5%) against a 1,199-of-19,681 statewide baseline (6.1%), so the base rate is low and the discriminator is thin. Secondary risks: OR+WA capture only part of Weyerhaeuser's roughly 10.4M-acre portfolio, and Southern timberlands — a large offsetting segment — have far weaker permit disclosure; and WY's Wood Products segment plus rates/housing beta can swamp a Timberlands volume surprise on any single print, which is precisely the argument for the market-neutral owner-versus-mill expression over the naked directional one. Requires a 3-4 year backtest of notified acres against reported segment harvest volumes, with the conversion rate estimated as a function of contemporaneous log prices, before sizing above a token allocation.

## 15. The Letting Tape

**Arena:** financial-market (US small/mid-cap equities: infrastructure contractors and aggregates; equity + short-dated options around quarterly prints)
**Domain:** Municipal finance & local government

- **Data source:** Public-agency construction **letting results / bid tabulations** — the "apparent low bidder" sheets and line-item unit-price tabs that state DOTs and county/city purchasing departments publish within hours-to-days of each sealed-bid opening — resolved through a hand-built **operating-subsidiary → ticker crosswalk**. The bid tab names the winner, the contract value, every losing bidder, and the unit prices ($/ton hot-mix asphalt, $/cy structural concrete, $/lb rebar). Critically, the winner is listed under its legacy local legal name ("Wiregrass Construction Company, Inc.", "The Scruggs Company", "Fred Smith Company", "King Asphalt", "Everett Dykes Grassing Co.") — never as Construction Partners (ROAD). Three derived series: (1) per-ticker public-award inflow, (2) national bidder-count / bid-dispersion index, (3) realized unit-price index.
- **How to collect it:** State DOT leg (free, no scraping obstacles): ALDOT `alletting.aldot.gov` (Bid Tabs by letting year + "Item Bid Summary" run after each letting; AGC mirrors the Low Bid Sheet at alagc.org); FDOT `bidletting.fdot.gov` — the **Preliminary Letting Results Report** is posted per district essentially the day of the letting, with full bid tabs after; GDOT via Bid Express `ui.bidx.com/GADOT/lettings` (free account); NCDOT `connect.ncdot.gov`; SCDOT and TDOT equivalents; TxDOT `txdot.gov` Bid Tabulations Dashboard (one of the few that also publishes the **engineer's estimate**); ODOT posts Excel "Average Bid Item Prices" with every bid item and bidder rank; WSDOT "Unit Bid Analysis"; Iowa DOT bid-tab PDFs. Cheap backfill/QA: `bidtabs.us` (free, ~20 states, every bidder + every unit price) and `dotestimate.com` (paid, CSV/XLSX export). Local-government leg (no aggregator exists — this is the moat): OpenGov procurement portals `procurement.opengov.com/portal/<county>`, CivicPlus `…/Bids.aspx` pages, Bonfire and Periscope tenants, plus "Notice of Intent to Award" postings and county-commission agenda packets. Crosswalk sources: 10-K **Exhibit 21** subsidiary lists, acquisition press releases, `constructionpartners.net/our-companies`, and — best of all — **state DOT contractor prequalification rosters**, which give exact legal entity names plus each firm's approved maximum bidding capacity (a working-capital proxy the company never discloses).
- **The bet / trade:** **Primary:** Construction Partners (NASDAQ: ROAD), long or short into the quarterly print. FY ends Sept 30; results land ~5–7 weeks after quarter end. Each quarter, sum reconstructed public awards to ROAD subsidiaries across AL/GA/FL/NC/SC/TN state DOTs + top ~60 counties, compare to the same 13-week window a year prior, and compare that growth rate to the backlog/revenue growth implied by consensus. Positive gap → long; negative gap → short. Enter 5–15 trading days pre-print, exit 1–2 days post-print. Express in equity, or modest OTM 1–2 month options when the implied move is below ~9%. **Secondary book:** same method on Sterling Infrastructure (STRL), Granite (GVA), Orion (ORN), Arcosa (ACA). **Third leg, longer horizon:** the national bidder-count + unit-price index as a 1–2 quarter directional tilt on aggregates pricing (VMC/MLM long-short vs. the road builders) — rising bidder counts and compressing high-vs-low bid dispersion signal contractor margin compression 2–4 quarters out.
- **Mechanism:** Public paving and highway work is awarded by **sealed competitive bid, low bidder wins mechanically** — no negotiation, no judgment. So the identity of the winner and the exact dollar value are fully determined the instant the envelopes are opened, and the agency posts them publicly within hours to days. The contractor books that dollar value into backlog only when the contract is awarded/executed, typically 2–6 weeks later, and reports backlog to the market only at the quarterly print. Backlog is the dominant driver of these stocks: ROAD's record $3.36B backlog (June 30, 2026) covers 80–85% of next-twelve-months contract revenue, and public work was ~65% of FY25 revenue — federal, state DOT, and local municipalities. Because ROAD's public footprint is concentrated in six or seven Sunbelt states, a near-complete census of those DOTs plus the large counties reconstructs most of the public backlog inflow before anyone reports it. Separately, the unit prices on the tab are **realized selling prices**, not survey estimates — a direct read on both the contractor's bid margin and the aggregate/asphalt producer's pricing power. And bidder count is a capacity-utilization meter: when contractors are full, few firms bid and prices firm; when the pipeline dries up, bid counts rise and prices soften. That leads reported margin by several quarters, because today's bid margin only reaches the P&L as the job gets built.
- **Who's on the other side:** Sell-side construction analysts model backlog **top-down** — from management guidance, state STIP program budgets, and IIJA obligation data. They do not scrape lettings, and they get blindsided: ROAD gapped +18.8% on a quarter where revenue beat by 4.2% and backlog hit a record; STRL fell 11.3% on an 11.5% EPS beat because backlog conversion timing disappointed. Quant and alt-data funds skip it for a specific, unglamorous reason: the payload lives in per-letting PDFs and per-district HTML across ~50 incompatible schemas plus thousands of county portals, and **the ticker's name never appears in the data**. Entity resolution across a serial acquirer that bids under dozens of legacy local brands is a manual crosswalk that decays with every closed deal. That maintenance tax is exactly the kind of cost systematic shops decline to pay. The vendors who *do* aggregate bid tabs — BidTabs.us, DOTestimate, HCSS HeavyBid, Dodge, ConstructConnect — sell to **contractors** as estimating and competitive-intel tools, priced and packaged for estimators, with zero ticker mapping and no local-agency coverage. And retail/momentum holders simply trade the headline backlog number on the day it prints.
- **Information lead:** Versus the earnings print: a single award is known 1 day to ~13 weeks before quarter end, and the print comes 5–7 weeks after quarter end — median lead on any given award ≈ 3 months, and the **complete quarterly census is finished 5–7 weeks before the release**. Versus the company's own backlog recognition: days to ~4 weeks (bid opening precedes award/execution). Versus the market's official price series: the FHWA National Highway Construction Cost Index is quarterly and *revises the prior two quarters* as states dribble in award data — an effective 2–3 quarter lag; building it from raw tabs gives a 2–4 week lag, a 4–8 month lead on the "official" number. Versus any press release: ROAD and its peers do not announce individual DOT awards, so there is no competing public channel at all — the bid tab is the only place the information exists until the 10-Q.
- **Why still under-exploited:** The raw data is free, abundant, and has been public for decades — but it has never been assembled *for investors*. Every aggregator that touches it points the other way, at contractors pricing their next job. The binding constraint is the subsidiary crosswalk: ROAD, STRL, Arcosa and the private roll-ups (Colas, Vinci/Eurovia, Summit) all bid under legacy local names, so a naive scrape of "Construction Partners" returns nothing, and building the mapping requires reading Exhibit 21s, acquisition PRs, and state prequalification rosters — then redoing it after each deal. The county/city leg, which is a large share of paving work and squarely municipal-finance, is scattered across thousands of OpenGov, Bonfire, and CivicPlus portals and commission agenda packets with no aggregator whatsoever. The targets are $3–20B caps that most large alt-data budgets skip, and the alt-data industry has concentrated on consumer (card, web, geolocation) and energy (satellite, AIS) rather than public procurement. Finally, record IIJA and state fuel-tax money is flowing through lettings right now, and ROAD/STRL have become momentum names where backlog surprises cause double-digit gaps — the signal is unusually valuable at exactly the moment nobody has built it.
- **Rough +EV:** Roughly 16–20 event windows/year across a ROAD/STRL/GVA/ORN basket. Recent realized earnings-day moves in these names run large: ROAD +18.8%, +15.9%, +12.2%; STRL −11.3% on a beat — call the median absolute move 9–12%. Assume the letting census calls the *direction* of the public-backlog surprise correctly ~60–63% (not higher — private work, weather, and guidance conservatism are genuinely exogenous). A 1x notional directional position into the print earns about (0.62 × 10%) − (0.38 × 10%) ≈ **+2.4% per event gross, ~1.5–2.0% net** of slippage in these wide-spread small caps. Sized at 20–25% of book per event across 16–18 events, that is roughly **6–9% annualized alpha** from the event leg. Options add leverage but implied moves into these prints are already 8–11%, so most of the convexity is priced — prefer equity or modest OTM. The unit-price / bidder-count leg fires ~3–5 times a year with a 2–4% expected move but far higher capacity. Build cost: one determined person, ~6–10 weeks to stand up 8–10 DOT scrapers plus the crosswalk, ~4–6 hrs/week to maintain, with bidtabs.us and dotestimate.com as cheap history backfill. Strategy capacity is modest — a few tens of millions before you start moving ORN and ROAD yourself.
- **Confidence:** **Medium.** Biggest uncertainty: **the census only sees the public 65%, and the narrative right now is being driven by the private 35%.** ROAD's fastest-growing and most stock-moving work is private data-center site work and commercial subdivisions, which never appears in any public letting — if that is what drives the backlog surprise, a perfectly accurate public-award reconstruction still gets the quarter wrong. Mitigations: only take the trade when the public-inflow gap versus consensus-implied growth is large enough to swamp plausible private-side variance, and use the bidder-count/unit-price legs (which are sector-wide and unaffected by mix) as an independent confirm. Second-order risks: award-to-backlog timing can straddle a quarter boundary and flip the sign of a close call; joint ventures and subcontracted scope mean the low bidder on the tab is not always who books the revenue; and the crosswalk silently rots after every tuck-in acquisition, which is the single most likely way this quietly stops working.

## 16. The 45-Day Guarantee

**Arena:** financial-market
**Domain:** Labor actions, strikes & workforce disruption

- **Data source:** Public recruiting posts from third-party strike/"crisis" contingency-staffing agencies — the firms companies hire to run a plant or hospital through a walkout. Huffmaster, U.S. Nursing, Strom Engineering, RapidStaff (TotalMed), Medical Solutions and AMN advertise, in advance, everything about an anticipated labor dispute EXCEPT the client's name: state, facility/industry type, exact job classifications, hourly rate, weekly hour guarantee, minimum commitment length, and the anticipated date of the job action. Verified live examples: "Huffmaster Crisis Response is recruiting experienced Licensed Registered Nurses for a potential labor dispute that could occur at an Acute Care Facility in Pennsylvania early to mid-January 2026"; "...experienced CNC Machinist to work during a possible labor dispute for a Manufacturing company located in Michigan, with the job action potentially occurring early June 2026"; a Feb 24 2025 post listing nine classifications (RI-licensed Pharmacist, Medical Coders, Social Workers...) for an "Acute Care Facility in Rhode Island" anticipated "end of March 2025" at $100-$200/hr, 60-hour weekly guarantee, 12-hour shifts, 45-day minimum commitment, travel and lodging provided. The redacted client is recoverable; the highest-value field is the minimum commitment length, which is the employer's own private forecast of how long it intends to fight.
- **How to collect it:** Daily poll and diff of: (1) huffmaster.com/jobs/ — a WP Job Manager archive filterable by job category, location state, and job type "Temporary Strike Assignment"; detail pages sit at huffmaster.com/jobs/<slug>-<id>/ with sequential numeric IDs (e.g. /jobs/general-labor-ri-75908/), so you can enumerate and diff. Note /jobs/feed/ and /wp-json/wp/v2/job_listing both 404 — scrape the archive HTML plus its "Load More" AJAX. Location-specific reqs (currently e.g. Altoona PA, Los Angeles CA) are the signal; "Travel Throughout US" evergreen reqs are noise. (2) facebook.com/huffmasterjobs ("Huffmaster Strikes", industrial) and facebook.com/huffmasterhealthcare — the richest text; these carry the anticipated job-action date and get retroactively prefixed "SETTLED!" when a deal lands. (3) usnursing.com/strikes — U.S. Nursing's "Strike Job Action Alerts" map. (4) rapidstaff.com — publishes a plain-English aggregate status ("We are not currently supporting any active strike engagements"). (5) Strom Engineering, which runs a passive Avionté database (hire.myavionte.com standalone careers portal; stromengineering.myavionte.com) and publishes no listings, so track it via its Indeed employer page (~15 strike reqs) and recruiter LinkedIn/Facebook posts. (6) Standing Google/Indeed alerts on the exact boilerplate: "possible labor dispute", "potential labor dispute", "Crisis Response is recruiting", "job action". De-anonymization join keys: the DOL OLMS Online Public Disclosure Room CBA file (olmsapps.dol.gov/olpdr/, full-text agreement PDFs organized by employer with expiration dates) and NLRB election/petition data for which facilities are represented and by which union — filter to employers in that state and industry with a CBA expiring in the stated month.
- **The bet / trade:** When a new location-specific contingency requisition appears, de-anonymize it and buy a 2-3 month straddle (or a put spread if the scale read is large) on the employer's equity, opened 3-8 weeks before the stated job-action date and held through contract expiry plus 3-4 weeks. Size the directional put tilt off the posting's implied contingency scale — headcount x rate x guaranteed weeks. A 45-day-plus minimum commitment across many classifications means the employer is budgeting for a siege, not a symbolic 3-day walkout: skew to puts and pair with a long in the obvious substitute (competitor taking share, or the logistics alternative). Inverse trade: when a widely-watched expiry approaches with NO contingency requisition, or an existing one flips to "SETTLED!", sell the strike-risk premium / fade the strike-panic drawdown. Tradeable universe is names where one struck site is material — Boeing, Deere, CNH, Cleveland-Cliffs, Molson Coors, TreeHouse, Kellanova, Olin, Ardent Health, IOU utilities — not diversified hospital chains where a single facility is noise.
- **Mechanism:** Contingency staffing is expensive and slow. At $100-$200/hr with 60-hour guarantees plus travel and lodging, and 4-12 weeks needed to recruit, credential, badge and pre-train replacements, an employer only commits when its own labor-relations team has privately concluded that (a) a strike is materially likely and (b) it intends to operate through it rather than concede. Strom's own aerospace case study describes 1,000+ FAA-certified mechanics pre-deployed and fleet-familiarized, audited by FAA inspectors, "months in advance of contract expiration." The requisition is thus a leak of the employer's private probability estimate and its bargaining posture, and the guaranteed-duration field is a leak of its private duration forecast. Both are the variables that determine the P&L impact: strike probability sets whether the event happens, duration sets whether it is a rounding error or a guidance cut. Long stoppages at capital-intensive single-site operations produce missed deliveries, fixed-cost deleveraging and withdrawn guidance — repricings of 8-20% that were not in the option premium because nothing in the vol surface flagged the date.
- **Who's on the other side:** Three slow counterparties. Sell-side analysts and event-driven funds know CBA expiry dates but model strike probability as a static base rate of roughly 5-10%; they observe the union's rhetoric and never the employer's revealed preference. Option market makers tag earnings, PDUFA dates and court rulings in their event calendars but have no "CBA expiration" event type, so an expiry-dated straddle is priced at ordinary calendar vol with zero event premium. And the job-posting alt-data vendors (Revelio, LinkUp, Aura and the Greenhouse/Indeed scrapers) attribute these listings to the posting entity — Huffmaster, a small private staffing firm — so a dozen reqs disappear as noise inside a hundred-million-row panel; their pipelines are built to measure an issuer hiring for itself, not a vendor hiring on an anonymous client's behalf. Nobody in that chain performs the manual state + industry + classification + date join against OLMS CBA PDFs, because it requires reading Facebook posts. Union members do spot these postings and circulate them, but they are organizing, not trading.
- **Information lead:** Measured on verified posts. Healthcare: the Rhode Island req went up Feb 24 2025 for a job action anticipated "end of March 2025" — roughly 3 weeks ahead of the NLRA Section 8(g) 10-day strike notice, which is the market's first legally mandated public signal, and 4-5 weeks ahead of the strike itself. Industrial leads are longer: reqs naming "early June 2026" and "early to mid-January 2026" job actions were indexed well before those windows. Aerospace/defense is longest — pre-deployment and training "months in advance of contract expiration." Realistic lead over the tape: 2-8 weeks for healthcare and mid-cap industrials, up to 3+ months for large aerospace. There is also a settlement lead in the other direction: Huffmaster retroactively prefixes a post "SETTLED!" when a tentative agreement lands, occasionally ahead of broad wire coverage. Note the asymmetry — postings that appear AFTER a 10-day notice (one verified Michigan example posted Oct 10 2024 for an Oct 20 strike) carry no lead and must be discarded; the edge is entirely in reqs that precede any public notice.
- **Why still under-exploited:** It is not a product and nothing resembling it is sold. Searching for strike-staffing postings as an investment signal surfaces only generic job-posting alt-data marketing and academic ML papers on "labor relations early warning" — no vendor, no dataset, no coverage. The mechanics actively repel scale: Huffmaster's board exposes no RSS and no WordPress REST endpoint (both return 404), so it must be scraped through a "Load More" AJAX archive; the richest text lives on two Facebook pages; Strom publishes literally zero listings and runs a passive Avionté candidate database; the rest is nurse-forum diaspora. Volume is tiny — Huffmaster's live board shows about a dozen reqs of which only two or three are location-specific — which is precisely why coverage-oriented alt-data shops skip it and why it stays uncrowded. Most importantly the field that makes it valuable, the client's identity, is deliberately redacted, so the source reads as useless until someone builds the OLMS/NLRB join; that manual reconstruction step is the actual moat. And because vol surfaces carry no labor-event tag, there is no crowded expression already sitting in the price.
- **Rough +EV:** Roughly 20-40 location-specific reqs per year across all agencies; perhaps 8-15 map to a publicly traded employer with material single-site exposure. Unconditional probability of a strike at a given US CBA expiry is on the order of 3-8%; conditional on a scaled, dated contingency requisition I would put it at 35-55%. Because expiry-dated options carry no event premium, you are typically buying 25-35 vol against a realized distribution with a fat 8-20% adverse tail. Rough expectation: +10-20% on premium deployed per event, with a wide distribution concentrated in 2-4 large winners per year and many small premium burns. The duration read is where most of the edge sits — the guarantee length separates a 3-day symbolic walkout from a quarter-wrecking siege, and that distinction is worth more than the strike/no-strike call. Capacity is small, single-digit millions, capped by mid-cap options liquidity.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Biggest uncertainty is de-anonymization error: "Acute Care Facility in Pennsylvania, mid-January" can map to several employers, and a large share of US hospitals are nonprofit or private, meaning many healthcare reqs resolve to no tradeable instrument at all — so the industrial, food, utility and aerospace subset, where reqs are rarer, carries most of the real value and the effective sample is thin. Second-order risk: preparation is endogenous. Visible contingency staffing raises the employer's credibility at the table and can itself deter the strike, which structurally caps the directional hit rate and is the main argument for owning vol rather than direction. Third: these agencies could redact the anticipated date or stop posting publicly at any time, since the leak is inadvertent.

## 17. The Unlit /16 (Dark Address Inventory)

**Arena:** financial-market — US/EU listed equities and equity options (cloud/AI-infrastructure names)
**Domain:** Space, spectrum & telecom infrastructure

- **Data source:** The join of two free public datasets that nobody joins: (1) Regional Internet Registry IPv4 transfer logs + delegated-extended allocation files — a continuously updated, org-named ledger of exactly which company acquired which address blocks on which date; and (2) global BGP routing archives, which show which of those blocks are actually being ANNOUNCED to the internet, i.e. actually racked, powered and serving. The derived metric that exists nowhere else: "Dark Address Inventory" = addresses a company owns/controls but has not yet lit in BGP. That backlog is pre-purchased, not-yet-live compute capacity. The day a large block flips from unannounced to announced is the day that capacity goes live — observable worldwide within minutes, for free. Confirmed scale: ~29.7M addresses transferred in H1 2026 (record pace, ~59M annualized), with AWS/Microsoft/Google/Oracle absorbing ~60% of secondary volume over five years; AWS alone bought 9M addresses from T-Mobile in 2026.
- **How to collect it:** Transfer/allocation leg: ARIN transfer log JSON at account.arin.net/public/transferLog.xhtml plus daily `delegated-arin-extended-YYYYMMDD` files at ftp.arin.net/pub/stats/arin/; RIPE at ftp.ripe.net/ripe/transfers/ and ftp.ripe.net/pub/stats/ripencc/; APNIC at ftp.apnic.net/public/transfers/; LACNIC/AFRINIC equivalents. Format documented at arin.net/reference/research/statistics/transferlog_format/. Org-name resolution via ARIN RDAP/Whois-RWS and the RIPE database, plus a hand-built subsidiary map (e.g. "Amazon Data Services Ireland" -> AMZN) — this messy step is most of the moat. Routing leg: BGPKIT Broker free public REST API (indexes RouteViews + RIPE RIS, 70+ collectors, 1000+ full-feed peers, <5 min indexing delay) with bgpkit-parser for MRT dumps; RIPE RIS Live websocket for real-time announce/withdraw events; RIPEstat routing-history API for per-prefix announcement history. Earlier tell: RPKI ROA creation (RIPE RPKI repository archives / rpki-client output) usually precedes the BGP announcement by days-to-weeks. Attribution: PeeringDB API (net, fac, netfac, netixlan) to map the announcing ASN to a specific facility/metro, then a reverse-DNS PTR walk of the newly-lit prefix — operators encode site codes in router rDNS, naming the exact data center. Total cost: a few hundred dollars a month of compute; everything else is free.
- **The bet / trade:** Primary: directional equity/options on public-cloud names where public IPv4 maps closely to revenue-generating instances — DigitalOcean (DOCN, cleanest mapping: essentially every Droplet gets a public IPv4), Nebius (NBIS), OVHcloud (OVH.PA, the least-covered vehicle), CoreWeave (CRWV), Akamai (AKAM, via Linode). Direction: go long into the next print when a name's dark inventory is large AND its lighting rate (addresses moving unannounced->announced per week) is accelerating above its own trailing trend; fade/short when acquisitions have stopped, the backlog is fully lit, and nothing is replenishing — that is a capacity ramp running out of runway. Expression: buy calls (or puts) dated 2-4 weeks past the next scheduled earnings date, or hold delta into two consecutive prints. Horizon: 1-6 months, i.e. through one to two earnings cycles. Secondary/cheaper overlay: on the sell side, a small-cap telco or hosting company suddenly transferring out millions of addresses is a liquidity/asset-monetization tell — trade the credit or the equity into the next 10-Q.
- **Mechanism:** Hard physical causality, not correlation. You cannot turn up an internet-facing server without a routable address, and IPv4 is a scarce purchased input with real procurement lead time — you buy it in bulk from brokers/RIRs months before the racks exist, because you cannot buy it on the day you need it. So the transfer log is a forward capital commitment: the company has spent real money on an input that is useless until servers exist. Then, because operators only announce a prefix into BGP when the hardware behind it is physically racked, powered, addressed and about to carry traffic, the BGP announcement is a timestamp of actual go-live — not of a press release, not of a groundbreaking, not of a signed lease. The gap between the two timestamps is the pipeline. Chain: address purchase (T-2 to T-4 quarters) -> RPKI ROA created (T-2 to T-6 weeks) -> BGP announcement = capacity live (T-0) -> traffic and billing begin -> revenue recognized -> disclosed on the earnings call (T+4 to T+12 weeks). Every step before the last one is public and free. Aggregate the lit-capacity curve across a company's whole address estate and you have a bottom-up nowcast of deployed capacity that the company itself only discloses quarterly, in rounded aggregate, with heavy narrative framing.
- **Who's on the other side:** Sell-side infrastructure analysts modeling capacity from company-guided megawatts, press releases and supply-chain checks on GPU shipments — all of which measure intent and procurement, not live serving capacity, and all of which are guided by the company itself. Alt-data funds in this space are buying satellite imagery of data center construction (sees roofs and cranes, not deployed servers — a shell can sit dark for two quarters), job postings, and app/card data that is useless for infrastructure. The IPv4 broker industry (Brander Group, IPXO, IPv4Center, Hilco/IPv4.Global) sees the transfer half and publishes monthly market reports — but they are in the business of selling addresses to network operators, they report aggregate market prices and volumes rather than per-issuer timestamped series, they never touch BGP announcement state, and they have no equity readership. Internet-measurement academics (CAIDA, RIPE Labs, MANRS) do the BGP half beautifully and publish it into peer-reviewed venues with zero financial motive and zero interest in which ticker it maps to. Both halves of this signal are lying in the open in two different communities that have never been introduced to each other. The finance side is slow because the data is in 1990s fixed-format text files on FTP servers, org names are a swamp of LIRs, brokers and shell subsidiaries, and the routing side requires parsing MRT binary dumps — three unglamorous engineering problems stacked in a row.
- **Information lead:** Three distinct leads. (1) On procurement: RIR transfer logs post within days of a transfer completing; the company never discloses IP procurement at all, in any filing, ever — so the lead here is effectively permanent, and it front-runs the associated capacity by roughly one to two quarters. (2) On go-live: the BGP announcement is visible globally within minutes via RIS Live; the company confirms the capacity on its earnings call 4-12 weeks later. RPKI ROA creation pushes this a further 2-6 weeks earlier. (3) On the derived inflection — is the ramp accelerating or stalling — you get a clean read roughly 6-10 weeks before the quarter closes and 10-16 weeks before it is reported, which is the window that actually matters for positioning into a print. The practical, defensible number: about one quarter of lead on capacity direction, with minute-level precision on individual go-live events.
- **Why still under-exploited:** Nothing packages this for finance. My search of the alt-data vendor landscape returned zero BGP, zero PeeringDB and zero RIR products across every major "alternative data for hedge funds" listing — the category is web scraping, geolocation, card transactions and satellite imagery, full stop. The transfer half is published only by IPv4 brokers as marketing content for address buyers, in aggregate monthly form, with no issuer mapping and no time series a trader could use. The routing half is an academic and network-operator resource. Critically, the JOIN — dark inventory, lit-vs-unlit per issuer over time — appears to be computed by nobody, in either community, for any purpose. It is also structurally unattractive to a vendor: it requires maintaining a hand-curated corporate-subsidiary-to-LIR mapping that decays constantly, which is exactly the kind of grinding, unautomatable work that keeps a signal from being commoditized. And it is newly interesting: record transfer volumes in 2025-2026 driven explicitly by AI infrastructure and by the $42B BEAD broadband buildout mean the dataset only recently acquired enough signal density to matter.
- **Rough +EV:** Modest but real; an overlay, not a standalone book. Watchlist of ~10-15 names produces perhaps 4-8 high-conviction setups per year (the constraint is that most quarters show no divergence from consensus). On names carrying 60-100% implied vol and 10-25% single-day earnings moves, a 60-65% hit rate on a directional, defined-risk options expression into a print is worth roughly 8-20% expected return on risked capital per setup. Portfolio contribution plausibly a few hundred basis points a year for a two-person team, with the bigger practical value being as a confirming or vetoing overlay on theses sourced elsewhere — it is very good at killing a long when the ramp story is not showing up in lit capacity. Build cost is low: two to three months of engineering, then a few hundred dollars a month. The seller-side liquidity tell is a cheap free option on top, firing maybe 3-5 times a year across distressed telecom/hosting small caps.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Biggest uncertainty by far: whether public-IPv4 count still tracks revenue-generating capacity tightly enough per name. Three things erode it — IPv6 adoption and carrier-grade NAT let operators serve more customers per address every year, so the addresses-per-dollar ratio drifts and every signal needs to be measured against a name's own trailing trend rather than an absolute level; address LEASING (via IPXO and brokers) never appears in transfer logs at all, which blinds the procurement leg entirely for some operators — though it does not blind the BGP-lighting leg, since leased space still gets announced under the lessee's ASN and gets a fresh ROA, making the routing half materially more robust than the registry half; and AI TRAINING clusters are IP-light (a large cluster can sit behind a handful of public addresses), so the signal is strong for VM/inference/public-cloud businesses like DOCN, OVH and Nebius and weak-to-useless for training-colo landlords like IREN or APLD. Second-order risk: corporate-subsidiary attribution errors will generate false positives, and this must be validated by backtesting lit-capacity curves against 8-12 quarters of reported results per name before sizing anything.

## 18. The Embargoed Sounding

**Arena:** financial-market (Kalshi event contracts — daily high/low temperature, KXHIGH/KXLOW)
**Domain:** Weather-market & climate event contracts (Kalshi temperature, hurricane, snowfall)

- **Data source:** Real-time aircraft-derived vertical temperature profiles (AMDAR / MDCRS met reports) pulled directly off unencrypted VHF ACARS and VDL Mode 2 radio above Kalshi's own settlement airports. Every commercial jet climbing out of or descending into KDEN, KMDW, KLAX, KPHL, KMIA, KAUS, KDFW, KHOU downlinks static air temperature, wind, and pressure altitude every few seconds — producing dozens to hundreds of high-resolution soundings per airport per day, taken within a few km of the exact ASOS sensor that settles the contract. The moat: NOAA's own MADIS policy page (madis.ncep.noaa.gov/acars_restrictions.shtml) states this data "cannot be made available to the general public until 48 hours have elapsed," that real-time access "explicitly excludes commercial entities," and that it "cannot be used to create commercial products or services." Airlines retain proprietary interest. So NWS forecasters and NWP models ingest it live; every commercial weather-trading tool is legally locked out. But the airlines are broadcasting it in the clear, over the air, right now — and receiving radio requires nobody's permission.
- **How to collect it:** Hardware: RTL-SDR v4 (~$30) plus a quarter-wave VHF airband ground plane cut for ~136 MHz, sited within line-of-sight (~50-150 km) of the target airport; a Raspberry Pi per site. Software (all open source): `acarsdec` for multi-channel VHF ACARS (131.550/130.025/131.725 etc.), `szpajder/dumpvdl2` for VDL Mode 2 (136.975 primary), `szpajder/libacars` for ARINC 620/622 sublabel decoding, `acars_router` for routing, and `sdr-enthusiasts/docker-acarshub` as a turnkey Docker stack with local DB and web UI. Target messages: ACARS label H1 with sublabel M1 and the ARINC 620 Appendix C SMI mappings — these carry the MDCRS/AMDAR met payload (lat/lon, pressure altitude, static air temp, wind vector, timestamp). Zero-hardware alternative for coverage you can't site yourself: app.airframes.io — an ADS-B-Exchange-style volunteer aggregator for ACARS/VDLM2/HFDL/SATCOM with a live public stream and free API, already fed by receivers near most major US airports. Free backtest corpus (no SDR needed): the same data at 48h+ delay from madis-data.ncep.noaa.gov (GDC-ABO/acars) — build the entire historical study legally, then trade live off the radio. Free real-time cross-check: WVSS-II aircraft data, which MADIS explicitly exempts and publishes in real time. Settlement truth: NWS CLI products via api.weather.gov/products/types/CLI. Market data: Kalshi public API for KXHIGH/KXLOW order books.
- **The bet / trade:** Kalshi KXHIGH (and KXLOW) daily temperature bucket ladders, same-day, entered roughly 14Z-18Z local morning and held to settlement that night/next morning. Trade the distribution, not a single strike: buy a contiguous 3-4 bucket block centered on the mixing-method max and sell the block the model consensus is pricing, when the two disagree by a full bucket or more. Highest-conviction venues: KLAX (marine-layer inversion base), KDEN (cold-pool depth and downslope erosion), KMDW (lake-breeze/inversion), KPHL and NYC (Appalachian cold-air-damming wedge depth), KMIA (subsidence/Saharan-air capping). Avoid overcast, frontal, and precipitating days — the signal is a boundary-layer signal.
- **Mechanism:** On any day with daytime heating, the surface maximum is set by the classic forecaster mixing method: descend a dry adiabat from the top of the growing convective boundary layer to the surface. The max is therefore pinned by the temperature of the air sitting just above the morning inversion — the cap at roughly 900-800 mb. Model max-temp error is dominated by errors in exactly that layer: inversion depth, cap temperature, and the 0-3 km lapse rate. Models initialize this from ~70 CONUS radiosondes, twice daily, mean spacing ~315 km (and several sites reduced or suspended in recent NWS staffing cuts). Between the 12Z balloon and the afternoon max, no new balloon data exists anywhere. Aircraft profiles fix this: they resolve the inversion top and cap temperature an order of magnitude better, hourly, directly over the settlement sensor. Compute your own mixing-method max distribution from the composite morning profile; where it disagrees with the market's model-anchored distribution, that disagreement resolves in your favor when the surface temperature either stalls at the cap or punches through it.
- **Who's on the other side:** Retail Kalshi weather traders anchored on Weather.com, the morning NBM, and consumer apps. The small semi-pro tooling layer that has grown up around these markets (minutetemp.com at $14.99/mo, betterweatherbettor.com, bettingonweather.com, PillarLab) — every one of which is downstream of model output and surface observations, and all of which NOAA's restriction language bars from real-time aircraft data as commercial entities. And market makers quoting off NBM percentiles. All of them are solving the wrong problem: the entire visible ecosystem is obsessed with reconstructing the current surface temperature to the tenth of a degree, while the day's maximum is being determined 1,500 m overhead by a layer none of them can see. NWS forecasters DO have this data live, which is why their NDFD hand-edits sometimes beat NBM — but the market largely ignores NDFD, and the forecaster's edit lags their realization.
- **Information lead:** Two distinct leads. Tactical: you know the cap temperature by roughly 15-17Z; the market learns it only when the surface temperature visibly stalls or overshoots in early-to-mid afternoon — a 3-6 hour lead inside the same liquid trading session, on the day the contract settles. Structural: the 48-hour public embargo means no competitor can legally build or sell a product on this input, and no one can even audit your thesis until two days after settlement. On the specific busts — LAX marine layer, Denver cold pool, Philly damming wedge — the model-vs-reality gap can be 5-15°F, and the aircraft profile shows it before sunrise heating has even started.
- **Why still under-exploited:** It sits in a gap that no institution can cross. NWP centers have it but are contractually forbidden from revealing individual measurements or letting it reach commercial products. Weather-market vendors want it and are explicitly excluded. Quant funds don't do VHF radio engineering and would balk at a data source with an airline proprietary claim attached. Meanwhile the hobbyist community that actually receives these transmissions — the acarsdec/dumpvdl2/airframes.io crowd — collects them for aircraft-spotting and message-hunting and has no idea the label-H1 met payload is a tradeable boundary-layer sounding. The aggregation infrastructure (airframes.io) is only a few years old. It is genuinely weird, fragmented across ARINC 620 sublabel formats, requires physical presence near each airport, and the bridge between "amateur radio hobby" and "Kalshi temperature ladder" has, as far as I can find, never been built.
- **Rough +EV:** Fires on maybe 20-30% of city-days (well-mixed or capped days where the profile materially disagrees with the model) — higher at KLAX where the marine layer governs nearly year-round and at KDEN in cold-pool season. Across 6-8 tradeable cities that is roughly 10-15 firing events per week. When it fires, a one-bucket shift in the distribution is worth about 8-20 cents of gross mispricing on the buckets taken; net of ~1c spread and Kalshi's fee (~0.07·P·(1-P), ~1.7c at P=0.25), call it 4-9 cents net edge per contract, or ~5-10% ROI per event. At $1,000-3,000 risk per event that is a few hundred dollars per fire and low thousands per week — capacity-constrained by book depth to well under $10k risk per city-day, so this is an individual/small-team strategy, not a fund strategy. Variance is brutal: weekly standard deviation is several multiples of weekly mean, and you need several hundred events before the edge is statistically distinguishable from luck. Trade blocks, never single strikes.
- **Confidence:** Medium. The data source, its legal embargo, its radio accessibility, and the target markets are all verified and real — that part I am confident in. The biggest uncertainty by far: HRRR and RAP already assimilate this same AMDAR data hourly, so any trader running the 15Z/18Z HRRR has some of the information secondhand. My belief is that the information degrades badly on the way through HRRR's land-surface model and 2-m temperature bias correction, and that almost nobody applies the raw mixing method to the actual profile over the actual settlement sensor — but that is the crux and it is testable. Do not deploy capital before backtesting the mixing-method max against HRRR's T2m and against settled CLI values using the free 48-hour-delayed MADIS archive; if the raw-profile signal adds nothing beyond HRRR, the whole idea collapses. Secondary risks: aircraft temperature probes have known biases (~0.3-0.5°C, aircraft-type-dependent) requiring per-tail calibration, and receiver coverage varies sharply by city.

## 19. The Rule 65 Bell

**Arena:** gambling — US college football and college basketball markets (game spreads/totals, season win totals, conference/CFP futures) at US retail books, offshore books, and Kalshi/Polymarket-style event contracts
**Domain:** College sports & recruiting markets

- **Data source:** Raw federal and state court docket traffic in NCAA athlete-eligibility injunction litigation — specifically FRCP Rule 65 TRO / preliminary-injunction motions, hearing-setting entries, minute entries, and stay orders in cases naming the National Collegiate Athletic Association as defendant. The signal is not the lawsuit's existence (which gets written up eventually) but the timestamped docket event itself: (a) the *notice of hearing* that tells you exactly when a player's eligibility will be decided, days ahead; (b) the *minute entry / order* that decides it, which posts to CM/ECF before any human writes a sentence about it. Since Pavia v. NCAA (M.D. Tenn., Dec. 2024) courts have become the de facto eligibility authority: as of Aug 2026 there is a certified nationwide class and injunction in Wisne v. NCAA (D. Colo., Judge Charlotte Sweeney, PI + class cert July 31, 2026), an NCAA emergency stay motion briefed at the Tenth Circuit, a proposed MDL consolidating eight cases (Morton/N.D. Ga., Fuller & Wiley/D. Nev., Zeigler, Pavia, Boyd, Patterson/M.D. Tenn.) into M.D. Tennessee, and parallel state-court actions (Borovicanin v. NCAA, 24 basketball players, Ohio state court; an Oakland Univ. player's TRO in Oakland County, Mich. Circuit Court). Each docket is a roster-changing event feed that no sports data vendor carries.
- **How to collect it:** Three tiers, all buildable by one person for a few hundred dollars a year. (1) BREADTH — poll the public CM/ECF RSS endpoints at https://ecf.{court}.uscourts.gov/cgi-bin/rss_outside.pl. Roughly 173 ECF instances expose these; they are free, require no login, refresh about hourly, carry a 24-hour lookback, and include docket-entry text. Free Law Project's Juriscraper (github.com/freelawproject/juriscraper, `PacerRssFeeds`) already parses them and tracks per-court scope quirks (some feeds omit orders, some include everything). Regex the entry text for "National Collegiate Athletic Association", "NCAA", "eligibility", "temporary restraining order", "preliminary injunction". (2) DEPTH/SPEED — for the ~10-20 live cases that matter, drop the RSS latency by polling the PACER Docket Report directly at 60-second intervals during hearing windows (docket report pages are $0.10/page, capped, and waived under $30/quarter — pennies per week). Layer CourtListener's free RECAP Search Alerts and v4 REST API (courtlistener.com/api/rest/v4/, alerts endpoint) as a safety net and for backfilling party/attorney metadata. (3) THE BLIND SPOT — hand-built scrapers for the free name-searchable state portals in college-town counties: Hamilton County OH (courtclerk.org name search), Oakland County MI Circuit, Knox/Davidson County TN, Tyler Odyssey portals across TX/GA/CO. No RSS, no API, no aggregator — you poll a name search for "National Collegiate Athletic Association" every five minutes. The leading academic tracker (collegesportslitigationtracker.com) explicitly concedes its "tracking capabilities are limited" in state systems. Then join plaintiff names against roster data (year of first enrollment, minutes/snaps, returning-production share) from free sources — CollegeFootballData.com API, Sports Reference, team roster pages — to pre-compute a per-player line impact before the ruling lands.
- **The bet / trade:** Two linked trades. (A) SINGLE-PLAYER SPEED TRADE: on a TRO/PI order hitting the docket, immediately take the affected team's next game spread and total in the direction of the ruling (buy the team if eligibility granted, fade if denied or stayed), sized biggest on Group-of-5 and low-major games where the number is model-driven. Holding horizon: minutes to hours; settles that game. Also buy/sell the team's season win total (available Aug–Sep for football, Oct–Nov for basketball) — horizon to season end, with an option to unwind into the repriced number within 24–72 hours. (B) CORRELATED CLASS-WIDE TRADE, live right now: the Tenth Circuit's ruling on the NCAA's stay of the Wisne injunction is a single binary that flips a nationwide class of fifth-year players on or off simultaneously, weeks before the season. Pre-build the exposure table (every D-I team ranked by returning production attributable to 2022-cohort fifth-years), then on the order posting to the appellate docket: stay GRANTED → hammer UNDER on the 15–30 most-exposed teams' win totals plus fade their Week 1–3 spreads; stay DENIED → the mirror image, OVERs and buy-side. Horizon: futures to December, game lines same-week.
- **Mechanism:** Court order → player's eligibility status changes → team's true strength changes → the posted line is now wrong. It is unusually clean because the causal chain has no intermediate probability: a granted injunction means the starting quarterback plays Saturday, full stop, and the effect size is directly estimable from the same returning-production models the market already uses. The 2024–2026 antitrust wave (Pavia, Elad, Fourqurean, Wisne) transferred eligibility authority from an NCAA process that announced decisions through press releases on the NCAA's own schedule to a judicial process that announces them through docket entries at unpredictable hours, often the day before a game. That transfer is the whole trade: the news now originates in a system that sportsbooks do not read.
- **Who's on the other side:** Sportsbook traders and the recreational money on the other side of a stale number. NCAAF and NCAAB are the widest, thinnest-staffed products a book runs — 136 FBS teams and 360+ D-I basketball teams, hundreds of simultaneous lines, priced off power ratings plus roster feeds from Sportradar/SportsDataIO. Those feeds update when a school updates its official roster or a player appears in a box score, which is days after a ruling. Books react to *news*, meaning a tweet from a credentialed beat reporter or a burst of sharp money — neither of which exists in the first window after a docket entry. The sharp syndicates that would normally arbitrage this have pipelines built around injury Twitter, practice reports, beat-writer relationships and in-arena runners; PACER is simply not in anyone's stack, and the search for any handicapper monitoring dockets as an alpha source returns nothing. The one genuinely fast competitor is sports-law Twitter (Sportico's McCann, Daniel Wallach), but they read, verify, and write before posting, they cover only the marquee cases, and they do not systematically watch state courts at all.
- **Information lead:** Three different leads stacked. Docket entry → national media post: roughly 20 minutes to 6 hours for a marquee federal case; 1–5 days, or never, for a Sun Belt lineman or a Horizon League guard. Docket entry → book actually moves a mid-major number: 2 hours to several days, and sometimes the number never corrects because nobody bets enough into it to force the issue. The largest and least contested lead is the hearing calendar: a "Motion Hearing set for [date/time] before Judge X" entry gives you 3–21 days of advance notice that a specific team's roster has a scheduled binary resolution — that information is currently worth zero to everyone but the parties' lawyers, and it lets you pre-position or, just as valuably, avoid dead capital in a market about to gap. State-court cases run at the extreme: a TRO can issue ex parte within hours in a county circuit court with no wire service watching at all.
- **Why still under-exploited:** It sits in the seam between three fields that do not talk to each other. Sports-data vendors sell rosters, injuries, weather and line movement; none of them ingest court records — there is no NCAA-litigation product from any betting data company. Legal-tech vendors (Lex Machina, Docket Alarm, Bloomberg Law) sell docket monitoring to law firms priced and packaged for litigation strategy, with no roster join, no line-impact model, and latency measured in hours because their customers do not care about minutes. And the college-sports beat covers this as a narrative story, not a machine-readable feed. The plumbing is also genuinely ugly in a way that repels productization: 173 heterogeneous RSS feeds with inconsistent event scope, plus a long tail of county clerk portals with no API, CAPTCHAs, and different name-search semantics — exactly the fragmented manual work that a determined individual can do and a company will not. Finally the whole category is brand new: eligibility-by-injunction did not exist before December 2024, so no incumbent has had time to build for it, and the volume has grown from one case to an MDL plus a certified nationwide class in twenty months.
- **Rough +EV:** Frequency: 30–60 actionable docket events per year at current litigation volume, of which perhaps 15–25 involve a player whose absence or presence measurably moves a number, plus 1–3 correlated class-wide events like the pending Wisne stay ruling. Per-event edge: a top-two contributor flipping status is worth roughly 2.5–5 points on a mid-major basketball spread and 0.8–1.5 wins on a football win total; betting a stale number at -110 with a 3-point true edge is worth roughly 12–18% ROI on stake, and stale win totals somewhat more with longer settlement. Capacity is the binding constraint, not signal quality: realistically $1,000–3,000 per single-player event spread across a book portfolio, and $20,000–40,000 across 15–30 correlated tickets on a class-wide event at an 8–15% edge. Blended, that is roughly 10–20% ROI on $50,000–150,000 of annual turnover, so $10,000–35,000 a year against maybe $400 of PACER fees and a weekend of scraper work — with the class-wide events, which are lumpy and rare, carrying most of the upside.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high on the information edge, medium on realized P&L. The signal is verified and the lead is structural, not a race I have to win against a peer — nobody else is in this lane. The biggest uncertainty is monetization capacity: college win totals and mid-major sides carry $500–$2,500 limits, books take numbers down fast when they smell a steamer, and an account that repeatedly hits stale mid-major numbers within minutes of a court order will get limited within a season, so the strategy likely decays into a multi-account, multi-book operation whose real ceiling is set by bookmaker risk management rather than by the alpha. Secondary uncertainty is event-stream durability: MDL consolidation into M.D. Tennessee would centralize the docket (easier to watch, but also easier for a reporter to camp on), and a Tenth Circuit merits ruling, a settlement, or federal legislation could shut the litigation wave off within 12–24 months. The edge is real but I would treat it as a two-season window, not a franchise.

## 20. The Addendum B Crew Book

**Arena:** financial-market
**Domain:** Agriculture ground truth & food supply

- **Data source:** The ETA-790/790A H-2A "agricultural clearance order" filings of US custom combine harvesters — specifically the requested worker count, the employment begin/end dates, and the Addendum B multi-worksite itinerary (the ordered list of states/counties the crew will cut, with dwell windows). Custom harvesters cut a large share of Great Plains hard red winter wheat and ~75% of their workforce is foreign labor on H-2A, so they must publicly pre-declare, 60–75 days ahead of first date of need, exactly how many combine/truck crew they are bringing and which states the run covers. This is a grower-side, cash-committed forward book on harvestable wheat acres — filed months before USDA surveys it. Verified live: e.g. ETA case H-300-25335-444016, Jeff Paplow Harvesting LLC (Roscoe, SD), 35 workers, employment 2026-02-01 to 2026-12-01, order posted 2025-12-18; H-300-25029-651001, Morrison Chopping LLC, 12 workers. Job-order text confirms the structure verbatim: "Custom harvesting duties will only be performed at the locations listed in Addendum B" and "workers must be able to work for the entire itinerary."
- **How to collect it:** Three free pulls, no vendor. (1) REAL-TIME: the DOL 790/790A JSON data feed at https://api.seasonaljobs.dol.gov/datahub-search/sjCaseData/zip/jo/YYYY-MM-DD — a ZIP of JSON containing the complete data elements for every H-2A job order submitted or updated in the prior 20 days, refreshed daily at midnight ET. Companion 9142A feed (Notice-of-Acceptance stage) at .../zip/h2a/YYYY-MM-DD. Field dictionaries (XLSX) are linked from https://seasonaljobs.dol.gov/feeds. Pull daily and diff — amendments and withdrawals are the highest-value events. (2) PER-ORDER DETAIL: https://seasonaljobs.dol.gov/jobs/<ETA case number> renders the full order including the Addendum B worksite list; archive search at https://seasonaljobs.dol.gov/archive. (3) BACKTEST: OFLC quarterly disclosure files at https://flag.dol.gov/programs/H-2A (H-2A_Disclosure_Data_FY20xx.xlsx) give worksite, workers certified, and dates back to the 2020 form redesign. Filter: SOC 45-2091 (Agricultural Equipment Operators) plus title/description regex for custom harvest|combine|harvester|swather, then keep orders whose Addendum B touches TX/OK/KS/CO/NE/SD/ND/MT. Build the operator universe by cross-referencing the U.S. Custom Harvesters Inc. member directory (~500 members) so you track the SAME operators year over year. Total build: one engineer, a week; running cost, a cron job. The only public scraper of this feed today is a labor-rights nonprofit's (github.com/ResearchActionDesign/seasonal-job-scraper).
- **The bet / trade:** Primary: KC hard red winter wheat futures (CME ticker KE), September or July contract. Enter late March / first week of April once the season's crew book is essentially complete; exit into the mid-May Kansas Wheat Quality Tour and the May 12 Crop Production/WASDE first winter-wheat production forecast. Horizon 4–10 weeks. Direction: LONG KE when aggregate declared crew-days in KS/OK/TX contract materially YoY on a same-operator basis (harvesters are pre-declaring less wheat to cut than USDA's abandonment assumption implies); SHORT when the book expands. Preferred risk-adjusted expression: the KE–ZW spread (KC minus Chicago SRW), long the spread on a contracting book — this isolates the HRW-specific abandonment story and strips out global wheat, dollar, and macro noise. Secondary: KE July/September calendar spread — a compressed, front-loaded Kansas itinerary means a small fast harvest and less harvest-window pressure, so buy July / sell September. Tertiary in-season overlay: any operator's mid-season amendment dropping Kansas worksites or cutting headcount is a same-week add to the long-KE position.
- **Mechanism:** Abandonment — planted acres never harvested because drought or freeze made the stand not worth running a combine over — is the single largest and least-forecastable swing in HRW production; Kansas abandonment has ranged from roughly 5% to 35% across recent years, and USDA carried ~17% into 2026 while the actual crop showed "widespread abandonment" that the market was "somewhat surprised by." Custom harvesters know abandonment before anyone because they contract acres directly with the same farm customers annually, and by February–March those farmers have told them which fields will be grazed out, hayed, or zeroed. The harvester then sizes his crew against that contracted acre book — and that sizing is a cash decision (visa fees, consular processing, housing, transport, DOT compliance, roughly $3–5k per worker before a wheel turns), so it is an honest signal, not talk. Federal law then forces him to publish it: 20 CFR 655.121 requires the job order be filed no more than 75 and no fewer than 60 calendar days before the first date of need, and Addendum B requires him to enumerate every worksite on the itinerary. Aggregate a few hundred of those filings and you have reconstructed, for free, the private farm-gate abandonment expectation of the people who physically cut the crop. The dwell structure matters more than the headcount: days-declared-in-Kansas is acre-driven, while raw headcount is partly policy-driven. USDA's own pipeline cannot see this — NASS condition ratings measure the crop still standing, not the acres already written off, which is precisely why abandonment is USDA's largest recurring revision in HRW.
- **Who's on the other side:** Managed money in KC wheat, which positions off drought monitor, NOAA/ECMWF precip anomalies, weekly NASS crop condition ratings, and the mid-May Kansas Wheat Quality Tour. All four measure the standing crop's health, none measure acres already abandoned — so the spec crowd systematically arrives at the abandonment number in mid-May with the tour, and again on June 30 Acreage. On the other side of the spread trade sit index and macro flows that treat KE and ZW as interchangeable wheat beta. Why they're slow: (a) the data is disguised as immigration compliance paperwork, and no commodity desk has an analyst whose job includes reading DOL forms; (b) the feed is a rolling 20-day ZIP of JSON with the itinerary buried in an addendum, so it looks like plumbing rather than a dataset; (c) the ag-econ literature that does use OFLC data uses it for wage and labor-supply studies, never for production; (d) there is no vendor packaging it — Kpler/Vortexa/YipitData/M Science have no ag-labor product, and the satellite crop vendors (Descartes/Gro/Planet) sell greenness, which is exactly the standing-crop signal that misses abandonment. Commercial millers and exporters hear harvester chatter individually but nobody aggregates it, because the aggregation requires stitching several hundred small LLC filings to a member directory by hand.
- **Information lead:** Hard and statutory, not estimated. The 60–75 day filing rule means a crew book for a late-May Texas/Oklahoma start is on file by roughly mid-to-late March; harvesters running longer contracts file even earlier (the verified Paplow order was posted Dec 18 for a Feb 1 start). Against the market's information calendar that is: days to ~3 weeks ahead of Prospective Plantings (Mar 31); ~6–8 weeks ahead of the first winter wheat production forecast (Crop Production/WASDE, May 12); ~8 weeks ahead of the Kansas Wheat Quality Tour (mid-May); ~13 weeks ahead of Acreage (Jun 30); and ~5–6 months ahead of the definitive abandonment print in the September Small Grains Summary. Separately, the daily feed diff gives a 2–6 week lead on the tour and on harvest-progress reports every time an operator amends headcount or strikes a state from Addendum B mid-season — those amendments are real-time abandonment confirmations from the field.
- **Why still under-exploited:** There is no commercial product for it and essentially no competing scraper. A targeted search for H-2A/labor-certification data used as a crop-production or futures signal returns nothing — the alt-data buyer guides cover satellite, card, web, and sentiment, and skip labor filings entirely. The only public tooling built against seasonaljobs.dol.gov is a worker-advocacy nonprofit's scraper. The dataset is unattractive by every heuristic a data-sourcing team uses: it is a government compliance form, the entities are hundreds of unlisted family LLCs, the useful field lives in an addendum, the format is a rolling 20-day ZIP rather than a clean historical table, and the surface reading (headcount) is contaminated by immigration policy — which reads as noise unless you know to normalize per-operator and lean on itinerary dwell instead. Crucially, this is the inverse of the crowded satellite trade: everyone is buying imagery of the crop that is still there, and nobody is buying the declaration of which fields will never see a combine. Custom harvesting is also a genuinely obscure trade — ~500 USCHI members running a six-month convoy from Texas to the Canadian border — so the domain knowledge required to even ask the question is rare on a commodity desk.
- **Rough +EV:** Low frequency, decent size. The core HRW signal fires once per year (late March), with a secondary fall read for corn/milo/sunflower itineraries and 2–5 in-season amendment adds — call it 1–2 clean setups annually. Payoff scale: an abandonment surprise of 5+ percentage points in Kansas is worth roughly 30–80 cents on KE, and 20–50 cents on the KE–ZW spread. If the crew book calls the direction of the abandonment surprise ~60–65% of the time (plausible given it is a cash-committed forecast from the people holding the contracts, but only ~5–6 clean post-2020-redesign seasons exist to prove it), sizing to a 25-cent stop with a 55-cent objective gives roughly +12–20 cents expected per event, or ~15–30% on risked capital per trade. Standalone Sharpe realistically 0.5–0.8 given one shot a year; materially better run as the KE–ZW spread and combined with the in-season amendment adds. Capacity is small — a few hundred KE contracts before you move a thin spread — which is itself the moat and the reason no fund will bother building it.
- **Confidence:** Medium. The data source, the feed URL, the 60–75 day statutory lead, and the Addendum B itinerary structure are all verified against live filings, so collection risk is near zero and the information lead is real. The biggest uncertainty is ATTRIBUTION: crew counts move for reasons that have nothing to do with acres. The Adverse Effect Wage Rate has risen sharply and pushed some harvesters to shrink or exit H-2A; consular and visa processing delays cut arrivals independent of demand; a 2026 FMCSA rule is currently blocking some H-2A workers from obtaining CDLs, which mechanically suppresses truck-driver headcount this very season for a purely regulatory reason; and larger combines mean fewer crew per acre secularly. If you read raw headcount you will trade policy noise as if it were abandonment. The mitigation — same-operator YoY normalization plus weighting the Addendum B dwell/state-sequence structure (acre-driven) over headcount (policy-driven) — is untested out of sample, and with only ~5–6 seasons of Addendum-B-era disclosure data the backtest is too thin to distinguish a real edge from a lucky fit. Trade it small until you have observed two live seasons.

## 21. The Burner-Tip Census

**Arena:** financial-market
**Domain:** Industrial capacity & manufacturing ground truth

- **Data source:** FERC/NAESB-mandated "Informational Postings" on interstate natural gas pipeline electronic bulletin boards — specifically the Operationally Available Capacity by Location tables, which publish, per gas day and per nomination cycle, the Design Capacity (DC), Operating Capacity (OPC), Total Scheduled Quantity (TSQ) and Operationally Available Capacity (OAC) at every receipt/delivery meter ≥15,000 MMBtu/d. Critically, the delivery meters are named for the actual end user. Verified live on Panhandle Eastern (PEPL): location names include "OWENS-BROCKWAY GLASS CONTAINER", "ARDAGH GLASS", "GUARDIAN GLASS", "FUYAO GLASS ILLINOIS", "GENERAL MOTORS", "UNITED STATES STEEL", "SEVERSTAL DEARBORN (RIVER ROUGE)", "PCS NITROGEN FERTILIZER", "POET BIOREFINING - ALEXANDRIA", "MICHIGAN ETHANOL PLANT - VALERO RENEWABLE FUELS", "BASF", "DOW CHEMICAL USA", "INEOS USA", "HARBISON-WALKER BRICK PLANT - VANDALIA, MO". That is one pipeline. There are ~35-40 interstate systems with the identical posting obligation. Assembled, this is a free, daily, plant-by-plant census of US factory throughput.
- **How to collect it:** Scrape each pipeline's iPost/EBB portal directly; most are plain HTML tables with CSV/XML/Excel download links and URL-parameterized gas day and cycle. Verified working pattern: https://commoncarrier.energytransfer.com/ipost/capacity/operationally-available-by-location?asset=PEPL&cycleDesc=Final&gasDay=03%2F17%2F2026 (swap asset= for PEPL, TGR, Trunkline, Sea Robin, Transwestern, etc.; Downloads: CSV, Excel, ODS, PDF, RTF, XML). Peers: northernnaturalgas.com/infopostings (Locations page has ?download=true CSV; Capacity/Pages/OperationallyAvailable.aspx), Williams/Transco 1Line, Kinder Morgan pipeline portals (NGPL, TGP, EPNG), TC Energy/ANR, Boardwalk (Texas Gas, Gulf South), Enbridge (Algonquin, Texas Eastern), Southern Star, Enable/Energy Transfer. Two mechanics that matter: (1) pipelines that post only OPC and OAC still hand you scheduled flow, since TSQ = OPC - OAC; (2) EBBs are only required to keep 90 days of data online, so you must run a daily cron from day one — there is no back-file to buy. Build a one-time crosswalk of Loc Code -> meter name -> corporate parent -> plant nameplate capacity (~2,000-4,000 industrial delivery meters nationally; a few weeks of manual work using EPA FRS/ECHO, county assessor records, and Title V permits to resolve stale operator names like SEVERSTAL DEARBORN = Cleveland-Cliffs). Pull all five cycles (Timely, Evening, ID1, ID2, ID3) so you capture intraday revisions, not just the final.
- **The bet / trade:** Two legs off one dataset. (1) Core repeatable bet: pre-earnings volume-surprise trade on single-process, gas-intensive manufacturers whose output is nearly a linear function of gas burn. Convert summed daily plant TSQ into physical tons/gallons using published heat rates, compare to sell-side consensus volumes, and take a directional equity position entered ~10 trading days before the print and held 2-4 weeks through it. Highest-conviction current names: CF Industries (CF, ammonia complexes), O-I Glass (OI, Owens-Brockway container furnaces), Green Plains (GPRE) and Alto Ingredients (ALTO, ethanol). Direction is set by the panel, not a permanent tilt: long when meter-derived output runs >5% above consensus, short when >5% below. (2) Event overlay: when intraday cycle revisions show an unplanned multi-day trip at a North American ammonia complex (Koch Enid, Nutrien Geismar/Augusta, Iowa Fertilizer Wever, LSB El Dorado, CF Port Neal/Donaldsonville), buy nitrogen-price exposure — CF equity as the liquid proxy, or NOLA urea/UAN swaps — with a 1-4 week hold, unwinding when the outage shows up in the trade press.
- **Mechanism:** For these processes gas is not overhead, it is the feedstock or the furnace, and the conversion ratios are public and stable: ammonia ~33-36 MMBtu per short ton NH3; dry-mill ethanol ~23-26 MMBtu per 1,000 gallons; container glass ~4-6 MMBtu per ton of glass pulled. So daily scheduled gas at a named meter is daily production, with a mapping error of a few percent rather than a directional guess. Second, these are step-function assets. A glass furnace is either hot or drained; an ammonia train is either at rate or tripped; an ethanol plant is either running or idled on negative crush. Those steps land in the meter tape the same day the operator makes the decision, because the plant must cut its gas nomination at the next cycle or pay imbalance penalties — the physical constraint is what forces the disclosure. Third, the *cycle* microstructure separates planned from unplanned: a turnaround appears as a scheduled ramp-down at the Timely cycle one to three days ahead, while a trip appears as a large negative revision at Intraday 1/2/3 on the same day. And the restart ramp tells you when a turnaround is running long — the single hardest thing to learn about an outage.
- **Who's on the other side:** Three slow counterparties. (a) Sell-side and long-only analysts covering CF, OI, GPRE, PKG, X: they model volumes from management guidance and capacity-utilization assumptions, and most have never heard of a FERC informational posting. Their volume estimate is refreshed quarterly; yours is refreshed daily. (b) The gas-data vendors who do read these feeds — Criterion Research, Wood Mackenzie/Genscape, LSEG — sell to gas and power desks and bucket industrial meters into an aggregate "industrial demand" line for supply-demand balancing. They do not maintain a meter-to-corporate-parent-to-nameplate-capacity crosswalk, because their customers don't care which company owns the burner tip. Notably, Genscape's equity-oriented product ("Equity Production Insight") tracks upstream oil and gas *producers*, not manufacturers — the downstream manufacturing application is unoccupied. (c) Fertilizer physical traders, who learn about a competitor's outage from Argus/Profercy/Green Markets weeklies, broker chatter, or the counterparty declaring force majeure — all of which happen after the gas nomination was already cut.
- **Information lead:** Unplanned outage: 2-8 hours. The plant cuts its nomination at the next intraday cycle (ID1 nom deadline ~10:00 CT, ID2 ~14:30, ID3 ~19:00) and the revised TSQ posts immediately. Trade press typically confirms 1-7 days later, and often only in a weekly. Turnaround overrun: days to weeks — nobody else can see that a 21-day turnaround is now on day 34 until the company says so. Quarterly volumes: 3-7 weeks. You hold a near-complete daily production series through the last day of the quarter; the company reports 3-7 weeks after quarter-end and consensus does not move until then. Permanent capacity decisions (a glass furnace drained for good, an ethanol plant idled indefinitely): typically one to two quarters ahead of the restructuring announcement or impairment.
- **Why still under-exploited:** Four durable reasons. (1) Fragmentation: ~35-40 separate pipeline portals with no common schema, no central API, no FERC aggregation, and meter names that are free text with decades-old operator names, duplicate spellings, and multi-meter plants. Nobody has done the crosswalk because it is boring manual work sitting between two disciplines. (2) No back-file exists. EBB archives are only required to stay online 90 days, so this dataset literally cannot be purchased retroactively — you either started collecting or you didn't. That is why no vendor productized it for equities and why a small team that starts today builds a compounding, non-replicable asset. (3) Audience mismatch: the people who read these feeds trade gas basis; the people who would trade OI or GPRE on it don't know the feeds exist. The data is hiding in plain sight on the wrong desk. (4) It reads as a compliance artifact, not a dataset — "Operationally Available Capacity by Location" is a tariff-transparency posting for shippers, and nobody markets it as manufacturing ground truth.
- **Rough +EV:** Event leg: roughly 15-30 detectable material North American nitrogen outages per year. Assume 60-65% directional hit rate and ~1.5-3% average 5-day move in the nitrogen proxy conditional on a confirmed multi-week competitor trip, net ~60-120bp gross edge per event, ~15-25% gross annual on that sleeve, high variance and lumpy. Earnings leg: ~4 prints/year across a rotating 6-10 name basket, taking positions only on >5% consensus divergence (fires maybe 8-14 times/year); these names move 5-15% on volume surprises, so call it 100-200bp of edge per firing, ~10-20% gross annual on that sleeve. Combined, a plausible 12-20% gross annualized on a concentrated multi-strategy sleeve before costs, with meaningful drawdown risk because the position count is low. Setup cost is a few weeks of scraping plus a few weeks of crosswalk work and roughly $0 in data fees; the real cost is that you must collect for two to three quarters before the panel is usable. Capacity is modest — CF is liquid enough for eight figures, OI/GPRE/ALTO are not — call it $20-80M before market impact bites.
- **Confidence:** Medium. The data source is verified real, free, granular to the named factory, and daily. The biggest uncertainty is coverage and attribution, and it is not small: a plant only appears if it takes gas through a direct interstate pipeline tap. Plants served by a local distribution company are hidden behind an aggregated city-gate meter, and intrastate pipelines largely have no posting obligation (FERC Order 720's requirement on major non-interstate lines was rolled back after Fifth Circuit litigation), which blanks out much of the Texas and Oklahoma industrial corridor — including some CF and Koch sites. A plant can also draw across multiple meters or through third-party marketers, so a single named meter may be only a partial read on its burn, and TSQ is *scheduled*, not metered actual flow (close for firm-supply industrials, but not identical). Practical mitigation: before trading a name, spend the first quarter validating meter-derived output against the company's own reported volumes; only trade the names where that regression is tight, and treat the rest as unmodelable.

## 22. The Roe Window

**Arena:** financial-market
**Domain:** Maritime, fisheries & ocean economy. RULES: observe institutions/infrastructure, never individuals' private behavior; collect only via sanctioned paths (public/documented APIs, published filings, own sensors) — never anti-bot evasion, harvested credentials, side channels, or robots-blocked endpoints

- **Data source:** Iceland's per-vessel landing and quota-status tape from Fiskistofa (Directorate of Fisheries). Every landing in Iceland is weighed at a certified harbour scale and written into Fiskistofa's central "Lóðs" database with vessel registration number, species, tonnage, landing date, harbour, gear, catch condition and — critically — the processing/utilisation form (meal & oil vs. freezing vs. roe). Vessel-level quota allocation (aflamark), catch-to-date, and quota transfers are published continuously. Because vessels map to owners via Fiskistofa's public skipaskrá, this is a live, legally-audited revenue tape for the four Nasdaq Iceland seafood companies. The crown-jewel signal is the capelin (loðna) season: fishing opens in December with 100% of catch going to low-value meal & oil, and the high-value roe/frozen-female window for the Japanese market runs only ~15 February to ~21 March. Same raw tonne is worth roughly $400-500 as meal input versus ~$2,500/t as roe-bearing frozen product (Feb 2026 "mix" roe settled ~$2.50/kg). So *when* and *in what form* each company lands its capelin, not just how much, drives the year's biggest margin swing.
- **How to collect it:** Three sanctioned paths, all documented. (1) Free public tables at fiskistofa.is (English: "Quotas and catches → Total catch and quota status"; Icelandic: Landanir, Aflamarksstaða skipa, Flutningur aflamarks, Skipaskrá) plus the legacy portal gamli.fiskistofa.is. (2) The official Fiskistofa "Gagnaveita" REST API — application form and docs at island.is/s/fiskistofa/vefthjonustur-fiskistofu, OAuth credentials, Swagger at gagnaveitan.azurewebsites.net/swagger, ISK 111,200/yr (~US$800). It returns per-vessel quota status by species, landing records (volume, condition, storage/processing method, harbour, date), licences and gear, vessel registry with owner/operator and ownership changes, and average fish prices. (3) Free supporting feeds: hafsja.fiskistofa.is for real-time emergency area closures; hafogvatn.is for Hafró survey bulletins and capelin advice; px.hagstofa.is (PX-Web JSON API) for monthly export volume/value by product and destination to back out realised unit prices. Build a nightly job: pull landings, join vessel→owner, bucket by species × processing form × week, and roll up to SVN / ISF / BRIM. No scraping evasion, no credentials, no robots-blocked endpoints.
- **The bet / trade:** Equities on Nasdaq Iceland, held 4-10 weeks into scheduled results. Core trade: from roughly 1 March (once the roe-window landing split is visible) go long Síldarvinnslan (SVN.IC) and/or Ísfélag (ISF.IC) when the tape shows (a) capelin quota being fully caught and (b) an above-normal share landed for freezing/roe inside the 15 Feb – 21 Mar window; hold through the Q1 print (SVN reported Q1 2026 on 21 May, i.e. ~7 weeks after quarter-end). Take the opposite side — underweight/short the quota holders and go long Iceland Seafood International (ICESEA.IC), the quota-less value-added processor that buys raw fish and is squeezed by scarcity — when the tape shows the season aborting early or landing overwhelmingly into meal & oil. Secondary trade: directional positioning into the three dated Hafró capelin-advice catalysts (initial advice ~June, post-autumn-survey revision ~October, post-winter-survey revision ~late January), sized off Hafró's interim cruise bulletins. Same machinery runs year-round on blue whiting (spring), mackerel and NSS herring (summer/autumn) and demersal, but Q1 carries the most signal. Roughly 8-12 tradeable events per year.
- **Mechanism:** Iceland runs a pure ITQ system: quota is a hard legal cap and a hard legal entitlement per vessel, so company revenue is almost mechanically (own-vessel tonnes by species) × (product mix) × (export unit price). The first two terms are state-registered in near-real-time; the companies disclose them ~7-9 weeks later. Capelin is the swing variable — quota has ranged 0 to ~900kt across recent years, Ísfélag holds ~19% and Síldarvinnslan ~17.5% of the Icelandic capelin allocation, and Q1 is by far their largest quarter (SVN Q1 2026: €113.6m revenue, €35.1m net profit against a ~$1.1bn market cap). Within the season, biology sets a hard calendar: roe must reach a specific maturity before it can be cut and frozen for Japan, which happens only in the last ~5 weeks. Bad weather, a late migration, or an emergency area closure that pushes landings before 15 February converts high-value roe tonnes into low-value meal tonnes at roughly a fifth of the price, with no way to recover them. The landing tape's date and processing-form columns show that conversion in real time; the market sees it only in the printed accounts.
- **Who's on the other side:** Icelandic pension funds and domestic retail, who trade the printed quarterly. Sell-side coverage is a handful of analysts at Landsbankinn, Arion and Íslandsbanki working almost entirely off company disclosure; international coverage is essentially zero — Ísfélag only listed in December 2023 and Síldarvinnslan in 2021, so there is barely any institutional modelling history. They are slow for structural reasons, not stupidity: the data is Icelandic-language, split across four agencies, the good API requires a written application plus OAuth onboarding and a fee, and using it requires mapping ~500 registered vessels to owners including charters, leases and mid-season quota transfers. The specialist trade press (Undercurrent News, The Fishing Daily) does report aggregate season progress and even Japan price negotiations — but in prose, at the national level, never per company and never by processing form, and no equity investor is wiring that prose into a per-company revenue model.
- **Information lead:** Versus the company report: you have the entire volume-and-mix half of the Q1 number by ~21 March; SVN printed Q1 2026 on 21 May — a ~9-week lead, and up to ~5 months of lead on the January portion. Versus the trade press: Undercurrent/Fishing Daily publish national season totals with a 1-3 week lag and no company attribution, so ~1-3 weeks even on the aggregate and effectively unlimited lead on the per-company split. Versus Hafró's formal quota revisions: the institute's interim cruise bulletins during the autumn and winter acoustic surveys precede the published advice by ~3-10 days. Versus Statistics Iceland export data — the only other public read on realised value — those land monthly with a ~5-6 week lag while the landing tape updates within days of the fish hitting the scale.
- **Why still under-exploited:** No vendor productizes it. Kpler/Vortexa are hydrocarbons; Global Fishing Watch is AIS-based effort data with no ownership or product-mix layer and no revenue framing; YipitData/M Science do not cover Nasdaq Iceland. The dataset exists for fisheries-compliance transparency, not investors, and it is published in Icelandic across Fiskistofa, Hafró, Hagstofa and Nasdaq Iceland. Three compounding frictions keep it clear: a paid written-application API, a non-trivial vessel-to-owner mapping problem, and market caps of roughly $0.5-1.2bn with daily turnover under $2m — too small for any fund with the budget to build the pipeline. There is also a domain-knowledge moat: the date and processing-form columns look like compliance metadata unless you already know that roe maturity is what separates a $500/t tonne from a $2,500/t tonne, which is a fisheries fact, not a data fact.
- **Rough +EV:** Post-results moves in these names run 3-10%. Assume ~8-12 high-signal events per year across SVN, ISF, BRIM and ICESEA, direction called correctly ~62-68% of the time, capturing on average ~4% of the absolute move — that is roughly 1.0-1.5% net edge per event after ~0.3-0.5% round-trip costs and ISK/EUR hedging, so approximately 10-18% gross annually on deployed capital, with the pair (quota holders vs. ICESEA) removing most of the Icelandic-market and ISK beta. Capacity is the binding constraint, not edge: realistic deployment is $2-6m, scaled over several days per leg. This is a genuine but small-book strategy, not a scalable one.
- **Confidence:** Medium. The tape resolves the volume and product-mix half of the earnings surprise with near-certainty; the biggest uncertainty is the price half, which it cannot see — the roe and frozen-female price is set in annual negotiations with Japanese buyers that can stall or reprice (Undercurrent, 17 March 2026: "Japan price talks stall"), and realised results are further muddied by inventory-timing, ISK/EUR translation, the 2025 veiðigjald fishing-fee hike, and one-offs. Second-order risks: the Gagnaveita application could be denied or throttled, forcing reliance on the slower free public tables; landings may post with a several-day rather than same-day lag; and a 2-4 day settlement on a 1% average daily volume constraint means a fast pre-print repricing could leave you unable to build the position at all.

## 23. The Checkride Census

**Arena:** financial-market (US equities: SkyWest SKYW primary; DAL/UAL/LUV/ALGT and JETS as the sector leg; SKYW earnings options as the levered expression)
**Domain:** Aviation economics & airline capacity. RULES: institutions not individuals; sanctioned collection only

- **Data source:** The FAA Airmen Certification Branch "Releasable Airman Directory" monthly bulk file — specifically PILOT_CERT.csv, which carries 99 wide TYPERATING columns per airman (A/B-737, A/A-320, A/BD500 [=A220], A/CL-65 [=CRJ], A/ERJ-170 [=E175], A/EMB-145, etc.), keyed by UNIQUE ID, plus PILOT_BASIC.csv with CITY/STATE/ZIP and MED CLASS / MED EXP DATE. A type rating is the legal prerequisite to fly a given airliner, and adding one is a discrete FAA-recorded event. The file has NO per-rating issue date, and the FAA retains only the current and prior month on the server — so the only way to date a type rating is to diff consecutive monthly snapshots, and the historical series does not exist anywhere unless you built it yourself. I confirmed all of this by pulling and diffing the real files, not by reading about them.
- **How to collect it:** Direct download: https://registry.faa.gov/database/CS{MM}{YYYY}.zip (e.g. CS082026.zip, ~57 MB; the fixed-width twin is FX{MM}{YYYY}.zip). Landing page: faa.gov/licenses_certificates/airmen_certification/releasable_airmen_download. Gotcha I hit and solved: Akamai 403s a bare curl — you must send a browser User-Agent and a faa.gov Referer. Verified live: CS082026.zip and CS072026.zip download fine; CS062026.zip is already a 404 HTML page, confirming the ~2-month retention window. Pipeline: cron a pull on the 1st-3rd of each month (FAA states "uploaded Aug 3, next upload Sep 1"), unzip PILOT_CERT.csv (119 MB, 681k rows; 419k with TYPE='P') and PILOT_BASIC.csv, build {UNIQUE_ID -> set(TYPERATING1..99)}, and diff against the prior snapshot. My actual Jul->Aug 2026 diff produced: A/B-737 +268, A/A-320 +167, A/CL-65 +58, A/ERJ-170 +57, A/BD500 (A220) +42, A/EMB-145 +37, A/B-787 +52; 201 pilots upgraded C->A (new ATPs); 165 pilots who already held a regional type newly added a mainline type. Attribution works: of 42 new A220 ratings, 10 were Minnesota residents — the A220 is essentially Delta/JetBlue/Breeze only, and MSP is Delta's A220 base. New A/CL-65 clustered UT/MN/NC (SkyWest SLC, Endeavor MSP, PSA CLT); new A/ERJ-170 clustered WA/OR/NC/UT (Horizon, SkyWest, Envoy/PSA). Bonus channel in the same file: 128,850 airmen hold a first-class medical with a populated expiry date — diffing renewals vs. lapses is an independent read on professional-pilot attrition and age-65 retirements.
- **The bet / trade:** PRIMARY: SkyWest (SKYW) common, directional into quarterly earnings. Signal = 3-month rolling "net regional captain pipeline" = (new A/CL-65 + A/ERJ-170 + A/EMB-145 type ratings) minus (holders of those types who newly added A/B-737, A/A-320, A/BD500, A/B-787, A/A-330 — i.e. confirmed mainline defections). Long SKYW when the net pipeline is expanding >15% YoY; short or flat when contracting >15%. Enter within a week of the file drop that completes the quarter (e.g. the Oct 1 file completes Q3), hold through the late-October print, exit 1-2 sessions after. ~4 fires/year. Levered version: 30-45 DTE directional calls/puts or a skewed call spread over the print, given SKYW's habitual 8-15% earnings gaps. SECONDARY, 1-2 quarter horizon: the mainline gauge (3-month new A/B-737 + A/A-320 + A/BD500 + A/B-787) as a 2-4 quarter leading indicator of domestic ASM growth — express as long JETS / short-the-index when the gauge is contracting (capacity discipline = RASM upside) and the reverse when it is surging (a training surge today is an ASM glut in 9 months). TERTIARY, event-driven: a sustained collapse in A/EMB-145 issuance flags accelerated ERJ retirement and small-city de-hubbing; a surge in A/BD500 flags a Delta/JetBlue A220 utilization ramp.
- **Mechanism:** Regional airline revenue is block hours flown x contracted rate. Block hours are gated by CAPTAINS, not by aircraft — SkyWest has repeatedly parked aircraft it owned because it lacked qualified left-seaters, and its own 10-Q forward-looking language names "captain staffing" and "pilot attrition trends" as the determinants of expected production levels. A captain must hold an ATP plus that fleet's type rating; that rating is issued via a checkride and lands in this file. So: type ratings issued in fleet F -> line-qualified crews 4-12 weeks later (IOE, base award) -> block hours next quarter -> revenue and margin. The drain side sits in the same file at the same granularity: a pilot who held A/CL-65 last month and holds A/B-737 this month has been hired by a mainline and will be gone from the regional within weeks. So you observe supply and attrition as two sides of one individual-level ledger — 165 such crossings in a single month in my test. Upstream of that, mainline narrowbody type-rating volume is the faucet that sets regional attrition 2-4 quarters out and mainline domestic ASM growth 3-4 quarters out, because airlines train to a plan they set 6-9 months before the schedule loads.
- **Who's on the other side:** Sell-side airline analysts and long-only funds who model capacity from OAG/Cirium SSIM schedule files plus management guidance. Schedules tell you what a carrier INTENDS to fly; nothing public tells you whether it has the crews to actually operate it — which is why completion factor and block-hour production have blown through guidance in both directions repeatedly since 2022. Their only crew-supply input is qualitative color on the quarterly call, which is exactly the thing this file front-runs. The alt-data industry misses it for a structural reason: the mainstream workforce products (Revelio, LinkUp, job-posting scrapers) measure DEMAND for pilots — openings, class announcements — not certificated SUPPLY, and openings routinely fail to convert. The specialist pilot-supply shops (FAPA, Future & Active Pilot Advisors, university programs) sell to pilots as career guidance, are built on announced hiring targets, and produce nothing back-testable. Meanwhile the file itself is hostile to casual use: 57 MB monthly zip, 99 sparse wide columns of fixed-width-padded rating codes, Akamai-blocked to naive scrapers, no rating dates, and the archive overwritten monthly.
- **Information lead:** Two clocks roughly cancel and then leave a large gap. FAA record-processing lag (checkride -> database) is on the order of 3-8 weeks; training-to-revenue-flying is 4-12 weeks. Net, you observe the crew supply for quarter Q at approximately the start of Q. The market learns it: (a) at the earnings print, 6-14 weeks later — the file that landed Aug 3, 2026 covers crews that fly Q3, and SKYW reports Q3 in late October, so ~11-12 weeks of lead on the single line item that drives the print; (b) in DOT T-100 block-hour actuals, 4-6 months later; (c) never, in any independent form. There is no substitute series: FAA publishes U.S. Civil Airmen Statistics ANNUALLY and it contains no type-rating breakdown whatsoever. On the mainline gauge the lead is longer still — a training surge is visible 2-4 quarters before the ASMs show up in reported capacity, and before the carrier's own guidance reflects it. And there is a hard structural moat on top of the lead: because the FAA retains only two months, anyone starting today begins with zero history and cannot buy it, so the lead compounds for whoever archives first.
- **Why still under-exploited:** Three compounding frictions. (1) The FAA's own published statistics are annual and omit type ratings entirely, so the number simply is not reported by anyone. (2) The bulk file has no per-rating issue date — the flow is only recoverable as a longitudinal diff, which requires having thought to archive it before you needed it, and the FAA deletes the archive every month (I verified June 2026 is already gone). No vendor sells the history because the history was never retained. (3) The visible ecosystem around this file is HR credential-verification lookup tools — AviatorDB, AviationDB, AviationDepth — which serve one-airman-at-a-time queries to recruiters and flight schools and produce no aggregates. None of the mainstream financial alt-data catalogs carry it. It is not a product; it is a 57 MB zip behind a bot filter that has to be diffed by hand, which is exactly the profile of an under-exploited source.
- **Rough +EV:** Sizing the noise honestly: monthly per-fleet counts are small (42-268 in my live diff), so everything runs on 3-month rolling sums. A ~350-count trailing regional pipeline carries roughly Poisson sampling noise of +/-19 (~5%), so a 15-20% YoY swing is comfortably outside noise — the signal has real resolution, but only at quarterly frequency, not monthly. Expected edge: SKYW earnings moves are typically 8-15%. If the net-pipeline gauge calls the direction of the production/block-hour surprise ~60-63% of the time versus a 50% baseline, a directional equity position sized to catch ~10% of gross move yields roughly 2-3% per trade x 4 prints = 8-12% annualized on deployed capital before costs, with wide dispersion; the options overlay could 2-3x that with proportionally worse drawdowns. The sector leg (mainline gauge -> ASM growth -> RASM) fires 1-2 times a year and is lower-hit-rate but larger when it works, since it is a call on the capacity cycle turning. Realistic characterization: a standalone Sharpe in the 0.3-0.5 range, but with near-zero correlation to card-spend, schedule, and web-traffic signals — its real value is as an independent overlay/tiebreaker inside an airline book rather than a strategy on its own. Sunk cost to build: one afternoon of scripting plus ~700 MB/month of storage; the binding cost is 12-18 months of patience while the history accumulates.
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM. The data is verified real, free, bulk-downloadable, and the diff mechanically works — I ran it and the A220-to-Minnesota clustering confirms airline attribution is genuinely recoverable, which was the part I most doubted. The single biggest uncertainty is the FAA's record-update lag from checkride to database: it is undocumented, plausibly batch-processed at the Oklahoma City registry, and — critically — may be VARIABLE rather than merely long. A lag that wanders by 3-4 weeks quarter to quarter would smear the signal across quarter boundaries and destroy the earnings alignment that the whole primary trade depends on. That is empirically measurable, but only by collecting 12-18 months of snapshots and regressing the type-rating flow against SkyWest's reported block hours to fit the true lag distribution — which means you cannot validate this before committing to the archiving discipline. Secondary risks: airline attribution is inferential (fleet code plus home-state clustering), so a regional signal read as SkyWest may in fact be Endeavor or PSA; and airmen who file the Airman Release Form to suppress address release create a coverage hole of unknown and possibly drifting size, which is why the trade must key off the change, never the level.

## 24. The Rule 24 Shadow

**Arena:** financial-market (US small/mid-cap equity single-name shorts and listed puts; HY bonds/CDS for levered names)
**Domain:** Court dockets & legal outcome handicapping. RULES: institutions not individuals; sanctioned collection only (PACER/CourtListener/official dockets)

- **Data source:** Docket entries in *private* federal civil lawsuits filed by the United States as a NON-party — i.e., DOJ Antitrust Division or a U.S. Attorney's Office moving under Fed. R. Civ. P. 24(b) to "intervene and stay (or partially stay) discovery" to protect an ongoing, otherwise-secret grand jury investigation. The high-value payload is not the headline but the motion's scope: it names which district's grand jury is sitting and which specific defendants' discovery is frozen — i.e., DOJ's target list — plus the requested stay length and every subsequent extension motion, which together form a public clock on the grand jury's life. Second tier of the same screen: "Notice of Related Criminal Proceeding," "In re Grand Jury Subpoena to [Company]" miscellaneous (mc) dockets, and SEC/CFTC/FTC intervention-and-stay motions in private suits.
- **How to collect it:** Free, no PACER login required for detection: ~173 federal ECF instances publish a public CM/ECF RSS feed of new docket entries at https://ecf.&lt;court&gt;.uscourts.gov/cgi-bin/rss_outside.pl (~20-minute refresh, 24-hour lookback, sealed activity excluded). The authoritative list of which courts have feeds and their scope is at https://pacer.uscourts.gov/psco/cgi-bin/links.pl. Pipeline: (1) enumerate feeds, poll every 15 min; (2) regex docket text for /United States|USA/ AND /interven/ AND /stay/, plus /grand jury/, /Notice of Related Criminal/; (3) for the courts whose feed scope is orders-only, backstop with CourtListener's RECAP Search API (v4, type=r full-text over docket-entry text) and CourtListener docket alerts placed on every open cv docket with NOS 410 (antitrust) / 850 (securities) / 370 (fraud) that names a US-listed defendant; (4) on a hit, pull the motion PDF from PACER for a few dollars and parse the stay's party scope and duration; (5) map defendant captions to tickers. Backtest set is buildable the same way: CourtListener full-text search over 2010–2026 district dockets for "intervene" + "stay discovery" filtered to NOS 410. Verified live examples: In re Broiler Chicken Antitrust Litig. (N.D. Ill.), DOJ motion filed 6/21/2019; In re PVC Pipe Antitrust Litig. (N.D. Ill.), DOJ motion filed 10/7/2025, granted 10/10/2025, extensions still being litigated in July 2026.
- **The bet / trade:** Short the common / buy 9–12 month puts ~15–25% OTM on each US-listed defendant whose discovery the DOJ stay explicitly covers, weighted hardest toward names where (a) the cartel product is &gt;25% of revenue, (b) market cap &lt; $10B, and (c) no grand jury subpoena has yet appeared in the company's SEC filings. For levered names, buy CDS / short the HY bonds instead. Holding horizon 6–18 months, rolled at each DOJ stay-extension motion (each extension = grand jury still alive = keep the position on). Pair leg: when one defendant on the same docket settles early and cheaply with a cooperation clause, that is almost certainly the ACPERA leniency applicant — cover/go long that one (single damages, no criminal fine) and press the rest. Critical exit rule: close at the indictment/plea headline, do not hold through it. Pilgrim's Pride fell 13% on indictment day (6/3/2020) and 44% across 2020, but *rallied* on the 10/2020 guilty plea because the $110.5M fine cleared the overhang.
- **Mechanism:** DOJ Antitrust does not spend the institutional cost of a public Rule 24 intervention unless a grand jury is already sitting on real document productions — it is a costly, self-verifying signal. Private antitrust complaints are cheap and mostly die on Twombly; the DOJ intervention is the single best public discriminator between a lawyer-driven fishing complaint and a real cartel. If the cartel is real, four things follow on a datable schedule: (1) the defendants' historical margins during the conspiracy window contain an overcharge, so consensus forward margins — modeled off cartel-era comps — are structurally too high, which is a permanent derate, not a one-time fine; (2) customers who read the same docket begin renegotiating price, hitting revenue before any legal outcome; (3) a cascade of dated catalysts arrives — 10-Q disclosure of the subpoena, officer departures, indictments, pleas, class certification, and in antitrust the opt-out claims from large direct purchasers, which routinely dwarf the class settlement; (4) treble damages plus joint-and-several liability mean the last non-settling defendant can owe the whole overcharge. The stay-extension cadence is itself a timing instrument: DOJ asks for six months and renews; when renewals stop, charges or closure are imminent, which lets you pick option expiries instead of guessing.
- **Who's on the other side:** Sell-side equity analysts and quant funds, who do not read district court dockets and get no Bloomberg headline when a DOJ trial attorney files a Rule 24 motion in a mid-cap's civil case. Index and passive holders react to nothing. The people who see it within minutes — the antitrust defense bar, plaintiffs' firms, MLex and Law360 subscribers — are lawyers barred by their own firms' personal-trading policies and by MNPI concerns from acting on it, and their coverage sits behind legal-vertical paywalls no PM reads. The issuer itself is a slow, conflicted counterparty: materiality judgment lets a company sit on a grand jury subpoena for several quarters (Otter Tail received its PVC subpoena in August 2024), and its first public framing is usually "we have not been named a target." Retail and momentum flow keeps buying a name whose reported margins still look excellent — precisely because the overcharge is still in them.
- **Information lead:** Three separable leads. (1) Latency: the docket entry is public in the free RSS feed within ~20 minutes; for a mid-cap defendant no wire ever covers it, and legal-trade press covers it a day later at best — hours to permanently. (2) Versus company disclosure: typically one to three quarters, and in the cleanest cases the DOJ motion is the *first public evidence anywhere* that a grand jury exists — broiler chicken was exactly this, the 6/21/2019 motion being how the world learned of the poultry grand jury. (3) Versus the market-moving event: broiler chicken ran 6/21/2019 intervention → 6/3/2020 first indictments, an 11.5-month lead on a -13% single-day, -44% annual move. Separately, the motion's party-scope — which defendants are frozen and which are not — is functionally never reported in the press and is a target list you cannot get any other way.
- **Why still under-exploited:** Structurally invisible to every existing tool. Legal-analytics products (Bloomberg Law, Lex Machina, Docket Alarm) are keyed to party-name and nature-of-suit fields and sold per-seat to law firms — but here the United States is a NON-party, appearing only inside free-text docket entries and as a movant, so it never shows up in a party-name screen or an NOS filter. No financial data vendor resells CM/ECF RSS at all; the feeds are an obscure court-IT artifact that Free Law Project scrapes for a nonprofit archive, not a commercial signal. The event frequency — single digits to low tens per year — is far too low for a vendor to productize but ideal for a concentrated discretionary short book. And the last mile nobody has built is the mapping from private antitrust docket captions ("Diamond Plastics," "Prime Conduit," "Claxton Poultry") to the handful of listed parents and tickers inside a sprawling defendant list. The known consumers of this information are lawyers who are contractually and ethically prohibited from trading it.
- **Rough +EV:** Roughly 5–12 qualifying DOJ intervention-and-stay events per year with at least one US-listed defendant (ATR cartel cases plus USAO fraud interventions), yielding ~15–30 tradable tickers. Conditional on an ATR intervention, charges or a &gt;$50M resolution follow in an estimated 60–70% of matters. Equity impact is highly dispersed: large diversified defendants where the cartel product is a small revenue slice (Tyson, Westlake) barely move. Filtered to the concentrated-exposure, sub-$10B, not-yet-disclosed subset, I estimate a median 6–18 month abnormal return of -15% to -20% on the short leg with a ~60% hit rate and a fat left tail (-30% to -50%: Pilgrim's Pride, Lannett/Akorn in the generic-drug matter, Eargo-type names). Expressed as 9–12 month puts at 1–2% notional per name across 15–25 positions, that is roughly 150–400 bps/yr of alpha on a $50–200M book — real but hard-capped by event frequency and by the small-cap borrow and option liquidity in exactly the names that work best. The ACPERA leniency-applicant pairs leg adds a second, lower-variance return stream at essentially no incremental research cost.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Biggest uncertainty is sample size and selection contamination: the historical event set is only a dozen or so clean analogs (broiler chicken, generic drugs, canned tuna, capacitors, ready-mix, PVC pipe), and in most of them at least one defendant had already disclosed a grand jury subpoena before DOJ intervened — so the intervention was confirmation, not revelation, and the -15% median is estimated from a regime where the true "first public revelation" subset may be only one to three events per year. That makes the strategy's real capacity and its true edge both hard to pin down until the screen has run live for a few years. Secondary risks: some courts' RSS feeds carry orders only, so you catch the ORDER granting the stay a few days after the motion rather than the motion itself; sealed or ex parte interventions never appear in RSS at all; and the biggest defendants often absorb a cartel finding with no lasting derate, so position selection — not detection — is where the strategy actually lives or dies.

## 25. The Item 21 Census

**Arena:** financial-market
**Domain:** Education & demographic institutions. RULES: institutions not individuals; sanctioned collection only

- **Data source:** USAC E-Rate "Item 21" line-item filings — the itemized bill of materials that every US school district and library must file with the FCC's Universal Service Administrative Company to get its network gear subsidized. Each certified FCC Form 471 line item carries the equipment MAKE, MODEL/part number, QUANTITY, and UNIT COST; the linked FRN Status record carries the winning reseller (SPIN name), the number of competing bids, the pre-discount contract value, the funding-commitment (FCDL) date, and the service-delivery deadline. In aggregate this is a SKU-level, arm's-length transaction census of ~$2.4B/yr of US K-12 enterprise networking — 793,837 access points and 271,536 switches in funding year 2026 alone — with realized street prices per part number. The forward leg is the Form 470 (the district's public RFP), which carries requested manufacturer, function and quantity, plus attached RFP documents, and is filed 8-14 months before shipment. ## 26. The EM_WASTE Ledger

**Arena:** financial-market
**Domain:** Defense & government procurement. RULES: institutions not individuals; sanctioned collection only

- **Data source:** EPA e-Manifest — the national, per-shipment hazardous waste manifest record set inside RCRAInfo (launched June 2018, now covering electronic AND scanned paper manifests). Every shipment carries: generator EPA handler ID, designated receiving facility, shipped/received dates, and per-waste-line container counts, QUANTITY + units, federal/state waste codes, and management method (tables EM_MANIFEST, EM_WASTE, EM_WASTE_CODE, EM_TRANSPORTER, EM_REJECTION, EM_IMPORT, EM_PCB). Refreshed WEEKLY; each manifest becomes public 90 days after receipt at the receiving facility. Used as a per-plant physical production meter for defense manufacturing sites. Verified anchor: Aerojet Rocketdyne, East Camden AR = EPA ID ARD091688283, Large Quantity Generator.
- **How to collect it:** (1) Bulk: https://rcrapublic.epa.gov/rcra-public-export/?outputType=CSV (and ?outputType=Fixed) — EPA's public export generator, weekly refresh, 8 relational tables joined on MANIFEST_TRACKING_NUMBER. Note it 302s into the JS app at https://rcrapublic.epa.gov/rcra-hwip/ (I tested it — curl alone lands on the app shell), so drive it with a headless browser/session cookie. (2) API: RCRAInfo RESTful API, free key via rcrainfo.epa.gov; docs at https://usepa.github.io/e-manifest and github.com/USEPA/e-manifest. Public manifest search UI at rcrapublic.epa.gov/rcra-hwip. (3) Handler→site mapping: ECHO bulk downloads (echo.epa.gov/tools/data-downloads) — RCRA_FACILITIES.csv, RCRA_NAICS.csv (verified these file names exist). (4) Site→parent-company mapping: EPA Facility Registry Service parent-org fields, cross-checked against 10-K Item 2 property lists, Army GOCO plant operator rosters, and DoD facility lists. (5) State mirrors with extra detail for CA sites: DTSC Hazardous Waste Tracking System, hwts.dtsc.ca.gov. Build once: a ticker↔EPA-handler-ID crosswalk for ~40 US-listed defense manufacturers and their ~250 production sites. That crosswalk is the whole moat.
- **The bet / trade:** Core: monthly-rebalanced cross-sectional long/short equity book over ~40 US-listed defense manufacturers, ranked on trailing-3-month change in PROCESS-waste manifest frequency and tonnage across each name's mapped EPA IDs, deseasonalized against a 3-year site baseline. Long top decile, short bottom decile (or short ITA as the hedge), 1-quarter holds, upsized into earnings. Event overlay A (long): when a newly commissioned plant's EPA handler ID starts issuing process-waste manifests, buy 9-12 month calls on the parent — live candidates: L3Harris (LHX) on Aerojet SRM lines at Camden AR / Orange VA / Huntsville; Olin (OLN) on Lake City AAP; General Dynamics (GD) on OTS Mesquite TX and Scranton 155mm. Event overlay B (short): mapped-site process-waste shipment frequency down >40% y/y for two consecutive quarters → 6-month puts, held through the next two prints. Also works on Huntington Ingalls (HII) for Newport News/Ingalls throughput, where the market is repeatedly blindsided by shipyard productivity.
- **Mechanism:** Hazardous waste is a near-stoichiometric byproduct of the physical operations that generate defense revenue: degreasing/cleaning solvents (F001–F005), plating and metal-finishing sludge (F006 — cartridge cases, projectile rotating bands, landing gear), spent acids and caustics (D002), reactive process residues (D003), chromate/paint booth waste, and spent blast media. Manifest count and tonnage per site scale with machine-hours and units produced. Two asymmetries make it lead reported revenue: (a) during a rate ramp, qualification lots, scrap, and rate-tooling generate waste with NO deliverable attached, so waste rises before revenue is recognized; (b) RCRA accumulation limits force a Large Quantity Generator to ship at least every 90 days, giving the series a mechanical floor frequency — when a line idles, the shipment cadence visibly breaks, which is a cleaner signal than tonnage. So: waste flow → machine-hours → units delivered → segment revenue and backlog burn.
- **Who's on the other side:** Sell-side and long-only defense analysts model production from MONEY, not metal: appropriations, P-1/R-1 quantities, DoD 5pm contract announcements, and reported backlog. The entire 2023–2026 munitions and missile story is an execution gap between dollars appropriated and rounds delivered, and there is no public high-frequency production series to close it — so the market re-prices only at quarterly prints and at ribbon-cuttings. Quant alt-data shops ingest FPDS/USAspending, satellite, card, web traffic, and shipping AIS; nobody ingests EPA RCRA compliance telemetry. The natural owners of this data are EHS compliance software vendors (Encamp, VelocityEHS, ERA Environmental) selling to plant environmental managers, and ESG risk raters (MSCI, RepRisk, Sustainalytics) who convert it into violations scores — both use it as a compliance/risk artifact, never as a production index. Defense-specific alt-data specialists (Govini, GovWin, Bloomberg Government) live entirely inside the procurement data ecosystem and never leave it.
- **Information lead:** Four different leads. (1) Facility-level utilization: never disclosed by anyone, ever — permanent lead. (2) Vs. the quarterly segment print: the 90-day publication rule plus weekly refresh means roughly two of the three months of a quarter are readable before the company reports. (3) Vs. "is the new line actually running at rate": 2–4 quarters ahead of revenue recognition and typically 6–12 months ahead of management confirming a rate, because companies press-release the ribbon-cutting and then go silent on throughput. (4) Vs. a line going dark: visible ~3–5 months after the last shipment, still usually 1–2 quarters before the program wind-down is disclosed or shows in guidance.
- **Why still under-exploited:** e-Manifest is only ~8 years old and only became genuinely national once paper manifests were folded in. It ships as multi-gigabyte weekly CSV/fixed-width dumps across 8 relational tables keyed on EPA handler IDs with free-text handler names, no ticker or parent-company field, and no NAICS-to-program mapping — the join work is brutal and manual. The bulk export URL redirects into a JavaScript app rather than serving a file, so it isn't trivially scriptable (I confirmed this). Signal extraction additionally requires knowing RCRA waste-code semantics well enough to separate process waste from remediation waste. And the two constituencies who already hold the data — EHS compliance vendors and ESG raters — have zero incentive to build a defense-production nowcast. There is no commercial product that maps EPA handler IDs to defense tickers.
- **Rough +EV:** Cross-sectional L/S: if site process-waste flow correlates ~0.35–0.50 with true site production activity and mapped sites cover 40–70% of a name's manufacturing footprint, expect roughly 1.5–3% per quarter on the decile spread across ~40 names, rebalanced monthly. It decays fast on diversified megacaps and is strongest where 1–3 plants are most of the business. The real EV sits in the event overlay: expect 3–8 qualifying "new line lights up" or "line goes dark" signals per year across the universe, each worth a 10–30% move over 6–12 months when right; at 1–2% of book per event in 9–12 month options with a ~40–50% hit rate, that plausibly adds 4–8% annually. Total: a modest but genuinely uncorrelated 6–11%/yr sleeve, capacity-limited by small/mid-cap liquidity. Setup cost is one quarter of crosswalk-building and backtesting against 2019–2025 reported segment revenue.
- **Confidence:** Medium-low. The source, the fields, the 90-day rule, and the weekly refresh are all verified real and pullable. The uncertainty is signal-to-noise. Two specific hazards: (1) energetics and propellant plants destroy most reactive waste ON-SITE via RCRA-permitted open burn/open detonation — Aerojet's Camden site explicitly "treats waste energetic materials on-site" — so those streams never generate a manifest at all; the signal is therefore far stronger for metal-parts, plating, airframe, electronics and shipyard operations than for nitration and propellant mixing, and I would not build the thesis on Radford/Holston-type sites. (2) Environmental remediation digs generate enormous manifest volumes completely uncorrelated with production and would produce false longs, so you must filter to process waste codes (F001–F006, D001–D003, U/P-listed) while dropping F039, contaminated-soil lines, and landfill-destined shipments, and weight shipment FREQUENCY over raw tonnage. If the backtest against 2019–2025 reported segment revenue shows correlation below ~0.3 after those filters, the cross-sectional book is dead and only the discrete new-line/dark-line event overlay survives.

## 27. The Rendition Ladder

**Arena:** financial-market
**Domain:** Data centers, power & AI infrastructure buildout. RULES: institutions not individuals; sanctioned collection only

- **Data source:** County business-personal-property (BPP) appraisal rolls — the annual, parcel-and-owner-level assessed value of *taxable business equipment* (servers, GPUs, switchgear, UPS) sitting inside named data center buildings as of January 1. Two things nobody in markets reads: (1) the **account-level roll**, which lists the *equipment owner's* name, the situs address, and the appraised value — and because BPP is taxed to whoever owns the gear, not the landlord, colocation **tenants appear by name at the colo's address**, de-anonymizing hyperscale colo tenancy and sizing each tenant's hardware in dollars; and (2) the **published depreciation ladders** the assessors apply. Loudoun County's 2026 "Data Center Equipment (DE)" schedule is 60% / 45% / 30% / 15% / 10% / 5% of original capitalized cost for acquisition years 2025 / 2024 / 2023 / 2022 / 2021 / 2020-and-prior. Because the ladder is published and invertible, a few consecutive years of assessed values per account solve back to the **original-cost vector by vintage** — i.e., the dollars of AI hardware installed in that specific building by that specific owner, in that specific year. New for tax year 2026, Loudoun explicitly requires taxpayers to "summarize and report the total capitalized costs by year of acquisition," which sharpens the inversion further. Scale check: Loudoun's FY2026 computer-equipment personal property tax is ~$1.37B at $4.15/$100, implying roughly $33B of *assessed* value and, run back up the ladder, on the order of $80-110B of original-cost IT equipment in one county — a bottom-up installed-base number that exists nowhere in any company filing.
- **How to collect it:** All public records, pulled from official portals — no scraping of anything gated. **Texas (roll is public even though the rendition document is confidential under Tax Code §22.27):** Harris CAD bulk "Real & Personal Property Database" text files at hcad.org/pdata/pdata-property-downloads.html; Collin CAD's Open Data Portal (Texas.gov); Johnson CAD's downloadable BPP preliminary appraisal file; Travis (traviscad.org/propertysearch), Tarrant, Dallas, Bexar, Ellis, Hood, Taylor (Abilene/Stargate) via each CAD's export or records request. Notices of appraised value mail mid-April to mid-May and post to CAD sites immediately; certified roll July 25. **Georgia:** qPublic/Beacon (qpublic.schneidercorp.com) "Personal Property Records Search" by owner name — Douglas, Coweta, Fulton, Fayette, Bartow, Newton, Walton, Bulloch, Richmond; PT-50P returns due April 1, notices May-June. **Virginia:** Loudoun's published BPP assessment schedules at loudoun.gov/6301 (the ladder itself), plus Loudoun and Prince William budget/CAFR documents for aggregate computer-equipment assessed value; returns due May 1. **Also:** Maricopa County AZ assessor BPP; Washoe/Clark NV; NC county tax portals; and Business Oregon's annual enterprise-zone report, which names the firm and the abated assessed value. The real work — and the moat — is entity resolution: mapping shell LLCs to operators (Amazon's Vadata, Meta's animal-named LLCs, etc.) using Secretary of State registrations, county deed grantee names, and FCC ULS license filings, which carry the licensee name plus exact site coordinates. Roughly two to four months of one person's time to stand up ~40 county pipelines and the LLC crosswalk.
- **The bet / trade:** Long/short pair trade inside the AI-hosting complex, sized off measured installed-equipment dollars versus each company's claimed deployed/energized capacity. Enter as spring appraisal notices land (mid-April through late July), hold through the Q2 (August) and Q3 (November) prints — a 3-6 month horizon. Short the names whose Jan-1 measured BPP at their own named campuses runs materially below what their "contracted and energized MW" narrative implies; long those running ahead. Universe: CRWV, APLD, IREN, NBIS, CIFR, WULF, GLXY, plus ORCL's OCI sites and the colo REITs DLR/EQIX. Second leg, higher-conviction and more asymmetric: when a securitized data center building's *tenant-owned* equipment value collapses year over year ahead of a lease expiry, that is hard early evidence of churn — fade the junior tranches of that data center ABS/CMBS deal, or short the landlord. Third leg, macro: sum computer-equipment assessed value across the top ~40 data center counties into a bottom-up "installed US AI hardware dollars" series and compare it against cumulative Nvidia/Broadcom US datacenter revenue; a widening gap means chips shipped but not installed (power-bottlenecked or sitting in inventory) and argues for fading NVDA/AVGO into the next print, a narrowing gap the reverse.
- **Mechanism:** Property tax is assessed on the owner of tangible equipment at its physical situs on January 1, at cost less a published depreciation schedule. So the assessed value of a BPP account is, by arithmetic rather than inference, a depreciated dollar count of the hardware physically installed in that building. Three consequences fall out. First, because the taxpayer is the *equipment owner* and not the building owner, the roll names which company's GPUs sit in which colocation hall — the single most closely guarded fact in the industry. Second, because the depreciation ladder is published and steep in the early years (60/45/30/15/10/5), year-over-year change in assessed value decomposes cleanly into new capital installed minus computable roll-off of the existing stack; new money per site per year drops straight out. Third, a *decline* in an account's value beyond scheduled depreciation means gear physically left the building — the first hard evidence of decommissioning or a tenant walking, months before it shows up in occupancy or revenue. Chained together: installed dollars imply installed MW, which implies revenue capacity, which is exactly the quantity the market is arguing about and can only guess at from disclosure.
- **Who's on the other side:** Sell-side analysts modeling AI deployment work from company disclosure plus supply-chain checks — Taiwan ODM monthly revenue, TSMC CoWoS allocation, hyperscaler capex guidance. All of those measure *shipments*, not *installations by owner at a location*. The gap between shipped and installed is precisely what the market has repeatedly mispriced through the power-constrained-deployment debate. Colo REIT analysts are structurally blind here: DLR and EQIX never name tenants, and data center securitization documents disclose tenant credit *buckets*, never identities. The elegant part is who does know: property tax consultancies (Ryan, Altus, DMA, ITC Tax) see this data daily — but they are fee-for-service appeal shops bound by client confidentiality and have no vehicle or legal room to monetize it as market data. The people with the information are contractually barred from trading it; the people who trade don't know the dataset exists. Meanwhile the assessors publish it because statute compels them, in 300-plus mutually incompatible portals, under shell-LLC owner names, with inconsistent personal-property schemas. Fragmentation, not secrecy, is what keeps it cheap.
- **Information lead:** Two different leads. On the **cross-sectional facts** — which operator owns how many dollars of hardware in which specific building — the lead is effectively unbounded, because these facts are never disclosed through any official channel at all. Nvidia does not publish end-customer allocation; hyperscalers do not publish per-site capex; colos do not publish tenants. You are not early, you are the only one holding the number. On the **level and trajectory**, the valuation date is January 1 and the data surfaces April through July, so you hold a 4-7 month-old but 100%-precise measurement roughly one to two quarters before it becomes inferable from utility load disclosures, occupancy commentary and company reporting. A useful bonus channel: many CADs create new BPP accounts mid-year as businesses register, so a new taxpayer name appearing at a campus address flags a tenant move-in well ahead of the annual roll.
- **Why still under-exploited:** There is no commercial product for this. CoStar, Attom and the real-estate data vendors cover *real* property — land and buildings — not business personal property, which is a separate roll, a separate filing regime, and a separate schema in every county. The alt-data shops that could build it (YipitData, M Science) work from transactions, app telemetry and web exhaust, not tax rolls. The property-tax firms that do hold it are confidentiality-bound service providers, not data sellers. And the assembly cost is genuinely ugly in exactly the way that deters funded competitors: forty-plus county portals with no common API, owner names that are deliberately opaque shell LLCs, jurisdictions that abate or exempt in different ways, and no ground truth to validate against. It is manual, unglamorous, and legally clean — the classic profile of something real that stays cheap. It is also arriving at its moment: Loudoun's requirement, brand new for tax year 2026, that taxpayers report capitalized cost by year of acquisition means the largest data center market on earth is about to start producing vintage-resolved capex data on the public record.
- **Rough +EV:** Low-frequency, high-payoff — a scalpel, not a machine gun. The signal fires at full strength once a year on the spring roll, with partial mid-year updates from new account creation. Across a ~15-25 name universe, expect roughly five to eight situations annually where measured installed dollars diverge more than ~30% from the company's stated deployment narrative. In names this high-beta, a 10-25% move over the 3-6 month holding window on a correct divergence call is reasonable; haircutting for the ones that don't resolve, call it 4-8% expected edge per position on ~6 positions a year — a few hundred basis points of alpha on a dedicated sleeve, plus the occasional asymmetric hit (the genuinely empty building, or the tenant who quietly pulled their racks) worth 30-50% on a single position. The macro leg — the bottom-up installed-versus-shipped gap — is lower frequency still but potentially the most valuable output, since it bears directly on the largest sizing decision in the market. Setup cost is two to four months of one competent person; ongoing maintenance is light.
- **Confidence:** Medium. What is not in doubt: the data is public, the mechanism is arithmetic rather than inference, and every load-bearing element was verified — Loudoun publishes the exact 60/45/30/15/10/5 data center equipment ladder and ~$1.37B in FY2026 computer-equipment tax, Harris/Collin/Johnson CADs publish downloadable BPP roll files, Georgia counties expose personal-property search by owner via qPublic, and BPP is taxed to the equipment owner so colo tenants surface by name. The biggest uncertainty is **coverage and abatement opacity**: Ohio abolished business personal property tax outright, which blacks out the entire Columbus/New Albany cluster, and Iowa, Nebraska, Arizona and Washington exempt or heavily abate — so you likely see only 55-65% of US capacity, and where value is abated some districts publish only the reduced taxable figure rather than full appraised value. Close behind: GPUs at a neocloud site may be owned by the customer or held on an operating lease by a financier, which puts a different name on the account. That is partly a feature — it exposes financing structure — but it is also a live misattribution risk that will produce wrong answers until the entity crosswalk is mature. The annual cadence is the third real limitation; this will not carry a fast book.

## 28. The Spielbericht

**Arena:** gambling
**Domain:** Tennis, boxing/MMA & combat-sport betting. RULES: observe institutions, equipment, venues and official data — never individuals' private behavior; sanctioned collection only

- **Data source:** National tennis federation CLUB-LEAGUE match reports ("Spielberichte") — real, competitive, officially-scored singles and doubles matches played by ATP/WTA-ranked pros in domestic team leagues that are invisible to ATP/WTA/ITF records and therefore to every pricing model's form/fatigue features. Verified core: Germany's 1./2. Tennis-Bundesliga (10 clubs, 9 matchdays, 4 singles + 2 doubles per tie, best-of-3 with a 10-point deciding champions tiebreak). 2026 matchdays are Sundays 5/12/19/26 July and 2/9 Aug plus Fridays 31 Jul and 7 Aug — sitting directly on top of Hamburg/Bastad/Gstaad/Umag/Kitzbühel/Washington/Toronto and ~20 European Challengers. 2026 club rosters include Luciano Darderi (ATP 16), Valentin Vacherot (20), Arthur Rinderknech (28), Stefanos Tsitsipas (87); recent seasons featured Mensik, Griekspoor, Cobolli, Baez, Struff, Hanfmann. Parallel leagues: FFT Interclubs Pro A/Pro B + N1–N4 (France, May — mid clay season), FITP Serie A1/A2 (Italy, autumn), Czech Extraliga (autumn/winter), plus Austria, Swiss Interclub, Dutch Eredivisie, Belgium.
- **How to collect it:** All public, no login, sanctioned scraping of federation results pages. Germany: DTB's nuLiga portal at dtb.liga.nu (legacy WebObjects URLs, e.g. /cgi-bin/WebObjects/nuLigaTENDE.woa/wa/federationNews?federation=DTB) → Bundesligen → matchday → "Spielbericht", which gives the fielded lineup, player-vs-player scores and the date, with archives back to summer 2004; mirrored fixtures/results at sport.de/tennis/deutschland-dtb-bundesliga/spielplan/. France: Ten'Up / fft.fr "Interclubs seniors Pro A / Pro B" results. Italy: FITP Serie A1 portal. Czechia: Extraliga federation pages. Pipeline: (1) nightly diff each league portal for new Spielberichte; (2) fuzzy-join localized player names to ATP/WTA/ITF player IDs (this join is the real moat — names are rendered inconsistently and clubs use nicknames); (3) join to the tour calendar (ATP/Challenger/ITF entry lists + order of play) to find every tour match played within 96h of a club tie; (4) also diff the announced team sheet against who was actually fielded.
- **The bet / trade:** Three linked bets, all short-horizon, settling on a single tennis match. (A) LOAD FADE: lay/fade the player's moneyline and take the opponent on the games handicap in their next ATP-250/Challenger/ITF singles match when they played a club-league singles tie (stronger if singles + doubles) within 72 hours with a cross-border hop. Horizon 1–3 days. (B) INJURY TAPE: when a player is named on a published club team sheet and is not fielded, or retires/withdraws mid-tie, lay them on Betfair or take their opponent's ML in their next tour match — and take the "retirement/walkover" side where offered. Horizon 1–4 days. (C) COMEBACK CALIBRATION: for a player returning from a long layoff, price their comeback-tournament outright and first-round line off their club-tie results (did they beat a 250-ranked club pro cleanly, or lose to a 500?), taken in the 2–6 weeks before their sanctioned return.
- **Mechanism:** A Bundesliga Sunday tie is not an exhibition: it is a best-of-3 singles on clay against a ranked pro, frequently followed by a doubles rubber, plus a flight in and out — two to four hours of competitive load and a travel day, 24–72h before a Monday/Tuesday tour first round. Every book and every public model computes "days since last match", recent-form and fatigue features exclusively from ATP/WTA/ITF match history, so this load does not exist in their feature space. The break runs both ways: a model that flags a player as "rusty after 21 days off" may be looking at someone who played two competitive singles matches last weekend, and a model that flags a player as "fresh" may be looking at someone who played six sets on Sunday. The injury leg is cleaner physiology-free logic: a pro who took the club's appearance money and is then quietly not fielded, or retires in a rubber, has a physical problem the tour market cannot see. The comeback leg is pure evidence timing — the club tie is often the first competitive match a returning player plays, weeks before their sanctioned return, and it is scored and published.
- **Who's on the other side:** Books and syndicates pricing Challenger/ITF/ATP-250 markets from Elo plus official-feed features (Sportradar, Genius, ATP's Tennis Data Innovations). They are wrong for three structural reasons. (1) Club-league results never enter ATP/WTA/ITF official records, so they never enter any player rating; public Elo systems (Tennis Abstract, Ultimate Tennis Statistics) exclude them by construction. (2) Where a livescore feed does carry a league — Czech Extraliga and the German Bundesliga show up on Sofascore/BetsAPI — it is carried as a separate "exhibition" competition with its own player entities, never joined to the tour player ID, so no downstream model can consume it as a feature on a tour match. (3) The primary sources are German/French/Italian federation portals on 2004-era web frameworks with no API. Nobody funds that integration for markets with €500–3,000 limits, which is precisely why the edge persists.
- **Information lead:** Load signal: 12–72 hours of hard lead — nuLiga and sport.de post Sunday tie results the same afternoon, while books are opening and shaping Monday/Tuesday first-round lines that night. In practice the lead is effectively permanent, because the market never ingests the data at all rather than ingesting it late. Injury tape: you see a fielded-then-scratched or retired-mid-tie player on Sunday evening; the tour market typically learns at the on-site withdrawal or mid-match retirement, one to four days later. Comeback calibration: 2–6 weeks — the club tie is the first competitive evidence, and comeback-event prices and outrights are already posted the whole time.
- **Why still under-exploited:** No vendor sells it. Sportradar, Genius, ATP TDI, Tennis Abstract and Ultimate Tennis Statistics all define a player's match history as sanctioned ATP/WTA/ITF play; club-league results are outside that boundary by definition, so the "hidden match" problem is invisible even to people who model tennis fatigue for a living. Academic tennis-prediction work uses the same official histories. The raw data lives in eight-plus national federation portals, in four languages, behind fragmented HTML with no API and no consistent player identifier — the join to tour player IDs has to be built and maintained by hand. It is fragmented, non-English, unglamorous, and pays off in low-limit Challenger and ITF markets, which is exactly the profile of a source a large operator will never build and a determined individual can.
- **Rough +EV:** Universe: roughly 300–700 linkable observations per year (a ranked pro playing a club tie within 96h of a sanctioned tour match) across Germany in Jul–Aug, France in May, Italy/Czechia in Oct–Dec, plus Austria/Switzerland/Netherlands/Belgium. Load fade: I'd put the true effect at 1.5–3 percentage points of win probability for singles+doubles plus travel inside 48h; against Challenger/ITF margins of 4–7% that supports roughly 2–4% ROI on a filtered subset of ~200–400 bets/year at €300–2,000 limits, so €5–25k/yr. Injury tape: fires perhaps 40–80 times/year but is worth far more per event — an 8–25 point swing in win probability, and layable on Betfair rather than fighting book limits. Comeback calibration: maybe 10–20 usable situations/yr, but in outright markets with real liquidity. Realistic all-in for one person: low-to-mid five figures a year, scaling somewhat by adding leagues and by trading the same information in-play.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Biggest uncertainty by far is the size of the pure LOAD effect: club tennis is lower intensity than a tour match (doubles spends only ~a third of match time above 85% HRmax), elite pros recover fast, and a lot of Bundesliga singles are lopsided 55-minute wins. If the true load effect is under ~1 point of win probability, leg (A) does not clear the vig and the strategy collapses to the lower-frequency legs (B) and (C), which I believe in much more strongly. Second risk is the name-join: localized, inconsistently formatted federation data will produce false matches, and a naive backtest poisoned by bad joins will look better than reality — the join needs manual validation before any capital goes on. Third risk is selection bias: players who accept club money in a given week may systematically be the ones not in a tour main draw, so the "next match" sample is not random and needs careful conditioning.

## 29. The Slipped Slot

**Arena:** financial-market
**Domain:** Emerging-market macro & sovereign signals. RULES: institutions only; sanctioned collection

- **Data source:** The World Bank Group Board Calendar — a rolling PDF of the Executive Directors' forward work program that names, by country and operation title, every financing item scheduled for Board approval on a specific future date, including Development Policy Operations (DPO/DPF), the fast-disbursing budget-support loans that go straight into a sovereign's FX reserves. The Bank's own page states it covers "the current and the two following months" and that "all items in the Board calendar are tentative and subject to change" — meaning the schedule is revised between vintages, and the revisions are the signal. Verified live: the Jan-2026 vintage (doc 099723205142614094) runs Jan–Jun 2026 and names dated DPOs for Egypt (GROWTH 2 DPF, discussed "IBRD Regular" slot), Angola, Zambia, Morocco, Armenia, Kazakhstan, Bhutan, Rwanda, Seychelles, São Tomé, Niger, Benin, Philippines; the Jan–Dec 2025 vintage names 50+ policy operations including Ghana Second Resilient Recovery DPF, Ethiopia Second Sustainable and Inclusive DPO, Argentina Fiscal Sustainability DPF, Mauritania, DRC, Somalia, Guinea-Bissau, Ecuador, Cameroon, PNG, Madagascar, and Senegal's Fiscal Sustainability PforR. 273 calendar vintages exist in the documents portal. Second, independent channel: the Projects API exposes forward-dated `boardapprovaldate` on Pipeline-status projects — I pulled 275 future-dated entries today running from 2026-08-15 out to 2027. Third layer: the calendar distinguishes two approval tracks — "Board AOB" (approved without discussion; 68 blocks in the Jan-2026 vintage) versus a scheduled "Board Meeting / Regular" (contested or salient; 28 blocks) — so an operation migrating from AOB to Regular signals a shareholder objection.
- **How to collect it:** Three free, sanctioned, institution-published endpoints, polled daily and snapshotted for diffing. (1) Discover new calendar vintages: GET https://search.worldbank.org/api/v3/wds?format=json&qterm=%22Board%20Calendar%22&fl=docdt,display_title,pdfurl&rows=20&srt=docdt&order=desc — returns the PDF URL under documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/<id>/pdf/IDU-<hex>.pdf. (2) Parse each PDF: it is a fortnightly grid that defeats naive text extraction and defeats WebFetch entirely (the fetcher returned "unreadable, compressed format"); pdfminer.six extract_text plus whitespace-rejoining recovers entries as "<Country> - <Operation Name>" grouped under a day number and a track header ("Board AOB / IDA AOB" vs "Board Meeting - 10:00 AM ... IBRD Regular - Chair: <name>"). Build a panel of (operation, vintage_date, scheduled_board_date, track, chair). (3) Structured cross-check: GET https://search.worldbank.org/api/v3/projects?format=json&rows=900&fl=id,project_name,countryshortname,boardapprovaldate,status,lendinginstr,curr_total_commitment&srt=boardapprovaldate&order=desc, keep rows where boardapprovaldate > today, snapshot daily, diff `boardapprovaldate` per project id. Landing page for the human-readable index: worldbank.org/en/about/leadership/directors/boards-calendar. Extend the same diffing to the AfDB (projectsportal.afdb.org), AsDB, and IsDB project pipelines for corroboration. Total cost: one cron job and ~200 lines of Python.
- **The bet / trade:** Event-driven long/short in frontier and distressed EM sovereign USD Eurobonds (and 5y CDS where it exists), triggered off vintage-to-vintage revisions, held 2–8 weeks. BEARISH leg (the larger edge): when a policy-based operation for a stressed sovereign has its scheduled Board slot pushed, disappears from the next vintage entirely, or is demoted from "Board AOB" to a discussed "Regular" Board Meeting — sell the front-end Eurobond or buy 5y protection, hold 4–8 weeks through the next IMF mission concluding statement or review announcement. BULLISH leg: when a DPO for a sovereign the market believes is stalled newly APPEARS on the rolling calendar with a firm date — buy the shortest-dated Eurobond or sell protection, hold to three business days past the board date, exit into the World Bank approval press release. Names with the right combination of tradable external debt and DPO dependence: Ghana, Zambia, Angola, Egypt, Senegal, Kenya, Mozambique, Ecuador, El Salvador, PNG, Cameroon, Sri Lanka macro-linked bonds, Malawi. Secondary expression: sell the local currency via 1–3m NDF (NGN, EGP, KES, ZMW) on the bearish leg, since a slipped DPO removes a dated hard-currency reserve injection.
- **Mechanism:** The Bank Policy on Development Policy Financing (OP 8.60) makes a DPO disbursable only if the Bank certifies the country maintains an "adequate macroeconomic policy framework," determined by the Bank with inputs from IMF assessments, and only after every agreed prior action is completed. A DPO sitting on the Board calendar with a date is therefore a dated, institutional certificate that (a) the IMF review is on track and (b) the painful prior actions — fuel subsidy cuts, FX unification, tax measures, arrears clearance — are actually done. For a frontier sovereign, a $200m–$1.5bn DPF is same-day, fully fungible hard currency landing in reserves, frequently the single largest scheduled inflow of the quarter and often 5–25% of gross reserves; it is the marginal determinant of whether the next Eurobond coupon is paid and whether the peg or crawl holds. The causal chain for the bearish leg: an IMF review starts slipping → World Bank country staff learn internally, because the two institutions coordinate the macro-framework assessment continuously rather than at announcement → the Bank cannot take a DPO to the Board without the macro certification, so it quietly re-slots the item on its internal work program → the change surfaces in the next published calendar vintage → weeks later the IMF issues a mission statement or fails to announce a staff-level agreement, and the bond repriced. The calendar leaks the IMF's verdict because the Bank must act on it before the Fund publicizes it. The AOB-to-Regular demotion is the same logic at higher resolution: it means a shareholder chair wants the operation discussed rather than nodded through.
- **Who's on the other side:** Real-money EM funds, crossover credit funds, and sell-side strategists who build reserve and balance-of-payments bridges out of IMF and World Bank press releases and country budget documents — both of which are event-driven and publish only at the outcome. They learn a DPO was approved on approval day, from the World Bank's press release; they learn it was cancelled essentially never, because nobody announces the absence of a board item. They are slow for a structural reason: the calendar is published as a PDF under an opaque IDU-hexstring filename, is in no dataset catalogue, has no RSS feed, no email alert, no press release, and its fortnightly-grid layout breaks ordinary text extraction. Nobody on a trading desk is paid to diff a PDF. The document's actual audience is accountability NGOs (Bank Information Center, Bretton Woods Project) timing safeguards advocacy, and academics — a 2024 study of EM equity responses to World Bank loan approvals explicitly codes the Board calendar to measure "how much advance notice markets may have had," which simultaneously confirms the signal has documented market impact and that its exploitation is retrospective and academic rather than systematic. The header disclaimer "tentative and subject to change" causes institutional readers to discount the document, which is exactly backwards: the tentativeness is the information. Finally, the marginal price-setter in a Ghana, Malawi, or PNG bond is a thin crossover book reacting to Reuters headlines, not a dedicated multilateral-flow tracker.
- **Information lead:** Against the World Bank's own approval press release: 2–10 weeks, from the rolling current-plus-two-months calendar, and 1–14 months from the Projects API pipeline board date (verified today running out to 2027). Against wire services: effectively total — Reuters and Bloomberg do not cover board-calendar revisions, so there is no headline at all until either the approval press release or an IMF statement. The lead that actually matters, on the bearish leg: a policy operation slipping or vanishing from a calendar vintage should precede any public IMF signal (mission concluding statement, staff-level agreement, or the conspicuous absence of one) by roughly 3–8 weeks, because the Bank must re-plan its work program on information the Fund has not yet published. That specific lead is the load-bearing claim and the one requiring backtest, since 273 archived vintages make it directly measurable.
- **Why still under-exploited:** It is a PDF, not a product. No vendor sells it: Kpler and Vortexa are physical-commodity flows, YipitData and M Science are consumer transactions, Ortex is equity short interest — none touch multilateral operational calendars. Frontier analysts do track "when is the World Bank money coming," but they do it manually, on two or three names they already own, from press releases and country budgets, not by systematically diffing ~50 policy operations across every IBRD/IDA borrower on every vintage. The three frictions that preserve the edge are real and I hit all of them: the document is indexed under meaningless IDU-hex filenames with no feed or alert; WebFetch cannot read it at all (it returned "the actual content is encoded in a way that makes it unreadable"), so it is invisible to LLM-based research pipelines and to anything short of a local pdfminer parse plus grid reconstruction; and the value lives entirely in the diff between vintages, which requires having archived prior vintages before you knew you needed them. Nothing here is scraped from a private source, gated, or gray-market — it is a public multilateral institution publishing its own board agenda, exactly the "institutions only, sanctioned collection" constraint. The cross-domain arbitrage is that the artifact was built for governance transparency and is consumed by NGOs and academics, while its financial content — dated hard-currency injections and a live readout of IMF program health — goes unpriced.
- **Rough +EV:** Roughly 50–60 policy-based operations reach the Board per year; perhaps 20–25 are for sovereigns with tradable, event-sensitive external debt. Actionable revisions — a meaningful add, slip, removal, or track demotion on a name with enough liquidity to trade — realistically 15–30 per year. Move sizes: bullish confirmations are worth ~1–3 points on a frontier bond trading 60–85 cents, and are partly anticipated; bearish slips are worth ~3–8 points over 4–8 weeks because a DPO freeze almost always coincides with an IMF stall and is a genuine surprise. Costs are the binding constraint: frontier Eurobond bid-offer runs 0.5–1.5 points round trip, and CDS exists for only ~15 EM sovereigns, mostly not the frontier ones where the edge is largest. Net expectation: ~1–3 points of edge per actionable event after transaction costs, hit rate plausibly 55–62%, so on the order of a few hundred bps per year on capital deployed in a concentrated frontier book. The asymmetry matters — I would size the bearish leg two to three times the bullish leg. Capacity is genuinely low, single-digit millions of dollars per name before moving the price, which is part of why it stays unexploited. This is best run as a timing and entry overlay on positions a frontier book would take anyway, not as a standalone strategy.
- **Confidence:** Medium. The data source is verified beyond doubt — I downloaded and parsed three calendar vintages, extracted named dated DPOs for Egypt, Angola, Ghana, Zambia, Ethiopia and others, confirmed the two approval tracks, confirmed the "tentative and subject to change" language on the Bank's own page, and confirmed 275 forward-dated pipeline board dates in the live Projects API. The biggest uncertainty is not existence but LEAD DIRECTION: whether calendar revisions genuinely precede public IMF signals, or whether the Bank only re-slots an operation after the Fund's mission statement is already out — in which case the lead collapses to zero and the strategy is merely automating a known process. That is the single thing to backtest first, and it is directly testable: reconstruct the (operation, scheduled_date) panel from the 273 archived vintages and measure whether the first observed slip precedes the first public IMF or press signal. Two secondary risks: calendar publication cadence is irregular (I found gaps — nothing indexed between Sep 2024 and Jan 2025, and nothing after Jan 2026 as of today), so the near-term PDF channel may be unreliable and the Projects API channel, though more dependable, is noisier; and many board-date slips have mundane causes — document translation, chair scheduling, holiday clustering, an Inspection Panel case — so single-slip false positives will be high and the signal needs a filter restricting it to policy-based operations plus a corroborating second source.

## 30. The Tracking-Customer Leak

**Arena:** financial-market
**Domain:** Drug pricing, reimbursement & payer decisions. RULES: institutions only; sanctioned collection

- **Data source:** The VA National Acquisition Center semimonthly pharmaceutical price file (TPharmPrices / vaFssPharmPrices) — an NDC-level dump of every drug price under VA Federal Supply Schedule 65 I B and VA National Contracts, refreshed on/around the 2nd and 16th of every month. I downloaded the 2026-07-15 file: 21,744 rows, columns verified as ContractNumber, VendorName, ContractStartDate, ContractStopDate, NDCWithDashes, SubItemIdentifier, PackageDescription, Generic, TradeName, VAClass, Covered (T/F = subject to the statutory Federal Ceiling Price under PL 102-585), PrimeVendor, Price, PriceStartDate, PriceStopDate, PriceType (FSS / Big4 / NC), Non-TAA. Distribution: 15,391 FSS rows, 5,381 Big4 rows, 972 National Contract rows; 12,609 rows flagged Covered=T. The tradeable object is not the price level — it is the semimonthly DIFF, and specifically mid-contract Big4/FSS price cuts on Covered brand drugs. Those cuts are largely compelled by the FSS Price Reductions Clause: the contract is priced against a designated confidential commercial "tracking customer," and if the manufacturer deepens the discount to that customer it is contractually obligated to pass the same percentage through to the federal price and to notify VA within 15 days. VA even maintains separate transaction types for this — rfmPackage65IB-PriceDecreasePrc.zip (Price-Reduction-Clause-compelled) versus rfmPackage65IB-PriceDecreaseVoluntary.zip. So a confidential commercial price concession is mechanically converted into a public, dated, NDC-level number within weeks.
- **How to collect it:** Three free, sanctioned pulls, no login, no scraping of anything private. (1) Current file: GET https://department.va.gov/procurement-acquisition-and-logistics/national-acquisition-center/federal-supply-schedule-service/ and regex the page for TPharmPrices{YYYY-MM-DD}.xlsx under /wp-content/uploads/sites/56/{YYYY}/{MM}/ — the WordPress upload path rotates monthly so the page must be re-parsed each pull (verified live: .../2026/07/TPharmPrices2026-07-15.xlsx, 1.88 MB, HTTP 200). (2) Rolling archive, stable URL pattern: https://www.va.gov/opal/docs/foia/vaFssPharmPrices{YYYYMMDD}.xlsx — I confirmed 20250101 through 20260201 return 200 and 20160115/20180601/20200115/20220701/20240115 return 404, i.e. VA keeps roughly the trailing 18 months. That is ~38 semimonthly snapshots of instant backtest history; deeper history needs Wayback or a FOIA request, and forward history is a free cron job. (3) Point lookups and vendor/contract context: VA NAC Contract Catalog Search Tool, https://www.vendorportal.ecms.va.gov/nac/Pharma/List (exposes NDC, FSS price, NC price, Big 4 price, contract number, vendor). Pipeline: parse sheet1.xml directly (raw openpyxl/regex is fine, 20k rows), key on (NDCWithDashes, PriceType), diff consecutive snapshots, classify each delta as Jan-1 statutory FCP reset vs. mid-contract change, then crosswalk NDC labeler code to issuer via the FDA NDC Directory / DailyMed, and product-to-revenue-share via 10-K product tables. Total build: a weekend; ongoing cost: zero.
- **The bet / trade:** Single-name equity, event-driven. Trigger: a mid-contract (i.e., PriceStartDate not 01/01) Big4 price cut of >=15% on a Covered=T brand NDC whose product is >=15% of a US-listed issuer's revenue. Trade: short the equity or buy 3-6 month puts, sized 1-2% notional, held through the next TWO earnings reports (roughly 3-7 months) — the concession lands in reported net revenue one to two quarters after it takes effect. Inverse long trigger: a new Covered=T Big4 row appearing at a price implying Non-FAMP (Big4 / 0.76) close to WAC — the manufacturer's own filed projection that its launch gross-to-net will be tight — go long into the first two reported quarters of launch. Third leg, relative value: a VA National Contract award to a biosimilar/generic (PriceType=NC) is a visible, dated share-shift — e.g. Fresenius Kabi ustekinumab awarded NC at $350/0.5mL effective 06/05/2026 — pair long entrant vs. short originator. For a lower-variance expression, run it as a systematic sleeve: rank SMID-cap pharma each quarter by revenue-weighted Big4 price delta and hold a market-neutral long/short basket for one quarter.
- **Mechanism:** Under Section 603 of the Veterans Health Care Act of 1992, any manufacturer wanting its brand drugs covered by Medicaid, Part B, or 340B MUST hold a VA Master Agreement and list every covered drug on FSS 65 I B at or below a Federal Ceiling Price of 76% of the prior year's Non-Federal Average Manufacturer Price (less an additional discount if the drug's price outran CPI). Non-FAMP is the wholesaler-level average price net of cash discounts, chargebacks, rebates and similar concessions. Layered on top is the FSS Price Reductions Clause: the awarded federal price is tethered to a named commercial tracking customer, and any commercial discount that disturbs that ratio must be passed through and reported to VA within 15 days. Causal chain: manufacturer loses a contracting fight (biosimilar entry, PBM re-bid, GPO renewal, IRA maximum-fair-price spillover, a payer demanding deeper rebates) → grants a deeper confidential discount to the tracking customer → PRC forces a matching federal price cut → the cut appears in the next public semimonthly file with an exact effective date → one to two quarters later that same erosion shows up as net revenue below consensus and a guidance cut. The federal price is a legally-mandated public shadow of a private negotiation the company would never disclose. Empirically real: diffing 2026-02-15 against 2026-07-15 gave 863 NDC price decreases, 481 increases, 1,016 new rows, and 114 Big4 cuts on Covered=T brands. The top of that list reads like a net-price stress report — Ionis TRYNGOLZA -91% Big4 effective 04/14/2026; Lilly Lyumjev/Humalog -60% to -75% effective 03/01/2026; Boehringer Jardiance and the entire Synjardy family -28% effective 03/01/2026 (Lilly books alliance revenue on Jardiance); Vifor Korsuva -32%; Azurity Sutab -41%; and on the FSS side Zydus arsenic trioxide -84%.
- **Who's on the other side:** Sell-side pharma analysts and long-only holders, who model net price as gross scripts (IQVIA/Symphony) times an assumed gross-to-net haircut anchored on management's own vague "low-single-digit net price erosion" language. They have no per-product net price and no way to get one — gross-to-net is the single largest unobservable in a pharma model. They are slow for a structural reason: this file lives in the government-contracting world, not the investing world. The people who read it fluently are FSS compliance consultants, government-pricing lawyers, and manufacturers' own contract-compliance teams — all of whom are either not investors or are legally walled off from trading on it. Academic and policy users (CBO, Urban Institute, VA HERC) cite FSS prices for cross-sectional level comparisons and never diff the time series. And the file actively resists casual use: the URL path rotates every month, the FOIA archive silently truncates at ~18 months, there is no changelog or delta view, and the raw output is a 21,744-row spreadsheet where the Jan-1 statutory reset (10,490 rows all stamped 01/01/2026 in the July file) drowns out the ~23/month informative mid-contract cuts unless you explicitly filter it out. Nobody has done the boring plumbing.
- **Information lead:** Two distinct leads. (1) Latency lead on the commercial event: PRC requires notification within 15 days of the tracking-customer change, and the next public file drops within 15 days of that — so the concession is public roughly 15-30 days after it happens, versus 1-2 quarters (up to ~4.5 months from a quarter-open event to the earnings call) before it surfaces in reported net revenue, and even then only as an aggregate price/volume comment, never per product. Net lead: about one to two quarters on direction. (2) Calendar lead on the annual reset: the Jan-1 FCP recalculation is driven by the PRIOR calendar year's Non-FAMP, so the file published in the first week of January encodes each covered product's full prior-year net-price change — landing roughly 3-6 weeks before Q4/FY earnings and next-year guidance. On the per-product decomposition itself the lead is effectively infinite: the market never gets that number at all.
- **Why still under-exploited:** No alt-data vendor sells it — searching for FSS pharmaceutical pricing as an investment dataset returns only Federal Signal Corp (ticker FSS) and government-procurement law firm decks. It is not in the MMIT / Policy Reporter / Managed Markets universe, which covers formulary tier and prior-auth status, not federal contract price. It is not NADAC (retail acquisition cost, already picked over by 46brooklyn), not Medicare ASP (quarterly, widely watched, 2-quarter lag, Part B only), and not IQVIA gross scripts. The vendors who touch it — Prescription Analytics, The Coalition for Government Procurement, Blank Rome — sell FSS compliance services to manufacturers, which is the opposite side of the desk. Three practical moats keep it that way: the current-file URL is a rotating WordPress uploads path that breaks any naive scraper monthly; VA prunes the stable FOIA archive to ~18 months so nobody accidentally accumulates a panel; and the signal only appears after you strip the 10,000-row annual statutory reset and hand-build an NDC-to-labeler-to-ticker-to-revenue-share crosswalk. It is fragmented, manual, and filed under "procurement" rather than "healthcare" — exactly the profile of an unworked source.
- **Rough +EV:** Frequency, measured not guessed: 114 Big4 cuts on Covered=T brands in a 5-month window ≈ 23/month. After filtering to mid-contract (excluding the Jan-1 reset), >=15% magnitude, and a product that is >=15% of a US-listed issuer's revenue, expect roughly 2-5 tradeable single-name events per month, call it 30-55 per year. Edge: on concentrated single-product SMID names (the Ionis TRYNGOLZA -91% archetype) I'd expect ~55-62% directional hit rate over two quarters with a 4-8% average adverse move on losers of the net-price fight — an event-trade EV of maybe 1.5-3% per position, lumpy and fat-tailed. On diversified large caps the same signal is real but diluted to 0.5-2% moves, so it is worth more as a model input than a trade. Run as a systematic quarterly-rebalanced market-neutral SMID pharma sleeve I'd underwrite 2-4% annualized gross alpha at low capacity (this signal will not absorb more than $50-150M before the names it fires on move). Costs are effectively zero — free public files, one cron job. The honest deductions: Big4/FSS volumes are ~2% of US pharma, so the price cut is a tell, not a revenue event; a meaningful fraction of decreases are pure noise (generic commodity churn, contract renewals, product deletions, CPI-penalty mechanics); and the whole thing needs a hand-maintained NDC-to-ticker map that decays.
- **Confidence:** Medium. ## 31. The HUID Tape

**Arena:** financial-market
**Domain:** Collectibles, luxury & alternative assets. RULES: institutions/commerce only; sanctioned collection

- **Data source:** India's BIS (Bureau of Indian Standards) HUID hallmarking portal — specifically the public "Articles Received for Hallmarking during the Month" counter on the hallmarking home page, plus the companion Assaying & Hallmarking Centre (AHC) registry, the cancelled/suspended-AHC list, and the registered-jeweller list. Under the Hallmarking of Gold Jewellery and Artefacts Order, no gold jewellery can be legally sold at retail in the 380 mandated districts unless it has been assayed at a BIS-recognised AHC and stamped with a unique 6-character HUID registered in this system. The counter is therefore a near-complete national census of UNITS of gold jewellery entering the Indian retail channel — roughly 4-4.5 lakh articles/day, 60+ crore cumulative since July 2021. Silver was brought into HUID in Sept 2025 (IS 2112:2025) and is a separate, brand-new series that nearly doubled to 59 lakh articles in FY26.
- **How to collect it:** Poll https://huid.manakonline.in/MANAK/HallmarkingHomePage (redirect target of https://www.manakonline.in/MANAK/HallmarkingHomePage) 2-4x daily and parse the month-to-date article counter; diff consecutive snapshots into a daily series. The portal 503s / resets connections from many non-Indian IPs — I hit exactly that from this sandbox — so run the poller on a ~$5/mo Indian VPS (Mumbai region). Monthly, snapshot the AHC registry at https://www.manakonline.in/MANAK/AHCListForWebsite and the licensed-jeweller list linked from https://www.bis.gov.in/hallmarking-overview/ to build an active-capacity denominator by state. Calibrate/anchor the scraped level against PIB milestone releases (e.g. pib.gov.in PRID 2073332: "over 40 crore items hallmarked", 361 districts, Nov 2024) and against BIS annual reports; backfill centre-wise monthly history via RTI to the BIS Hallmarking Division (Hallmarking@bis.gov.in), which is the sanctioned route to the granular data. Control for the mechanical drift from phase expansion using the published district notifications (361 districts Nov 2024 → 380 districts from 2 Mar 2026).
- **The bet / trade:** Long or short Titan Company (NSE: TITAN, ~$35-40bn cap, deep monthly futures and options) into its quarterly business update, which lands ~7 days after quarter end. Enter 2-3 weeks before quarter end using the accumulated intra-quarter hallmarking unit-volume series versus consensus buyer-growth expectations; exit on the business-update print. Holding horizon 2-4 weeks, four scheduled events per year. Second expression: a pair — long Kalyan Jewellers (KALYANKJIL) / short Titan (or the reverse) when national unit growth and rupee-value growth diverge, since Kalyan is volume/value-led and Titan is ticket-size/premiumisation-led. Third expression: fade or press the Akshaya Tritiya / Dhanteras muhurat-calendar shift when festive dates straddle a quarter boundary and the hallmarking pull-forward shows up in the counter weeks before anyone can see it in sales.
- **Mechanism:** Hallmarking is a legally mandatory gate that sits UPSTREAM of the retail counter: a jeweller sends articles to an AHC, they are assayed, HUID-stamped and registered, and only then can be displayed and sold — typically a 2-8 week physical lead over sell-through. So the article counter measures units of gold jewellery entering retail, in real time, nationally. That matters right now because the entire Indian jewellery equity debate is volume versus price. In Q1 FY27 MCX gold averaged a record ~Rs 1,51,108/10g, up 81% YoY; national gold demand fell 6% to 131.4t and jewellery tonnage fell 15% to 75.1t — yet Titan printed +38-46% rupee jewellery growth on ~5% buyer growth and ~31% ticket-size inflation. The stock trades on the buyer/unit-growth number, not the revenue number, because ticket size is just the gold print. Article counts are a direct, high-frequency proxy for the disputed variable (units and therefore buyers), and they also give you the down-trading signal: articles holding up while tonnage falls means lighter/lower-carat pieces, which is the gross-margin story. Nothing else available in real time does this — gold import data is tonnage dominated by bars/coins and distorted by duty arbitrage and smuggling; MCX gives you price only; WGC gives tonnage quarterly and late.
- **Who's on the other side:** Indian domestic long-only funds, insurers and FIIs holding Titan — one of the most crowded consensus compounders on the NSE — plus the sell-side that models jewellery growth off three slow inputs: (1) monthly gold import data from Ministry of Commerce / DGCIS, released ~15th of the following month and dominated by bars and coins; (2) World Gold Council Gold Demand Trends, published roughly 4-5 weeks after quarter end, i.e. AFTER Titan's own business update has already moved the stock; (3) channel checks across a few dozen stores in a market with ~1,060 AHCs and hundreds of thousands of jewellers. They are slow for structural reasons, not stupidity: the BIS counter lives on a standards-bureau licensing portal, not a statistics site; it has no CSV, no API, no Bloomberg ticker and no archive; it is month-to-date cumulative only, so the historical time series simply does not exist anywhere unless you built it by snapshotting; and it is frequently unreachable from foreign IPs. Equity analysts do not think of a compliance portal as market data.
- **Information lead:** The market learns quarterly unit/buyer growth when Titan files its business update ~7 days after quarter end (Q4 FY26 on 7 Apr 2026, stock +4%; Q1 FY27 update preceded 7 Aug results), and learns national tonnage from WGC ~30-40 days after quarter end. With a daily counter you know the quarter's unit trajectory continuously: a 30-90 day lead on the business update and 40-130 days on WGC and the P&L. On a single-month basis you beat the DGCIS import print by 2-6 weeks. And because hallmarking physically precedes retail display by 2-8 weeks, you are also ahead of the transaction itself, not merely ahead of its disclosure.
- **Why still under-exploited:** No vendor packages it. Kpler/Vortexa/YipitData/M Science-style shops cover shipping, card panels and web transactions; nobody covers a national standards bureau's assay-licensing counter, and there is no Indian analogue selling it. Crucially, the source destroys its own history — the page shows month-to-date only, so there is no back-series available for purchase at any price; whoever starts logging owns a dataset that cannot be replicated backwards except by RTI. It geo-blocks or throttles foreign IPs, which excludes most offshore quant shops by default. The numbers reach the public only as sporadic PIB milestone press releases ("40 crore items", "59 lakh silver articles in FY26") — annual, cumulative and untradeable. Meanwhile the Indian gold-research world is fixated on imports, domestic discount/premium and WGC tonnage, none of which measures units. And the source is still expanding: mandatory hallmarking has rolled from 256 to 361 to 380 districts, and silver HUID only began in Sept 2025 with BIS now scaling silver assay labs nationwide as buyers rotate out of unaffordable gold — a brand-new series that is also the cleanest real-time read on Indian silver retail demand, the single largest swing factor in the global silver balance.
- **Rough +EV:** Titan's business-update day moves the stock roughly 3-8% (Q4 FY26 +4% on the print; Q3 FY25 +8% over three days). Assume the hallmarking series calls the direction of the buyer-growth surprise ~60-65% of the time against a mean absolute event move of ~4.5%. Raw edge per event ≈ (0.62 × 4.5%) − (0.38 × 4.5%) ≈ +1.1% on the underlying before costs, materially levered in ATM monthly options. Fire rate: 4 Titan events/year, plus 4 each for Kalyan, Senco, PN Gadgil and Thangamayil, plus the quarterly WGC tonnage print, plus 2 festive-calendar setups — call it 15-20 directional firings and a similar number of pair trades annually. Realistic dedicated-capital return in the good case is ~8-20% annualised, on capacity of maybe $2-10m before Titan/Kalyan option liquidity and slippage bite. Running cost is trivial: an Indian VPS, a cron job and a few RTI filings.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Biggest uncertainty: the exact refresh behaviour and granularity of the public counter. I confirmed the page exists and is titled "Articles Received for Hallmarking during the Month," and the magnitudes cross-check cleanly (PIB's ~4 lakh articles/day and the 40 crore → 60+ crore cumulative jump imply ~13 crore/year), but the portal 503'd and reset connections from this environment so I could not read the live figure — I could not confirm whether it ticks daily or only settles monthly, nor whether a state/centre breakdown is exposed publicly. If it only updates monthly and gets revised, the lead compresses from ~60 days to ~15-30 days and the Sharpe falls sharply though the trade survives. Secondary risk is the mapping: Titan is taking share from unorganised jewellers, so national units can fall while Titan grows (exactly FY26) — the series must be used as the national-cycle input to a buyer-growth surprise model, not as a naive read-through, and it must be de-trended for district-phase expansion, new AHC openings and ongoing formalisation of the trade.

## 32. The Thinner Ledger

**Arena:** financial-market (equities: SGX / China A-share / HK shipbuilders; secondary leg vs. shipowner equities and FFAs)
**Domain:** Shipbuilding, drydock & heavy industry cycles. RULES: institutions only; sanctioned collection

- **Data source:** Chinese shipyards' mandatory pollutant-discharge-permit EXECUTION REPORTS (排污许可证执行报告) — quarterly and annual — filed to and auto-published on China MEE's national permit platform. Under the governing technical standard HJ 1124-2020 (排污许可证申请与核发技术规范 — 铁路、船舶、航空航天和其他运输设备制造业; shipbuilding is industry code C373, bundled with rail/aerospace, which is why it never surfaces in a "shipbuilding" search), each yard must report, per reporting period: actual product output, raw and auxiliary material consumption — critically COATINGS and THINNERS/SOLVENTS in tonnes, plus welding consumables and steel — production-line operating hours, and actual VOC / particulate / blasting-wastewater discharge against permitted caps. The permit itself carries a legally binding ceiling on coating+thinner tonnage and VOC load, i.e. a hard, published, physical cap on how many CGT of hull that yard may lawfully paint. Same platform also publishes new-permit issuance and permit variations (变更), plus self-monitoring (自行监测) and inspection records.
- **How to collect it:** 1) permit.mee.gov.cn public end (公开端). Use 许可信息公开 search (https://permit.mee.gov.cn/perxxgkinfo/syssb/xkgg/xkgg!licenseInformation.action) filtered by 行业类别 = 船舶及相关装置制造 (C373) × province (Jiangsu, Shanghai, Zhejiang, Liaoning, Guangdong, Shandong, Guangxi, Hubei). 2) Each holder gets a public detail page whose execution-report tab is a stable endpoint pattern: /perxxgkinfo/xkgkAction!xkgk.action?xkgk=getxxgkContentzxbg&dataid=<uuid> — I verified this live and it currently serves linked Q1 2026 and Q2 2026 quarterly reports alongside enforcement and self-monitoring tabs. 3) Discovery/backfill shortcut: IPE's 蔚蓝地图 (ipe.org.cn) already harvests 排污许可执行报告 from 31 provincial and 337 city/county bureau sites and is free — use it as an index layer, permit.mee.gov.cn + provincial 生态环境厅 审批公示 pages as source of record (the latter are effectively a daily RSS-able feed of new/varied permits). 4) The real work, and the moat: hand-build the crosswalk from ~50-80 permit-holding Chinese legal entity names to listed parents — Jiangsu Yangzijiang Shipbuilding / Jiangsu New Yangzi / Jiangsu Yangzi-Xinfu / Yangzi-Mitsui → SGX:BS6; Jiangnan, Hudong-Zhonghua, Waigaoqiao, Guangzhou Shipyard International, Huangpu Wenchong, Dalian Shipbuilding, Beihai → 600150.SS; Hengli Heavy (reactivated STX Dalian) → 002082.SZ; COSCO Shipping Heavy yards → 1919.HK/517.HK. 5) Parse Chinese-language PDF/OFD tables (non-standard layouts; OCR + table extraction). No bulk API, session-verification shield on the portal — deliberately boring, which is the point.
- **The bet / trade:** PRIMARY (event trade, 3-6 weeks): directional position in Yangzijiang Shipbuilding (SGX: BS6, STI constituent, ~US$40-70m ADV) taken when the annual execution report posts (late Jan/Feb) and when the Q2 quarterly posts (15 Jul), held into the semi-annual results. BS6 reports only TWICE a year — 1H2026 landed 6 Aug 2026, next print 24 Feb 2027 — so there is a ~6.5-month vacuum containing no hard physical data except this filing. Rule: if trailing coating+thinner tonnage across the four Yangzijiang permit entities runs >10% above the throughput implied by consensus delivery/CGT schedules, go long into the print; >10% below, go short. Beta-hedge with a short basket of 600150.SS / HD Hyundai 329180.KS. Express in cash equity or SGX CFD/options. SECONDARY (structural, 6-18 months): maintain a running census of NEWLY ISSUED and materially VARIED shipyard discharge permits and the net change in nationally permitted coating/thinner + VOC tonnage as a forward Chinese shipbuilding capacity index; when net permitted capacity inflects upward, put on short shipbuilders (BS6, 600150.SS, 002082.SZ) vs. long shipowners / long FFAs; reverse when it flatlines.
- **Mechanism:** Hull coating is the terminal, non-substitutable, non-deferrable step of ship construction: every square metre of steel is blasted and painted before block erection, launch and delivery, and paint is thinned to spray, so coating+thinner tonnage is close to linear in coated steel area, which maps to CGT. Steel and welding-consumable draw maps to work 12-24 months out; paint draw maps to hulls that will float out and deliver in 1-2 quarters. Chinese yards recognise revenue on percentage-of-completion, so physical progress IS revenue and margin — the throughput print is a direct read on the number the company will announce weeks later. Separately, because VOCs are a capped regulated pollutant, the yard's PERMITTED coating/thinner tonnage is a legal ceiling on physical hull throughput — capacity expressed in a unit nobody in finance has ever measured. A dormant or expanding yard cannot lawfully coat a hull until a new permit or an upward variation is granted and publicly posted, which happens ~6-12 months before first steel and 18-36 months before first delivery. That makes the permit census the earliest possible read on the single most important variable in the shipping cycle: whether Chinese capacity is genuinely constrained (supporting newbuild prices, secondhand values, and shipowner NAVs) or quietly reflating.
- **Who's on the other side:** Sell-side and long-only funds covering BS6, CSSC and Hengli, who model deliveries off the Clarksons orderbook plus management guidance and receive physical data only on results day; index/passive flow in BS6 (STI member) that is price-insensitive; and the retail/momentum base that trades Chinese shipbuilders off headline order-win press releases. The AIS/satellite vendors (Kpler, Spire, VesselsValue) and the brokers (Clarksons) all measure DISCRETE, FENCE-EXTERNAL events — contract signed, launched, delivered. None of them measure work-in-progress inside the yard. The filings' actual audience is EHS consultants, green-supply-chain auditors and ESG raters (IPE indexes violations and emissions for pollution scoring, not production quantities). They are slow because the data is public but not LEGIBLE: Chinese-language PDFs, permit-holder legal entity names that don't match ticker names, an industry code shared with railway and aerospace, no bulk API, and no financial data vendor indexing it.
- **Information lead:** Hard, calendar-anchored, and verified. Quarterly execution reports are due by the 15th of the first month of the following quarter (15 Apr / 15 Jul / 15 Oct) and the annual by end-Jan (extended to end-Feb in recent cycles), and the platform auto-publishes on submission. Versus A-share disclosure deadlines (Q1 30 Apr, interim 31 Aug, Q3 31 Oct, annual 30 Apr) that is a 15-16 day lead on Q1/Q3, ~45 days on the interim, and 60-90 days on the annual. Versus Yangzijiang, which reports only semi-annually, the Jul-15 quarterly lands ~3 weeks before H1 results and the Jan/Feb annual ~3-6 weeks before FY results — and is the only hard physical datapoint in an otherwise ~6.5-month blackout. Versus Clarksons delivery statistics, paint consumption leads actual delivery by 1-2 quarters. Versus ANY published capacity estimate, a new or upward-varied permit leads first steel by 6-12 months and first delivery by 18-36 months. Note the fine print is itself the edge: the Q2 quarterly (15 Jul) beats the interim by six weeks precisely because the environmental calendar and the securities calendar were never harmonised.
- **Why still under-exploited:** No financial alt-data vendor touches it. Kpler/Vortexa/Spire are AIS-and-satellite; Clarksons/VesselsValue are broker-reported orderbook databases; YipitData/M Science/Ortex cover consumer transactions, US corporates and securities lending — none index Chinese environmental compliance filings, and none has ever published a shipyard physical-throughput series. The one aggregator that does harvest these reports, IPE's 蔚蓝地图, is a Beijing environmental NGO whose product is green-supply-chain compliance and ESG scoring; it surfaces violations and emissions, not the production-quantity and raw-material-consumption fields buried inside the execution reports, and it markets to procurement teams and green-finance desks, not to trading desks. Four independent frictions keep it unexploited: (1) the shipbuilding technical standard HJ 1124-2020 bundles ships with railway and aerospace so the sector is invisible to keyword search; (2) permit-holder legal entities bear names with no obvious mapping to listed tickers and the crosswalk must be built by hand; (3) the portal has no bulk API and runs session verification, with reports as Chinese-language PDF/OFD with non-standard tables; (4) it's genuinely NEW — the quarterly execution-report regime was only fully digitised and enforced through the 2021-2024 permit-system build-out and tightened again by the 2024 《排污许可管理办法》 revision, so a usable panel effectively starts around 2022. It is a legally mandated, government-published, free industrial-throughput census that has existed in tradeable form for roughly four years and that nobody has read as a financial dataset.
- **Rough +EV:** Event leg: fires 4x/year per A-share name and 2x/year for BS6, so ~8-10 tradeable windows/year across BS6 + 600150.SS + 002082.SZ. Conditioning on a >10% divergence between reported coating/thinner throughput and consensus-implied build progress (expect that condition to trigger on roughly 40-50% of windows), I'd underwrite a ~60-65% hit rate on the direction of the results-day move. BS6 results-day moves have run ±5-12%; a 3-6 week beta-hedged position sized to that gives ~1.5-3% expected edge per fired event, so ~6-15% annualised gross on deployed capital, hedged Sharpe plausibly 0.6-1.0. Transaction costs are trivial (SGX/A-share liquidity is deep); the real cost is 3-6 person-months of entity-crosswalk and PDF-parser build plus ~1 FTE-week/quarter of maintenance. Structural leg is lower frequency and much larger: correctly calling the Chinese capacity inflection 12-18 months early on a shipbuilder-vs-shipowner pair is a 30-60% spread trade, but it fires maybe once every 3-4 years. Total programme: modest, durable, and cheap relative to the moat.
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM. Biggest uncertainty by far: self-reporting bias and CAP CENSORING. These are compliance filings, not audited production statistics — yards have a standing incentive to report coating and VOC quantities at or just under their permitted ceiling rather than truthfully, which means the series may saturate exactly in a boom, i.e. precisely when you most want to detect upside throughput. That is the single thing that would kill the trade, and it must be tested before any sizing: backfit reported coating tonnage against Clarksons delivered CGT per yard for 2022-2025 and confirm the paint-to-delivery elasticity is stable and non-truncated. Secondary risks: some yards may sit in 简化管理 (annual-only reporting), cutting frequency; state-owned naval-adjacent yards (Jiangnan, Dalian, Huludao, Bohai) may redact or withhold filings on security grounds, biasing the sample toward private/commercial yards (which, conveniently, is where BS6 and Hengli sit); and the platform periodically restructures URLs and hardens against automation. Mitigations: use blasting-wastewater volume and the independent 自行监测 hourly VOC series as orthogonal cross-checks, and treat the permit-issuance census (which is a legal act, not a self-reported quantity, and therefore not gameable) as the higher-conviction half of the programme.

## 33. The CNS Counter (Charged-Off Portfolio Consignment Tape)

**Arena:** financial-market (US small/nano-cap equities: HGBL primary; PRAA/ECPG secondary; fintech-consumer complex tertiary)
**Domain:** Credit, lending & consumer finance plumbing. RULES: institutions only; sanctioned collection

- **Data source:** The public and buyer-registered "current offerings" tapes of the US charged-off / non-performing consumer receivable secondary market — i.e., the deal boards of the handful of brokers who intermediate when a lender decides to sell its charged-off (and sometimes still-performing) loan paper. The single most exploitable artifact is Garnet Capital Advisors' sequential Sale IDs (CNS-1190, CML-1241, etc.): a monotone integer counter of how many portfolios have come to market, sitting in plain sight on an unauthenticated web page. Around it: Debexpert's live + archived auction table (face value, asset class, state, performing vs non-performing, auction date), NLEX's Current Offerings (registration-gated; NLEX is the largest charged-off broker, >$250B face brokered since the 1980s, owned by Heritage Global / HGBL), DebtX (CRE loan sale tape, acquired by HGBL effective 1/1/2026), and DebtMarket/Triton. This is the only public window on the loss and liquidity experience of the entire private fintech consumer-lending complex (Upgrade, Best Egg/Marlette, Avant, LendingPoint, Achieve, Happy Money, Prosper, Mission Lane, BNPL) plus subprime auto — none of which disclose anything publicly.
- **How to collect it:** Daily HTTP snapshot + diff of five endpoints, ~1-2 hrs/week of reconciliation: (1) https://www.garnetcapital.com/default/currentofferings — no login; capture Sale ID, prefix (CNS/CML/RES/CO), loan type, amount/flow size, seller name or "Undisclosed Seller", description, open date. Verified: 10 live offerings, sequential IDs. (2) https://www.debexpert.com/portfolios-for-sale plus its "past portfolios" archive — no login; capture face value, asset class, state, performing/non-performing, auction date (verified: entries render as e.g. "$180K Non-Performing Consumer Loans @TX, Aug 11"). (3) https://www.nlex.com/Current-Offerings/ — free legitimate buyer registration required (NLEX onboards hundreds of buyers; they also email a new-offerings bulletin to registered buyers); capture sale number, creditor, asset description, face. (4) debtx.com sale calendar. (5) debtmarket.net/hub. Derive four series weekly: total face by asset class; new-seller flag (first-ever appearance of a creditor); relist detection (same portfolio reappearing = failed auction = collapsing bid); and performing-vs-non-performing mix. No personal data touched — masked account-level data rooms sit behind NDA and are deliberately not used; only institution-level portfolio metadata.
- **The bet / trade:** Primary: long/short HGBL (Heritage Global, ~$1.06, ~34.7M shares, ~$37M cap) into each quarterly print, sized off the summed brokered face value in the tape for that quarter. Q3 2026 ends Sep 30, reports ~early-mid Nov 2026 — accumulate/short Aug-Sep, hold through the print (6-14 week horizon, repeat 4x/yr). Secondary: long PRAA and ECPG when the tape shows supply surging AND relists rising (cheap paper = strong forward vintages, while the tape's macro read makes the market sell all consumer-credit names); flat/short when the tape thins and prices firm — same quarterly horizon. Tertiary: when a lender that has never sold appears, or when a lender lists PERFORMING paper on a spot marketplace (a funding event, not a credit event), short the fintech-consumer read-across basket (UPST, PGY, AFRM, LC, OPRT, OppFi) and/or buy protection on consumer ABS / regionals with heavy Call Report RC-C 10.d "loans to consumer credit intermediaries" exposure; 1-6 month horizon.
- **Mechanism:** NLEX and DebtX are pure agency brokers — they earn a commission on face value brokered, and revenue is recognized when the sale closes. HGBL just announced (Aug 2026) the substantial wind-down of Heritage Global Capital, its specialty lending arm, taking a $21.7M non-cash charge. That wind-down removes the one thing that was polluting the read: from Q3 2026 forward, HGBL's Financial Assets segment is essentially NLEX + DebtX + Boston Note brokerage commission on face brokered. So face value on the tape maps near-linearly to a reported revenue line, one quarter ahead of the print. Separately, the tape is the physical clearing venue for the marginal supply of charged-off consumer paper: a lender only lists on a spot board when it is outside (or has lost) its bilateral forward-flow contract. So a first-time seller means the lender has decided to monetize losses; a relist means bids came in under reserve; a performing-paper listing means the lender needs cash, not loss recognition. Those three events precede, respectively, an earnings surprise, a collapse in debt-buyer purchase-price multiples (PRAA/ECPG's single most important variable), and a private-lender funding failure of the Tricolor/PrimaLend type.
- **Who's on the other side:** HGBL: essentially no sell-side (one small-cap shop, PT recently nudged $4.00→$4.50 off a DCF, not off volume), nano-cap, no data vendor covers debt-sale brokerage volume at all. Holders model it from industrial-auction commentary plus generic "consumer charge-offs are rising" macro — and that macro is actively misleading, since HGBL itself flagged *lower* NLEX revenue from recurring clients in 2025 while headline consumer charge-offs rose. PRAA/ECPG: covered by a handful of specialty-finance analysts whose entire read on "supply and pricing" comes from management's answer on the earnings call — management is structurally incentivized to say supply is abundant and pricing is attractive. Nobody audits it. The deeper reason this sits unpriced is market segmentation: the people who can see this tape are debt buyers, collection agencies, and collection law firms — an industry that does not trade equities — while the people who trade the equities have never heard of a sale ID.
- **Information lead:** Against HGBL's reported revenue: a portfolio listed in early July closes in ~3-6 weeks and is reported ~40-45 days after quarter end — roughly a 4.5-month lead on the earliest flow, ~3 months on average across a quarter, and a minimum ~6-week lead on the last week's listings. Against PRAA/ECPG's disclosed purchase volume and purchase-price multiple: 6-10 weeks, and the relist signal front-runs their pricing commentary by a full quarter. Against private fintech lender distress: there is no public alternative whatsoever, so the lead versus the market's first hard information (a broken ABS deal, a warehouse pull, or a bankruptcy filing) is 6-18 months — that is the Tricolor-shaped window.
- **Why still under-exploited:** The data is spread across 5-8 broker websites in incompatible formats, with no CUSIPs, no tickers, no standard schema, sellers frequently listed as "Undisclosed," and part of it behind free-but-vetted buyer registration. The asset class is unglamorous and the pure-play equities are nano-cap, so no vendor has bothered — YipitData/M Science/Ortex chase card spend, app installs, and short interest, not charged-off receivable auction calendars. Crucially, the cleanest version of this signal is brand new: the HGC wind-down announced in Aug 2026 is what collapses HGBL into a near-pure brokerage, so the linear face-value-to-revenue mapping only becomes valid starting with the Q3 2026 quarter now in progress. And the sequential Sale ID counter — the cheapest, most reliable deal-flow proxy in the whole complex — is an accidental artifact of a broker's CMS that nobody has ever thought to log.
- **Rough +EV:** Signal updates weekly and is continuously observable; ~8-12 tradeable events per year across HGBL, PRAA and ECPG, plus 1-3 idiosyncratic first-time-seller/performing-paper alerts per year. HGBL: expect to nowcast Financial Assets revenue within roughly ±10-15%; the stock routinely moves 15-40% on prints at a $1 handle post-writedown. At ~60-65% directional hit rate on a 20% average move, that is ~4-8% expected per event before costs — but liquidity is likely well under $300k/day, so capacity is maybe $100-300k and realistic annualized edge on that capacity is 20-40% with brutal variance and gap risk. PRAA/ECPG: $1-3B caps, liquid, listed options; edge per quarterly event is smaller, ~2-4% expected on direction, but capacity is several million and the relist-driven contrarian long (buy debt buyers precisely when supply headlines are scaring people out of consumer credit) is the highest-Sharpe leg. Total build cost: a scraper, a free buyer registration, and about 1-2 hours a week.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Biggest uncertainty by far is sample representativeness: the brokered spot tape is only the marginal slice of charge-off flow — the majority of large-issuer paper moves through bilateral forward-flow contracts that never touch any public board. That means the tape can be a biased sample of the market AND, worse for the HGBL leg, HGBL's own revenue is driven as much by client retention as by market-wide volume (they explicitly attributed 2025 softness to "lower revenues from recurring clients"). If NLEX loses a recurring seller, market volume can rise while HGBL revenue falls, and the nowcast inverts. Secondary risks: NLEX's registration-gated listings may be too coarse to sum face values reliably, DebtX/Boston Note now blend CRE and residential note flow into the same segment, and HGBL's sub-$40M cap and $1 price carry real delisting and liquidity hazards. Mitigation is to weight the PRAA/ECPG and private-lender-alert legs, which depend only on the tape's shape (relists, new sellers, performing-paper mix) rather than on any single broker's market share.
