# The Edge Ledger

A growing catalog of **underutilized data sources that are predictive of future market or gambling outcomes** — in the spirit of jockey weights, satellite parking-lot counts, Google Trends, presidential tweets, and weather-vs-commodities.

Each entry names the exact data source, how to collect it, the **specific bet or trade** it implies, the causal mechanism, who is on the other side, the real information lead, why it is still under-exploited, and a rough +EV estimate with confidence.

This is **open-ended discovery**, not a pass/fail screen. Entries are hypotheses worth investigating, ranked by nothing but the order they were found. No source is ever repeated. Research notes, not investment advice — every entry needs its own backtest before real money.

**Discipline carried over from earlier work:** before staking anything here, measure the expected move in *dollars* against the tick/spread/vig, and confirm the information lead is real (is the reference number already published free?). Those two checks killed ~760 prior candidates.

## 1. PurpleAir Ballpark Fade

**Arena:** gambling (MLB — and MLS — game/player props and team totals at mainstream sportsbooks: DraftKings, FanDuel, Pinnacle)
**Domain:** Sports betting props & live markets

- **Data source:** The PurpleAir citizen air-quality sensor network: crowd-deployed sensors reporting raw PM2.5 (plus temp/humidity/pressure) at the individual-sensor level, updating roughly every 2 minutes, with several units typically within 1-2 km of most downtown outdoor ballparks. This is hyperlocal, sensor-level particulate at the stadium — not the sparse, hourly EPA/AirNow regulatory grid that books (if they look at air at all) would see. Pair it with EPA AirNow's CO feed, because the umpire study found CO has an even larger error effect than PM2.5.
- **How to collect it:** Free PurpleAir Developer API key (Google-linked). Query sensors inside a bounding box around each outdoor MLB/MLS stadium; apply the published EPA correction factor to raw PM2.5 and drop dead/divergent sensors (dual-laser flag). Pull CO from the EPA AirNow API. Join to confirmed lineups via the free MLB Stats API (statsapi.mlb.com) and to opening/closing totals via an odds API (e.g., The Odds API). Backtest against historical PurpleAir (data to 2016), Retrosheet/Statcast game logs, and closing lines to fix the sign and threshold before staking. Whole pipeline is buildable by one person.
- **The bet / trade:** Single-game MLB props, entered after lineups post and before first pitch, held to settlement. Primary: ROAD team-total UNDER when stadium PM2.5 is elevated AND materially above the road team's home-city baseline (visitors are hit ~2-3x harder than acclimatized home hitters). Secondary: game-total UNDER and starting-pitcher strikeouts OVER on the same trigger. Trigger band: corrected PM2.5 ~35+ ug/m3 (sub-advisory drift days the book ignores) up through wildfire-smoke spikes (55-200+), excluding levels high enough to threaten postponement.
- **Mechanism:** Elevated fine-particulate/CO exposure causes short-term cognitive and aerobic impairment. Three reinforcing channels suppress scoring: (1) hitters lose reaction time and bat speed, degrading contact quality — worse for the ROAD team, which isn't acclimatized to that city's air; (2) umpires make measurably more incorrect ball/strike calls, adding strike-zone noise that favors pitchers; (3) position players fatigue faster, cutting late-game rallies. Net effect: fewer runs and more strikeouts, concentrated on the visiting offense.
- **Who's on the other side:** The book and sharp syndicates on the other side of the total. Every serious book buys a weather feed (temp, wind, humidity, precip — DTN/NWS-type) and models park factors, but PM2.5/CO is not in those feeds and not in any standard totals model. They react to air only on a news event (postponement chatter, an AQI advisory, TV showing haze). Official regulatory monitors are hourly and often 5-15 km from the park, so even attentive sharps see degraded air late and coarse. On ordinary drift/inversion days below the news threshold, no one on the other side is pricing air at all.
- **Information lead:** On smoke-plume days: ~30-90 minutes, the gap between a 2-minute hyperlocal sensor at the park and the hourly, distant regulatory monitor / eventual broadcast mention. On sub-advisory days (PM2.5 ~35-90, common during western/midwestern/northeastern smoke season and urban inversions): effectively unlimited — the market never prices it, so the entire study-documented effect is uncaptured by the line.
- **Why still under-exploited:** Air quality is absent from every commercial sports-betting data product (weather is universally bought; particulate is not). PurpleAir is fragmented citizen-sensor data that needs correction factors, bad-sensor flagging, and geospatial matching to stadiums — no vendor packages it for bettors. The causal evidence lives in an environmental-economics journal (JAERE 2018 umpire study) and public-health/soccer journals, not sports-analytics circles, so it hasn't crossed over. It is exactly the weird/manual/unpackaged source that stays mispriced.
- **Rough +EV:** Modest, seasonal, concentrated. Documented effects are small per unit (10 ug/m3 PM2.5 ~ +2.6% umpire errors; road-hitter pass/accuracy analogs ~2-8% in soccer), implying maybe 0.1-0.4 runs of true suppression on a triggered game. Fires on only ~3-8% of the slate in a normal week, spiking hard during June-October smoke events. On triggered games I'd estimate a ~2-4% edge on road-total unders and pitcher-K overs — beats -110 vig with discipline, but low volume and clustered risk.
- **Confidence:** Medium-low. Biggest uncertainty: the umpire study proves error rate rises, not that RUNS fall — I'm inferring net run-suppression direction and magnitude from adjacent soccer/athlete-performance literature, and the umpire-error channel could partially cancel (zone expansion vs. contraction is unproven). The sign and size must be confirmed by a historical PurpleAir-vs-closing-line backtest before real money; if the net run effect is smaller than ~0.15 runs it won't clear vig.

## 2. Cold-Air Bleeder Fade

**Arena:** Gambling — US parimutuel Thoroughbred racing (win pool plus vertical/horizontal exotics; lay on exchanges/fixed-odds where legal)
**Domain:** Horse & greyhound racing / parimutuel

- **Data source:** The unpriced signal is a three-way intersection that no one assembles: (1) per-start Lasix/furosemide medication designations and "bled / bled through Lasix" chart footnotes from Equibase charts and past performances — Equibase is the official DB and every US state is required to list Lasix in the charts — which identify CHRONIC BLEEDERS now entered in a Lasix-PROHIBITED condition; (2) the HISA national Veterinarians'/EIPH bleeder list (hisaus.org); (3) hyperlocal ambient temperature + dewpoint at that specific track and post time from a free weather API. The novel, unmodeled factor is the ambient-temperature × forced-no-Lasix × bleeding-history interaction. This is only exploitable because HISA banned race-day Lasix in 2yo and stakes races (effective May 2023, reaffirmed 2026), creating a brand-new population of chronic bleeders running pharmacologically UNPROTECTED.
- **How to collect it:** Scrape Equibase result charts / DRF Formulator PP files for each entrant's full medication history (L = on Lasix, L1 = first-time Lasix, blank = none) and footnote comments; a horse showing "L" across recent allowance/claiming starts but now entered in a stakes or 2yo race = "first time OFF Lasix," i.e., an unprotected chronic bleeder. Pull the HISA Vet's/bleeder list. Geocode each track and query NWS api.weather.gov or Open-Meteo for the hourly forecast (temperature, humidity/dewpoint) at post time — available days ahead; optionally add PurpleAir particulate AQI near the track. Flag rule: entrant in a Lasix-prohibited race (2yo or stakes) + established prior Lasix/bleeder history + forecast post-time temp roughly below 55-60°F (dry/cold).
- **The bet / trade:** Fade the flagged unprotected bleeder in a single race, settled at race completion the same day. In pure parimutuel: exclude it from your win bet and from exacta/trifecta/superfecta keys, and build "against" tickets spreading the rest of the field to capture the overlay its suppressed chance leaves on every other runner. Where fixed-odds or exchange betting is legal (international books, US fixed-odds markets), lay the horse to win / back the field.
- **Mechanism:** Cold, dry air raises EIPH (exercise-induced pulmonary hemorrhage) severity — veterinary literature ties ambient temps under ~68°F to markedly higher EIPH risk. A chronic bleeder built its recent, market-visible form WITH Lasix, but HISA bars furosemide in 2yo and stakes races, so it must run unmedicated. On a cold day it bleeds harder, and horses with moderate/severe EIPH are about 4x LESS likely to win and ~2x less likely to hit the board than mild/no-EIPH horses. Its true win probability therefore collapses relative to the Lasix-aided, warm-weather form line the crowd is pricing off of.
- **Who's on the other side:** Recreational bettors and speed-figure/form models keying on recent Beyer figures — all earned WITH Lasix in warmer conditions — take the other side by overbetting the horse. Even sharp handicappers who note the "first-time-off-Lasix" angle essentially never quantify ambient temperature or EIPH severity, and the tote crowd prices neither. Trainers and vets know the horse bleeds but are legally barred from treating it and cannot act on the information — a clean asymmetry favoring whoever mines the medication line plus the forecast.
- **Information lead:** Real and durable. Entries (which fix both the horse's Lasix status and the Lasix-prohibited race condition) publish 2-4 days before post; the temperature forecast is available days ahead. The tote never incorporates ambient temperature or the off-Lasix×cold interaction at any point, even at post time — so you identify the setup days before the market forms AND the edge persists all the way to pool close because nobody re-prices it. Lead is days over when (if ever) the book learns it.
- **Why still under-exploited:** The enabling structural change — HISA's race-day Lasix ban limited to 2yo and stakes races — has only existed since May 2023 and is still politically contested, so the "unprotected chronic bleeder" population is a genuinely new feature of the game. No commercial handicapping product (EquinEdge, TrackSmart AI, Alezan.ai, DRF) crosses an individual horse's bleeding history with hyperlocal race-day temperature; the AI tools chase figures and trip, not physiology-vs-weather. Extracting it requires manually stitching Equibase medication chart lines + "bled" footnotes + HISA bleeder lists + a weather API per track — fragmented, manual, unglamorous, and outside every existing data product.
- **Rough +EV:** Fires narrowly: a chronic bleeder, in a Lasix-prohibited (2yo/stakes) race, on a cold day, at a track carding such races — perhaps 0-3 qualifying horses per cold race-day at northern/winter meets. Where it fires the effect size is large (up to ~4x win-rate suppression), so fading a horse bet like a 3-1 second choice whose true chance is depressed 25-40% leaves a meaningful overlay across the rest of the field. Rough: +5-15% ROI on the against-tickets/lays when the setup triggers, at low volume — a specialist angle, not a high-frequency machine.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Biggest uncertainty: whether ambient temperature predicts race-day EIPH SEVERITY (not merely diagnosis risk) strongly and consistently enough at the single-race level to beat noise — an individual cold day does not guarantee a severe bleed. Secondary: volume is thin, and 2yos often lack an established bleeding history, concentrating the reliable plays on older stakes horses whose entire recent form was built on Lasix they can no longer use.

## 3. Ad-Reservation Surrender Index (FCC OPIF)

**Arena:** gambling — regulated/crypto prediction markets (Kalshi + Polymarket), individual-seat and chamber-control election contracts
**Domain:** Elections & prediction markets (Polymarket/Kalshi)

- **Data source:** The FCC Online Public Inspection File (OPIF) "Political Files" — the raw, per-station broadcast and local-cable ad contracts, order revisions, and CANCELLATION/non-renewal notices that campaigns, party committees (NRSC/DSCC/NRCC/DCCC), and super PACs (Senate Leadership Fund, etc.) file. The alpha event is specifically a cancellation or failure-to-renew of reserved fall airtime in the DMAs covering a competitive 2026 seat (and, inversely, brand-new reservations appearing). This is a distinct, faster, richer source than FEC ad-spend filings: OPIF captures super-PAC/dark-money issue ads, forward-dated reservations, and cancellations in near-real-time, none of which FEC shows early.
- **How to collect it:** publicfiles.fcc.gov REST API (developer portal) to enumerate every TV/radio/cable entity in each target DMA, then poll each station's "Political File" folder for newly uploaded or revised documents (each carries an upload timestamp). Download the order-form/contract/invoice/cancellation PDFs, OCR + parse advertiser name, flight dates, week count, and gross spend, and DIFF against the prior order to detect reductions, cancellations, or unconverted reservations. Attribute each advertiser to a partisan side via the FEC committee registry. Supplement with NCC (local-cable) order forms, also in OPIF. Mechanically this is ProPublica's "Free the Files" dataset — but automated, event-diffed, and wired to market prices instead of crowdsourced by hand.
- **The bet / trade:** On the specific-seat Kalshi/Polymarket contract: SHORT (buy the opponent's YES) the side that cancels or declines to renew reserved airtime in that seat's markets; LONG the side that books NEW reservations. Aggregate detected pullouts across the tipping-point seats to trade the Senate-control and House-control contracts. Horizon: days to ~3 weeks — hold until the pullout surfaces publicly and the seat reprices, or ride to Election-Day settlement.
- **Mechanism:** Ad money is the most honest signal in politics because it is costly and set by private internal tracking polls. Committees and PACs reserve airtime months ahead, then cancel or decline to renew when their own polling says a seat is unwinnable (or safely banked). A cancellation is a near-concession; a fresh reservation is a wager that the seat just became competitive. The contract change hits the station's OPIF political file almost immediately, and the win-probability implication is strong and directional — historically the side that pulls out loses that seat.
- **Who's on the other side:** Retail and semi-pro prediction-market traders anchored to public polls, topline forecasts (Cook/Sabato/538-style), and vibes; they reprice a seat only when a "Party pulls out of Race X" headline lands or the race is called. Even sharper desks that eyeball marquee ad cancellations rarely wire down-ballot OPIF filings to the specific thin seat contracts, and commercial ad-trackers (AdImpact, Vivvix/Kantar) sell cleaned data to campaigns and TV networks for ad intelligence — it surfaces as lagged journalism, not as market positioning.
- **Information lead:** Cancellations/revisions post to the station political file within roughly 24–48h of the contract change — typically several days ahead of the Axios/Politico/Hill scoop for marquee races, and for unreported down-ballot or single-market pullouts the OPIF is the ONLY early signal, giving days-to-weeks of lead before the market ever notices. You learn the "surrender" the moment the traffic order changes; the book learns it when a reporter writes it up or the race is called.
- **Why still under-exploited:** OPIF political files are thousands of non-standardized, per-station PDFs (order forms, invoices, hand-annotated revisions) with no clean structured feed — ProPublica had to crowdsource the OCR in its "Free the Files" project precisely because the data is public but unparseable at a glance. Cleaned commercial versions exist (AdImpact, Vivvix) but are sold to campaigns and networks, not connected to prediction markets, and no one runs an automated OPIF cancellation-detector feeding Kalshi/Polymarket seat contracts. The parsing friction IS the moat.
- **Rough +EV:** When a side pulls reserved airtime from a competitive seat in the final ~6 weeks, its win probability historically craters (NH-2022 Bolduc, AZ-2022 Masters, WV-2018 Morrisey all lost after their side's pullout). If the abandoned side is still priced ~30–40c when the cancellation files versus a true ~10–15%, that is a ~15–25 percentage-point edge per event. It fires an estimated 8–15 times per midterm cycle across competitive seats, plus off-year and special races. Thin-market liquidity caps position size (hundreds to low-tens-of-thousands of dollars per contract), so per-trade edge is large but absolute capacity is modest.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Biggest uncertainty: reliably parsing OPIF at scale AND separating a true "surrender" from benign cancellations — rate re-shopping, a shift of dollars to digital/streaming (which OPIF does not cover), or pulling from a genuinely SAFE seat. False positives erode the edge, and for marquee races the media scoop closes the lead fast, so the durable, repeatable edge concentrates in thin down-ballot, state, and single-market contracts rather than the headline Senate seats.

## 4. Lot Rot

**Arena:** financial-market (US single-name equities: RV / marine / powersports manufacturers — WGO, THO, PII, BC, MBUU, MCFT, PATK, LCII)
**Domain:** Single-name equity operational exhaust

- **Data source:** Real-time DEALER ON-LOT inventory for RVs, boats, and powersports — the physical units sitting on dealer lots right now, tracked per manufacturer/brand: unit counts, days-on-lot (listing age), model-year mix (aging), and list-price cuts / discount depth. This is the channel-inventory metric management itself guides on (Thor literally reports "North American independent dealer inventory ~75,000 units"; Winnebago's guidance cuts cite "measured dealer ordering" and "dealer caution on inventory replenishment"). Crucially, it is NOT the same as the incumbent paid product: Statistical Surveys Inc. (owned by Trader Interactive) sells lagged, revised RETAIL REGISTRATION data (sell-through from state DMVs), not real-time on-lot channel inventory (sell-in / lot fullness).
- **How to collect it:** Scrape the national trader marketplaces by make/model: RVTrader.com, BoatTrader.com, ATVTrader / PWCTrader / CycleTrader (all Trader Interactive), plus individual franchised-dealer inventory pages. Structured feeds exist for a small team: MarketCheck's Recreational Vehicles API advertises "real-time dealer inventory"; Boats Group / boats.com exposes a REST Inventory API; Apify has ready BoatTrader scrapers. Pull make/model/year/price/dealer/listing-date daily, dedupe by VIN/hull-ID, and build a per-brand index of (a) new-unit lot count, (b) median days-on-lot, (c) share of prior-model-year units (aging), (d) discount depth vs list. Map brands to parent tickers (e.g., Grand Design/Jayco/Airstream -> THO; Winnebago/Grand Design->WGO; Bennington/Sea-Ray/Boston Whaler/Mercury->BC; Malibu/Cobalt/Pursuit->MBUU).
- **The bet / trade:** Event-driven around quarterly prints. When your scraped on-lot index shows lot counts rising + days-on-lot lengthening + discounting deepening beyond the seasonal norm ahead of a print, get short (or buy puts) on the manufacturer 2-6 weeks before earnings and hold through the report/guidance. Reverse (cover/long calls) when lots are drawing down and aged model-year units clear — the setup for a wholesale restock and shipment beat. Primary names: WGO, THO (RV), BC, MBUU, MCFT (marine), PII (powersports); PATK/LCII as component read-throughs. Horizon: weeks (position into the print), event settlement at the guidance update.
- **Mechanism:** Dealers set wholesale orders off two things: how full their lots are and how fast units turn. When on-lot units climb, age, and require discounting to move, dealers slam the brakes on replenishment ordering. That directly cuts the manufacturer's WHOLESALE shipments and forces guidance down — the exact "measured dealer ordering / destocking" language that gaps these stocks 5-15%. On-lot inventory + aging + price cuts is the leading input; reported shipments and the guidance revision are the lagging output you can measure the cause of, continuously and in advance.
- **Who's on the other side:** Sell-side and consensus, who wait for (1) Statistical Surveys' lagged/revised RETAIL registration prints (measures sell-through, not channel fill) and (2) management's own quarterly channel-inventory disclosure on the call; plus passive/index and retail holders. They're slow because assembling a per-brand on-lot count + aging + discount index across thousands of fragmented dealer sites and four separate trader marketplaces is tedious, unglamorous engineering, and the packaged incumbent (SSI) sells the wrong metric (retail regs), so almost no one is nowcasting the channel-inventory number management actually guides on.
- **Information lead:** Daily/real-time vs. a quarterly disclosure: roughly 1-3 months ahead of the company's own channel-inventory number and guidance revision, and ahead of SSI's lagged, multi-week-revised retail registration reports. You watch dealer lots fill and units age in near real time while the Street is still working off last quarter's management color and stale registration scans.
- **Why still under-exploited:** The raw listing data is sold to BUILD marketplaces (MarketCheck, Boats Group), not as an equity signal; the recognized paid alt-data product in this space (Statistical Surveys/Trader Interactive) is RETAIL registrations, a different and lagged metric. No one packages a "dealer on-lot channel-inventory + aging + discount nowcast" as trading alpha the way Yipit/M Science do for consumer names. These are thinly-covered mid/small-caps in a fragmented, niche, manual-scrape corner — precisely the kind of operational exhaust that stays under-exploited.
- **Rough +EV:** Fires ~4x/yr across ~8 names (~30 setups/yr); only a subset show a channel signal materially diverging from consensus. Base-rate move on a guidance-cut/beat surprise in this group is high single to mid-double-digit percent single-day. Expect a handful of high-conviction, double-digit-edge setups per year plus many small ones; positioned via defined-risk options into prints, comfortably +EV if the index leads the disclosed channel-inventory delta even 60-65% of the time.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Biggest uncertainty: online listings blend new vs. used and don't cleanly separate wholesale sell-in from retail sell-through, some dealers under-list online, and you must strip out strong seasonality plus judge whether the channel read is already in consensus — so the signal is most reliably NEW information at inflection points and for the thinner-covered names, less so for a fully-telegraphed destocking cycle everyone is already modeling.

## 5. River-Temp Reactor Derate Nowcast

**Arena:** financial-market (European power: EEX French/German baseload futures + day-ahead auction; optionally the France-minus-Germany spread)
**Domain:** Energy & commodity physical telemetry

- **Data source:** Real-time French river water-temperature and river-flow telemetry, cross-referenced reactor-by-reactor against EDF's legal per-site thermal-discharge thresholds. The underexploited combination is: (1) EDF Open Data dataset "Seuils et limites des rejets thermiques aux abords des centrales nucleaires d'EDF" (per-site max downstream river temperature, e.g. 28C on the Garonne at Golfech, ~26-28C at Rhone/Loire/Moselle sites, plus min-flow limits); (2) Hub'Eau "Temperature des cours d'eau" API (near-real-time river temps, synced live since July 2022); (3) Hub'Eau "Hydrometrie" API (dense real-time river flow/debit at gauges up/downstream of each riverside reactor). This lets you compute a live per-reactor "distance to legal cutoff" that governs curtailment.
- **How to collect it:** All free REST/JSON-CSV: hubeau.eaufrance.fr (temperature + hydrometrie endpoints) for live river temp and flow; opendata.edf.fr dataset API for the threshold table per reactor; RTE eCO2mix / RTE data portal for real-time actual nuclear generation (to measure the realized derate); EDF's REMIT "Unavailability and message list" for the official curtailment notices you are front-running. Drive a simple river-thermal nowcast with ECMWF/Meteo-France air-temp and precip forecasts feeding each reactor's upstream gauge, geolocated to the ~19 riverside sites (Golfech, Bugey, St-Alban, Tricastin, Cruas, Blayais, Chooz, Cattenom, Civaux, the Loire cluster, etc.).
- **The bet / trade:** Long EEX French front-month baseload power futures and/or the day-ahead auction (alternatively long the France-minus-Germany baseload spread) when the nowcast projects river-temperature curtailment that the curve is underpricing; and the mirror trade: SHORT the day-ahead "heat premium" when a forecast hot spell will NOT actually breach thresholds because rivers are still cool or high-flow. Horizon: intraday/day-ahead out to front-month (days to ~3 weeks), concentrated June-September and worst in drought years.
- **Mechanism:** River temperature rises with a 2-4 day lag behind air temperature and is amplified nonlinearly by low flow. When the downstream temperature (or the plant's discharge delta-T) crosses the ASN legal limit, EDF is legally required to derate or shut the riverside reactor. France loses gigawatts of its cheapest baseload, its normal daytime export surplus collapses (in June 2026 it fell from ~11-12 GW to under 3 GW within days), and the marginal MWh flips to gas or imports. French wholesale power spikes (month-ahead moved up to ~+11.7% in the June 2026 episode) and France's usual discount to Germany compresses or inverts.
- **Who's on the other side:** Naive weather-driven power algos, CTAs, and macro flow that trade the heatwave AIR-temperature headline with no river-flow context, plus retail/CFD power flow and participants who wait for EDF's official REMIT confirmation. They are slow/wrong because curtailment is nonlinear in river flow: the same 35C spell barely bites after a wet spring (high flow, cool rivers) but is severe after drought (low flow, rivers already near threshold). Air-temp-only models therefore systematically over-price short hot spells on cool/high-flow rivers (your fade) and under-price prolonged heat on drought-stressed rivers (your press).
- **Information lead:** Roughly 1-4 days on the specific curtailment magnitude and timing versus EDF's REMIT unavailability message (which is typically issued only a few days to ~a week ahead and gets revised), plus a continuous accuracy edge over air-temperature-only models across the whole episode. You know which reactors will breach, by how much, and for how long, before the official notice and before the naive crowd reprices.
- **Why still under-exploited:** It is not a packaged product like Kpler/Vortexa/Genscape — there is no commoditized "river-temp-to-reactor-derate" feed. The edge requires bespoke assembly of three fragmented free sources (EDF's threshold table, sparse Hub'Eau temperature stations of which only ~50 are active, and separate flow gauges) plus reactor geolocation and a small river-thermal model. Because station coverage is patchy and per-reactor thresholds live in legal decrees, most participants fall back on crude air-temperature heat forecasts as a proxy rather than modeling the actual physical constraint.
- **Rough +EV:** Event-driven and seasonal: roughly 5-15 tradable episodes per summer (June-Sept), larger and more frequent in drought years. Each curtailment call the curve underprices can be worth several to low-tens of EUR/MWh on day-ahead / front-month; assuming a ~55-60% hit rate with disciplined sizing gives modest absolute EV but an attractive concentrated-summer Sharpe. The fade side (heat that won't breach) adds symmetric opportunities and reduces outright weather beta if expressed as the France-vs-Germany spread.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Biggest uncertainty: whether a small team's nowcast reliably beats well-resourced EU power desks that already model nuclear availability. Secondary risks: sparse real-time river-temperature station coverage (forcing flow + air-temp + EDF's published downstream temps as proxies), and ASN "security-of-supply" derogations that let EDF legally run reactors above the normal limit when the grid needs it — which loosens the derate-to-price mapping and injects noise. EEX power access is also a hurdle for non-institutional traders (needs an FCM, prop desk, or CFD route).

## 6. The Multisig Signature Window

**Arena:** financial-market
**Domain:** Crypto on-chain & behavioral

- **Data source:** Not-yet-executed transactions sitting in project treasury / foundation / DAO multisig queues: (a) off-chain proposals collecting signatures in the Safe Transaction Service, and (b) on-chain governance Timelock transactions already queued with a fixed execution ETA. These reveal a treasury's exact intent (recipient, amount, calldata) hours-to-days before the transaction actually lands on-chain.
- **How to collect it:** Two free, public feeds, no auth: (1) Safe Transaction Service REST API — GET api.safe.global/tx-service/{chain}/api/v1/safes/{safeAddress}/multisig-transactions/?executed=false (verified live: returns JSON with to/value/data/nonce/confirmations/confirmationsRequired/submissionDate; per-chain slugs eth, arb1, base, oeth, matic, etc.). (2) An Ethereum/L2 archive node or log-subscription (own node, or Alchemy/QuickNode free tier) filtering for Compound/Uniswap Timelock QueueTransaction(bytes32,address,uint256,string,bytes,uint256) and OpenZeppelin TimelockController CallScheduled events. Supporting infra you must build: a curated map of token -> its treasury/foundation Safe addresses + governance Timelock contracts (from docs, Etherscan tags, Safe app URLs) across chains; a calldata decoder to classify intent (ERC20 transfer vs proxy upgradeToAndCall vs param change); and a recipient label set (CEX deposit clusters, OTC desks, market-maker wallets, vesting/airdrop distributors) from Arkham/Etherscan tags plus your own deposit-address clustering. Poll every 1-5 min.
- **The bet / trade:** When a monitored treasury/foundation queues (but has not executed) a large ERC20 transfer whose decoded recipient resolves to a CEX deposit address, OTC desk, or vesting/airdrop distributor, SHORT the token via perpetual swap (or buy a short-dated put where options exist). Size to the smaller of borrow/liquidity limits. Entry on detection; hold through execution and the ~24-72h sell-through window, then cover. Mirror-image long setups exist for queued bullish calldata (proxy upgrade shipping a launched product, new Curve/Balancer gauge, major-protocol integration), but the bearish supply signal is the higher-conviction core trade.
- **Mechanism:** Multisig-governed treasuries cannot move funds atomically: they collect M-of-N signatures off-chain in the Safe service, or queue on-chain behind a Timelock, before anyone can execute. The full intent — who gets how much of what token — is legible at proposal time. A queued transfer to an exchange deposit address / OTC desk / distributor is net new sell-side supply that has not yet hit the tape. Price only reacts once the tokens actually move and are sold; you position in the gap between proposal and execution, front-running the mechanical supply shock the queue has already committed to.
- **Who's on the other side:** The liquidity providers and momentum traders on the perp who take the short side, plus everyone whose alerting fires only on executed transfers. Nansen Smart-Money/exchange-flow alerts, Arkham, DeBank, and CryptoQuant all trigger on confirmed on-chain movement — they are structurally blind to an off-chain Safe proposal and mostly do not decode Timelock queue calldata for treasury transfers. The tools that DO read the queue (Safe Watcher, BlockSec Phalcon Safe{Wallet} Monitor) are sold to the wallet's own owners to catch hacks, not to traders. So the other side literally does not have the data yet; they are slow because their pipelines are keyed on block confirmation, not signature-collection state.
- **Information lead:** Off-chain Safe queue: minutes to several days between first proposal and reaching the signature threshold + execution — treasuries with distributed, multi-timezone signers routinely take hours to 1-2 days. On-chain Timelock: a hard, deterministic delay (Compound 2 days; many DAOs 2-7 days) with the exact executable block window known in advance from the ETA. Versus the market, which learns only when the transfer confirms and the coins start selling, the lead is the entire signing/timelock window — hours to a week.
- **Why still under-exploited:** The reading infrastructure exists but is framed 100% defensively (protect your own multisig), so no one packages queued-multisig intent as a trading feed — there is no Kpler/Ortex equivalent for it. The work is unglamorous curation, not a clever model: you must hand-maintain a token->safe/timelock address map across a dozen chains, decode arbitrary calldata to separate a real sell from a benign internal reshuffle, and keep a fresh CEX/OTC/MM deposit-label set as addresses rotate. That fragmented, manual, per-chain plumbing is exactly the kind of moat that keeps quant desks (who want clean turnkey datasets) and retail (who watch price) out, leaving the pre-execution window on the table.
- **Rough +EV:** Event-driven and episodic. Across a watchlist of a few hundred mid/small-cap governance tokens, clean qualifying signals (sizable queued transfer to a genuine exchange/OTC/distributor endpoint) fire on the order of dozens per month. On the qualifying subset where the queued outflow is meaningful vs daily volume (>~0.5-2x ADV), expect roughly 55-65% directional hit rate and ~1-3% average adverse move captured per event over the 1-3 day window, net of false positives (internal moves, superseded/cancelled proposals). Edge concentrates in small-caps where borrow and depth cap size; large-caps absorb it. Realistic as a steady stream of small, low-correlation, repeatable event trades rather than any single big hit.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Biggest uncertainty is recipient classification: distinguishing a true sell (CEX/OTC/distributor) from a benign treasury reshuffle depends on a good, constantly-refreshed deposit-address label set, and mislabels turn into whipsaw. Secondary risks: coverage is patchy (some teams sign fully on-chain or via private relayers and never touch the Safe service; Timelock adoption varies), and queued != executed, so cancelled/superseded proposals cost you some entries. Liquidity and borrow on the smallest names — where the edge is largest — cap deployable capital.
