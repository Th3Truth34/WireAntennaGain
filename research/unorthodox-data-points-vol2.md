## Short interest, stock borrow & fails-to-deliver

### 1. FINRA bi-monthly short interest & days-to-cover
- **Signal:** Broker-dealers must report settlement-date short positions to FINRA twice a month; the data is published roughly nine business days later on FINRA and exchange websites, and is screened as short-interest-percent-of-float and days-to-cover (short interest divided by average daily volume).
- **Trade:** Buy squeeze candidates (high SI%-of-float, rising days-to-cover, positive catalyst) in stock or short-dated calls over days-to-weeks around publication dates; at the index level, use aggregate short interest to scale S&P 500 futures/SPY exposure over multi-week horizons.
- **Mechanism:** Reported short interest reveals crowded bearish positioning that is stale by construction, so the publication itself moves prices; when days-to-cover is high, any rally forces shorts to buy back into thin liquidity, amplifying moves, while high short interest also predicts negative returns because shorts are informed.
- **Evidence:** Rapach, Ringgenberg and Zhou, 'Short Interest and Aggregate Stock Returns' (Journal of Financial Economics 2016), found aggregate short interest to be arguably the strongest known predictor of market returns (out-of-sample R2 ~13%); the January 2021 meme-stock squeeze screens (GME reported near 140% of float short) were built directly off FINRA/exchange short interest files.
- **Access:** Free — FINRA equity short interest files and Nasdaq/NYSE publication schedules; mirrored by free sites (Fintel, MarketBeat). The catch is the ~2-week staleness, which vendors like Ortex/S3 exist to fill.

### 2. SEC fails-to-deliver (FTD) file and T+35 close-out cycles
- **Signal:** The SEC publishes twice-monthly, ticker-level CNS fails-to-deliver counts (with a few weeks' lag); traders map FTD spikes forward to Reg SHO Rule 204 close-out deadlines, including the T+35 window available for deemed-to-own sales and legacy market-maker fails.
- **Trade:** Buy stock or short-dated calls in names with large, persistent FTD spikes timed into the projected forced close-out window roughly 35 calendar days after the fail dates; horizon of days around each projected date.
- **Mechanism:** Large fails represent undelivered (synthetic) share supply sold into the market; when close-out obligations come due, the failing party must buy shares in, creating price-insensitive forced demand clustered around predictable dates.
- **Evidence:** Evans, Geczy, Musto and Reed, 'Failure Is an Option' (Review of Financial Studies 2009), documented an options market maker deliberately failing to deliver instead of borrowing hard-to-borrow stock; the SEC's October 2021 GameStop staff report analyzed GME FTD spikes, and 'FTD cycle' strategies were traded and widely documented across the 2021 meme-stock episode.
- **Access:** Free — SEC.gov 'Fails-to-Deliver Data' files (two half-month files per month); free aggregators such as ChartExchange chart per-ticker FTD history.

### 3. Reg SHO threshold securities lists
- **Signal:** NYSE, Nasdaq and FINRA publish daily lists of securities whose fails at NSCC exceed 10,000 shares and 0.5% of shares outstanding for five consecutive settlement days; time-on-list matters because 13 consecutive settlement days triggers mandatory buy-ins for market makers.
- **Trade:** Go long stock or calls in hard-to-borrow names approaching the 13-day mandatory close-out while borrow is unavailable (buy-in pressure), and treat chronic list residency as a red flag/short signal on multi-week horizons; day-count timing gives a days-scale catalyst.
- **Mechanism:** Threshold listing is a public flag of settlement stress and naked-short pressure in a specific name; the rule then converts that stress into forced future buying, while academically the listing itself marks overvalued, hard-to-borrow stocks that subsequently underperform.
- **Evidence:** Event studies of Regulation SHO found significantly negative abnormal returns in the 30-60 days after a stock joins the threshold list (e.g., work by Autore, Boulton and Braga-Alves on FTDs and overvaluation); Overstock.com's years-long threshold-list residency was the centerpiece of the mid-2000s naked-shorting controversy and litigation.
- **Access:** Free — daily threshold lists on NYSE.com, Nasdaqtrader.com, and FINRA's OTC threshold list page.

### 4. Ortex intraday borrow, utilization and cost-to-borrow
- **Signal:** Ortex aggregates securities-lending data from agent lenders and prime brokers into intraday on-loan quantity, lender utilization, cost-to-borrow, and a modeled daily short-interest estimate that bridges the gap between FINRA's bi-monthly prints.
- **Trade:** Enter squeeze longs (stock or near-dated calls) when utilization pins at ~100% and cost-to-borrow spikes in a rising name, and exit or fade when borrow pressure decays; horizons from intraday to about two weeks.
- **Mechanism:** Lending-market stress shows shorts are at capacity: 100% utilization means no incremental borrow to short, and exploding fees bleed existing shorts until they cover, so borrow-market data front-runs the covering flow that official short interest only reveals weeks later.
- **Evidence:** Ortex was the reference dataset of the GameStop/AMC squeezes — Reuters and Benzinga repeatedly cited its short-seller loss estimates (e.g., ~$1B in a week) and 100% utilization readings, and its January 22, 2021 estimate that ~140% of GME's float was short appears in the documented record of the squeeze.
- **Access:** Cheap — retail subscriptions at ortex.com for roughly $30-100/month; the institutional analogue (S3 Partners Black App) is sold via Bloomberg at institutional pricing.

### 5. Interactive Brokers stock-loan feed via iBorrowDesk
- **Signal:** IBKR publishes a machine-readable feed (FTP/API) of shortable share counts and indicative borrow fee rates per ticker, updated multiple times intraday; the free site iBorrowDesk archives and charts this single-broker inventory history.
- **Trade:** When IBKR shortable shares collapse toward zero and the fee rate jumps, take short-horizon squeeze longs or buy puts instead of shorting stock; shorts use it pre-trade to avoid names where a buy-in or fee spike is imminent; intraday to days.
- **Mechanism:** One large retail-facing broker's live inventory is a high-frequency canary for short-side crowding: availability dropping to zero precedes borrow-fee spikes, forced buy-ins, and squeeze dynamics before any official data updates.
- **Evidence:** iBorrowDesk (built on IBKR's public shortable-securities data) was a standard free dashboard during the 2021 GME/AMC episode as availability went to zero and fees spiked triple digits; IBKR has since productized the same data as its Securities Lending Dashboard for clients including hedge funds.
- **Access:** Free — iborrowdesk.com requires no account; the raw feed is IBKR's public shortable-stocks FTP, and the fuller dashboard comes with any IBKR brokerage account.

### 6. FINRA daily short-sale volume and the SqueezeMetrics Dark Index (DIX)
- **Signal:** FINRA's Reg SHO daily files report, for every ticker, the fraction of each day's off-exchange (TRF/dark-pool) volume marked as short sales; SqueezeMetrics dollar-weights this ratio across S&P 500 constituents into the Dark Index (DIX).
- **Trade:** High DIX readings are a buy signal for S&P 500 futures/SPY on a days-to-weeks horizon (low readings warn of weakness); single-name daily short-volume-ratio extremes are used for short-term mean-reversion entries.
- **Mechanism:** In dealer-intermediated dark venues a customer buy is printed as a market-maker short sale, so a high dark-pool short-volume ratio counterintuitively reveals passive institutional accumulation — inventory that supports prices in following weeks.
- **Evidence:** SqueezeMetrics' 'Short is Long' whitepaper documents the inversion and shows strongly positive forward S&P 500 returns after high DIX readings (~+9.7% annualized after high vs. negative after low readings); the indicator is widely tracked and has open-source replications on GitHub built from the raw FINRA files.
- **Access:** Free — FINRA publishes the daily short-sale volume files publicly, and the DIX itself is posted free each evening at squeezemetrics.com.

### 7. Options-implied borrow cost from put-call parity
- **Signal:** The gap between a stock's actual price and its synthetic price from conversions/reversals (put-call parity) implies the market-clearing stock-borrow fee in real time, computable from any live options chain before lending data or short interest updates.
- **Trade:** Treat sudden implied-fee spikes as a hard-to-borrow/squeeze alarm: express bearish views via puts (already priced for the fee) rather than stock shorts, avoid longs in names where high implied fees predict negative returns, and trade single-stock options on horizons of days to weeks.
- **Mechanism:** Options market makers hedge with stock and pass their borrow cost into option prices, so parity deviations reveal what shorting actually costs right now; high implied borrow fees identify overpriced, short-constrained stocks that subsequently underperform.
- **Evidence:** Muravyev, Pearson and Pollet show option-implied borrowing fees explain most 'options predict stock returns' anomalies ('Is There a Risk Premium in the Stock Lending Market?', Journal of Finance 2022, and companion JFE work); Ofek, Richardson and Whitelaw (JFE 2004) documented these parity violations in short-constrained dot-com stocks.
- **Access:** Free/cheap — computable by an individual from any real-time options chain (IBKR, Thinkorswim); clean historical implied-fee datasets (OptionMetrics) are institutional.

### 8. EU/UK public net short position disclosures (SSR registers)
- **Signal:** Under the EU Short Selling Regulation and its UK successor, any fund's net short position reaching 0.5% of a company's issued shares must be publicly disclosed by the next business day in national registers (BaFin, FCA, ESMA-linked registers), with each subsequent 0.1% change updated.
- **Trade:** Piggyback fresh disclosures by reputable activist/fundamental shorts via short positions, puts, or CFDs on European single names over days-to-weeks; conversely, go long for covering rallies when large disclosed positions step down below thresholds.
- **Mechanism:** The registers reveal the identity, size, and daily direction of informed short sellers name-by-name; disclosed shorts predict continued underperformance, disclosures cluster (later shorts follow first movers), and visible position reductions signal squeeze fuel being removed.
- **Evidence:** Jones, Reed and Waller, 'Revealing Shorts' (Review of Financial Studies 2016), found -5.23% 90-day abnormal returns after EU disclosures; tracker Breakout Point documented eight funds' disclosed Wirecard shorts (~8.7% of capital, including TCI and Marshall Wace) gaining ~€800m in a day as Wirecard collapsed in June 2020.
- **Access:** Free — national regulator registers and aggregators like shorttracker.co.uk for the UK; Breakout Point offers cheap aggregated EU coverage aimed at individual traders.

### 9. ETF operational shorting (ETF short interest >100% plus ETF-concentrated FTDs)
- **Signal:** ETF-level short interest exceeding total shares outstanding (SPDR S&P Retail XRT has printed 200-600% of shares outstanding) combined with fails-to-deliver concentrated in ETFs, revealing market makers selling ETF shares they have not yet created ('operational shorting').
- **Trade:** Trade short-term reversals in the ETF and its underlying basket: buy squeeze/covering rallies when extreme ETF short interest unwinds (XRT's SI fell from ~366% to under 175% in two weeks in November 2017, powering a retail-sector rally), and avoid holding shorts in such ETFs into creation-driven buy-ins; days-to-weeks horizon.
- **Mechanism:** A regulatory exemption lets ETF market makers sell shares first and create later, so measured short interest balloons without matching bearish intent; the eventual creations force buying of the underlying stocks, and the paper links operational shorting to short-term return reversals in the affected names.
- **Evidence:** Evans, Moussawi, Pagano and Sedunov, 'ETF Short Interest and Failures-to-Deliver: Naked Short-Selling or Operational Shorting?' (published as 'Operational Shorting and ETF Liquidity Provision', JFE) — ETFs are ~10% of US market cap but ~79% of all failures-to-deliver; Bloomberg documented the November 2017 XRT short-covering rally in retail stocks.
- **Access:** Free/cheap — built from the same public FINRA short interest and SEC FTD files screened at the ETF level; Fintel and ChartExchange chart XRT-style ETF short interest free.

## Synthetic shares & share-count forensics

### 10. SEC CNS Fails-to-Deliver files
- **Signal:** Twice-monthly SEC files listing, for every equity CUSIP, the aggregate shares that failed to settle at NSCC's Continuous Net Settlement system each day. Rising FTD balances flag naked/operational short supply and settlement stress in a specific ticker.
- **Trade:** Buy shares or short-dated calls in stocks with fast-accumulating fails (forced buy-in pressure ahead), or use fading FTDs as a signal a squeeze is exhausted; horizon days to a few weeks around the file's publication lag.
- **Mechanism:** Fails represent shares sold but never delivered — synthetic supply that must eventually be closed out under Reg SHO, converting into forced future buying; the data reveals hidden short pressure before it resolves in price.
- **Evidence:** Boni, 'Strategic delivery failures in U.S. equity markets' (Journal of Financial Markets, 2006) and Fotak, Raman & Yadav, 'Fails-to-deliver, short selling, and market quality' (Journal of Financial Economics, 2014) both built on this SEC dataset; retail forensics communities tracked GME FTD spikes throughout the 2021 GameStop episode, and the SEC's own Oct 2021 GameStop staff report analyzed the same data.
- **Access:** Free: the SEC publishes the CNS fails data as downloadable half-month text files on sec.gov, with roughly a two-to-four-week lag.

### 11. Reg SHO Threshold Securities List
- **Signal:** Daily lists published by NYSE, Nasdaq and FINRA (OTC) of securities with aggregate fails of 10,000+ shares and at least 0.5% of shares outstanding for five consecutive settlement days. Days-on-list is a countdown clock to mandatory close-outs.
- **Trade:** Track consecutive days on the list: names approaching the 13-settlement-day mandatory close-out face forced buy-ins, setting up long share/call trades over 1-3 weeks; conversely avoid initiating shorts in listed names where borrow recalls and buy-ins are likely.
- **Mechanism:** Once a stock is a threshold security for 13 settlement days, participants must close out fails by buying shares in the open market — a schedulable forced flow that pushes price up and squeezes remaining shorts.
- **Evidence:** Overstock.com's 10-Q filings and CEO Patrick Byrne's campaign made its 600+ consecutive days on the threshold list a famous public battleground over naked shorting; academic work (e.g., the UNCW study on Regulation SHO's efficacy and Boni's FTD research) documents abnormal returns and persistent fails for threshold-list stocks.
- **Access:** Free: published every trading day on nasdaqtrader.com, NYSE.com and FINRA's OTC threshold page.

### 12. ETF operational-shorting tracker (ETF short interest >100% plus ETF FTD spikes)
- **Signal:** Combines FINRA biweekly ETF short interest (often exceeding 100% of shares outstanding) with SEC FTD data on ETF CUSIPs to measure 'create-to-lend' and operational shorting by authorized participants who sell ETF shares before creating them, using the market-maker T+6 settlement exemption.
- **Trade:** Spikes in ETF fails predict imminent AP creation baskets (mechanical buying of the underlying stocks or bonds) and flag ETFs — especially corporate-bond ETFs like HYG/JNK — prone to NAV dislocations and liquidity air-pockets over days to two weeks; trade the ETF-vs-basket convergence or position in underliers ahead of creation flow.
- **Mechanism:** APs short ETF shares to absorb buy demand and delay creation to avoid financing costs, building a queue of fails; the eventual creations force purchases of every underlying security, and clustered operational shorting concentrates settlement risk in a few market-making firms.
- **Evidence:** Evans, Moussawi, Pagano & Sedunov, 'ETF Short Interest and Failures-to-Deliver: Naked Short-Selling or Operational Shorting?' (SSRN 2961954, presented at Wharton's Jacobs Levy Center) documents that ETFs are ~10% of market cap but 78% of all failures-to-deliver, and links operational shorting to market-maker liquidity provision and systemic risk.
- **Access:** Free: FINRA short interest files and SEC FTD files are public; commercial refinements (S&P Global ETF analytics) are institutional.

### 13. Deep-ITM buy-write / married-put 'reset transaction' detection
- **Signal:** Screens OPRA options tape for anomalous prints in hard-to-borrow, threshold-list stocks: large deep-in-the-money call volume in series with near-zero prior open interest, same-day buy-write pairs, married puts, or very short-dated FLEX options — the fingerprint of shorts 'resetting' Reg SHO close-out obligations instead of actually buying shares.
- **Trade:** Detected resets mean the real short position was never covered: stay long shares/calls in the affected name because the delivery obligation recurs and eventually forces genuine buy-ins; horizon days to a few weeks, keyed to threshold-list day counts.
- **Mechanism:** A short with a close-out obligation buys stock while simultaneously selling deep-ITM calls (or buying married puts) to a market maker who immediately reclaims the shares via exercise/hedging — the fail is papered over, not cured, so synthetic supply persists and buy-in pressure compounds.
- **Evidence:** SEC OCIE's August 2013 Risk Alert 'Strengthening Practices for Preventing and Detecting Illegal Options Trading Used to Reset Reg SHO Close-out Obligations' describes the exact tape signatures; SEC v. optionsXpress, Thomas Stern and customer Jonathan Feldman (2012 administrative proceeding) documented thousands of sham deep-ITM buy-writes used as resets.
- **Access:** Cheap: deep-ITM call volume and open interest are visible in any retail options chain, and full OPRA time-and-sales is available from low-cost vendors (e.g., Polygon.io, CBOE DataShop); no institutional feed required.

### 14. Ex-dividend 'dividend play' open-interest and volume spikes
- **Signal:** On the last cum-dividend day, market makers trade enormous matched lots of deep-ITM calls to position for dividend-spread arbitrage; the tell is call volume in a single series exploding to many times open interest the day before a stock or ETF goes ex-dividend.
- **Trade:** Overnight horizon: exercise (or sell) your own deep-ITM calls before ex-date rather than donating the dividend to writers, or join the arb by writing deep-ITM calls into the spike to capture the windfall from unexercised open interest; also flags temporarily distorted volume/liquidity signals in that name's options.
- **Mechanism:** A predictable fraction of deep-ITM call holders irrationally fail to exercise before ex-date; arbitrageurs who write calls get assigned on only part of their short position and pocket the dividend on the rest, so observed OI/volume anomalies map directly to a measurable transfer of money on a known date.
- **Evidence:** Hao, Kalay & Mayhew, 'Ex-dividend Arbitrage in Option Markets' (Review of Financial Studies, 2010) documents the strategy and the windfall from unexercised open interest; exchanges and FINRA later fined floor firms over 'dividend play' trades, and the massive cum-day volume spikes remain visible in OPRA data.
- **Access:** Free: options volume, open interest and ex-dividend calendars are available from any retail broker; executing the pure arb at scale requires exchange-member fee structures, but the exercise-avoidance and signal uses are open to individuals.

### 15. Transfer-agent / DRS share-count reconciliation (GameStop episode)
- **Signal:** Compares shares directly registered with the issuer's transfer agent (Computershare) against total shares outstanding and reported institutional/insider holdings, using the DRS counts GameStop began disclosing in every 10-Q/10-K from October 2021 (peaking around 76.6 million shares, ~25% of the company).
- **Trade:** Trade GME shares and short-dated options around each quarterly DRS disclosure: accelerating registration implied a shrinking lendable float (borrow-rate and squeeze risk), while the 2024 stall/decline deflated the thesis; horizon is the days around earnings filings.
- **Mechanism:** Shares moved into DRS leave the DTC/street-name pool that securities lenders draw on, so the disclosed count is a direct public measurement of removable lendable supply — and any excess of claimed beneficial ownership over the reconciled count is evidence of synthetic shares.
- **Evidence:** GameStop's SEC filings (10-K FY2023/FY2024) disclose the exact registered-holder share counts each quarter; the retail forensics site drsgme.org and outlets like Shacknews tracked every disclosure as a market event during 2021-2024 — the only large-cap real-time share-count census ever run by retail investors.
- **Access:** Free: DRS counts are in GameStop's EDGAR filings, and community tallies are public; any individual can also see their own transfer-agent holdings via Computershare.

### 16. Proxy over-voting and vote-reconciliation forensics
- **Signal:** Monitors annual-meeting vote tabulations, 8-K vote results, adjournments and tabulator disputes for cases where broker-submitted votes exceed shares outstanding or a broker's DTC position — direct evidence that more beneficial owners exist than real shares.
- **Trade:** An over-vote revelation in a heavily shorted name confirms large synthetic supply that must eventually be covered: go long shares or calls around contested meeting dates and recount news, over a days-to-weeks horizon; also a red flag to exit shorts before buy-ins.
- **Mechanism:** Shares lent to shorts get voted by both lender and new buyer, so uncovered over-votes quantify the hidden short/fail overhang; contested tallies can also force brokers to reconcile and recall loans, tightening borrow ahead of record dates.
- **Evidence:** A 2005 Securities Transfer Association review found over-voting at all 341 shareholder meetings it examined; the canonical episode is Taser International's 2005 annual meeting, where roughly 82 million votes were cast against only ~61 million shares outstanding amid intense short-selling, covered by Barron's and cited in the SEC Investor Advisory Committee's 'proxy plumbing' recommendation.
- **Access:** Free but irregular: vote totals appear in 8-K filings and proxy-contest litigation; there is no continuous feed, so this is an event-driven forensic source assembled from EDGAR, court dockets and press reports.

### 17. Real-time securities-lending feeds: cost-to-borrow, utilization and short-interest estimates (Ortex / S3 Partners / S&P Global Securities Finance)
- **Signal:** Daily-to-intraday data from lending-program participants: stock borrow fee, lendable-supply utilization, shares on loan, and modeled short interest as a percent of float — including anomalies like GameStop's S3-estimated 139% of float short in January 2021.
- **Trade:** Long squeeze candidates where borrow fees spike with utilization near 100% (shares plus weekly calls, intraday to two weeks); use borrow-fee collapse as the cover-is-done exit signal; short-side, avoid or size down crowded names before recalls.
- **Mechanism:** Borrow fee and utilization price the scarcity of real, deliverable shares; when demand to short exceeds lendable supply, marginal shorts face escalating financing costs and recall risk, making forced covering — and its price impact — mechanically predictable.
- **Evidence:** S3 Partners' Ihor Dusaniwsky's short-interest and borrow-fee updates (new GME shorts paying 80%+ fees, ~$20 billion in January 2021 mark-to-market losses) were quoted by CNBC and Yahoo Finance and visibly moved trading during the GameStop squeeze; Ortex's short-interest estimates became a standard retail squeeze-screening tool in the same episode.
- **Access:** Cheap to institutional: Ortex sells retail subscriptions for tens of dollars per month and Interactive Brokers exposes its borrow rates and shortable-share counts free to customers; S3 Partners (Bloomberg app) and S&P Global (ex-IHS Markit) Securities Finance are institutional products.

### 18. ADR pre-release and cross-listing parity forensics
- **Signal:** Tracks depositary-bank ADR outstanding counts against the ordinary shares actually held in custody, plus the ADR-versus-home-listing price gap and ADR borrow cost — detecting depositary receipts issued without underlying shares ('pre-released' ADRs) and the fungibility games built on them.
- **Trade:** Trade ADR-vs-ordinary convergence pairs over days to weeks when parity gaps blow out, and around structural events: the SEC's 2017-2019 pre-release crackdown shrank synthetic ADR supply, spiking ADR borrow fees and repricing dividend-arb and hard-to-borrow ADR names.
- **Mechanism:** Pre-released ADRs inflated a foreign issuer's tradeable U.S. share count, supplying cheap synthetic stock for short selling and dividend (withholding-tax) arbitrage; when regulators forced the receipts to be backed by real ordinaries, that supply vanished and borrow/parity relationships snapped back.
- **Evidence:** The SEC's ADR pre-release enforcement sweep produced 15 actions and nearly $432 million in settlements, including Deutsche Bank (~$75M, 2018), BNY Mellon (~$54M, 2019) and Citibank ($38.7M), with SEC orders describing how unbacked ADRs enabled abusive short selling and dividend arbitrage; Gagnon & Karolyi's multi-market arbitrage research documents the tradeable ADR-ordinary parity deviations.
- **Access:** Mostly free: DR outstanding and program data are published by depositaries (JPMorgan's adr.com, Citi, BNY) and prices for both lines are public, but executing cross-listing pairs requires a broker with foreign-market access and stock-loan capability — feasible for a sophisticated individual, easier for institutions.

## Financial-statement & XBRL forensics

### 19. Lazy Prices 10-K/10-Q text-diff signal
- **Signal:** Machine-computed similarity (cosine/Jaccard) between a company's current and prior 10-K/10-Q, flagging firms whose MD&A, risk-factor, litigation, or CEO/CFO-related language changed materially year over year. Managers only rewrite boilerplate when something real changed.
- **Trade:** Short the 'changers' and long the 'non-changers' in single-name equities or with puts, entering the day the filing hits EDGAR and harvesting the drift over the following days to weeks since there is no announcement-day pop to miss.
- **Mechanism:** Language changes encode bad news (litigation, executive turmoil, deteriorating operations) that almost nobody reads at filing time; the information seeps into prices slowly as earnings, news, and even bankruptcies later confirm it, creating a post-filing drift.
- **Evidence:** Cohen, Malloy & Nguyen, 'Lazy Prices,' Journal of Finance 75 (2020): the long non-changers / short changers portfolio earned up to 188 bps per month of alpha with no announcement effect; replicated publicly by Alpha Architect and adopted widely by quant equity desks.
- **Access:** Free for DIY — EDGAR bulk/full-text APIs plus a diff script; vendors such as sec-api.io and Bloomberg sell pre-computed filing-similarity scores at retail-friendly prices.

### 20. Beneish M-Score earnings-manipulation screen
- **Signal:** An eight-ratio probit score (receivables vs sales, asset quality, depreciation slowdown, accruals, leverage, etc.) computed from each new 10-K/10-Q that estimates the probability reported earnings are manipulated; scores above -1.78 flag likely manipulators.
- **Trade:** Short or buy puts on high-M-Score stocks specifically into their next earnings and filing windows, where misses, writedowns, and restatement announcements cluster — a repeated days-to-weeks catalyst trade; also used defensively to screen shorts' targets.
- **Mechanism:** Firms stretching receivables, capitalizing costs, and booking soft assets are borrowing earnings from the future; the arithmetic eventually forces a miss or restatement, and the score front-runs the public confession.
- **Evidence:** Cornell MBA students using Beneish's model publicly flagged Enron in 1998 while sell-side analysts still said buy; Beneish, Lee & Nichols, 'Earnings Manipulation and Expected Returns' (Financial Analysts Journal 2013) show high-M-Score stocks underperform within every size/value/momentum/short-interest decile and that the model flagged 71% of famous frauds in advance.
- **Access:** Free — the formula is public and computable from EDGAR XBRL data; pre-computed on retail screeners (GuruFocus, Portfolio123, quant-investing.com) for tens of dollars a month.

### 21. Sloan accrual anomaly traded at subsequent earnings dates
- **Signal:** Total accruals (reported earnings minus cash flow, scaled by assets) from each filing; extreme high accruals mark earnings propped up by their least persistent component.
- **Trade:** Short highest-accrual-decile stocks (long lowest) in concentrated multi-day windows around their next few quarterly earnings announcements, where a large share of the anomaly's annual return is realized — an event trade repeated each quarter rather than a buy-and-hold.
- **Mechanism:** Investors fixate on the headline earnings number and overweight accruals, which mechanically reverse; the correction arrives as negative surprises at subsequent announcements, so the mispricing resolves in short bursts.
- **Evidence:** Sloan (1996, The Accounting Review) documented both the anomaly and the concentration of returns around subsequent earnings announcements; it became a staple of quantitative equity funds and is catalogued with tradeable specifications on Quantpedia.
- **Access:** Free — computable from EDGAR XBRL or any fundamentals feed; exposed on cheap retail screeners, though crowding has shrunk the raw edge since the 2000s.

### 22. XBRL custom-tag rate / accounting reporting complexity (ARC)
- **Signal:** The count of XBRL tags and, especially, the share of non-standard 'extension' elements a company invents in its filings instead of using the standard US-GAAP taxonomy — a machine-readable fingerprint of how bespoke or machine-hostile its accounting is.
- **Trade:** Fade or avoid filers with spiking extension rates/ARC into their subsequent filings and earnings dates, and use it as a put-buying overlay on names also flagged by accrual or M-Score screens; payoffs arrive at restatement and material-weakness announcements over days to weeks.
- **Mechanism:** Bespoke tags mark either genuinely convoluted accounting or a deliberate attempt to make line items non-comparable; both correlate with weak internal controls, so high-ARC firms disproportionately produce the misstatement and material-weakness news that hits prices.
- **Evidence:** Hoitash & Hoitash, 'Measuring Accounting Reporting Complexity with XBRL' (The Accounting Review 2018) show ARC predicts misstatements and material-weakness disclosures better than other complexity proxies; follow-on studies and SEC staff commentary target excessive custom-tag use, and Calcbench commercializes the metric.
- **Access:** Free-to-cheap — SEC Financial Statement Data Sets and the XBRL US API are free; Calcbench and idaciti sell cleaned tag-level data at prices an individual can stretch to.

### 23. Auditor-change 8-K feed (Item 4.01) and audit-fee jumps
- **Signal:** Real-time monitoring of 8-K Item 4.01 filings for auditor dismissals and, critically, resignations — parsing for disagreement, 'unable to rely on management representations,' and going-concern language — plus outsized year-over-year audit-fee increases disclosed in proxies.
- **Trade:** Short the stock or buy puts on an auditor resignation 8-K within the filing-day/next-day window; event studies show the negative reaction continues for roughly 30 days, supporting a days-to-weeks hold.
- **Mechanism:** The auditor is the one outsider with private books access; walking away from guaranteed fees signals risk it refuses to underwrite. Abnormal fee jumps proxy for audit effort against elevated misstatement or fraud risk before anything is publicly wrong.
- **Evidence:** Event studies (Wells & Loudder 1997; Griffin & Lont, 'Do Investors Care About Auditor Dismissals and Resignations?', SSRN) document significantly negative filing-window returns and post-announcement drift for resignations; Audit Analytics (now Ideagen) built an institutional business selling exactly this feed.
- **Access:** Free for DIY via EDGAR full-text/RSS alerts filtered to Item 4.01 (cheap via sec-api.io tiers); the curated Audit Analytics database itself is institutional-only.

### 24. Form 12b-25 late-filing (NT 10-K / NT 10-Q) notices
- **Signal:** EDGAR NT 10-K/NT 10-Q filings in which an issuer admits it cannot file its report on time, including the stated reason — boilerplate versus substantive — and any language about internal investigations, accounting reviews, or restatement analysis.
- **Trade:** Short the stock or buy puts as the NT filing hits EDGAR — the documented reaction is in the two-day filing window — holding days to weeks when the excuse cites accounting reviews or investigations, since these names cluster with later restatements and delistings.
- **Mechanism:** A company that cannot close its own books on time is signaling control failures or a dispute with its auditor; vague excuses are adverse selection, as firms with benign reasons say so and firms hiding bad news go generic.
- **Evidence:** Bartov & Konchitchki, 'SEC filings, regulatory deadlines, and capital market consequences' and the 'No news is bad news' study (Journal of Accounting and Public Policy 2010) show significantly negative two-day abnormal returns for late filers with non-substantive attributions, versus benign reactions to well-explained delays.
- **Access:** Free — NT 10-K/NT 10-Q are distinct EDGAR form types with real-time RSS; filing-alert services push them for free or a few dollars a month.

### 25. SEC comment-letter releases (UPLOAD/CORRESP correspondence)
- **Signal:** Corp Fin review correspondence posted to EDGAR at least 20 business days after a filing review closes, revealing which accounting items the SEC challenged (revenue recognition, segments, non-GAAP adjustments) and how many rounds the company fought.
- **Trade:** Short or underweight names receiving revenue-recognition-related comment letters at public release and ride the documented negative drift of roughly 1-5% over the next ~50 trading days; also used as a target-generation screen for deeper short theses with put positions.
- **Mechanism:** The letters expose exactly which numbers the regulator doubts before most investors notice — they are hard to find on EDGAR and absent from company IR pages — and insiders demonstrably sell ahead of release, so the public posting lets outsiders catch up while attention is still minimal.
- **Evidence:** Dechow, Lawrence & Ryans, 'SEC Comment Letters and Insider Sales' (The Accounting Review 2016) document abnormal insider selling before release and the negative post-release drift; research and practitioner accounts note short-oriented hedge funds are active consumers of this correspondence.
- **Access:** Free — search form types UPLOAD and CORRESP on EDGAR full-text search; Audit Analytics and Bloomberg Law resell tagged versions to institutions.

### 26. Reg SHO fails-to-deliver file and daily threshold securities lists
- **Signal:** The SEC's twice-monthly per-security fails-to-deliver file (shares failing at CNS, published with a ~2-4 week lag) plus the daily Reg SHO threshold lists from Nasdaq/NYSE/Cboe naming stocks with persistent large settlement fails — direct evidence of settlement stress and synthetic share supply.
- **Trade:** Buy stock or calls in high-short-interest names showing large, persistent fails and threshold-list residency, front-running mandatory Rule 204 close-outs and buy-ins — a days-to-weeks squeeze trade; conversely, use it to avoid pressing crowded shorts.
- **Mechanism:** Persistent fails indicate shares sold that were never delivered; the 13-consecutive-settlement-day close-out requirement converts those fails into forced buying, which in a crowded short becomes reflexive squeeze fuel.
- **Evidence:** GameStop, January 2021: SEC data showed roughly $359 million of GME shares failed-to-deliver around Jan 28 (Bloomberg) and GME sat on the threshold list for an extended stretch, detailed in the SEC's GameStop staff report; the Overstock naked-shorting litigation of the 2000s ran on the same datasets.
- **Access:** Free — the SEC posts the FTD file on sec.gov and each exchange publishes its threshold list daily; the main honest caveat is the FTD file's multi-week publication lag.

### 27. Securities-lending borrow fees, utilization, and real-time short interest
- **Signal:** Daily-to-intraday stock-borrow cost, lendable supply, utilization, and modeled short interest from securities-finance data aggregators (S3 Partners, S&P Global/IHS Markit, DataLend, ORTEX), with Interactive Brokers' shortable-shares/fee file as a free proxy — showing what short sellers are actually doing and paying weeks before official exchange short interest.
- **Trade:** Avoid or fade longs when borrow fees on a name spike (predicts underperformance), and buy squeeze candidates when utilization approaches 100% and fees explode while price turns up; horizons of days to a few weeks, with knock-on trades in options where hard-to-borrow inflates put prices via put-call parity.
- **Mechanism:** Borrow demand is informed bearish positioning: rising fees reveal private negative information being expressed, while exhausted lendable supply plus rising prices means shorts must pay up or cover, mechanically fueling squeezes.
- **Evidence:** Cohen, Diether & Malloy, 'Supply and Demand Shifts in the Shorting Market' (Journal of Finance 2007) show shorting-demand increases predict -2.98% abnormal returns the following month using proprietary lending-desk data; S3 Partners' Ihor Dusaniwsky's GME short-interest estimates were market-moving headlines throughout January 2021.
- **Access:** Mixed — S3 and S&P Global securities-finance feeds are institutional (five-figure, via Bloomberg apps); ORTEX offers a retail tier around $25-50/month; iBorrowDesk republishes Interactive Brokers borrow rates and availability for free.

## Market plumbing & settlement exhaust

### 28. SEC CNS Fails-to-Deliver files + Reg SHO Threshold List
- **Signal:** Twice-monthly SEC files list per-CUSIP shares that failed to settle in NSCC's Continuous Net Settlement system; daily exchange threshold lists flag stocks with persistent large fails. Spiking FTDs reveal naked/operational shorting, synthetic share supply, and forced buy-in pressure building under Reg SHO close-out rules.
- **Trade:** Buy calls or shares in hard-to-borrow names showing rising FTDs ahead of the T+35 mandatory close-out date, or fade the pop after forced covering; horizon days to five weeks. GameStop's price waves in 2021 tracked these cycles.
- **Mechanism:** When market makers and short sellers fail to deliver, Reg SHO forces purchases by T+35; clustered close-out deadlines create predictable forced buying in illiquid, heavily shorted stocks, which then mean-reverts once the covering wave passes.
- **Evidence:** The SEC's October 2021 GameStop staff report documents GME's persistent FTDs and threshold-list residence; a 2023 Czech Journal of Economics and Finance paper, 'Confirmation of T+35 Failures-To-Deliver Cycles: Evidence from GameStop Corp,' empirically ties FTD cycles to GME price waves, and an ad hoc academic committee (Columbia/Notre Dame) critiqued the SEC for underweighting this data.
- **Access:** Free: SEC.gov publishes the FTD files (with a roughly 2-4 week lag) and NYSE/Nasdaq publish daily threshold lists; the lag is the main handicap versus institutional users.

### 29. Fed H.4.1 discount-window and 'other credit extensions' lines
- **Signal:** The Fed's Thursday 4:30pm H.4.1 release breaks out primary-credit discount-window borrowing, BTFP-type facility usage, and 'other credit extensions' (loans to FDIC bridge banks). Jumps reveal which parts of the banking system are quietly funding-stressed days before earnings or ratings actions say so.
- **Trade:** Short regional-bank equities/ETF (KRE, or single names via puts) and buy front-end rates (SOFR/fed funds futures, 2-year notes) when discount-window usage spikes; horizon days to a few weeks around the weekly release.
- **Mechanism:** Discount-window stigma means banks only borrow when private funding is failing; a rising line item implies deposit flight and forced balance-sheet shrinkage, which feeds bank equity selloffs and rate-cut repricing.
- **Evidence:** March 2023: discount-window borrowing hit a record ~$153bn and 'other credit extensions' ~$180bn for the SVB/Signature bridge banks, moves traders tracked weekly while shorting First Republic and KRE; Yale SOM's program on financial stability documents how the weekly H.4.1 print itself drives stigma and market reaction.
- **Access:** Free: published every Thursday on federalreserve.gov and in FRED, same moment for everyone — one of the few level playing fields in bank-stress data.

### 30. Standing Repo Facility take-up and the SOFR-IORB spread
- **Signal:** The NY Fed publishes daily results of its Standing Repo Facility operations and the full SOFR distribution (including 75th/99th percentile rates). SRF draws above token size, and SOFR printing well above interest on reserve balances, are real-time tripwires that dealer balance sheets and bank reserves have become scarce.
- **Trade:** Buy SOFR futures / receive front-end rates on evidence the Fed will halt QT or inject liquidity, position for the year-end and quarter-end repo turn, and de-risk leveraged basis trades; horizon intraday to weeks.
- **Mechanism:** Banks and dealers pay penalty rates at the SRF only when private repo is unavailable; visible take-up forces the Fed's hand on QT and reserve management, repricing the entire front end, while funding squeezes force levered relative-value players to unwind.
- **Evidence:** On October 31, 2025 SRF usage hit a record ~$50bn as SOFR printed 32bp over IORB, shortly after which the Fed confirmed the end of QT — an episode dissected in real time by rates desks (covered by Investing.com, Seeking Alpha's 'Standing Repo Facility Watch,' EconReporter); the September 2019 repo spike to 10% is the canonical precedent.
- **Access:** Free: daily operation results and SOFR percentile data on newyorkfed.org, published every morning.

### 31. Primary-dealer Treasury settlement fails (FR 2004 weekly data)
- **Signal:** The New York Fed's weekly FR 2004 statistics report cumulative fails-to-deliver and fails-to-receive in Treasuries, agencies and MBS among primary dealers. Rising fails in a sector signal specific-collateral scarcity and delivery squeezes invisible in outright prices.
- **Trade:** Trade the on-the-run/off-the-run spread, Treasury futures basis (cheapest-to-deliver squeezes), and repo specials when fails build in a maturity sector; horizon one to three weeks.
- **Mechanism:** When an issue is scarce, shorts cannot source it for delivery; fails cascade through settlement chains, driving the issue's repo rate special and its cash price rich versus the curve until the Treasury reopens the issue or the TMPG fails charge forces resolution.
- **Evidence:** Michael Fleming and Kenneth Garbade's NY Fed studies ('Explaining Settlement Fails,' 'When the Back Office Moved to the Front Burner') document the 2001 and 2003 fails episodes; fails topped $5 trillion cumulative after Lehman in October 2008, prompting the TMPG's 3% fails charge in May 2009, and the June 2014 10-year note fails spike drew market-wide attention.
- **Access:** Free: weekly FR 2004 releases on newyorkfed.org, with roughly a one-week lag — slower than dealers' internal views but tradable on multi-week squeezes.

### 32. NY Fed SOMA securities-lending auction results (per-CUSIP)
- **Signal:** Every business day at noon the Fed auctions specific Treasury CUSIPs from the SOMA portfolio to primary dealers, publishing which issues were borrowed and at what fee the same afternoon. High award rates on a specific bond are a public, daily read on which exact issue dealers are desperately short — the closest free proxy to the opaque specials repo market.
- **Trade:** Buy the scarce issue versus neighbors (on-the-run richening), trade the Treasury futures basis when the cheapest-to-deliver goes special, or lend the issue via repo; horizon intraday to a few weeks.
- **Mechanism:** Dealers only pay the Fed's penalty lending fee when they cannot borrow a CUSIP privately; scarcity depresses the issue's repo rate and richens its cash price, and futures shorts facing an unobtainable CTD must pay up to exit before delivery.
- **Evidence:** Macro Dispatch's 'Cornering the Curve' study built a specialness monitor from this data, documenting fee spikes such as 511bp on the 10-year on March 13, 2020 and 684bp on the 20-year in September 2024; the Fed itself relaxed SOMA lending limits during the 9/11 and 2008 fails crises (Fleming-Garbade) precisely because the facility is the marginal source of scarce collateral.
- **Access:** Free: daily operation results on newyorkfed.org markets pages; genuinely obscure — few outside rates desks parse the per-CUSIP awards.

### 33. ETF daily shares-outstanding create/redeem tape
- **Signal:** Every ETF's shares outstanding change daily as authorized participants create or redeem blocks in the primary market; the tape (issuer sites, exchange files, vendors) reveals real institutional cash entering or exiting an exposure, stripped of secondary-market noise. Big redemption runs in credit ETFs like HYG/JNK flag forced selling by fast money.
- **Trade:** Trade HYG/JNK/LQD direction or CDX-versus-cash-ETF relative value off multi-day creation/redemption streaks, and front-run the underlying bond flows APs must execute; horizon days to two weeks.
- **Mechanism:** Creations/redemptions force APs to buy or sell the underlying basket of illiquid bonds; because ETFs hold a large share of high-yield ownership, primary flows mechanically move cash-bond prices and spreads with a lag, and flow streaks proxy institutional risk appetite before it shows in spreads.
- **Evidence:** Academic work documents the channel — Han Xiao's 'The Economics of ETF Redemptions' (AEA 2023) and an EFMA 2022 paper showing HYG creation/redemption basket inclusion moves individual bond liquidity and holdings; ETF.com/FactSet publish the daily flows tape that credit desks and CFRA's flow-analysis research consume.
- **Access:** Free to cheap: issuers publish daily shares outstanding and ETF.com posts daily create/redeem flows; clean historical panels come from FactSet/Bloomberg at institutional cost.

### 34. Securities-lending borrow fees, utilization and recall data (Ortex/S3/Markit)
- **Signal:** Vendors aggregate wholesale stock-loan data — cost-to-borrow, utilization (share of lendable supply on loan), shares on loan, and loan recalls — updated intraday. Utilization near 100% plus a spiking borrow fee and recall activity reveals shorts trapped in a name before any squeeze is visible in price.
- **Trade:** Buy shares or calls in squeeze candidates (or exit shorts) when utilization saturates and cost-to-borrow accelerates; fade after fees collapse as covering completes. Horizon days to weeks in single-stock equities and options.
- **Mechanism:** Rising fees and lender recalls force involuntary short covering (buy-ins), converting short interest into forced demand; the borrow market therefore prices squeeze risk before the equity does.
- **Evidence:** S3 Partners' estimate that GameStop short interest hit ~141% of float in January 2021 was the most-quoted statistic of the episode, and Ortex cost-to-borrow/utilization prints drove AMC/GME trading through 2021 (fees over 200%); academically, Engelberg, Reed and Ringgenberg's 'Short-Selling Risk' (Journal of Finance 2018) shows Markit borrow-market data predicts returns.
- **Access:** Cheap for retail: Ortex subscriptions run tens of dollars a month and Interactive Brokers exposes live borrow fees/availability free to clients; the underlying S&P Global (Markit) and DataLend feeds are institutional-only.

### 35. NSCC clearing-fund margin dynamics (VaR + Excess Capital Premium charges)
- **Signal:** NSCC's clearing-fund formula — a public rulebook combining a VaR charge with an Excess Capital Premium that scales explosively when a broker's margin requirement exceeds its excess net capital — determines overnight cash calls on retail brokers. Modeling it against volatility in concentrated retail names predicts when brokers will be forced to restrict buying.
- **Trade:** When meme-name volatility implies crippling clearing charges for retail-heavy brokers, buy puts or short the squeeze names intraday-to-days in anticipation of position-close-only restrictions (and fade the restriction-driven crash once buying resumes).
- **Mechanism:** Clearing margin is the hidden choke point of the retail order flow machine: on January 28, 2021 Robinhood's $3.7bn NSCC call (including a $2.3bn ECP charge) forced it to ban buying in GME/AMC, collapsing GME from ~$483 to ~$112 intraday — plumbing, not fundamentals, set the price.
- **Evidence:** Documented in the SEC's October 2021 'Staff Report on Equity and Options Market Structure Conditions in Early 2021' and the House Financial Services Committee's June 2022 'Game Stopped' report, which records that Robinhood staff themselves used NSCC's publicly available ECP formula that morning; Risk.net covered the subsequent margin-waiver controversy.
- **Access:** Free but inferential: NSCC rules and the ECP formula are public on dtcc.com and daily volatility inputs are observable, but actual member charges are private — the trader reconstructs the stress, as Robinhood itself did.

### 36. Bond ETF premium/discount to NAV as a fire-sale and price-discovery gauge
- **Signal:** The gap between a bond ETF's exchange price and its official NAV, published daily (with intraday iNAV), measures stress in the underlying OTC bond market: deep discounts mean the ETF is trading ahead of stale bond marks and that APs are unwilling or unable to arbitrage — a visible fingerprint of forced selling.
- **Trade:** Buy heavily discounted credit ETFs (LQD, HYG, MUB) against a CDX or futures hedge and hold for basis convergence, or use the ETF's real-time price as the lead indicator for cash-credit direction; horizon days to two weeks, especially around policy responses.
- **Mechanism:** In a liquidity crunch the ETF is the only thing that trades, so its discount embeds true clearing prices before NAVs catch up; discounts of that size also invite policy intervention, and convergence is mechanical once APs resume redemption arbitrage.
- **Evidence:** In March 2020 LQD closed roughly 5% below NAV (March 11-12) and jumped ~6% after the Fed's March 23 corporate-credit announcement — an episode analyzed for the SEC's Fixed Income Market Structure Advisory Committee ('Pricing and Liquidity of Fixed Income ETFs in the Covid-19 Crisis') and in Haddad, Moreira and Muir's 'When Selling Becomes Viral' (Review of Financial Studies 2021).
- **Access:** Free: issuers publish daily premium/discount and NAV histories on fund pages, and iNAV ticks are disseminated during market hours; no subscription needed.

## Derivatives positioning & dealer-flow exhaust

### 37. CFTC Commitments of Traders (Disaggregated / Traders in Financial Futures)
- **Signal:** Weekly breakdown of long/short futures and options positions by trader class (dealer, asset manager, leveraged money) for every major US futures contract, published Friday 3:30pm ET as of the prior Tuesday. Extremes and week-over-week swings in 'leveraged money' net positioning reveal crowded speculative trades vulnerable to squeezes.
- **Trade:** Fade record spec extremes over days to a few weeks: e.g., buy VIX futures or VIX calls when leveraged-fund net-short VIX positioning hits records, or fade crowded net-longs in crude (CL), currency futures, or ES/NQ.
- **Mechanism:** Crowded one-sided speculative positioning must eventually be unwound through the market; when a shock hits, forced covering by the crowded side amplifies the move in the opposite direction, so positioning extremes predict asymmetric squeeze risk.
- **Evidence:** The record net-short speculative VIX futures position visible in COT data before the February 2018 'Volmageddon' is the canonical documented episode; academic work such as 'Trader positions in VIX futures' (Journal of Empirical Finance, ScienceDirect) shows trader positions are determinants of VIX futures prices and the VIX premium, and CTAs and macro funds have used COT as a sentiment input for decades.
- **Access:** Free: published on cftc.gov every Friday, with free historical archives and free charting front-ends (Tradingster, Macroption and similar).

### 38. Dealer gamma exposure (GEX) estimates from options open interest
- **Signal:** Daily estimate of aggregate market-maker gamma (and vanna/charm) exposure built from SPX/equity option open interest, indicating whether dealer hedging will dampen moves (long gamma) or amplify them (short gamma), plus the strikes where hedging flow pins price.
- **Trade:** In deep positive-GEX regimes, sell SPX/ES intraday range (iron condors, straddle sells) and trade mean-reversion toward the high-open-interest 'pin' strike into expiry; when GEX flips negative, buy straddles or trade ES momentum breakouts. Horizon: intraday to the next monthly expiration.
- **Mechanism:** Dealers who are net long gamma sell rallies and buy dips to stay delta-neutral, suppressing realized volatility; when net short gamma they hedge in the direction of the move, mechanically amplifying it — so knowing the sign and strike-map of dealer exposure forecasts the tape's behavior.
- **Evidence:** SqueezeMetrics' 2016-17 GEX white paper introduced the metric and documented that the largest intraday SPX moves cluster in negative-gamma states; Barbon & Buraschi's academic paper 'Gamma Fragility' and vendors SpotGamma, Tier1Alpha and MenthorQ (whose GEX levels are widely cited on sell-side desks and financial media) provide the commercial track record.
- **Access:** Cheap for individuals: SpotGamma/MenthorQ subscriptions run roughly $50-150/month, and a rough DIY version can be computed free from Cboe delayed open-interest data.

### 39. JPMorgan Hedged Equity (JHEQX) quarterly collar roll
- **Signal:** The ~$18bn JHEQX fund complex rolls a known three-leg SPX collar (long put spread, short OTM call, ~35,000-40,000 contracts per leg) on the last business day of each quarter, and its exact strikes are public via the fund's disclosed holdings — making one whale's entire derivative position and its roll date observable in advance.
- **Trade:** Trade SPX options and ES futures around quarter-end: position for spot pinning toward the short call strike in the days before the roll, and for the dealer delta-hedge impulse (and post-roll release) on the roll day itself. Horizon: a few days around each quarter-end.
- **Mechanism:** Dealers facing JPMorgan carry massive known gamma at the collar strikes; their hedging attracts price toward the short call strike as expiry nears, and the roll itself forces a large, schedulable delta and vega rebalance that other traders can position ahead of.
- **Evidence:** Extensively documented by SpotGamma ('JPM Collar' support articles and quarterly coverage), Bloomberg and Tickmill institutional commentary; the March 2023 and subsequent rolls were covered in real time by financial media as traders positioned around the publicly known strikes.
- **Access:** Free: JPMorgan Asset Management publishes the fund's holdings (the exact SPX strikes) on its website, and the roll calendar is fixed; SpotGamma-style paid services track it for ~$50-150/month.

### 40. DTCC swap data repository real-time public dissemination tape
- **Signal:** Under Dodd-Frank, every US interest-rate and credit-default swap trade is publicly disseminated within minutes (blocks with a 15-minute delay and capped notionals) on DTCC's free real-time DDR dashboard — price, size, tenor and structure of institutional OTC flow that was invisible before 2013.
- **Trade:** Trade Treasury futures, SOFR futures and swap-spread proxies intraday to multi-day after spotting outsized one-way received/paid flow or repeated capped-notional blocks in a tenor; trade CDX IG/HY index direction after large block prints.
- **Mechanism:** Large hedgers and macro funds execute swaps in slices; the tape reveals the footprint of an ongoing program before it is complete, and dealer hedging of those swaps flows directly into Treasury futures and cash bonds, so the tape front-runs the hedge pressure.
- **Evidence:** Clarus Financial Technology built the SDRView product on this tape and documents ('Patterns in the Swaps data') that clients get alerts on trade-type/size patterns to anticipate the next trade; ISDA's SwapsInfo service and academic/OFR research are built on the same public feed.
- **Access:** Free at the raw level: DTCC's real-time dissemination dashboard and ISDA SwapsInfo are public; cleaned, enriched versions (Clarus SDRView) are paid but priced for professionals rather than only large institutions.

### 41. Convertible bond new-issue delta-hedging pressure
- **Signal:** Announcements of new convertible bond issues (press releases, SEC 424B filings) flag imminent, mechanical short-selling: convertible arbitrage funds who buy the bond simultaneously short the issuer's stock in size to hedge delta, concentrated in the days around pricing.
- **Trade:** Short or underweight the issuing stock (or buy puts) from announcement through the first days after pricing, covering within roughly a week; alternatively buy the stock's listed-option volatility, which the convert issue tends to cheapen. Horizon: 1-10 days.
- **Mechanism:** Arb funds absorb most convertible issues and must sell short delta-equivalent stock immediately, creating predictable, information-free price pressure on the underlying that reverses once hedges are established.
- **Evidence:** Choi, Getmansky and Tookes, 'Convertible Bond Arbitrage, Liquidity Externalities and Stock Prices' (Journal of Financial Economics, 2009) documents arbitrage-induced short selling spiking at issuance using 1991-2005 issuance and short-interest data; the follow-up 'Convertible Bond Arbitrageurs as Suppliers of Capital' (RFS) confirms arb funds are the marginal buyer.
- **Access:** Free: issue announcements are on EDGAR and newswires in real time, and FINRA/exchange short-interest data is free; only the execution (locates for the short) requires a decent broker.

### 42. Korean autocallable issuance and knock-in barrier maps
- **Signal:** Korea's securities depository (KSD/SEIBro) and dealers publish issuance volumes of retail autocallable ELS notes, from which analysts map the cluster of knock-in barriers (typically 35-50% below initial index levels) on underlyings like HSCEI, EuroStoxx 50, KOSPI 200 and Nikkei — a map of where dealer hedging flips violently.
- **Trade:** When an underlying index approaches a dense knock-in zone, buy index puts and volatility (HSCEI/KOSPI 200/EuroStoxx options or variance) days to weeks ahead, anticipating the dealer hedging cascade; fade the move once the barrier cluster is cleared.
- **Mechanism:** As spot falls toward knock-in levels, autocallable issuers' books flip from short to long vega and their delta hedges force selling into the decline (peak-vega effect); crossing the barriers triggers abrupt re-hedging that amplifies the crash and spills into related markets like FX options.
- **Evidence:** The 2015-16 HSCEI collapse is the canonical episode: Risk.net documented dealers losing ~$300m ('Korean crunch: how HSCEI fall hammered exotics desks'), SocGen published the ~8,000 knock-in threshold, and hedging spilled into HKD FX options; Natixis set aside EUR160m on Korean autocallable hedging in 2020, and ex-Horseman CIO Russell Clark's newsletter uses Korean issuance data as a standing market-fragility indicator.
- **Access:** Cheap-to-institutional: Korean issuance statistics are free on KSD's SEIBro site (Korean language), while barrier-map analytics come from sell-side exotics desks and paid services like StructuredRetailProducts.com and Risk.net.

### 43. Index dividend futures as structured-product hedging exhaust
- **Signal:** Eurex EuroStoxx 50 dividend futures (and Nikkei/FTSE equivalents) trade at prices set largely by exotics-desk hedging of autocallable/structured-note dividend risk rather than by dividend fundamentals; the gap between futures-implied dividends and bottom-up analyst forecasts measures forced-flow stress.
- **Trade:** Buy deferred-year EuroStoxx 50 dividend futures (Eurex FEXD) when they collapse far below analyst dividend forecasts during equity selloffs, holding days to weeks for the mean reversion; also use a sudden dividend-future crack as a same-day warning to sell EuroStoxx futures, since it signals exotics desks are stopped out.
- **Mechanism:** Structured products embed long-dividend exposure that dealers hedge by selling dividend futures; in a selloff this hedging is mechanically ramped up regardless of actual dividend prospects, driving the futures to fire-sale levels that historically revert once the flow exhausts.
- **Evidence:** In March 2020 Eurex dividend futures volume hit a record 2.42m contracts as 2020-21 implied dividends halved; the Journal of Portfolio Management paper 'Euro Stoxx 50 Dividends — Reconciling Analyst Estimates and Dividend Future Prices' (2021) documents that hedging pressure, not fundamentals, drives the discount, and the 2008 dividend-futures crash showed the same dynamic.
- **Access:** Free to observe (Eurex publishes daily settlement prices; quotes on Investing.com); trading requires a futures broker with Eurex access, feasible for a well-capitalized individual.

### 44. VIX ETP end-of-day rebalancing flow (Volmageddon mechanics)
- **Signal:** Levered and inverse VIX ETPs (formerly XIV, today UVIX/SVIX/SVXY lineage) publish their holdings daily, so their mandatory end-of-day VIX-futures rebalance is computable in real time: required buying equals prior-day AUM times the day's percentage move in the futures index, executed into the 4:00-4:15pm ET settlement window.
- **Trade:** On days when front VIX futures are up sharply intraday, buy VIX futures (or UVIX/VXX) in the final hour and exit at/after the 4:15pm futures settlement, front-running the forced rebalance bid; the reverse on large down-moves. Horizon: hours.
- **Mechanism:** Both inverse and 2x-levered ETPs must buy VIX futures after a vol spike (shorts covering, longs re-levering) in a fixed, formulaic size at a known time, creating a feedback loop where anticipatory buying begets more required buying — the loop that terminated XIV on 5 February 2018.
- **Evidence:** Augustin, Cheng and Van den Bergen, 'Volmageddon and the Failure of Short Volatility Products' (Financial Analysts Journal, 2021) documents the ~280,000-contract ($280m vega) rebalance demand and the feedback loop on 5 Feb 2018; Cboe's own post-mortem 'After the Volpocalypse' and the SEC's subsequent scrutiny of the episode corroborate it.
- **Access:** Free: issuer websites publish ETP holdings daily and the rebalance formula is public (spelled out in prospectuses and the academic literature); execution needs only a futures or ETF brokerage account.

### 45. VIX settlement (SOQ) auction order imbalances
- **Signal:** Monthly VIX futures/options cash-settle to a Special Opening Quotation computed from a 30-minute SPX option auction on Wednesday mornings; the pre-auction imbalance feed and the pattern of volume in exactly the deep-OTM SPX puts that enter the VIX formula reveal whether large players are pushing the settlement print.
- **Trade:** Trade expiring VIX futures/options into settlement versus fair value, or submit SPX strategy orders into the SOQ auction itself to earn the liquidity premium paid by settlement-driven flow; alternatively hold offsetting SPX option positions through the open. Horizon: the final session before and the settlement morning itself.
- **Mechanism:** Because the VIX formula weights illiquid deep-OTM SPX options heavily and discontinuously, aggressive buying of those strikes at the auction mechanically moves the settlement value of the entire expiring VIX derivatives complex — so auction-morning order flow reveals (and transfers) tens of millions in settlement value.
- **Evidence:** Griffin and Shams, 'Manipulation in the VIX?' (Review of Financial Studies, 2018) documents settlement-time volume spikes concentrated in precisely the formula-relevant OTM options, moving settlements ~31bp on average over 2008-2015; the paper triggered CFTC/SEC scrutiny, investor lawsuits, and Cboe reforms to the SOQ process.
- **Access:** Mixed: settlement values, the auction imbalance data and the strike list are free from Cboe; participating in the SOQ requires a broker that accepts SPX opening strategy orders, and full order-book analysis needs paid Cboe data — feasible for a serious individual, easier for institutions.

## Ownership, insider & proxy forensics

### 46. Form 4 opportunistic insider cluster-buy signal
- **Signal:** Real-time parsing of SEC Form 4 filings (due within 2 business days of an insider trade) to flag windows where multiple distinct insiders buy in the open market, filtered to 'opportunistic' (non-routine, non-calendar-scheduled) traders whose history shows no fixed trading pattern.
- **Trade:** Buy the common stock or short-dated calls of cluster-buy names on the filing print and hold days to a few weeks; the drift after the filing hits EDGAR is where the short-horizon money is.
- **Mechanism:** Insiders trade on material private knowledge of their firm; several independent insiders buying simultaneously aggregates informed signals, and stripping out routine diversification sales isolates the informative trades, which the market underreacts to for weeks after disclosure.
- **Evidence:** Cohen, Malloy & Pomorski, 'Decoding Inside Information' (Journal of Finance, 2012) found opportunistic insider trades earn ~82bp/month value-weighted abnormal returns while routine trades earn zero; vendors InsiderScore (now VerityData) and 2iQ commercialized exactly this screen for hedge funds.
- **Access:** Free: EDGAR's real-time Form 4 feed and screeners like OpenInsider; institutional versions (VerityData, 2iQ) run thousands to tens of thousands of dollars a year.

### 47. 13F cloning and filing-day reveals
- **Signal:** Quarterly Form 13F-HR institutional holdings, published up to 45 days after quarter-end, mined for high-conviction managers' concentrated 'best ideas,' new positions, and position deletions; the four filing deadlines (mid-Feb/May/Aug/Nov) create a same-day event when a famous holder is revealed.
- **Trade:** Buy new top-conviction positions of cloned managers on the filing date and hold until the next filing cycle, or trade the intraday/next-day pop in smaller-cap stocks newly revealed in Berkshire, Pershing Square, or Tiger-cub filings; alpha decays with the 45-day staleness and with crowding.
- **Mechanism:** Skilled managers' concentrated bets contain information that survives the disclosure lag, and the filing itself triggers copycat demand — so both the clone portfolio and the announcement-day reaction are tradable, while decay comes from the lag and from too many cloners chasing the same names.
- **Evidence:** Antón, Cohen & Polk's 'Best Ideas' paper documents significant alpha in managers' most-concentrated holdings; AlphaClone ran an ETF (ALFA) on cloning, WhaleWisdom sells 13F backtesting, Goldman publishes its Hedge Fund VIP basket (GVIP), and Quantpedia catalogs the strategy and its decay.
- **Access:** Free from EDGAR and WhaleWisdom's basic tier; cheap ($30-100/month) for WhaleWisdom/HedgeFollow premium screens; the raw data is fully public.

### 48. Schedule 13D activist stake reveal
- **Signal:** Schedule 13D filings — required within 5 business days (10 before the SEC's Feb 2024 rule change) of an investor crossing 5% with intent to influence control — with Item 4 spelling out the activist's plans; full-text EDGAR alerting catches them seconds after dissemination.
- **Trade:** Buy the target's shares or near-dated calls on the filing print and hold from intraday through several weeks; the announcement pop plus continued drift means late entry on filing day has historically still paid.
- **Mechanism:** The filing reveals both a large informed buyer's completed accumulation and a coming governance catalyst; the market reprices the probability of operational change, payout increases, or a sale of the company, producing roughly +7% abnormal return around the reveal with no reversal.
- **Evidence:** Brav, Jiang, Partnoy & Thomas (Journal of Finance, 2008) documented ~7% announcement-window abnormal returns to 13D activism with no subsequent reversal; Ken Squire's 13D Monitor research service and 13D Activist Fund (DDDAX) monetize the signal commercially.
- **Access:** Free: EDGAR real-time full-text search and RSS alerts on '13D'; 13D Monitor's institutional research is subscription-only but unnecessary for the raw event.

### 49. 10b5-1 plan adoptions and terminations as insider tells
- **Signal:** Disclosures of executive Rule 10b5-1 trading-plan adoptions, modifications, and terminations — mandatory in 10-Q/10-K Item 408 since the SEC's December 2022 amendments, plus the 10b5-1 checkbox on Form 4 — read as tells: a hastily adopted plan followed by quick sales flags pending bad news, while a cancelled selling plan flags pending good news.
- **Trade:** Fade (short or buy puts on) companies where insiders adopt plans and start selling near the minimum cooling-off period, and lean long names where executives terminate selling plans; horizon of two weeks to two months around the next earnings or news event.
- **Mechanism:** The safe harbor lets insiders time plan starts and — crucially — cancellations using inside information without a trade occurring, so the metadata about the plan (adoption timing, single-trade plans, terminations) leaks the insider's private forecast even when individual trades look mechanical.
- **Evidence:** Jagolinzer, 'SEC Rule 10b5-1 and Insiders' Strategic Trade' (Management Science, 2009): plan participants beat the market ~6% over six months and terminations precede positive performance; Larcker, Lynch, Quinn & Taylor's Stanford study 'Gaming the System' identified quick-sale and single-trade plans as red flags, which the SEC cited in its 2022 rulemaking.
- **Access:** Free via EDGAR (10-Q/10-K Item 408 text and Form 4 checkbox field in the structured XML); VerityData sells a curated institutional version.

### 50. Insider pledged-share disclosures and margin-call cascades
- **Signal:** Proxy statement (DEF 14A) beneficial-ownership footnotes and 13D/G amendments disclosing how many insider shares are pledged as collateral for personal loans; in India, exchanges publish promoter share-pledge percentages outright. High pledge ratios map out forced-selling trigger levels below the current price.
- **Trade:** Short the stock or buy puts on high-insider-pledge names once the price approaches estimated loan-to-value triggers, riding the margin-call cascade over days to weeks; conversely, stand aside from longs until the collateral overhang clears.
- **Mechanism:** When pledged collateral falls below covenant levels, banks issue margin calls and can seize and dump the shares regardless of price, converting a modest decline into reflexive, non-discretionary supply — a mechanical accelerant that disclosure data lets you position for in advance.
- **Evidence:** Steinhoff, December 2017: Christo Wiese had pledged 628 million shares against a €1.6bn margin loan from Citi, Goldman, HSBC and Nomura; when accounting fraud surfaced the stock fell >60% in a day, lenders took control of and sold the collateral, and the pledge-driven unwind deepened the collapse — the canonical episode cited in Euromoney's and Moneyweb's post-mortems.
- **Access:** Free: pledge tables in DEF 14A filings on EDGAR and NSE/BSE promoter-pledge disclosures; aggregation across all US proxies is DIY or via institutional governance-data vendors (ISS, Audit Analytics).

### 51. CCASS custody-movement forensics (Hong Kong)
- **Signal:** HKEX publishes daily participant-level custody positions for every listed stock via the CCASS Shareholding Search; sudden overnight transfers of large blocks into obscure margin-lending brokers, or extreme concentration in a few participants, reveal undisclosed pledging, tight cross-held floats, and manipulation networks.
- **Trade:** Exit or short (via borrow, where available) Hong Kong small caps showing high CCASS concentration or block moves into margin brokers, on a days-to-weeks horizon; the payoff is avoiding or being short single-day collapses of 40-90% when pledged collateral is dumped.
- **Mechanism:** Cross-holdings kept below the 5% disclosure threshold and shares pledged to margin financiers are invisible in ownership filings but leave footprints in custody data; when one node of the network gets a margin call, the daisy chain of collateral liquidates simultaneously and the float-starved stocks crater.
- **Evidence:** David Webb's May 2017 report 'The Enigma Network: 50 stocks not to own,' built on CCASS and disclosure forensics, preceded the 27 June 2017 crash in which 40 of the 50 names collapsed (some >90% intraday), erasing ~US$6bn; Webb-site's CCASS Analysis System has archived and diffed the daily data since 2008.
- **Access:** Free: HKEXnews CCASS Shareholding Search (rolling 12 months) and the Webb-site database's historical participant-level analysis.

### 52. SEC fails-to-deliver data and Reg SHO threshold lists
- **Signal:** The SEC publishes security-level fails-to-deliver (FTD) counts twice a month, and NYSE/Nasdaq/Cboe publish daily Reg SHO threshold lists of stocks with persistent settlement failures (>=10,000 shares and 0.5% of shares out for 5 days) — a direct read on naked-short/synthetic share supply and settlement stress, including forced close-out and T+35 buy-in timing.
- **Trade:** Buy stock or calls in hard-to-borrow names accumulating FTDs as they enter the threshold list's 13-day close-out window or T+35 buy-in dates, playing squeeze mechanics over days to a few weeks; conversely, treat chronic threshold residence as a red flag on the long side.
- **Mechanism:** Persistent fails mean short-side participants have sold shares they cannot deliver; regulation eventually forces buy-ins of 'like kind and quantity,' converting hidden synthetic supply into scheduled forced demand — a calendarable flow that squeezes crowded shorts.
- **Evidence:** Evans, Geczy, Musto & Reed, 'Failure Is an Option' (Review of Financial Studies, 2009) documented options market makers strategically failing when borrow is expensive; GameStop's residence on the threshold list and its T+35 FTD cycles during the January 2021 squeeze were studied in a 2023 peer-reviewed paper ('Confirmation of T+35 Failures-To-Deliver Cycles: Evidence from GameStop Corp.').
- **Access:** Free: SEC FTD data downloads (sec.gov) and the exchanges' daily threshold lists; the FTD file's ~2-4 week publication lag is the main handicap.

### 53. European public net-short-position registers
- **Signal:** Under the EU Short Selling Regulation and its UK equivalent, any fund with a net short >=0.5% of a company's share capital must be publicly named in national registers (Germany's Bundesanzeiger, the FCA's daily short-positions spreadsheet, ESMA-linked registers), updated at every 0.1% step — a fund-by-fund, near-daily map of who is short what.
- **Trade:** Short alongside newly disclosed positions from high-hit-rate short sellers, or go long crowded names when the register shows sequential decrements (shorts covering) to time squeezes; horizon days to weeks, in single-name European/UK equities or their options and CFDs.
- **Mechanism:** Disclosed short increases come from informed managers and predict negative abnormal returns, while visible covering removes the marginal seller and can ignite squeezes; funds' documented bunching just below 0.49% also reveals where hidden short pressure sits.
- **Evidence:** Jank, Roling & Smajlbegovic, 'Flying Under the Radar' (Journal of Financial Economics, 2021), using Bundesanzeiger data plus confidential sub-threshold positions, showed disclosed shorts are informative and that investors deliberately stay below the threshold; Wirecard's register history was a widely tracked public record of the short campaign before its 2020 collapse.
- **Access:** Free: Bundesanzeiger, the FCA's daily CSV, and other national registers; aggregators like Breakout Point and Shortsell.nl repackage it cheaply.

### 54. UK Takeover Code Rule 8.3 dealing disclosures
- **Signal:** During any UK offer period, every person interested in >=1% of the target (or a stock bidder) must publicly file a Form 8.3 via RNS by 3:30pm the business day after each dealing, disclosing exact purchases, sales, prices, and derivative/CFD exposure — a daily ledger of merger-arb accumulation and distribution in live deal stocks.
- **Trade:** Trade the target's shares and the deal spread on a days-to-weeks horizon: sustained arb accumulation across 8.3 forms signals completion confidence or bump pressure supporting a tighter spread, while arbs flipping to net sellers flags deal risk before the spread blows out.
- **Mechanism:** Merger-arb funds are the informed marginal price-setters in deal stocks, and Rule 8.3 forces them to reveal every dealing with a one-day lag; aggregating the forms reconstructs positioning, crowding, and the supply available to a competing bidder — information the arbs themselves would rather hide.
- **Evidence:** The 2021 Wm Morrison bidding war between Fortress and CD&R generated daily Form 8.3 filings from Barclays, Natixis, and arbitrage funds that the financial press and arb desks tracked to gauge spread pressure and bump odds; the Takeover Panel's Rule 8 regime and public Disclosure Table exist precisely because these dealings move offer-period prices.
- **Access:** Free: all Form 8.3s hit the RNS newswire and the Takeover Panel's website lists companies in offer periods; aggregation across forms is DIY scraping or via institutional deal-data services.

## Credit, distress & bankruptcy paper trail

### 55. TRACE institutional-size bond prints leading distressed equity
- **Signal:** FINRA's TRACE feed disseminates every US corporate bond trade (price, size bucket, timestamp) near-real-time; the signal is large institutional-size prints in a stressed issuer's bonds hitting well below prior marks before the stock has moved. Retail odd-lot prints are noise — the information is in the block-size trades.
- **Trade:** Short (or sell) the issuer's listed equity and equity options within hours-to-days of anomalous downside bond prints, or buy the equity when bonds gap higher; also trade the bonds themselves off stale quotes. Horizon: intraday to two weeks.
- **Mechanism:** Credit-informed institutions (lenders, distressed desks) express negative private information in bonds first because that is where their mandate and information sit; for near-distressed firms equity value is a call option on the bond-implied enterprise value, so bond repricing mechanically implies an equity repricing that lags by hours to days.
- **Evidence:** Hotchkiss & Ronen (Review of Financial Studies, 2002) found high-yield bond prices are as informationally efficient as stocks; Ronen & Zhou (Journal of Financial Markets, 2013) show institutional-size TRACE trades in the most liquid 'top bond' lead stock returns, reconciling Downing, Underwood & Xing (2009). Distressed-debt desks routinely trade this bond-equity lead-lag.
- **Access:** Cheap: FINRA publishes TRACE data free with short delay on its Market Data Center and full historical files; true real-time TRACE feeds come bundled with many pro terminals and some retail bond platforms.

### 56. Single-name CDS runs and curve inversion (1y/5y) as a bank-insider tell
- **Signal:** Daily single-name CDS spreads across the curve; the specific signals are (a) abrupt spread widening with no public news and (b) the 1-year point trading above the 5-year (inversion), plus dealers switching quoting from running spread to upfront points — all marks of jump-to-default fear.
- **Trade:** Short the reference entity's equity or buy short-dated puts within days of an unexplained CDS run; credit traders buy protection or short the cash bonds. Curve inversion trades play out over days to a few weeks (GM/Ford 2005, monolines and Lehman 2008 are the templates).
- **Mechanism:** Relationship banks holding material non-public information (covenant breaches, revolver usage, failed refinancings) hedge in the OTC CDS market first, so CDS repricing embeds insider information before equity; inversion means the market is pricing default sooner rather than gradual decay, which historically precedes equity collapse.
- **Evidence:** Acharya & Johnson, 'Insider Trading in Credit Derivatives' (Journal of Financial Economics, 2007) documents information flowing from CDS to stocks for negative credit news, increasing with the number of relationship banks. The 2005 GM/Ford downgrade episode and 2008 monoline/Lehman CDS runs are the canonical market events.
- **Access:** Institutional-only in practice: clean single-name CDS marks come from S&P Global (formerly IHS Markit) or ICE and cost five figures; individuals can only proxy it via news reports of CDS levels and listed HY index products (CDX-tracking ETFs).

### 57. Covenant-loophole intelligence (Covenant Review 'trapdoor' flags)
- **Signal:** Lawyer-grade analysis of individual bond indentures and credit agreements flagging which instruments permit collateral-stripping: J.Crew-style IP drop-downs to unrestricted subsidiaries, Serta-style non-pro-rata uptiers, Chewy/Envision loopholes. Covenant Review (now CreditSights/Fitch Solutions) maintains a database scoring ~98% of index leveraged loans for these provisions.
- **Trade:** When a flagged issuer shows stress, sell or short its loans/unsecured bonds (via desks or HY ETFs against it) and position in the debt most likely to be primed or to participate in the exchange; on announcement of a liability-management exercise, the excluded tranches gap down over days. Horizon: days to a few weeks around LME headlines.
- **Mechanism:** Ratings and spreads do not price document loopholes until they are exercised; a sponsor exploiting a trapdoor transfers collateral value away from existing creditors overnight, so knowing ex ante which paper is 'primeable' predicts which prices gap on the announcement and which side of a coercive exchange wins.
- **Evidence:** Covenant Review built its franchise flagging the J.Crew 2016-17 IP transfer that let junior bonds leapfrog term lenders (documented in King & Spalding's 'The Original Trap Door' and NYU's 'Loan Market Response to Dropdown and Uptier Transactions'); CreditSights' recurring 'Lens on Loopholes' reports quantify J.Crew/Serta/Chewy/Envision loophole share across the loan index for subscribing investors.
- **Access:** Institutional-only: Covenant Review/CreditSights subscriptions run tens of thousands of dollars and are sold to funds and desks; individuals can partially replicate by reading indentures on EDGAR, but the curated loophole database is paywalled.

### 58. Credit-card ABS master-trust monthly reports (Form 10-D / monthly 8-K)
- **Signal:** Securitization trusts of Discover, Capital One, Synchrony, Amex, Citi and Chase must file monthly performance data on their credit-card receivables: charge-offs, 30/60/90-day delinquencies, payment rate, yield and excess spread. It is effectively a monthly, audited nowcast of consumer credit quality published mid-quarter.
- **Trade:** Trade card-issuer equities (COF, SYF, and historically DFS) and short-dated options around the monthly release when trust charge-offs/delinquencies inflect versus trend; also position consumer-lender baskets ahead of quarterly provisioning surprises. Horizon: days around the filing to the next earnings date.
- **Mechanism:** The trust receivables are a large, representative slice of the issuer's total book, so trust-level deterioration statistically leads the companywide credit metrics and loan-loss provisions reported at quarterly earnings — traders who parse the 10-D get the earnings surprise weeks early.
- **Evidence:** Discover's monthly trust data (Discover Card Execution Note Trust) is filed as 8-Ks/10-Ds on EDGAR and is covered by wire services as market-moving ('Discover Financial Reports Monthly Credit Card Data'); ABS research shops like Capital Advisors Group publish trust charge-off and excess-spread tracking, and bank analysts have long modeled quarterly credit costs directly from master-trust filings.
- **Access:** Free: all 10-D and monthly 8-K trust filings are on SEC EDGAR; an individual can scrape them or set EDGAR alerts at zero cost.

### 59. CLO trustee-report constraints (CCC buckets and OC-test cushions) as a forced-selling map
- **Signal:** Monthly CLO trustee reports disclose each vehicle's loan-by-loan holdings, its CCC-rated bucket versus the typical 7.5% cap, and overcollateralization (OC) test cushions. Thin cushions across many CLOs holding the same loan flag that loan as a candidate for mechanical, price-insensitive selling on the next downgrade.
- **Trade:** Short (or avoid) loans and the bonds/equity of issuers whose loans are widely held by constrained CLOs ahead of expected downgrades to CCC; buy the same loans, BKLN/SRLN-type loan ETFs, or CLO mezzanine tranches after the forced-sale flush, playing the documented price rebound. Horizon: one to several weeks around downgrade waves (March-April 2020 template).
- **Mechanism:** OC-test and CCC-bucket rules force CLO managers to sell downgraded loans regardless of view or price to protect their junior fees and equity distributions; because CLOs hold overlapping portfolios, downgrades trigger correlated fire sales that push prices below fundamentals and then revert — a predictable forced flow, not an information event.
- **Evidence:** Elkamhi & Nozawa, 'Fire-Sale Risk in the Leveraged Loan Market' (Journal of Financial Economics, 2022) uses CLO trustee-report holdings to show constrained CLOs sell CCC-downgraded loans and widely-held loans suffer temporary price depreciation; the COVID-2020 downgrade wave made these dynamics front-page in leveraged-finance markets.
- **Access:** Institutional mostly: full trustee-report libraries and holdings analytics come via Intex, Moody's Analytics or KopenTech at institutional prices; individual noteholders get reports from trustees, and individuals can only approximate via listed CLO funds' disclosures.

### 60. Bankruptcy Rule 3001(e) claim-transfer notices (who is accumulating the estate)
- **Signal:** Every transfer of a filed claim in a Chapter 11 case must be noticed on the docket under Bankruptcy Rule 3001(e), and claims agents (Kroll, Epiq, Stretto) publish live claims registers. Aggregating these notices shows which distressed funds are buying which class, how fast, and at what implied concentration — positioning data, not fundamentals.
- **Trade:** Follow accumulation by sophisticated loan-to-own funds into a specific class: buy the same bonds or claims, or buy/short the post-reorganization equity and when-issued securities as plan control becomes clear. Horizon: days to weeks around plan milestones (disclosure statement, plan votes, confirmation).
- **Mechanism:** Distressed specialists do months of recovery work before buying; their claim purchases reveal a private valuation of the estate and telegraph who will control the reorganization, which predicts class-level recoveries, plan design (who gets equitized) and hence prices of the traded debt and new equity.
- **Evidence:** Ivashina, Iverson & Smith, 'The Ownership and Trading of Debt Claims in Chapter 11 Restructurings' (Journal of Financial Economics, 2016) built exactly this dataset from claims-agent registers across 136 bankruptcies and links ownership concentration to outcomes and recoveries; Jiang, Li & Wang, 'Hedge Funds and Chapter 11' (Journal of Finance, 2012) documents hedge-fund entry points driving reorganization outcomes.
- **Access:** Cheap: claims registers are free on Kroll/Epiq/Stretto case websites and 3001(e) notices cost cents per page on PACER (free under the quarterly fee waiver); the work is in the scraping, not the price.

### 61. Vendor and customer trade-claims marketplace pricing (Claims Market / Xclaim)
- **Signal:** Live bid/ask indications for unsecured trade and customer claims against bankrupt estates, published by brokers like Cherokee Acquisition's Claims Market and the Xclaim platform. It is a real-time, dollar-denominated market estimate of recovery on estates with no listed securities — and pre-petition, weak vendor-claim bids on a stressed retailer signal suppliers losing faith.
- **Trade:** Buy underpriced claims outright, or use claim-price moves to trade related listed instruments — the debtor's bonds, post-reorg when-issued equity, or listed proxies (crypto equities during FTX) — within days of the claims market repricing on estate news. Horizon: days to weeks per catalyst.
- **Mechanism:** Claims buyers are specialists doing deep recovery analysis; their bids aggregate private diligence into a public-ish price that converges toward plan value ahead of official disclosure statements, so a step-change in claim pricing front-runs formal recovery announcements and related securities.
- **Evidence:** FTX customer claims tracked on Cherokee's Claims Market rose from roughly 10-20 cents in early 2023 to 73 cents by December 2023 as the estate's recovery emerged, covered by Bloomberg ('FTX Claims Reach 73 Cents on the Dollar on Key Trading Venue') and CoinDesk via Xclaim; distressed funds documented by DL News ('vulture firms') built nine-figure positions this way before the ~100%+ payout plan.
- **Access:** Free-to-cheap for the signal: Claims Market publishes indicative pricing openly and Xclaim published claim-sale data; actually buying claims requires meeting broker minimums (typically tens of thousands of dollars) but no institutional status.

### 62. SEC fails-to-deliver files and Reg SHO threshold lists (synthetic share supply)
- **Signal:** The SEC publishes twice-monthly, security-level fails-to-deliver (FTD) counts at CNS, and exchanges publish daily Regulation SHO threshold lists of securities with persistent large fails. Spiking FTDs reveal naked shorting, option market-maker exemption abuse, and settlement stress — synthetic share supply invisible in reported short interest.
- **Trade:** Short or buy puts on hard-to-borrow stocks whose FTDs are spiking (documented overvaluation), or position long for close-out squeezes when a heavily-failing stock hits the threshold list and Reg SHO Rule 204 forces buy-ins. Horizon: days to a few weeks per FTD cycle.
- **Mechanism:** When borrowing is costlier than failing, market makers and shorts deliver nothing, inflating effective float; persistent fails both mark stocks that are overpriced due to short-sale constraints and set up mechanical forced buy-ins once mandatory close-out triggers, creating predictable pressure in both directions.
- **Evidence:** Evans, Geczy, Musto & Reed, 'Failure Is an Option: Impediments to Short Selling and Options Prices' (Review of Financial Studies, 2009) shows options market makers deliberately fail in hard-to-borrow names; Autore, Boulton & Braga-Alves (Financial Review, 2015) link FTD spikes to overvaluation and negative subsequent returns; the GameStop 2021 episode made FTD files and the threshold list a retail-forensics staple.
- **Access:** Free: SEC FTD files download from sec.gov twice monthly (two-week lag) and NYSE/Nasdaq threshold lists publish daily; borrow-fee context is cheap via brokers (IBKR) or retail services like Ortex.

### 63. 8-K revolver-drawdown disclosures (the corporate 'dash for cash' tripwire)
- **Signal:** Material credit-line drawdowns must be disclosed in SEC 8-K filings (Items 1.01/2.03); full-text EDGAR monitoring for revolver draws, especially full draws by low-BBB and high-yield issuers, captures management's private assessment of its own liquidity in near real-time.
- **Trade:** On a defensive full drawdown, short the issuer's equity or buy puts and buy CDS/short its bonds versus sector peers within a day of the filing; in stress regimes trade the basket of drawers against non-drawers. Horizon: days to a few weeks while the market digests the liquidity signal.
- **Mechanism:** A company that fully draws its revolver is telling you it fears losing capital-markets access or a downgrade to junk (fallen-angel risk); the draw itself accelerates bank exposure and rating pressure, so the filing is both a distress signal and a distress catalyst, repricing equity and credit in the following sessions.
- **Evidence:** Acharya & Steffen, 'The Risk of Being a Fallen Angel and the Corporate Dash for Cash in the Midst of COVID' (Review of Corporate Finance Studies, 2020; NBER w27601) documents daily firm-level drawdowns in March 2020 concentrated in downgrade-risk BBBs; the March 2020 revolver run (Boeing's $13.8bn draw, followed by cruise lines and airlines) was traded exactly this way and each draw hit the tape via 8-K.
- **Access:** Free: EDGAR full-text search on 8-K filings for drawdown language costs nothing and can be automated; the only cost is building the alerting.

## Government procurement & spending tape

### 64. GAO bid-protest docket (filings, CICA stays, and 100-day decisions)
- **Signal:** Every federal bid protest filed at the Government Accountability Office appears in a searchable public docket with the protester, the contract at issue, and a statutory 100-day decision deadline — creating a pre-scheduled binary catalyst on who actually keeps a multibillion-dollar award. A protest filing also triggers an automatic stay of contract performance, freezing the announced winner's revenue.
- **Trade:** Pairs trade the protester against the announced winner (e.g., long BA / short NOC or EADS in 2008; MSFT vs AMZN around JEDI in 2019-20) into and on the GAO decision date; horizon days to a few weeks around filing, stay, and ruling.
- **Mechanism:** A sustained protest can reverse a mega-award and force a recompete, swinging a decade of backlog between two rivals; because the decision deadline is statutory, the event date is knowable in advance while the outcome is not priced.
- **Evidence:** On June 18, 2008 GAO sustained Boeing's protest of the $35B KC-X tanker award to Northrop/EADS, citing 'significant errors'; Boeing shares rose on the ruling while EADS fell 5.2% on the tanker setback, and the award was ultimately rebid and flipped to Boeing in 2011. The Amazon protest litigation over the $10B JEDI cloud award (2019-2021) similarly whipsawed Microsoft and Amazon headlines.
- **Access:** Free — GAO's bid-protest docket search at gao.gov shows filings, case status, and due dates in near real time; decisions are published the day they are released.

### 65. BARDA / ASPR Strategic National Stockpile procurement orders
- **Signal:** Purchase orders and sole-source notices from BARDA/ASPR for stockpile medical countermeasures (smallpox antivirals, anthrax vaccines, mpox vaccines), visible via SAM.gov intent-to-sole-source postings, ASPR announcements, and same-day company 8-Ks. For the handful of biodefense names involved, these orders ARE the income statement.
- **Trade:** Buy SIGA, Emergent BioSolutions, or Bavarian Nordic ADRs on stockpile-order headlines, or position when an outbreak makes new procurement near-certain; horizon intraday to a few weeks per order cycle.
- **Mechanism:** Companies like SIGA derive essentially all revenue from government stockpile contracts, so each delivery order is a direct, near-lossless earnings revision for a micro/small cap; outbreaks (mpox 2022) telegraph the next round of domestic and allied-government orders before they print.
- **Evidence:** SIGA's 19C BARDA contract contemplates up to $630M of TPOXX stockpiling; during the 2022 mpox outbreak SIGA ran to its highest level since 2011 across successive sessions as U.S. and international procurement orders rolled in (Seeking Alpha, FiercePharma, company 8-Ks), and the stock reliably gaps on each new BARDA/DoD order press release.
- **Access:** Free — SAM.gov solicitation and J&A notices, ASPR/BARDA announcements, and SEC 8-Ks; the only edge cost is monitoring latency.

### 66. FEMA disaster-response contract tape (emergency awards and amendments)
- **Signal:** FEMA-funded emergency awards and their contract amendments after hurricanes and disasters — grid repair, debris removal, tarps, generators — visible in FPDS/USAspending disaster-spending pages, agency releases, and recipient 8-Ks. Amendment velocity shows a small-cap's disaster windfall compounding in near-real time.
- **Trade:** Long small-cap responders (e.g., Mammoth Energy TUSK in 2017-18, Generac around landfall seasons) as emergency awards and upsizing amendments print; flip short on procurement-irregularity or clawback headlines. Horizon days to a few weeks.
- **Mechanism:** Emergency procurement bypasses normal competition, so awards land suddenly and can exceed a small company's entire market cap; the same irregular process later attracts DHS OIG/FBI scrutiny, making the tape a two-way signal (windfall on the way up, investigation risk on the way down).
- **Evidence:** Mammoth Energy's Cobra unit won an emergency PREPA grid contract (FEMA-funded) that grew from $200M in Oct 2017 to $945M by Feb 2018, driving TUSK sharply higher; shares then fell over 10% in three days on WSJ reporting of a DHS steering investigation and over 45% in two days on the June 2019 FBI criminal-inquiry report (documented in securities litigation records).
- **Access:** Free — USAspending.gov disaster emergency-spending tracker, FPDS award/mod records (few days lag), FEMA press releases, and company 8-Ks.

### 67. State DOT monthly bid lettings
- **Signal:** State transportation departments (TxDOT, FDOT, ALDOT, etc.) publish monthly 'letting' schedules and results — the dollar value of highway projects put out to bid and the winning bidders — which lead actual aggregates shipments and contractor backlog by one to two quarters.
- **Trade:** Position in aggregates and roadbuilding names — Vulcan Materials (VMC), Martin Marietta (MLM), Construction Partners (ROAD), Granite (GVA) — ahead of earnings when lettings in their footprint accelerate or stall; horizon a few weeks into the print.
- **Mechanism:** Lettings are the order book for public highway work: a surge in a state's lettings mechanically becomes aggregates volume and paving backlog for the quarry duopoly in that footprint, so summing letting calendars nowcasts revenue before management guides.
- **Evidence:** Nashville boutique Thompson Research Group built its heavy-materials sell-side franchise on state letting and heavy-materials channel surveys; ARTBA publishes monthly state bid-letting value data, and Martin Marietta's own CEO commentary (July 2023) attributed record first-half results to record state DOT budgets and lettings.
- **Access:** Free from each state DOT's letting-results webpage (aggregation is the work); ARTBA data is a cheap subscription, while TRG-style synthesized coverage is institutional.

### 68. State Medicaid managed-care RFP intent-to-award notices and protest dockets
- **Signal:** States re-procure multibillion-dollar Medicaid managed-care contracts via RFPs, and the intent-to-award notices post on state procurement portals (Virginia eVA, Florida DMS, Texas ESBD) — often before the losing insurer's press release — followed by a formal protest window that can reverse the award.
- **Trade:** Trade Molina (MOH), Centene (CNC), Elevance (ELV), UnitedHealth (UNH) on award-day postings and again on protest outcomes; horizon intraday on the notice to a few weeks through the protest cycle.
- **Mechanism:** A single state contract can represent billions in annual premium revenue for a Medicaid pure-play, so the award list mechanically re-rates multi-year EPS; because states publish scoring sheets and protest filings, the reversal risk is also readable before the market fully prices it.
- **Evidence:** In 2024 Molina lost re-procurements in Virginia's Cardinal Care and Florida's Medicaid program and publicly challenged both losses (Healthcare Dive), pressuring the stock; conversely its April 2021 Ohio Medicaid wins were a positive catalyst — the win/loss/protest cycle around state awards is a recurring documented mover of MOH and CNC.
- **Access:** Free — state procurement portals and Medicaid agency award pages; trade press (Healthcare Dive) covers within hours, but reading the portal posting directly is faster.

### 69. CMS program-integrity contract awards (RAC / audit contractor tape)
- **Signal:** CMS awards for Recovery Audit Contractor (RAC) regions and related Medicare/Medicaid program-integrity work, announced on CMS.gov procurement pages and via FPDS, which flow to a handful of thinly traded microcaps whose earnings depend on one or two such contracts.
- **Trade:** Buy the winning microcap — historically Performant Financial (PFMT) or HMS Holdings (HMSY, since acquired) — on the award posting; fade on protest or re-procurement risk. Horizon intraday to days.
- **Mechanism:** For a sub-$300M market-cap contractor, an 8.5-year CMS RAC region is a step-change in contracted revenue and effectively a franchise grant (contingency fees on recouped Medicare claims), so the award prints directly into the stock with little analyst coverage to arbitrage it away.
- **Evidence:** Performant Financial shares rose 5.1% intraday when CMS re-awarded it the RAC Region 1 contract in April 2021 (InvestorsObserver; BusinessWire release), and it subsequently repeated the pattern on the Region 2 award in Nov 2022 and a New York State RAC award in 2024.
- **Access:** Free — CMS.gov RAC program pages, FPDS/USAspending records, and company press releases; the edge is simply watching an obscure corner of the tape nobody covers.

### 70. VA / DoD electronic-health-record mega-award and milestone tape
- **Signal:** The procurement saga around VA and DoD EHR modernization — award announcements, signing delays, congressional strategic-review pauses, and renegotiations of the $10B+ contracts — tracked through VA announcements, congressional hearings, and trade press. Delay and resumption headlines are as tradeable as the award itself.
- **Trade:** Trade Cerner (CERN, now Oracle ORCL) and Leidos (LDOS, which won the $4.3B DoD DHMSM EHR contract with Cerner in 2015) on award, delay, pause, and restart headlines; horizon days around each milestone.
- **Mechanism:** A decade-long, ten-figure sole-source health-IT contract is large enough to move even a mid-cap's growth rate, so the market prices the overhang during delays and re-rates on signature; every subsequent rollout pause or renegotiation reopens the same repricing channel.
- **Evidence:** Cerner stock jumped 3.4% at the open when the VA signed the delayed $10B EHR contract in May 2018, with analysts saying it removed 'a significant overhang' (Healthcare Dive); the contract's earlier delays had already forced Cerner to cut 2018 projections, showing the tape moved the stock in both directions.
- **Access:** Free — VA press releases, congressional hearing schedules, FedScoop/Healthcare Dive trade coverage; no paid feed needed.

### 71. Contract-termination / de-obligation tape (DOGE 'wall of receipts' and FPDS stop-work mods)
- **Signal:** The reverse side of the awards tape: terminations for convenience, stop-work orders, and de-obligation modifications in FPDS — scraped at scale in 2025 from DOGE's published cancellation lists ('wall of receipts') and GSA directives targeting named consulting firms. It reveals revenue being removed from specific tickers before any company disclosure or earnings print.
- **Trade:** Short or underweight exposed govt-services names — Booz Allen (BAH), Accenture (ACN), Leidos (LDOS), ICF International (ICFI), Maximus (MMS) — when their contracts appear on termination lists; horizon days to a few weeks ahead of guidance cuts.
- **Mechanism:** Termination mods hit FPDS and agency postings weeks before the revenue loss shows up in guidance, so mapping cancelled contract numbers to prime contractors front-runs the earnings revision; concentration risk (BAH is ~98% government revenue) amplifies the move.
- **Evidence:** In spring 2025 the Pentagon cancelled $5.1B of consulting deals — about $4B held by Booz Allen, Accenture, and Deloitte — and 'Booz Allen, Accenture stocks fall on Pentagon contract cancellations' (Yahoo Finance); Goldman downgraded BAH citing the cancellations, and BAH later confirmed the damage with roughly 2,500 layoffs. Traders and journalists cross-referenced doge.gov receipts against FPDS to identify exposed tickers.
- **Access:** Free — FPDS termination/de-obligation modification records, doge.gov postings, and GSA memos; community-scraped datasets circulated on GitHub, and vendors like HigherGov package the same records cheaply.

### 72. Army Corps of Engineers bid-opening abstracts (dredging low-bidder reveals)
- **Signal:** USACE districts hold public bid openings for dredging and coastal-protection jobs and post bid abstracts naming every bidder and the apparent low bidder — often weeks before the formal award or any company press release. In a four-player Jones Act dredging oligopoly, the abstract tells you who just won backlog.
- **Trade:** Buy Great Lakes Dredge & Dock (GLDD) or Orion Group (ORN) when abstracts show them low bidder on large jobs (port deepenings can run ~$279M, like GLDD's record Charleston II award), before the award press release and quarterly 'low bids pending award' disclosure; horizon days to a few weeks.
- **Mechanism:** Low bidder on a USACE job converts to awarded backlog with high probability, and for a ~$500M-cap company a single deepening project is material; because the abstract precedes the 8-K/press release, the information exists publicly before most holders see it.
- **Evidence:** GLDD's own releases institutionalize the metric — e.g., '$265.3 million of low bids and options pending award' as of Q1 2025 and '$181.6M low bid' updates — confirming the market trades the bid-to-award pipeline; the underlying district bid abstracts are the primary source those disclosures summarize, and GAO protest records (B-421676) show rivals fighting over these exact bid results.
- **Access:** Free but laborious — bid abstracts on USACE district websites and SAM.gov contract-opportunity pages; no consolidated feed, which is why the inefficiency persists.

## Banking & money-flow telemetry

### 73. Fed H.4.1 discount-window and emergency-facility borrowing
- **Signal:** The Fed's Thursday 4:30pm ET H.4.1 release shows weekly aggregate borrowing at the discount window, plus telltale lines like 'other credit extensions' (FDIC bridge-bank loans) and, in 2023, BTFP usage. A spike reveals that some bank somewhere is quietly funding-desperate — stress the borrower is actively trying to hide.
- **Trade:** On a borrowing spike, buy puts on KRE/regional bank singles, buy front-end rate futures (SOFR/2-year notes) on flight-to-quality; horizon is the Thursday print through the following one to two weeks. Reserve-scarcity readings also feed repo/bill trades.
- **Mechanism:** Discount-window stigma means banks only borrow when private funding has dried up, so the weekly aggregate is a smoke detector for hidden runs; because the H.4.1 only reports district-level aggregates, markets sell the whole plausible-suspect cohort until the borrower is identified, then reprice single names.
- **Evidence:** Week of March 15, 2023: record $152.85B of discount-window borrowing (vs $4.58B prior week, beating the 2008 record of $111B) printed on H.4.1 as SVB/Signature collapsed, and regional banks sold off with each Thursday release. Armantier, Ghysels, Sarkar & Shrader, 'Discount Window Stigma during the 2007-2008 Financial Crisis' (Journal of Financial Economics 2015), quantified stigma at 44-126bp; Yale SOM's program on financial stability documents that the weekly report itself sustains the stigma traders exploit.
- **Access:** Free — federalreserve.gov H.4.1 release and FRED, every Thursday 4:30pm ET; individual-borrower identities come only with a two-year lag, which is exactly why the aggregate print moves markets.

### 74. Fed H.8 weekly commercial-bank balance sheets (deposit-flight census)
- **Signal:** Friday 4:15pm ET release of aggregated assets and liabilities of US commercial banks, split large vs small/domestic vs foreign: deposits, borrowings, C&I loans, securities. Week-over-week deposit changes at 'small domestically chartered banks' are a running census of silent deposit walks.
- **Trade:** Trade KRE and regional-bank single names (and 2-year Treasury futures) off the Friday print into Monday's open; during a stress episode the small-bank deposit line is a weekly catalyst for one-to-two-week positioning.
- **Mechanism:** Deposit flight forces banks to replace cheap funding with expensive wholesale money or to shrink lending, crushing net interest margins and raising failure odds; the H.8 reveals which size-cohort is bleeding before individual banks disclose anything, letting traders front-run quarterly filings.
- **Evidence:** During the March-April 2023 crisis the H.8 was a market event: NY Fed Liberty Street Economics ('Bank Funding during the Current Monetary Policy Tightening Cycle', May 2023) documented markets 'paying particular attention' to the weekly deposit reallocation from regional banks to money funds and G-SIBs, and financial media traded each Friday's small-bank deposit number against KRE.
- **Access:** Free — federalreserve.gov/releases/h8 and FRED, weekly with an 8-day lag.

### 75. FHLB advance and debt-issuance telemetry (the 'lender of next-to-last resort' gauge)
- **Signal:** Federal Home Loan Bank system debt issuance (trackable near-daily via the FHLB Office of Finance) and member-level advance balances from call reports and 8-Ks. Because stigmatized banks tap FHLB advances before they dare touch the Fed's discount window, surging FHLB issuance is an earlier, stealthier stress gauge.
- **Trade:** On an issuance surge, short the regional banks known to be heavy FHLB users (equity or puts on names like the 2023 cohort) and buy T-bills/front-end futures; when weekly issuance collapses back, cover — a days-to-weeks crisis-arc trade.
- **Mechanism:** A bank losing deposits pledges mortgages and MBS to its FHLB for advances, and the FHLBs fund those advances by issuing discount notes into the market — so system-wide issuance mechanically telegraphs the size of a bank run in near-real time, before any bank admits distress.
- **Evidence:** The week of March 13, 2023 the FHLB system issued a record $304B of debt (roughly double the Fed's $165B of crisis lending), and Bloomberg ran the reverse signal in April 2023 ('FHLB Debt Issuance Shrinks... Signal of Easing US Bank Crisis'); SVB had grown its San Francisco FHLB advances to $30B before failing. Ashcraft, Bech & Frame's 'The Federal Home Loan Bank System: The Lender of Next-to-Last Resort?' (JMCB 2010) documented the same pattern in 2007-08.
- **Access:** Free-to-cheap — FHLB Office of Finance publishes issuance and combined financials, quarterly member advances sit in FFIEC call reports; real-time discount-note flow tracking is easier with a Bloomberg terminal (institutional).

### 76. SEC Form N-MFP money-market fund security-level holdings
- **Signal:** Monthly SEC filings listing every security each US money-market fund holds — issuer, CUSIP, yield, maturity. Reading them across funds reveals which banks are quietly losing access to CP/CD funding, and which funds are reaching for yield in fragile names.
- **Trade:** Short the equity or buy CDS-proxy puts on banks whose unsecured paper is being rolled off by prime funds; a multi-week trade refreshed each monthly filing cycle. In 2011 the trade was short French bank ADRs/equity as US funds cut them.
- **Mechanism:** Money funds are hair-trigger creditors: when they cut a bank's CP/CD lines the bank must pay up elsewhere or shrink, and disclosed run-off precedes public funding-crisis headlines; N-MFP makes this creditor behavior visible security-by-security with only a short lag.
- **Evidence:** Chernenko & Sunderam, 'Frictions in Shadow Banking: Evidence from the Lending Behavior of Money Market Funds' (Review of Financial Studies 2014, originally 'The Quiet Run of 2011'), used N-MFP holdings to show the 2011 investor run on funds exposed to eurozone banks; Fitch's monthly MMF-exposure reports built on the same data were market-moving for French bank stocks in 2011. Crane Data commercializes the feed for money-market professionals.
- **Access:** Free — every N-MFP filing is on SEC EDGAR monthly (within 5 business days of month-end); cleaned/aggregated versions via Crane Data or iMoneyNet subscriptions (cheap-to-institutional).

### 77. SNB weekly sight deposits (FX-intervention tripwire)
- **Signal:** Every Monday at 9am CET the Swiss National Bank publishes total sight deposits — the balances commercial banks hold at the SNB. Because FX intervention is settled by crediting these accounts, the week-over-week change is a near-real-time confession of how many francs the SNB printed (or drained) fighting the exchange rate.
- **Trade:** Trade EUR/CHF and USD/CHF spot or futures on the Monday release: a jump in sight deposits confirms the SNB is selling francs (fade CHF strength); flat deposits during CHF appreciation signal the SNB is tolerating it (stay long CHF). Intraday-to-two-week horizon.
- **Mechanism:** Central banks hide intervention until quarterly/annual reports, but the plumbing leaks: francs sold against euros must land in domestic banks' sight-deposit accounts within the week, so the release converts secret central-bank flow into a public number that resets intervention expectations and moves the cross.
- **Evidence:** A canonical FX-desk indicator since the 2011-2015 EUR/CHF 1.20 floor: Reuters ('Swiss sight deposits jump spurs talk of central bank currency intervention') and bank research (e.g., Nomura's CHF-intervention analysis) explicitly trade off it, and sites like snbchf.com have tracked the deposits-vs-intervention mapping weekly for over a decade.
- **Access:** Free — SNB data portal, every Monday morning; also carried on all economic calendars.

### 78. NY Fed weekly custody holdings of foreign official accounts (plus monthly TIC)
- **Signal:** A memo line in the Fed's weekly H.4.1 reports the face value of Treasuries held in custody at the New York Fed for foreign central banks. Weekly drops flag foreign-official selling — reserve liquidation to defend currencies — months before the official (and 2-month-lagged) Treasury TIC data confirms who sold.
- **Trade:** On sustained custody drawdowns, short Treasury futures (TY/US) or position for curve steepening, and trade USD/CNH and EM FX in the direction of the reserve drain; signals play out over one to several weeks.
- **Mechanism:** A central bank defending its currency sells Treasuries from its Fed custody account and sells the dollars — 'quantitative tightening' by another name — creating price-insensitive supply in the Treasury market; the custody line reveals this forced flow weekly while the seller is still trying to be discreet.
- **Evidence:** In 2015-16 traders tracked China's reserve liquidation through the weekly custody data, and Deutsche Bank's Saravelos/Winkler formalized the trade in 'The Great Accumulation Is Over... Beware QT' (September 2015), which moved the QT debate market-wide; the 2014 'Belgium mystery buyer' episode (Euroclear custody masking China) was likewise reverse-engineered from TIC custody data.
- **Access:** Free — H.4.1 memo lines on FRED (series WMTSECL1 etc.) weekly; TIC data free monthly from treasury.gov with a ~6-week lag.

### 79. Primary-dealer Treasury settlement fails-to-deliver (FR 2004 data)
- **Signal:** The NY Fed's weekly Primary Dealer Statistics report dealers' cumulative fails-to-deliver and fails-to-receive in Treasuries, agencies and MBS. Rising fails expose settlement stress: acute collateral scarcity, strategic failing, or a squeeze in a specific issue that dealers would rather not advertise.
- **Trade:** Trade the on-the-run/off-the-run spread, cash-futures basis and swap spreads when fails surge (collateral scarcity richens specific issues and distorts repo); lend the scarce issue via repo at special rates. Days-to-weeks horizon around squeeze episodes.
- **Mechanism:** When an issue trades 'special' or shorts cannot source collateral, deliveries fail and the fails data quantifies the hidden squeeze; at zero rates failing was nearly free, so fails cascaded system-wide — a direct read on what shorts and repo desks are struggling to hide until pricing snaps (specialness, basis blowouts).
- **Evidence:** After Lehman (September 2008) Treasury fails reached unprecedented, systemically threatening volumes, prompting the TMPG to impose the 3% fails charge in May 2009 — documented in Garbade, Keane, Logan, Stokes & Wolgemuth, 'The Introduction of the TMPG Fails Charge' (NY Fed Economic Policy Review 2010) and Fleming & Garbade's post-9/11 fails studies; the June 2014 10-year note squeeze was likewise read through fails data.
- **Access:** Free — newyorkfed.org Primary Dealer Statistics, weekly with a short lag; issue-level repo specialness context requires broker screens (institutional).

### 80. Daily Treasury Statement cash balance and debt-ceiling X-date arithmetic
- **Signal:** The Treasury's Daily Treasury Statement (published each business day ~4pm ET) gives the government's operating cash balance, daily tax receipts, and remaining headroom under the debt limit — the raw inputs for nowcasting the debt-ceiling 'X-date' and near-term bill supply.
- **Trade:** Trade the T-bill curve kink: short (or avoid) the specific bill CUSIPs maturing just after your estimated X-date and buy adjacent maturities, unwinding as the date resolves; post-resolution, position for the bill-supply flood (bills cheapening vs OIS). Days-to-weeks horizon.
- **Mechanism:** Faster-than-expected cash burn or weak tax receipts pulls the X-date forward, concentrating default/delay risk into a narrow window of bill maturities; traders re-running the DTS arithmetic daily reprice those exact CUSIPs before official Treasury guidance updates.
- **Evidence:** May-June 2023: the 4-week bill maturing June 6 auctioned at a record 5.84% (highest bill auction since 2000) and one-month bill yields spiked near 6-7% as DTS cash fell toward $23B; Wrightson ICAP (Lou Crandall) built its franchise on exactly this daily DTS nowcasting, and Bipartisan Policy Center X-date models were traded against all spring.
- **Access:** Free — fiscal.treasury.gov publishes the DTS daily; Wrightson ICAP's institutional commentary is the paid version of the same arithmetic.

### 81. Bank deposit and CD rate scraping (funding-desperation census)
- **Signal:** Systematic collection of posted deposit and CD rates across thousands of banks — via S&P Global RateWatch/Curinos feeds or by scraping bank websites and aggregators like DepositAccounts.com. A bank suddenly posting far-above-market CD 'specials' or leaning on brokered CDs is paying up to plug a funding hole it has not yet disclosed.
- **Trade:** Short the equity (or buy puts) of individual banks whose posted rates jump to the top of the national league table out of character, especially alongside rising brokered-deposit reliance in call reports; a two-to-eight-week trade anticipating earnings misses, downgrades, or failure.
- **Mechanism:** Deposit pricing is a bank's revealed preference: healthy franchises ride low-cost core deposits, while a bank bleeding funding must buy replacement money at whatever rate retains it — so outlier rate hikes convert private balance-sheet distress into a public, scrapeable signal weeks before quarterly disclosure.
- **Evidence:** Acharya & Mora, 'A Crisis of Banks as Liquidity Providers' (Journal of Finance 2015), documented with rate-survey data that distressed and subsequently failed banks in 2007-09 systematically offered the highest deposit rates to survive; the same branch-level RateWatch data underpins Drechsler, Savov & Schnabl's deposits-channel work (QJE 2017), and S&P acquired RateWatch in 2018 precisely because banks and investors use it to monitor competitors' funding behavior.
- **Access:** Cheap for individuals — DepositAccounts.com and Bankrate rate tables are free to scrape, and brokered-CD offer rates are visible on Fidelity/Schwab platforms; the full branch-level census (RateWatch via S&P Market Intelligence, Curinos) is institutional.

## Index mechanics, fund flows & forced buyers

### 82. Russell reconstitution preliminary add/delete lists
- **Signal:** FTSE Russell publishes ranked preliminary addition and deletion lists for the Russell 1000/2000/3000 weeks before the June reconstitution effective date (rank day in late April/May, lists posted from late May with weekly updates). Because membership is rules-based on market cap, projected adds and deletes can be modeled even before the official lists drop, revealing exactly what ~$10T+ of indexed money must buy and sell at the closing cross.
- **Trade:** Buy projected Russell 2000 adds and short projected deletes from list publication into the June reconstitution close, then unwind at or just after the effective-date closing auction; expressed in single stocks, or Russell 2000 vs 1000 futures (RTY/ES spreads) over a 2-6 week horizon.
- **Mechanism:** Index funds tracking Russell benchmarks are forced buyers of adds and forced sellers of deletes at the reconstitution closing print regardless of price; anticipatory capital buys ahead of them and supplies liquidity at the cross, so adds outperform deletes into the effective date and partially revert afterward.
- **Evidence:** Madhavan's 'The Russell Reconstitution Effect' (Financial Analysts Journal, 2003) documented large abnormal returns to the strategy; CME Group and LSEG document reconstitution day as one of the biggest trading days of the year (~$217B traded at the 2026 recon close), and the crowding of the trade is why FTSE Russell moved to semi-annual reconstitution in 2026.
- **Access:** Free — preliminary and final add/delete lists are posted on the FTSE Russell/LSEG website, and rank-day snapshots can be replicated from public market-cap data by any individual.

### 83. Leveraged ETF end-of-day rebalance flow model
- **Signal:** Daily dollar rebalance demand of leveraged and inverse ETFs, computed mechanically from public fund AUM, leverage factor, and the day's index move: both 2x/3x long and inverse funds must trade in the same direction as the day's return near the close to reset leverage. On a big down day, levered long funds sell and inverse funds also sell, making the aggregate flow calculable by mid-afternoon.
- **Trade:** On days with large index moves (roughly |move| > 1-2%), trade S&P 500 e-mini or Nasdaq-100 futures, sector futures/ETFs (e.g., SOXX/SOXL complex), or MOC orders in the last 30-60 minutes in the direction of the predicted rebalance, exiting at the close or fading the overshoot overnight; an intraday-to-overnight horizon.
- **Mechanism:** Leveraged ETF sponsors hedge with swaps whose counterparties execute delta adjustments into the close; the flow is a deterministic function of observable variables, so it is pure forced, price-insensitive demand that amplifies late-day momentum and creates overnight reversion.
- **Evidence:** Cheng and Madhavan, 'The Dynamics of Leveraged and Inverse Exchange-Traded Funds' (Journal of Investment Management, 2009) derived the rebalance formula and showed it exacerbates close volatility; the February 5, 2018 'Volmageddon' collapse of XIV/SVXY was a live demonstration of the same end-of-day rebalance mechanics in VIX futures.
- **Access:** Free — fund AUM, leverage factors, and index levels are public, and the rebalance arithmetic fits in a spreadsheet; sell-side desks (e.g., JPMorgan, Nomura) publish the same estimates for institutional clients.

### 84. NYSE/Nasdaq closing auction imbalance feeds
- **Signal:** Exchange-disseminated order imbalance messages for the closing auction, published from 3:50 p.m. ET (NYSE every second; Nasdaq every 10 seconds, then every second after 3:55), showing paired quantity, imbalance size and side, and indicative clearing price for every stock. This is a direct, real-time X-ray of MOC/LOC pressure from index funds, pensions, and mutual funds before it hits the tape.
- **Trade:** Trade the imbalanced stock (or offsetting D-orders/LOC orders into the auction) in the final 10 minutes: ride large imbalances into the 4 p.m. print, then fade the auction dislocation overnight, since closing-price deviations revert by the next open; horizon is minutes to one day.
- **Mechanism:** Benchmark-tracking institutions must transact at the official closing price, so a large one-sided imbalance forces the auction to clear away from fair value; liquidity providers who see the feed supply the other side and capture the reversion as prices normalize after the close.
- **Evidence:** Bogousslavsky and Muravyev, 'Who Trades at the Close? Implications for Price Discovery and Liquidity' (Journal of Financial Economics, 2023) document systematic closing-auction price deviations that revert overnight; NYSE itself markets a Closing Auction Imbalance Analysis tool, and electronic market makers routinely trade the feed.
- **Access:** Cheap to moderate — imbalance data is included in NYSE/Nasdaq TotalView-style feeds and is passed through by retail-accessible platforms and brokers (e.g., Interactive Brokers imbalance fields, Bookmap/dxFeed) for tens of dollars a month; free delayed summaries circulate on trading Twitter each afternoon.

### 85. The Goldman Roll (GSCI/BCOM commodity index roll window)
- **Signal:** The fixed, published schedule on which commodity index money (S&P GSCI and Bloomberg Commodity Index trackers) rolls futures from the expiring front contract to the next: the 5th through 9th business day of each month, in pre-announced 20%-per-day increments. Index AUM makes the size of the forced calendar-spread flow estimable in advance.
- **Trade:** Buy the deferred contract and sell the front contract (calendar spreads in WTI, natural gas, corn, wheat, live cattle, etc.) several business days before the roll window opens, unwinding into days 5-9 as index flow moves the spread; a 1-2 week horizon each month.
- **Mechanism:** Hundreds of billions in passive index exposure must sell the front and buy the deferred contract on known dates regardless of price, cheapening the front and richening the deferred; front-runners pre-position in the spread and exit into the forced flow.
- **Evidence:** Yiqun Mou, 'Limits to Arbitrage and Commodity Index Investment: Front-Running the Goldman Roll' (SSRN, 2010) showed front-running strategies earned Sharpe ratios up to ~4.4 over 2000-2010 and cost index investors ~3.6% annually; the effect and its subsequent crowding/decay are further documented in the commodity-financialization literature and CFTC-cited studies.
- **Access:** Free — the GSCI/BCOM roll schedules and index methodologies are public, and the trade needs only a retail futures account; index AUM estimates come from CFTC Commitments of Traders index-trader supplements, also free.

### 86. SEC fails-to-deliver files and Reg SHO threshold lists
- **Signal:** Twice-monthly SEC files listing settlement failures (shares failed-to-deliver per security per day at CNS) plus daily exchange-published Reg SHO threshold lists of stocks with persistent large fails. Spiking FTDs reveal settlement stress: naked/undelivered short exposure, ETF create-to-lend games, or borrow scarcity that precedes forced buy-ins.
- **Trade:** Buy short-squeeze candidates (equity or short-dated calls) when FTDs spike and the stock joins the threshold list — Reg SHO close-out rules force brokers to buy in fails after 13 settlement days — or trade the richening of put options in hard-to-borrow names; horizon days to a few weeks.
- **Mechanism:** Persistent fails signal that share supply is partly synthetic and that mandatory close-outs create future forced buying; when borrow becomes impossible, options market makers can no longer hedge cheaply, so puts richen and violent squeezes become mechanically more likely.
- **Evidence:** Evans, Geczy, Musto and Reed, 'Failure Is an Option: Impediments to Short Selling and Options Prices' (Review of Financial Studies, 2009) linked settlement failures to options mispricing; during the January 2021 GameStop episode, SEC data showed ~$359M of GME shares failing to deliver (Bloomberg Law), and GME/XRT spent hundreds of days on the threshold list.
- **Access:** Free — FTD files are downloadable from SEC.gov (published with a ~2-4 week lag), and threshold lists are posted daily by NYSE and Nasdaq; aggregator sites parse them at no cost.

### 87. Securities-lending market data (Ortex/S3 Partners borrow fees and utilization)
- **Signal:** Daily intraday-updated securities-lending metrics — cost to borrow, lending-pool utilization, shares on loan, and estimated real-time short interest — sourced from agent lenders, prime brokers, and broker-dealers by vendors like Ortex (IHS Markit/S&P Global data) and S3 Partners. This exposes short positioning weeks ahead of the official twice-monthly FINRA short interest report.
- **Trade:** Buy stock or calls in names where utilization approaches 100% and cost-to-borrow accelerates while shorts sit on mark-to-market losses (squeeze setups), or short names where borrow suddenly loosens after a squeeze peaks; horizon days to a few weeks.
- **Mechanism:** Borrow fee and utilization measure the true scarcity of lendable shares: when utilization hits 100%, marginal shorting becomes impossible and existing shorts face recalls and escalating financing costs, converting them into forced buyers on any rally.
- **Evidence:** S3 Partners' Ihor Dusaniwsky was the most-cited positioning source through the January 2021 GameStop squeeze (short interest >100% of float, ~$6B short losses per Bloomberg); Ortex's 100%-utilization alerts on GME/AMC are timestamped on its public feed, and academic work on the equity lending market (e.g., Cohen, Diether and Malloy) links loan-fee spikes to future returns.
- **Access:** Cheap for retail — Ortex sells individual subscriptions for roughly $30-100/month and Fintel offers a budget tier; the underlying S&P Global/S3 institutional feeds cost tens of thousands per year.

### 88. Quarter-end portfolio-pumping reversal (window-dressing fade)
- **Signal:** The systematic pattern of mutual funds marking up their own holdings with last-minute buying on the final day of quarters — visible as abnormal last-half-hour returns in small-cap, fund-crowded stocks on quarter-end days, followed by reversal at the next open. Screening for stocks heavily held by momentum/small-cap funds (via 13F/N-PORT data) identifies the likely pump candidates.
- **Trade:** Short (or sell holdings of) small-cap stocks with concentrated mutual-fund ownership at the quarter-end close and cover on day one of the new quarter; or buy them in the final hours of the quarter to ride the markup. Horizon: hours to two days around each quarter turn.
- **Mechanism:** Fund managers' flows and fees depend on quarter-end NAV prints, so some buy existing illiquid positions minutes before the close to inflate reported performance; the buying is informationless, so the price impact reverses immediately once the NAV is struck.
- **Evidence:** Carhart, Kaniel, Musto and Reed, 'Leaning for the Tape: Evidence of Gaming Behavior in Equity Mutual Funds' (Journal of Finance, 2002) documented 0.5%-2%+ quarter-end NAV inflation concentrated in small-cap funds with reversal the next morning; the SEC has since brought multiple 'marking the close' enforcement actions, and follow-up studies confirm the pattern persists in fund families and ESG funds.
- **Access:** Free — the signal is built from ordinary price/volume data plus free 13F/N-PORT holdings from EDGAR; no vendor product is required.

### 89. Month-end pension rebalancing flow estimates
- **Signal:** Estimated buy/sell pressure from defined-benefit pensions, target-date funds, and balanced mandates that mechanically rebalance to fixed equity/bond weights at month- and quarter-end: the flow is proportional to the month's equity-minus-bond return gap times rebalancing AUM. After a big equity rally, pensions must sell stocks and buy bonds into month-end, and vice versa.
- **Trade:** Position in S&P 500 e-mini futures against Treasury futures (ES vs ZN/UB) over the last 3-5 trading days of month/quarter in the direction of the estimated rebalance, unwinding in the first days of the new month; also expressible with SPY/TLT. Horizon roughly one week.
- **Mechanism:** Rebalancers trade toward policy weights at calendar dates regardless of price, and month-end institutional cash needs force additional selling of equities days before month-end with reversal after; anticipating the flow front-runs a known price-insensitive counterparty.
- **Evidence:** Etula, Rinne, Suominen and Vaittinen, 'Dash for Cash: Monthly Market Impact of Institutional Liquidity Needs' (Review of Financial Studies, 2020) documented systematic month-end return patterns from institutional flows across global markets; in late March 2020 sell-side desks (JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs) publicly estimated up to ~$850B of quarter-end pension buying of equities, a call that preceded the sharp month-end rally and was widely covered by Bloomberg/Reuters.
- **Access:** Free to replicate — the flow estimate needs only public index returns and rough rebalancing-AUM assumptions; the polished versions (Goldman, JPMorgan, StoneX estimates) circulate in institutional research but are frequently quoted in the financial press.

## Orbital & spectrum exhaust

### 90. Space-Track/CelesTrak TLE anomaly mining (drag decay, station-keeping lapses, GEO drift)
- **Signal:** Algorithmic screening of the public two-line-element catalog for changes in a satellite's orbital elements: missed station-keeping burns, onset of longitudinal drift in GEO, or accelerating drag decay in LEO. These reveal propulsion failures, fuel exhaustion and dying spacecraft days-to-weeks before the operator's press release or 8-K.
- **Trade:** Short or buy puts on the affected listed operator (SES on Euronext, VSAT, EUTLF/ETL.PA, IRDM, SATS) or its bonds/CDS within hours of a detected drift or lapse, holding days to a few weeks until the anomaly is officially disclosed and priced.
- **Mechanism:** A GEO satellite is a nine-figure revenue asset; loss of station-keeping is the physical signature of a propulsion or fuel problem that becomes an impairment charge, lost transponder revenue and possible insurance fight. Physics leaks the information before investor relations does, so the TLE watcher front-runs the disclosure.
- **Evidence:** Maneuver/anomaly detection from TLE histories is formalized in Lemmens & Krag, 'Two-Line-Elements-Based Maneuver Detection Methods for Satellites in Low Earth Orbit' (J. Guidance, Control & Dynamics, 2014) and Kelecy et al. (AMOS 2007). In the SES AMC-9 episode (June-July 2017) independent trackers saw the satellite drifting off-station and shedding objects before SES's full disclosures, and Intelsat 33e's chronic propellant leak was legible in its degraded maneuver history for years before its October 2024 breakup.
- **Access:** Free: Space-Track.org requires only a registered account for the full catalog, and CelesTrak publishes cleaned TLEs and SOCRATES conjunction data; open-source libraries (python-sgp4, Skyfield) do the propagation. The alpha is in the screening code, not the data.

### 91. Commercial optical SSA telescope feeds (ExoAnalytic Solutions) for real-time GEO breakup and debris detection
- **Signal:** ExoAnalytic operates 300+ ground telescopes that stare at the GEO belt and detect tumbling, brightness pulsing, and debris shedding from individual satellites in near real time — far finer and faster than the military TLE catalog. It is the closest thing to a live health monitor on every listed operator's most valuable assets.
- **Trade:** Event-driven short in the operator's equity or credit the moment debris is observed: SES shares/bonds (AMC-9, 2017), Telkom Indonesia ADR TLK (Telkom-1 debris event, August 2017, which knocked out thousands of Indonesian bank ATMs), Intelsat credit around the 33e breakup (October 2024, ~500 tracked fragments). Horizon is intraday to two weeks as the loss gets quantified.
- **Mechanism:** Debris shedding converts an ambiguous 'anomaly' into a probable total loss with an impairment charge, customer-migration costs and, if uninsured, a direct equity hit. The telescope network sees the fragmentation hours-to-days before the operator confirms severity, creating a window where the physical truth diverges from the public narrative.
- **Evidence:** ExoAnalytic (named vendor) publicly released video of Telkom-1 erupting debris and tracked AMC-9's fragments in 2017 (Space.com, SpaceNews), and counted ~500 debris pieces from Intelsat 33e within days of the 19 October 2024 breakup (SpaceNews) — in each case ahead of or alongside terse operator statements.
- **Access:** Institutional-only for the live feed (ExoAnalytic/COMSPOC data subscriptions sold to operators, insurers and governments); individuals get the same events with a lag via SpaceNews, Jonathan McDowell's reports and Space-Track breakup alerts for free.

### 92. FCC IBFS/ELS satellite and experimental license filings as pre-announcement catalysts
- **Signal:** New applications, Special Temporary Authorities and grants in the FCC's International Bureau (IBFS) and Experimental Licensing (ELS) databases reveal constellation plans, commercial-service approvals and secret partnerships weeks before press releases — filings are public the day they hit the docket.
- **Trade:** Buy the licensee's equity or calls on the filing/grant and exit into the official announcement: ASTS has repeatedly jumped 8-13% on FCC authorization days, and Globalstar (GSAT) was the subject of months of spectrum-filing-driven Apple speculation before the stock spiked 42% (halted for news) on the 7 September 2022 iPhone SOS reveal. Horizon: days to a few weeks.
- **Mechanism:** Regulatory approval is the binding constraint on direct-to-device and NGSO business models, so a grant converts a story stock's optionality into a dated commercial milestone; because filings precede PR, docket-watchers own the position before the headline algorithms fire.
- **Evidence:** CNBC documented GSAT's halt-and-spike when the FCC-licensed Apple service was disclosed (Sept 2022); ASTS moves of ~10% on FCC commercial-authorization days are repeatedly documented, and satellite-policy analysts like Tim Farrar (TMF Associates) built a following mining exactly these dockets. Retail communities (r/ASTSpaceMobile) now scrape IBFS daily.
- **Access:** Free: fcc.gov IBFS and ELS are searchable by anyone, support saved queries, and the FCC Daily Digest emails every action; zero-cost RSS/scraper setups are common.

### 93. Spectrum 'use-it-or-lose-it' buildout-milestone compliance and FCC enforcement letters
- **Signal:** Tracking whether license holders actually meet FCC/ITU construction and bring-into-use deadlines — coverage drive-test data, milestone certification filings, and enforcement correspondence in the FCC's ECFS docket. This is forensic detection of what a spectrum-hoarding operator is hiding: paper compliance masking an unbuilt network.
- **Trade:** Trade EchoStar (SATS) equity, options and its distressed bonds around compliance events; the May 2025 Carr letter opening a buildout inquiry cratered SATS and pushed EchoStar to skip bond coupons, then resolution via the $23B AT&T spectrum sale on 26 August 2025 sent the stock up ~70% in a day with bonds ripping alongside. Horizon: days around each docket event.
- **Mechanism:** Spectrum licenses are the entire asset value of companies like EchoStar; a revocation threat converts $30B+ of spectrum marks to near-zero, while a forced sale crystallizes the value. The FCC's inquiry letters, extension requests and milestone certifications are all public filings, so the solvency swing is legible in the docket before the equity fully reprices.
- **Evidence:** Well-documented 2025 regulatory episode: FCC Chairman Carr's May 2025 letter to Charlie Ergen questioning 5G buildout compliance, EchoStar's subsequent missed interest payments, and CNBC's report 'EchoStar stock skyrockets 70% on AT&T deal to buy wireless spectrum for $23 billion' (Aug 26, 2025); DISH's June 2023 70%-coverage sprint is the precedent episode.

### 94. FCC spectrum-auction round-by-round proceeds (Auction 107 C-band playbook)
- **Signal:** During major FCC auctions the Public Reporting System posts gross proceeds and demand after every bidding round, several rounds per day. Watching the running total against consensus reveals in real time how much cash carriers are committing — months before winners are named.
- **Trade:** As proceeds blow through estimates: short/underweight carrier credit (Verizon and AT&T bonds/CDS, since they must fund the bill), buy tower REITs (AMT, CCI, SBAC) and the clearing-payment beneficiaries (SES, Intelsat paper entitled to $9.7B in accelerated relocation payments). Positions run days to the few weeks of the clock phase.
- **Mechanism:** Auction totals are a forced-flow tell: every incremental billion bid is future bond issuance by the bidders and future capex flowing to tower and fiber suppliers, plus incentive payments to incumbent satellite operators. The FCC publishes the tape but most investors only react at the end.
- **Evidence:** Auction 107 (Dec 2020-Feb 2021) clock phase reached $80.9B — about 170% above prior industry projections per MITRE's retrospective — with Light Reading and telecom analysts (e.g., New Street Research) tracking rounds daily; Verizon raised $12B during the auction and a record ~$25B bond right after, and the $81B total was assigned 96% to VZ/T/TMUS when results were unsealed.
- **Access:** Free: auctiondata.fcc.gov and the FCC's public reporting pages update in real time during every auction; no subscription required.

### 95. FAA launch licenses, NOTAM/TFR windows and hazard-zone notices for timing rocket-stock catalysts

- **Mechanism:** For pre-revenue launch companies, each flight is a make-or-break catalyst; the regulatory paper trail (license grants, NOTAM windows) is public days in advance, letting traders own gamma before the binary event while the outcome itself resolves on a livestream faster than press wires.
- **Evidence:** CNBC documented both the SPCE +39% FAA-license day (June 2021) and Astra's -26% live-failure day (Feb 2022, stock halted mid-webcast); the FAA license modification and test-window NOTAMs preceded Virgin Galactic's May 2021 flight that triggered the license.
- **Access:** Free: FAA Office of Commercial Space Transportation license list, notams.aim.faa.gov, Coast Guard Local Notices to Mariners, and launch webcasts; community calendars (nextspaceflight.com) aggregate it at zero cost.

### 96. ADS-B-derived GNSS jamming and spoofing maps (GPSJam.org / Flightradar24 interference layer)

### 97. Space insurance premium rates and claims pipeline as an operator-stress tell
- **Signal:** The small Lloyd's-centric space underwriting market (~$550M annual premium) publishes little, but broker chatter and trade press reveal claim filings, loss ratios and rate moves within days of an anomaly — telling you whether a failure is insured, how big the claim is, and whether all operators' future insurance costs are about to jump.

## Influence-industry exhaust

### 99. Deal-specific LDA lobbying registration surges
- **Signal:** New LD-1 registrations and quarterly LD-2 spend reports filed with the Senate Office of Public Records that name a specific pending merger or bill. Unlike aggregate lobbying-intensity indices, the tell is the event: a company suddenly retaining new K Street firms or tripling spend on one named transaction reveals management's private estimate of political blockage risk.
- **Trade:** Merger-arb spread in the target's equity and options (e.g., U.S. Steel (X) during the Nippon Steel CFIUS fight) over days to a few weeks as approval odds are repriced; new registrations post within days even though LD-2 spend lags quarterly.
- **Mechanism:** Firms buy influence roughly in proportion to perceived regulatory danger, so a disclosed lobbying blitz naming one deal is an insider read on how scared (or confident) the principal is before any official decision — arbs update deal-break probability accordingly.
- **Evidence:** Nippon Steel's LDA filings showed Q3 2024 lobbying payments of $1.3M to Akin Gump — more than the prior seven months combined — targeting Treasury, CFIUS-member agencies and the Executive Office of the President while merger arbs handicapped the U.S. Steel deal (reported by The Hill, Oct 2024); the deal was widely traded as a political-risk arb situation.
- **Access:** Free: Senate LDA database (lda.senate.gov), OpenSecrets, and ProPublica's lobbying API; anyone can set keyword alerts on new registrations.

### 100. OIRA EO 12866 meeting logs on reginfo.gov
- **Signal:** The White House OMB posts every rule under OIRA review plus a public log of Executive Order 12866 meetings with outside parties (date, participants, materials). A pile-up of industry meetings on one pending rule measures both the rule's economic stakes and the odds it gets weakened or delayed.
- **Trade:** Equities of the regulated industry around rule finalization windows — tobacco (MO, BTI) during the FDA menthol-ban review, managed care (HUM, UNH) around CMS rules — on a days-to-weeks horizon keyed to the review clock.
- **Mechanism:** OIRA is the last stop before a rule lands; political-science research (Susan Webb Yackee and co-authors) found rules subject to OIRA stakeholder meetings were 29% more likely to be changed, so the meeting log is a leading indicator of dilution or delay before any announcement.
- **Evidence:** The FDA menthol cigarette ban sat at OIRA through 2023–24 accumulating an extraordinary run of logged 12866 meetings from both industry and civil-rights groups before the administration's April 2024 indefinite delay — a sequence visible in real time on reginfo.gov and covered by Roll Call and CBS; Yackee's published work documents the 29% change rate for rules with OIRA meetings.
- **Access:** Free: reginfo.gov lists rules under review and the full 12866 meeting log; no subscription needed.

### 101. FARA foreign-agent registration filings
- **Signal:** Foreign Agents Registration Act filings at fara.gov disclose which foreign governments and companies hired which US lobbyists, the contract value, and (via informational-materials filings due within 48 hours) exactly what arguments they are pushing — exhaust from sanctions-relief and trade campaigns invisible in regular LDA data.
- **Trade:** Sanctioned or tariff-exposed listed assets on a weeks horizon: LME aluminum futures and Rusal's Hong Kong-listed shares (0486.HK) / EN+ GDRs around the 2018 OFAC delisting fight; sovereign credit and single names around other sanctions-relief pushes.
- **Mechanism:** A foreign principal paying millions for US influence reveals both the stakes and the campaign's trajectory; filed talking points and meeting disclosures let traders track whether sanctions or trade decisions are bending before Treasury or Congress acts.
- **Evidence:** Mercury Public Affairs registered under FARA for EN+ (deploying ex-Senator David Vitter) during the successful campaign that led OFAC to delist En+, Rusal and EuroSibEnergo in December 2018 — filings that told the story before the delisting collapsed the aluminum sanctions premium; documented in Treasury press releases, FARA documents and Washington Post reporting.
- **Access:** Free: FARA eFile database at fara.gov is fully public and searchable, with rapid filing deadlines for informational materials.

### 102. Revolving-door regulator hire announcements
- **Signal:** Announcements that a financial firm has hired a former Federal Reserve, SEC, FINRA, CFTC, OCC or FDIC official into a senior role, captured from press releases, LinkedIn moves and revolving-door trackers.
- **Trade:** Buy the hiring firm's equity on the announcement day and hold days; the effect concentrates in top-3 executive and board-level hires at banks and broker-dealers.
- **Mechanism:** An ex-regulator carries supervisory know-how and relationships that lower expected regulatory friction, and the market caps that value immediately — while the hire itself signals the firm anticipates heavier regulatory engagement worth paying for.
- **Evidence:** Shive and Forster, "The Revolving Door for Financial Regulators" (Review of Finance, 2017): a 2001–15 sample shows +0.6% average industry-adjusted announcement-day returns, +0.8% for top-3 executive and board hires, with no such effect for other executive changes.
- **Access:** Cheap to free: firm press releases and LinkedIn are free; LegiStorm and OpenSecrets revolving-door databases are free or low-cost for systematic tracking.

### 103. Politically connected board nomination announcements
- **Signal:** 8-K and proxy disclosures of ex-politicians, ex-cabinet officials and retired military officers being nominated to corporate boards, hand-classifiable by party connection — a firm's disclosed bet on buying policy access.

- **Mechanism:** Connected directors demonstrably steer procurement: firms whose boards were connected to the party winning control of Congress in 1994 saw significant increases in federal contract allocations, so a connected nomination raises expected government-revenue odds and the market reprices at announcement.
- **Evidence:** Goldman, Rocholl and So, "Do Politically Connected Boards Affect Firm Value?" (Review of Financial Studies, 2009) documents positive abnormal returns on nomination announcements and a connected-basket repricing after the 2000 election; their companion paper (Review of Finance, 2013) ties connections to procurement contract shifts.
- **Access:** Free via SEC EDGAR 8-Ks and proxies; BoardEx-style connection databases are institutional, but hand-classification is feasible for an individual.

### 104. HSR early-termination notice feed

### 106. Rulemaking-comment astroturf forensics
- **Signal:** Bulk analysis of regulations.gov and FCC ECFS comment corpora — duplicate-text clustering, NLP template detection, and identity-fraud markers — to detect industry-manufactured 'grassroots' campaigns and thereby infer which side is covertly spending to sway a rule and how much the rule threatens them.

### 107. Section 301 tariff-exclusion grant lists with the PAC-donation tell
- **Signal:** USTR's published batches of granted and denied China tariff exclusion requests, cross-referenced with FEC/OpenSecrets campaign-contribution and lobbying data — which turned out to predict who wins relief.
