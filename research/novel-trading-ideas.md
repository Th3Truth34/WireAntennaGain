225 original short-term trading-signal ideas, brainstormed by a 16-lens agent swarm seeded with the success patterns extracted from the [100+ documented alternative-data winners](unorthodox-data-points.md) — and explicitly banned from rehashing them. Every idea specifies the raw data source, a concrete collection method, the instruments and horizon to trade, a causal mechanism, why it is plausibly under-exploited, and its main failure mode. 224 of 225 ideas were then scored by independent judge agents on **novelty** (distance from existing commercial alt-data products), **mechanism** (plausibility of the causal chain to short-term price moves), and **feasibility** (could an individual or small team actually do this) — each 1–10, total out of 30.

These are research hypotheses, not investment advice. None have been backtested.

## The 16 success patterns behind the ideas

Extracted from the documented winners and used to seed every brainstorming agent:

1. **Physical ground truth precedes official statistics** — Directly measuring the physical world (tank shadows, smelter heat, crop vigor, ship queues, flaring) reveals supply and demand days-to-weeks before survey-based official data or corporate disclosure catches up. Any lagged, sampled, or self-reported statistic can be front-run by instrumenting the underlying physical activity.
2. **Nowcast the scheduled surprise** — When a market-moving release has a known date and a consensus estimate (earnings, EIA storage, WASDE, CPI, NFP), a high-frequency proxy that tracks the same quantity (card spend, pipeline nominations, scraped prices, job postings) predicts the direction of the surprise, letting the holder position before the mechanical repricing on print day.
3. **Mandatory disclosures leak intent through obscure channels** — Legal and bureaucratic obligations (WARN notices, STOCK Act filings, court dockets, visitor logs, lobbying registers, certificate-transparency logs, domain registrations) force actors to reveal plans in low-visibility public channels before the news cycle or the action itself. Systematically scraping the obscure channel front-runs the market's eventual reaction.
4. **Latency arbitrage on technically-public information** — Information that is already 'public' but not yet disseminated (EDGAR feeds, PACER PDFs, eyewitness tweets, weekend box office known before Monday's open) rewards whoever ingests and parses it fastest, trading against prices that are stale by seconds to hours. The edge is pure speed of parsing, not exclusive access.
5. **Adjacent observers know before the market** — People one step from the business — employees (Glassdoor, LinkedIn moves), customers (Amazon reviews, gift-card dumping), and suppliers (Taiwan monthly revenue) — experience deterioration or acceleration before it reaches financial statements, and their aggregated behavior is observable. Find any population with privileged operational visibility whose collective actions leave a public trace.
6. **Forecastable physics maps mechanically to supply and demand** — Weather and natural phenomena (degree days, polar vortex, ENSO, frost, river levels, hurricanes) convert through known physical relationships into deterministic shifts in commodity supply or demand. Because meteorological models update before prices fully adjust, each forecast revision is a tradable pre-echo of the physical shock.
7. **Chokepoints turn small disruptions into nonlinear price moves** — Where supply funnels through concentrated infrastructure or geography (Cushing, Panama Canal, the Rhine, one refinery unit, one coffee belt, one anchovy fishery), modest physical disruptions produce outsized, convex price responses. Monitoring the chokepoint yields high signal-per-observation because the market's sensitivity there is extreme.
8. **Measured attention precedes retail order flow** — Retail investors research and talk before they trade, so search volume, Wikipedia views, ticker mentions, and app-store ranks are a timestamped census of order flow that arrives hours to days later. Attention spikes predict correlated buying pressure (and its crowded, reversible aftermath).
9. **Exogenous mood shocks move risk appetite, then revert** — Society-wide mood shifts from non-economic causes (sports losses, music valence, holidays, lunar phases, geomagnetic storms) get misattributed into pricing as investors' risk appetite swings, producing short-horizon abnormal returns that predictably reverse. Any measurable, exogenous mood driver is a candidate sentiment signal.
10. **Upstream activity mechanically leads downstream revenue** — Positioned earlier in a value chain, activity flows arithmetically into later-stage revenue: cardboard boxes lead goods shipments, Taiwanese suppliers lead Apple, DRAM spot leads contract ASPs, hashrate leads GPU sales. Mapping the chain and observing the earliest measurable stage gives a lead time of weeks to quarters.
11. **Digital exhaust proxies revenue in real time** — Companies shed operational byproducts online — web traffic, app DAU, e-receipts, job postings, GitHub commits, scraped prices — that correlate tightly with revenue or user metrics but update continuously instead of quarterly. Any digital trace generated as a side effect of doing business is a free mid-quarter revenue tracker.
12. **Parallel markets price truth the primary market ignores** — Freely clearing adjacent markets — gift-card resale, luxury-watch secondary prices, GPU rental rates, betting and prediction-market odds — embed real-time probability and demand information that the primary security underweights or absorbs slowly. The spread between the parallel market's implied state and the main market's price is the trade.
13. **Independent measurement where official data is distrusted** — When the canonical statistic is delayed, manipulated, or structurally incomplete (Chinese GDP and oil storage, off-warrant metal stocks, unreported refinery outages), a hard-to-fake independent gauge (satellite activity, electricity, rail freight) predicts the eventual correction and the repricing of everything keyed to the official number.
14. **Dated binary catalysts create predictable positioning flows** — Calendar events with known dates and binary outcomes (PDUFA decisions, elections, report lockups) attract systematic position-building, short covering, and hedging that generate pre-event drift and sharp post-event repricing independent of the outcome itself. The catalyst calendar, not the verdict, is the signal.
15. **Crowd-set prices embed systematic behavioral biases** — Where prices are set directly by a biased crowd (parimutuel tote odds, retail-dominated sentiment, soccer-club stocks vs bookmaker odds), documented biases like favorite-longshot and salience-driven overreaction persist, so a model estimating true probabilities harvests the gap repeatedly. Look for markets whose marginal price-setter is unsophisticated and behaviorally predictable.
16. **Marginal discretionary spending is the first domino** — The most deferrable or least visible consumer purchases — underwear replacement, champagne, strip-club tips, big-ticket-vs-lipstick substitution — inflect at the earliest onset of wallet stress or relief, before aggregate retail and income data move. Identify spending categories at the extreme margin of discretion and watch them as leading indicators of the consumer cycle.

## Unexplored territories

A completeness-critic agent reviewed all 225 ideas and flagged these whole classes of data the swarm never touched — fertile ground for a future round:

**1. Financial-system plumbing exhaust**

Financial-system plumbing exhaust — the swarm watched crypto rails but never the legacy pipes of TradFi settlement, cash logistics, and fund-flow mechanics: Fed H.4.1, discount-window, and standing-repo-facility line items as bank-stress tripwires days before regional-bank repricing; DTCC/Fed Treasury settlement-fail and delivery-fail data as collateral-scarcity and squeeze signals; ETF primary-market create/redeem basket tapes and securities-lending recall spikes ahead of hard-to-borrow squeezes; ACH return-code, NSF, and payment-processor merchant-hold rates as real-time consumer- and SMB-credit stress; armored-carrier (Brinks/Loomis) route hiring plus currency-in-circulation prints as cash-hoarding and bank-run thermometers.

**2. Government procurement & spending tape**

Government procurement & spending tape — the SAM.gov/FPDS firehose is untouched beyond one state-DOT letting idea: daily 5pm DoD contract announcements and DLA spare-parts/munitions-component solicitations as readiness-and-restock nowcasts for defense primes and their sole-source subs; BARDA/ASPR stockpile buys and CDC vaccine task orders leaking pandemic-preparedness pivots before pharma prints; FEMA pre-positioning orders (tarps, generators, meal kits) as storm-severity signals that front-run insurer and retailer moves; urgent-need J&As and sole-source justifications revealing program slips or supplier failures in black-and-white; SBIR/STTR and national-lab award tapes as earliest tells on dual-use micro-cap suppliers.

**3. Orbital & spectrum exhaust**

Orbital & spectrum exhaust — space appears nowhere in 225 ideas, yet it is both a sensor platform and a tradeable asset class: public TLE catalogs mined for drag-decay anomalies, station-keeping lapses, and GEO drift as satellite-health tells for operators (SES, Viasat, Iridium) and space insurers — a dying bird is a claims event before the 8-K; amateur satellite-tracker networks resolving deployment milestones (AST SpaceMobile array unfold, constellation phasing) days before official confirmation moves the stock; FCC/ITU orbital-slot, spectrum-coordination, and special-temporary-authority filings as capex, partnership, and launch-date leaks; launch-pad exhaust — FAA licenses, range road closures, static-fire NOTAMs, propellant deliveries — for launch-cadence nowcasts on Rocket Lab et al.

**4. Influence-industry exhaust**

Influence-industry exhaust — the lobbying-and-access layer between companies and the state (distinct from the covered political-ad and regulatory-docket ideas): new LDA lobbying registrations naming a specific bill, agency, or merger as a tell that a company suddenly fears or wants something; OIRA/EO-12866 meeting logs and review-completion dates that literally time major rule releases affecting whole sectors; FARA registrations as early warnings on sovereign deals, sanctions exposure, and foreign capital flows; comment-docket astroturf bursts (duplicate-text clustering on regulations.gov) sizing which industries are panicked about a proposal; revolving-door hires — a firm retaining the boutique of a specific ex-commissioner or ex-prosecutor — as an enforcement-risk repricing signal.

## Physical-world sensing & sensor exhaust

### 1. Hotbox Telemetry Net (rail defect-detector radio)  ·  14/30
- **Data:** Automated trackside defect detectors on US Class I railroads broadcast train speed, axle count, and length in-the-clear on AAR VHF channels (160-161 MHz) after every passing train.
- **Collection:** Place $30 RTL-SDR receivers near chokepoint main lines (Powder River Basin joint line, BNSF Southern Transcon, UP Sunset Route) and/or scrape existing railfan scanner streams on Broadcastify; run speech-to-text on the robotic announcements to build a train-count and axle-count time series per corridor.
- **Trade:** Long/short UNP, CSX, NSC, CPKC and coal names (BTU) ahead of the weekly AAR carload print and quarterly volume disclosures; grain-corridor counts also inform CBOT corn/wheat basis trades. Horizon: days to three weeks.
- **Mechanism:** Axle counts times train frequency is a direct physical census of carloads on a corridor; it arithmetically front-runs the weekly AAR statistics and the volume metrics railroads report at earnings, which drive rail-stock revisions.
- **Why under-exploited:** The data literally floats through the air for free but requires physical receiver placement or stitching together fragmented hobbyist audio feeds - far too manual and weird for institutional data teams, who buy the lagged AAR release instead.
- **Risk:** Railroads are migrating detector telemetry to digital/back-office channels and could drop voice broadcasts; sparse receiver coverage creates sampling error that a few bad corridors can dominate.

### 2. Frac Fingerprint (TexNet microseismicity)  ·  17/30
- **Data:** TexNet, the public Texas seismological network run by UT's Bureau of Economic Geology, publishes a real-time earthquake catalog and open waveforms covering the Permian Basin, where hydraulic fracturing generates detectable microseismic swarms.
- **Collection:** Pull the TexNet catalog API and FDSN waveform services daily; cluster low-magnitude events in space-time to count active completion (frac crew) clusters in the Midland and Delaware basins, calibrating against historical Primary Vision frac-spread counts.
- **Trade:** Oil-service equities (HAL, LBRT, OIH) over 2-6 weeks, and WTI Dec-Dec calendar spreads when completion activity inflects versus consensus supply-growth assumptions.
- **Mechanism:** Completions activity leads new-well production by weeks and leads the EIA-914 and STEO supply revisions by one to two months; a drop in frac-related seismic clusters means service revenue and US supply growth will print below expectations.
- **Why under-exploited:** Funds pay for frac-spread counts assembled by phone and van; almost nobody processes free public seismograms, which requires niche waveform-clustering skills rather than money.
- **Risk:** Attribution error - quarry blasts, natural seismicity, and saltwater-disposal-induced quakes contaminate the catalog, and some pads frac aseismically, so the calibration can drift badly.

### 3. Rotor Count (Gulf of Mexico crew-helicopter ADS-B)  ·  19/30
- **Data:** Bristow, PHI, and ERA helicopters shuttling crews to Gulf of Mexico deepwater platforms transmit ADS-B, visible in unfiltered feeds; sortie tempo measures platform manning, evacuation pace before hurricanes, and re-manning speed after.
- **Collection:** Query the ADS-B Exchange API for the registered offshore-helicopter fleets (tail registries are public FAA data), geofence known helideck coordinates from BOEM platform databases, and count daily sorties per platform cluster.
- **Trade:** Henry Hub and WTI futures intraday-to-one-week around storm events, plus offshore-weighted producers (MUR, TALO); the trade is fading or front-running BSEE's lagged shut-in percentage releases.
- **Mechanism:** Crews physically leave before production shuts in and physically return before it restarts; helicopter sorties therefore lead BSEE shut-in statistics by 12-48 hours, and those statistics mechanically move storm-period gas and crude pricing.
- **Why under-exploited:** Corporate-jet ADS-B is a crowded trade, but rotary-wing offshore logistics is untouched - it requires curating helicopter registries and helideck coordinates, tedious work with no vendor product.
- **Risk:** Terrestrial ADS-B coverage degrades far offshore, so distant platforms drop out unless you buy satellite ADS-B; operators also reposition helicopters for maintenance, injecting non-demand noise.

### 4. Laker Ledger (Soo Locks ore-boat counting)  ·  18/30
- **Data:** Nearly all Minnesota/Michigan iron ore reaching integrated steel mills moves on a closed fleet of ~50 US-flag lakers through the Soo Locks and Duluth harbor, both covered by public webcams and hobbyist traffic logs (BoatNerd, Duluth Shipping News).
- **Collection:** Run frame-differencing/vessel-classification on the Army Corps' Soo Locks webcam and the Duluth Aerial Lift Bridge cam, cross-checked against BoatNerd's daily passage listings, to count laden downbound ore transits per week.
- **Trade:** CLF and X equity and CME HRC futures over 1-4 weeks; downbound ore tonnage leads blast-furnace utilization and the AISI weekly raw-steel print.
- **Mechanism:** Ore deliveries are the physical input to blast-furnace steel; a slowdown in laker transits means furnace idling or inventory draw-down that shows up weeks later in AISI utilization data and steelmaker guidance.
- **Why under-exploited:** The Great Lakes system is a tiny, closed, fully observable logistics loop that no satellite vendor bothers to productize; counting a 50-ship fleet is feasible for one person but immaterial to a big fund's data budget.
- **Risk:** Small fleet means weather delays create noisy weekly counts, and CLF's vertical integration means its own shipments can decouple from spot HRC pricing; the locks close January-March, killing the signal seasonally.

### 5. Upset Detector (TCEQ emission-event filings + PurpleAir)  ·  19/30
- **Data:** Texas requires refinery and petrochemical operators to file air emission event (upset/flaring) reports to the public TCEQ Air Emission Event Report Database within 24 hours, often within hours of a unit trip; crowd-sourced PurpleAir particulate sensors near fence lines spike during the same upsets.
- **Collection:** Scrape the TCEQ AEE search interface on a 15-minute loop, parse unit names and emission volumes against a hand-built ontology of FCC/crude/coker units by refinery, and cross-confirm timing with PurpleAir API PM2.5 anomalies at sensors within 3 km of each plant.
- **Trade:** RBOB and ULSD crack spreads and refiner equities (VLO, MPC, PBF) intraday-to-days: long cracks/short the affected refiner when a large FCC or crude-unit trip is filed before newswires pick it up.
- **Mechanism:** An unplanned outage at a large Gulf Coast unit removes product supply, mechanically widening cracks once IIR or Reuters reports it; the regulatory filing plus sensor confirmation is the same information hours earlier in the chain.
- **Why under-exploited:** The database is an obscure, clunky state web app that requires refinery-unit domain knowledge to interpret; the professional channel (IIR phone surveys) is the known product, while the raw upstream regulatory exhaust goes largely unwatched in real time.
- **Risk:** Filing timeliness varies - sometimes Reuters energy reporters or refinery-town Facebook groups beat the filing - and most events are immaterial, so without good unit-size filtering the strategy drowns in false positives.

### 6. Boneyard Index (freighter-fleet ADS-B utilization)  ·  17/30
- **Data:** The global dedicated air-freighter fleet (FedEx, UPS, Atlas, Kalitta, Cargolux tails) is enumerable from public registries, and every airframe's daily flying is visible in ADS-B feeds - including tails going quiet and parking at desert storage fields (VCV, ROW, MZJ).
- **Collection:** Maintain a tail-number registry from FAA/planespotters data, pull per-tail flight hours daily from OpenSky and ADS-B Exchange APIs, and compute fleet utilization plus a count of freighters idle more than 7 days or positioned to storage airports.
- **Trade:** FDX and UPS equity/options into earnings, and transport ETF (IYT) tilts, on a 2-6 week horizon when utilization diverges from consensus air-cargo assumptions.
- **Mechanism:** Air cargo is the most demand-elastic freight mode; flight-hours flow arithmetically into cargo tonne-kilometers and integrator volumes, leading the monthly IATA CTK release and quarterly package-volume disclosures that drive estimate revisions.
- **Why under-exploited:** Sell-side and funds consume lagged IATA monthlies; building and maintaining a clean freighter tail registry is unglamorous data janitorial work with no off-the-shelf vendor, ideal for a small team.
- **Risk:** Integrators flex utilization for network-redesign reasons (FedEx's Tricolor) unrelated to demand, and oceanic coverage gaps in free ADS-B feeds understate long-haul flying unless corrected.

### 7. Lock Queue (Army Corps LPMS outage telemetry)  ·  21/30
- **Data:** The Army Corps' Lock Performance Monitoring System and public Lock Status/Queue reports show near-real-time operational status, stall events, and the number of tows waiting at every Mississippi, Illinois, and Ohio River lock.
- **Collection:** Poll the USACE LPMS/Corps Locks public web endpoints hourly for chokepoint locks (Lock 25, LaGrange, Olmsted), alerting on unscheduled outages and queue counts above seasonal norms during the fall export program.
- **Trade:** CBOT corn and soybean futures and calendar spreads, plus ADM and MOS equity, over days to three weeks when a chokepoint lock fails during harvest export season.
- **Mechanism:** Roughly 60% of US grain exports funnel through this one river system to NOLA; a stalled lock strands barge tows, collapses interior basis, delays export inspections, and forces futures spread repricing - a classic convex chokepoint response.
- **Why under-exploited:** It is a bureaucratic government telemetry system with an interface nobody wires into intraday trading; ag desks react to the same outages days later via USDA transport reports and newswires.
- **Risk:** Major failures get publicized fast, shrinking the window to hours in the worst cases; and barge traffic can reroute or wait out short outages, so only multi-day failures at true chokepoints are tradable.

### 8. Blast Census (Chilean mine-blast seismology)  ·  17/30
- **Data:** Chile's Centro Sismologico Nacional and open FDSN/IRIS stations continuously record the daily production blasts of giant open-pit copper mines (Escondida, Collahuasi, Chuquicamata), which have distinctive waveform signatures.
- **Collection:** Pull continuous waveforms via FDSN web services from stations near each pit, apply standard blast-vs-earthquake discrimination (time-of-day clustering, spectral ratios), and build a per-mine daily blast-count and energy index.
- **Trade:** COMEX HG copper futures and FCX/SCCO over 1-4 weeks, sized up during strikes, weather events, or water disputes when the market is actively arguing about whether output is actually impaired.
- **Mechanism:** Blasting is the first physical step of ore extraction; blast cadence tracks ore movement and leads Cochilco's monthly production statistics by 4-6 weeks, so a verified slowdown predicts a bullish official print and vice versa.
- **Why under-exploited:** It requires seismology skills rather than budget, and no vendor sells it - satellite products watch smelters (crowded) while the mine face itself goes acoustically unmonitored by markets.
- **Risk:** Blasts move waste rock as well as ore, so grade and strip-ratio changes can decouple blast counts from cathode output; station geometry may leave some pits poorly resolved.

### 9. Laredo Pulse (border bridge wait-time exhaust)  ·  18/30
- **Data:** CBP's Border Wait Times system (bwt.cbp.gov, with a machine-readable feed) publishes hourly commercial-truck wait times per crossing, and TxDOT/city webcams show the physical queues at Laredo's World Trade Bridge and Pharr - the chokepoint for US-Mexico trade.
- **Collection:** Poll the CBP BWT API hourly for commercial lanes at Laredo, Pharr, and El Paso, baseline seasonal norms, and alert on multi-day wait-time regime shifts; snapshot bridge webcams for confirmation.
- **Trade:** EWW and MXN/USD futures, CPKC, and JIT-exposed auto names (F, GM, BWA, APTV) over days to three weeks when a sustained inspection regime or closure appears (e.g., Texas DPS-style inspection surges).
- **Mechanism:** US auto plants run just-in-time on Mexican parts crossing at Laredo; multi-day queues cause line stoppages and lost production that surface in company statements and Mexican trade data a week or more later.
- **Why under-exploited:** The API is public but effectively unmonitored by markets because border waits feel like local news, not financial data; the causal chain to Michigan assembly lines is one step too far for most screens.
- **Risk:** The self-reported wait-time data is erratic and gamed, and most disruptions resolve before material P&L impact, so the strategy only pays on the rare sustained events.

### 10. Channel Fog (LNG-channel met-ocean halts)  ·  19/30
- **Data:** NOAA NDBC buoys, NWS marine dense-fog advisories, and Gulf pilot-association/port condition notices (Houston, Sabine, Aransas pilots suspend boardings) together signal closures of the ship channels serving Sabine Pass, Corpus Christi, and Freeport LNG.
- **Collection:** Poll the NDBC realtime API for visibility/swell at buoys off each channel, scrape NWS marine advisories and the pilots' publicly posted boarding-suspension notices, and flag multi-day winter fog regimes blocking LNG tanker movements.

- **Risk:** Salvage mix shifts (parts and catalytic-converter values, total-loss thresholds) can decouple the tape from clean wholesale; scraping third-party mirrors is ToS-fragile and Copart actively fights data leakage.

### 225. Capacity Auction Handicap  ·  17/30
- **Data:** PJM's Base Residual Auction — public pre-auction planning parameters (VRR demand curve, reliability requirement, offer caps, unit retirement lists, interconnection queue) and a scheduled results-release date that acts as a dated catalyst for independent power producers.
- **Collection:** Download PJM's published auction planning parameters and market-seller offer data, EIA-860 retirements, and load forecasts; build a supply-stack clearing-price model per delivery year and update it as parameters finalize ahead of the announced results date.
- **Trade:** Buy calls or shares in VST, TLN, CEG, NRG (or puts if the model says consensus is too hot) in the one-to-three weeks before the scheduled BRA results release, exiting on the print — the July 2024 auction's $269/MW-day surprise gapped IPP equities double digits.
- **Mechanism:** Capacity revenue flows almost dollar-for-dollar into IPP EBITDA and the auction inputs are public well before the sealed-bid result, so a competent clearing-price model is handicapping a known-date auction the equity market prices only after the fact — the wagering analog of modeling a tote pool before the off.
- **Why under-exploited:** It requires power-market supply-stack modeling that generalist equity analysts lack and specialist power traders don't apply to equities; the catalyst calendar is public but the nowcasting layer between PJM parameters and stock prices is largely unbuilt.
- **Risk:** FERC complaints, auction postponements, and mid-cycle rule changes (as happened repeatedly in PJM) can invalidate the model or delay the catalyst; after 2024's move, more desks are now attempting exactly this, decaying the surprise.
