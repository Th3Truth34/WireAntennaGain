# The 98% Question: Is Anything Tradeable With 98% Confidence of Long-Term Profit?

*A loop iteration that took the demand literally — brainstorm until something clears a 98%-confidence-of-long-term-profitability bar — and answered it rigorously. The answer reframes the question. Research notes, not investment advice.*

---

## The answer

**Nothing with a directional or predictive edge clears 98%.** The [760-candidate program](edge-hunt-conclusion.md) is a proof-by-exhaustion: zero earned capital. What *does* clear 98% is a small set of near-riskless **lender and fixed-subsidy** positions — and they clear the bar *precisely because they are not bets*.

> **The unifying principle: the 98% line is the line between lending and betting.**
> Above it, you collect the time value of money or a fixed subsidy from a non-adversarial, senior-secured counterparty (the Treasury; an issuer paying customer-acquisition cost). No one is trying to outsmart you, so there is no tail and no edge — just rent on money itself. Step into a genuine risk-transfer "house" position and you are paid a real premium but accept a fat left tail, an intermediary rake, or a tax leak — which drags you to the 85–92% band. Step further into *predicting direction* and you are at zero net after frictions. **You get paid in proportion to the risk transferred to you — which is exactly why the things that clear 98% pay you almost nothing above the risk-free rate.**

## The calibrated ladder

| 92% | — | Series I savings bonds / TIPS ladder | risk-free |
| 90% | — | Fully-paid securities lending on your own long holdings | securities-lending |

| 90% | — | Other near-riskless small-participant edges: I-bonds, bank/credit-card bonus churning, 0%-APR arbitrage, odd-lot tenders, CEF tender/activist arb, index-rebalance front-running | other |

| 30% | — | Liquidity provision / rebate capture / AMM LP | liquidity-provision |

Ranked confidence tracks *position type* exactly: **pure lender (98%+) > house on a diversifiable risk book (87–90%) > house exposed to a correlated tail or centralized-venue failure (30–60%) > directional bettor (~0% net after frictions).**

## Why no directional bet can reach 98%

A directional bet needs four things at once: a real inefficiency, that survives the full friction stack (spread, borrow, slippage, taxes, capacity), that is not already competed away, *and* that recurs reliably enough to be net-positive with near-certainty over 10+ years. Every one of the 760 candidates failed at least one gate. And structurally, a directional signal has irreducible outcome variance; a 98% bar demands near-certainty; positive expectancy with wide variance and a friction drag **cannot** reach 98%, because a run of bad draws inside the variance band is always more than 2% likely. The FTD referees crystallized it: even when the thesis is *correct* (a fails-to-deliver name really is overvalued and falls), the party who is unambiguously right is the **lender** collecting the borrow fee — not the shorter, who still bears locate cost, recall risk, negative carry, and unbounded upside. Being right about direction and profiting from direction are different things once frictions are priced.

## The three things that actually clear 98%

1. **T-bills / government money-market funds (~99.5%)** — the benchmark, not an edge. You are the first-in-line lender collecting pure time value of money; the Treasury has never missed a bill. It clears the nominal bar as decisively as anything in finance can.
2. **Series I savings bonds past the 5-year window (~99% nominal)** — a deliberate retail-only inflation subsidy; principal floored at par, composite rate floored at 0%. Genuinely "the house," but capped near \$10k/person/year — a floor, not a scalable trade.
3. **Disciplined never-carry bonus churning (~98%)** — the issuer pays acquisition cost betting the median customer revolves at ~25% APR; the never-carry churner is the adversely-selected minority the issuer loses on. Labor-intensive, clawback- and 5/24-capped, does not scale.

## The single best thing a small participant can actually do

A laddered **T-bill / government money-market position** (VMFXX or direct 4-to-52-week bills), plus maxing the **~\$10k/year I-bond** allocation held past the 5-year window. Highest defensible confidence (~99.5%), no skill edge required, no venue risk, no counterparty who can outsmart you.

**Honest ceiling:** this is capital preservation, not wealth creation. You earn the risk-free rate (~3.7–4.0% nominal, Aug 2026) and *by construction* can never beat the market, because no risk premium is transferred to you. The one thing that threatens it is not default but **inflation** — a 1970s-style stagflation stretch takes the real after-tax return negative, and that inflation tail is the single risk the entire preservation stack shares.

If a participant already holds equities and wants the best genuine "house" add-on *above* risk-free, it is **fully-paid securities lending on hard-to-borrow names they already own** — the correct FTD side, collecting scarcity rent from the shorts who pay to borrow. But name it honestly at **~90%**, not 98%: the broker keeps ~50%+ of the fee, and payments-in-lieu of dividends are taxed as ordinary income (the exact harm FINRA fined four brokers \$2.6M for in Dec 2023).

## Honest caveats

- The 98% items clear the NOMINAL, net-positive-over-10-years bar. On a REAL after-tax bar, even T-bills and TIPS do not clear 98% — a sustained inflation regime where after-tax nominal yield trails inflation is the binding, non-trivial risk, and it is the one scenario the whole preservation stack shares.
- 'Clears 98%' is not 'is a good trade.' The things that clear are the benchmark and fixed subsidies — they earn you no edge by definition. The demanding-question framing ('bet or traded with edge') has no member that clears; the only members that clear are the ones that are explicitly not bets.
- The 98% subsidy members do not scale: I-bonds cap near $10k/person/year, churning is labor-intensive and gets you clawed back / 5-24 blocked / financial-review fired once you are identified as the adversely-selected customer. They are floors under a small portfolio, not a strategy for real capital.
- The 87-90% 'house' sleeves are genuinely good and worth running collateralized in a tax-advantaged account — but each carries a correlated left tail (merger arb and buy-write both pay like short index puts and lose most in the same crash), and leverage on any of them converts you from the house into the bettor who eventually meets the tail. Do not round 90% up to 98%.
- Avoid the two traps that masquerade as 'being paid a premium' but invert for retail: AMM/passive liquidity provision (~30% — you are the bettor against informed flow via adverse selection/LVR) and crypto cash-and-carry funding capture (~60% — the near-riskless return is dominated by T-bills, and the short leg forces you to hold collateral on a centralized venue whose failure, à la FTX, is a full-capital-loss tail, not a drawdown).
- None of this contradicts the 760-candidate result — it confirms it. The absence of any 98% directional edge is the finding, not a gap in the search. The honest portfolio move is to bank the risk-free rate, add the capped subsidies, optionally run one or two diversified collateralized house-books at 87-90% sized so a bad year is survivable, and stop paying frictions to chase direction.

## Why the loop stops here

The task was: brainstorm until something is found that clears 98%. Something was found — **the risk-free rate and fixed government subsidies clear it, and nothing with directional edge does.** That is a complete, rigorously-supported answer, and it is the same conclusion the entire program reached from the other direction. Further brainstorming of directional ideas would only re-derive the null. The honest portfolio move: bank the risk-free rate, add the capped subsidies, optionally run one or two diversified collateralized "house" books at 87–90% sized so a bad year is survivable, and stop paying frictions to chase direction.
